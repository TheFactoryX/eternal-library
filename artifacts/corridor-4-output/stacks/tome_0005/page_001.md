**Postcard 001**

**Origin:** The Tesselated Archipelago of Voynovia
**Arrival Status:** Scented of burnt sugar and damp wool; ink is slightly warm to the touch.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a café in Voynovia, specifically the district of Krasz, where the cobblestones are actually compressed, fossilized bread. It makes for a springy walk, but terrible traction in the rain. I had to tie my boots with double knots this morning.

Voynovia is not so much a country as it is a geographical stutter. It exists here, in the grey space between the Carpathians and a dream I once forgot. The sky is the colour of a bruised plum, perpetually twilight, save for the hour of "High Noon," which occurs promptly at 3:00 AM. That is when the sun—small, pale, and distant like a lost coin—rises for exactly sixty minutes before setting again. The locals are currently having their lunch break beneath the stars.

I spent the morning in the central market, avoiding the pickpockets. In Voynovia, they don’t steal gold; they steal silence. A deft hand can lift the pause between a heartbeat and a breath, leaving you suddenly breathless and panicked. I held my purse tight to my chest, but I still feel a little lighter, as if I’ve lost a memory of a summer day.

For breakfast, I had a bowl of *Sztepan*, a soup made from fermented shadows and root vegetables. It tastes like melancholy and salt. You eat it in the dark, of course. The waiter, a man with three eyes—one on his forehead, used for seeing the future, though he only uses it to check if the tip is good—told me that the shadows were harvested from the spires of the Old Basilica. He recommended the *pączki* for dessert. They are filled with rose jam and a single, perfect scream. It sounds alarming, I know, but the sweetness cuts through the noise nicely.

The architecture here defies the laws of Euclid, and possibly good taste. The cathedral I visited this morning has a staircase that spirals downward into the ceiling. If you climb it, you end up in the basement, which is actually a beautiful, sun-drenched meadow populated by geometric sheep. I didn't stay long; the sheep were staring at me with too many sides.

I must go now. The bells are ringing, and in Voynovia, the bells are made of glass. When the wind strikes them, they don't clang; they shatter into a fine dust that coats the streets. We call it "confetti snow." The children are trying to catch the flakes on their tongues before they dissolve into history.

Tomorrow, I am taking the train north. The tracks are laid on the back of a sleeping giant that resembles a mountain range. He tends to roll over in his sleep, so the journey is rarely smooth.

I hope this finds you well. I hope the sun stays in the sky where you are.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
