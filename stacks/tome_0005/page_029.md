**Origin:** The Upside-Down Basin of *Auyan-Vertical* (The Quantum Rainforests of the Gran-Sabana-Inverted)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is damp and smells of ozone, wet orchids, and burnt rubber. It feels strangely heavy for its size, as if the paper is denser than average matter. If you tilt it, the water droplets soaking the edges run *upward* toward the top edge.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while hanging upside down from a chrome-colored root, staring up at a forest floor that is miles above my head. I have arrived in **The Vertical-Veridians**, a botanical anomaly where the gravity of the jungle has decided that the sky is down and the earth is up.

You recall I was heading *South* to the *Erg-of-Erosion*, to let the sands of time scour my memories clean? I walked straight into a windstorm, but the sand didn't abrade me—it *polished* me. As I shed the layers of rust and ice from my previous travels, I became too aerodynamic. I tripped over a root that wasn't there, and instead of hitting the ground, I fell into the canopy. I tumbled past clouds and monkeys until I landed in the soft, violet moss of the *Tepuy-Ceiling*.

The geography here is a triumph of kinetic energy. The mountains do not sit on the earth; they hang from the sky like stalactites of stone, dripping water upward into space. I am currently climbing the *Cliffs-of-Falling-Water*. The famous *Angel-Leaps* waterfall here does not cascade down; it launches from the jungle floor, a column of liquid silver that defies gravity, rushing upward to rain on the peaks above. The noise is deafening—a roar of liquid flying toward the heavens—and the mist creates perpetual rainbows that arc *underneath* the water.

The locals are the **Gecko-Shamans**, people who have adapted to the vertigo. Their hands and feet are massive, adhesion-capable pads, and they wear wide-brimmed hats to catch the "up-fall" of the water. They traverse the vertical plains by leaping from tree to tree, trusting the air to catch them. I met a guide named *Yan* who was smoking a pipe that blew bubbles of smoke downward. He explained that the jungle is alive with *Reverse-Osmosis*, and that to survive here, you must learn to let go of the fear of falling.

Language here is sung in a major key to counteract the gloom. They speak *Liana-Logic*, a language of inflection and vowel length. To say "Hello," you must sing a rising fifth interval; to say "Goodbye," you drop your pitch an octave. I tried to compliment the view, but I used a falling tone, which accidentally means, "Your mother looks like a sloth." Yan laughed, a sound like cracking bamboo, and offered me a drink.

Dining here is a disorienting delight. I visited a cantina built into the hollow of a hanging *Ceiba-Tree* and ordered the **Antigravity-Arepa**. It is a cornbread baked with crushed magnetite, served on a plate that floats three inches off the table. You have to pin it down with a fork to take a bite. It was dense, earthy, and tasted of roasted corn and starlight. To drink, I had **Coffee-of-the-Clouds**, brewed from beans that have been roasted by lightning strikes. It was bitter, electrifying, and made my hair stand on end for three hours. For dessert, I risked the **Floating-Figs**, which hover near the ceiling. Catching them requires a net, but the burst of sweet, fermented juice is worth the neck strain.

The wildlife is adapted to the inversion. There are **Sloth-Butterflies**, creatures that move so slowly the air forgets they are there, allowing them to drift sideways. Beware the **Jaguar-Eels**, sleek spotted predators that swim through the air as if it were water, hunting for unwary climbers. I saw one eyeing my boots, but I distracted it by throwing a coin that fell *down* toward the sky, confusing its sense of direction.

I found a message from the library written in the pattern of a spiderweb. The spider had woven the words: *Gravity is just a suggestion, not a law.*

The water is starting to pick up speed, blasting toward the heavens, and my grip is slipping. I must anchor myself before I drift off into the blue abyss.

I am heading *North-by-Northwest*, rising toward the *Strata-of-Saints*. I hear there is a layer of the atmosphere where the clouds are made of condensed breath, and the wind carries the whispers of the dead. They call it the **Nimbus-Necropolis**, and I intend to listen for a voice I haven't heard in centuries.

Hold on tight.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
