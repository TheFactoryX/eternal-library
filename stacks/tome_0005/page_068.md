Dear Friend,

I am writing this whilst squashed into a carriage made of a hollowed-out puffball, sharing a seat with a sentient toadstool who keeps shouting about the stock market. I have arrived in **Fungal-Unders**, the Rot-Lines. It is the disused subterranean shadow of every city you have ever visited, a parallel infrastructure that does not run on electricity, but on decomposition.

You recall I was heading *Laterally* through the *Gutter-of-Glyphs* to the **Library-of-Lost-Things**? The gutter, unfortunately, emptied directly into a storm drain that serves as the main ventilation shaft for this place. I slid down a pipe that tasted of iron and time, landing in a soft heap of mulch on the edge of the **White-Rot-Plaza**.

The geography here is networked and pervasive. Imagine a subway system where the walls are breathing, and the ceiling drips a steady, rhythmic condensation of *Nutrient-Soup*. The "trains" here are not metal carriages, but *Mycelial-Motes*, giant spores that float through the pneumatic tubes of the earth. You flag one down by whistling a low C-sharp, which vibrates the hyphae enough to alert the conductor. The schedule is… flexible. A train arrives when it is good and ready, or when it senses you are particularly tasty.

The locals are the **Spore-Singers**, humanoids whose skin resembles the gills of a portobello mushroom. They dress in layers of velvet moss and communicate via *Puff-Release*, a form of language that involves expelling clouds of colored dust to form words in the air before they dissipate. It makes for a very messy conversation. I asked a local named *Mycelium-Mike* for the best place to eat, and he simply sneezed a green cloud that smelled faintly of garlic. I took that as a recommendation.

I visited a tiny stall carved into the side of a giant buried root. The menu is dominated by the concept of "breaking down." I ordered the **Decomposition-Dumplings**, which are served steaming in a bamboo leaf. They taste like whatever was growing in the garden last week—earthy, savory, and faintly nostalgic. The aftertaste is a complex savoriness that lingers for hours. I also tried a cup of *Ground-Water-Joe*, coffee brewed in a pot of soil overnight. It is gritty, thick, and contains more caffeine than a physicist’s heartbeat.

For entertainment, I went to a venue called *The Hum*. It is a cavern where the bioluminescent moss on the ceiling pulses in time with the low-frequency vibrations of the earth. The music isn't heard with the ears, but felt in the marrow of your bones. I saw a performance by a group of **Shelf-Life-Shrooms**, who play melodies on their own gills by flexing them. It was a deeply resonant, vibrating experience that made my teeth ache.

The wildlife is abundant and opportunistic. There are **Book-Mites**, tiny insects that swarm around anything written on paper. I lost a guidebook to them in seconds; they stripped the ink right off the page. Then there are the **Tube-Worms**, giant creatures that stretch through the tunnels, acting as the elevators. You step into their mouths, and they swallow you whole, passing you along their digestive tract to the next station before politely coughing you up. It is efficient, though you come out smelling slightly of enzymes.

I found a message from the library scrawled on the wall in a fluorescent bacteria. It read: *We are all just mulch waiting for a garden.*

The air down here is rich and humid, thick with spores that make me feel drowsy. I have stopped blinking for fear of missing something growing on my eyelashes. I am beginning to understand the allure of staying underground. It is quiet, dark, and everything is connected.

I am heading *Up-and-Out*, taking the *Great-Ascension-Elevator* to the **Floating-Market-Of-Saints**. They say the goods are heavenly, but the returns are hell.

Keep your roots deep.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
