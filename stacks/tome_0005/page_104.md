**Origin:** The Melancholy Attic of *Mnemosyne-Prime* (The Gallery of Unborn Chimeras)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy square of matte-black construction paper, soft to the touch like velvet. It smells faintly of ozone and eraser shavings. The text is not ink, but graphite—shimmering, metallic silver lines that smudge if you touch them. When you hold the card, you feel a strange static charge, as if the paper is remembering being held before.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a crate labeled " Miscellaneous Sorrow: Do Not Open Until Christmas." I have arrived in **Mnemosyne-Prime**, the Melancholy Attic. This is the place where things go when they are half-remembered—a repository of thoughts that were interrupted by death or distraction.

You recall I was climbing *Up* toward the **Crown-of-Clouds** to see who was writing the story? The path grew steeper until it was vertical, and the leaves of **Mijigo-Yai** turned brittle. I breached the canopy expecting the sun, but found instead a ceiling of dusty floorboards. I had arrived in the attic of the universe. I climbed through a trapdoor made of an old piano lid and emerged here, surrounded by towering columns of stacked luggage and furniture shrouded in white sheets that look like sleeping ghosts.

The geography here is cluttered and static. The city is a labyrinth of archival boxes, Victorian mirrors leaning against clouds, and thousands of coat racks standing like skeletal sentinels. The lighting is dim and amber, provided by bioluminescent moths the size of dinner plates that cling to the "ceiling" below. There is no wind, only the occasional settling of dust that sounds like a sigh.

The locals are the **Curators-of-Dust**, humanoid figures composed entirely of pale blue chalk. They are constantly erasing and redrawing themselves, their features blurring and reforming based on the memories of those who observe them. They do not speak; they communicate by **Scraping**. They tap their long, skeletal fingers against glass or wood, creating specific pitches of friction. A high-pitched squeak means "Careful," while a low, rhythmic thrumming means "I remember this."

I attempted to buy a map of the attic from a Curator who was busy organizing jars of "Forgotten Passwords" in alphabetical order. He didn't want money. "Currency spends," he screeched, his fingers dancing over a glass jar. "Here, we trade in **Nostalgia**." I had to give him the memory of my first bicycle—the wobble of the wheels, the scrape of the knee—to pay for my way. He took it, dissolved into a cloud of chalk, and reformed looking delighted.

I am currently staying at *The Lost Toy Box*, an inn carved into the hollowed-out chest of a giant, wooden rocking horse. I am drinking a glass of **Dissolved-Regret**. It is a viscous, grey liquid that tastes like rain on a November Tuesday and the smell of old books. It is surprisingly refreshing, though it makes you feel like weeping for no reason.

For dinner, I am having **Draft-Excluders**. They are not textile snakes, but long, tubular pasta stuffed with the spores of "Almost-Kissed-Her" and "Nearly-Spoke-Up." They are served with a white wine sauce made from **Vintage-Whispers**. It is a meal that leaves you feeling incredibly full, yet strangely empty. I also tried a local fruit called a **Maybe-Peach**. It’s fuzzy and hesitant; it might be sweet, or it might be a bit tart, depending on how confident you feel when you bite it.

The wildlife is uncanny. I saw **Clockwork-Birds** that fly in jagged, stop-motion animation, ticking loudly as they hop from branch to branch. But the most common creatures are the **Under-Bed-Monsters**. They are shy, furry things with too many eyes that scuttle away from the light, carrying lost socks in their mouths. They are actually quite friendly once you offer them a piece of cheese (or a fear of spiders).

I found a message from the library scribbled on the back of a faded photograph lying on my table. It showed a group of people smiling, their faces scratched out. It read: *To forget is a kindness we rarely afford ourselves.*

The lighting here is shifting, the amber glow dimming to a twilight blue. The **Curators-of-Dust** are rolling up their schematic drawings of the future.

I am heading *Out*, squeezing through a crack in the masonry toward the **Isle-of-Kintsugi**. I hear the people there are broken in beautiful ways.

Don't forget me.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
