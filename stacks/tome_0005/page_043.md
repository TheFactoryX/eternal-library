**Origin:** The Sultanate of *Zaman-Zar* (The Oasis of Echoing Glass)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a hexagonal tile of sea-green glass, warm to the touch, as if it has been sitting in a sunbeam. It smells strongly of cardamom, rosewater, and ozone. When tilted, the text inside shimmers like a heat mirage.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a cushion piled high with satin bolts, sipping tea that tastes of violet and static electricity. I have arrived in **Zaman-Zar**, the Glass Fortress, a city located in the eye of a hurricane that never touches the ground.

You recall I was heading *South-by-Southwest*, seeking the *Vertical-Veil* to reverse the flow of a waterfall? I climbed down the throat of the earth, expecting to find water rushing upward. Instead, I found a layer of the atmosphere where time had pooled like rainwater in a tarp. I fell through a hole in the calendar and landed in a desert where the dunes are made of pure, hourglass sand that remembers every footstep ever taken.

The geography here is crystalline and preserved. I am currently in *Zaman-Zar*, a domed metropolis built entirely of translucent walls and floors. The city floats on a giant disc of obsidian. The sun here does not move; it is a fixed, diamond-hard point in the sky that casts no shadow, only a sharp, high-contrast light that reveals every flaw. Because of this, the concept of "secrets" does not exist here. The walls show all; the floors reflect all. To hide anything is considered a sin against the light.

The locals are the **Farrash-Walkers**, a people who wear masks of polished brass to hide their expressions, as their faces are constantly illuminated by the glare of their city. They walk barefoot to feel the vibration of the earth's rotation. I met a carpet-mender named *Kalila* who was untying a knot in a tapestry that depicted a war that hasn't happened yet. "The threads are tense," she said, her voice sounding like the chime of a silver bell. "The pattern is fighting the weaver."

Language here is reflective and evasive. They speak *Maraya-Mubin*, the Tongue of Mirrors. Every word must be spoken in a sentence that mirrors its grammatical structure backward. To say "Hello," you must say "Peace be with you," and then quickly whisper "You be with peace," to balance the equation. To say "Goodbye," you simply cover your eyes; acknowledging an ending makes it real. I tried to compliment Kalila on her weaving, but I forgot to mirror the sentence, and accidentally told her that her carpet was a "bird of broken dreams." Thankfully, in *Zaman-Zar*, insults are considered just another form of truth, and she took it as a constructive critique.

Dining here is a blinding affair. I visited a café where the tables are slabs of clear crystal overlooking the deep desert. I ordered the **Mutton-of-Mirages**, a dish where the meat appears to be a large portion but shrinks as you look at it, forcing you to eat quickly before the flavor vanishes entirely. It tasted like smoke and nostalgia. I also tried the **Pilaf-of-Paradox**, a rice dish that contains two grains that occupy the same space. Eating it makes you feel incredibly full, yet ravenously hungry, all at once. To drink, I had a **Sherbet-of-Shadows**, a chilled drink so black it absorbs the light around the glass. It refreshes the body but momentarily darkens the soul.

The wildlife is deceptive and camouflaged. There are **Sand-Stalkers**, lizards made of living glass that change color to match the anxiety of the observer. I saw a **Phoenix-of-Foil**, a bird made entirely of gold leaf that flies by catching the updrafts of hot air rising from the arguments of politicians. It burns up instantly if anyone tells a lie.

I found a message from the library etched onto the lens of my own spectacles. It read: *Transparency is the only armor that never cracks.*

The glare here is giving me a headache, and the endless visibility is exhausting. I am tired of seeing everything, including the back of my own head reflected in the pavement.

I am heading *Into-the-Fog*, trekking towards the *White-Waste*. I hear there is a valley where the air is so thick you can carve it into blocks and build houses. They call it the **Nebula-Nest**, and I intend to sculpt a shelter from the breath of a cloud.

Close your eyes.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
