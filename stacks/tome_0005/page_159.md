**Origin:** The Numerical Wastes of *Al-Jabr-Al-Mantiq* (The Algebra of Dunes)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a sheet of heavy, cream-colored vellum that feels slightly warm to the touch, as if it has been baking in a sun that doesn't exist. The edges are charred. The ink is not ink, but a fine, black powder—rubbed charcoal—that smudges if you touch it, smelling faintly of sulfur and ozone. If you hold the card at an angle, the mathematical symbols in the background seem to rearrange themselves into new equations.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a dune that is trying very hard to remain a triangle, despite the wind's insistence on curves. I have arrived in **Al-Jabr-Al-Mantiq**, the Algebra of Dunes. This is a place where the physical landscape is dictated by the rigidity of abstract mathematics, a desert where the sands are compelled to obey the laws of logic rather than gravity.

You recall I was heading *To the Library of Lost Libraries*? I was walking through the gap between two seconds, just as the instructions on the femur suggested, when I tripped over a parenthesis. The gap turned out to be a fraction, and I fell through the denominator, landing here. The library will have to wait; here, the very ground is a puzzle.

The geography is Euclidean and unforgiving. The dunes here do not shift randomly; they slide along vectors. I am currently in the **Basin of Negative Roots**, a valley where the shadows cast by the rocks are longer and darker than the objects themselves, implying a light source from a dimension perpendicular to our own. To the east lies the **Peak of Asymptote**, a mountain that gets infinitely taller but never quite reaches the summit, no matter how high you climb. I have been watching climbers for days; they look like ants stuck on an infinite staircase.

The locals are the **Geometers**. They are robed figures who wear masks of polished brass etched with constantly changing formulae. They move with right angles; they do not walk so much as pivot. Their hands are long, compass-like instruments used to measure the truth of the earth. They speak a language called **Logic-Chant**. It sounds like the clicking of an abacus combined with the rushing of wind through a crack in a door. They do not greet you; they simply calculate your trajectory and nod. They greeted me with *“X-mun-d’la,”* which roughly translates to *"The variable of the path is unknown."*

They are obsessed with **proof**. I tried to pay for tea with a coin, but the merchant refused. He demanded I solve a riddle: *What is the weight of a shadow?* I had to stay up all night deriving the equation before he would serve me. The tea tasted like hot copper and satisfaction.

I am dining on **Radical Roots**. They are tubers that grow in perfect squares (2x2, 3x3). When you boil them, they absorb the water and expand into complex, interlocking shapes. They taste earthy, like the smell of rain on hot asphalt. For the main course, I am having **Integral-Goat**. It is a stew where the meat is cut into infinitely thin slices (ribbons of lamb, shaved so fine they are almost transparent) and summed up into a finite, delicious volume. It is served over a bed of **Fractional Rice**, grains that have been broken down into smaller and smaller pieces until they become a creamy paste.

The wildlife is… calculated. The **Fractal-Scorpions** are fascinating. They possess the ability to self-replicate when attacked; if you cut off their tail, the tail grows into a new, smaller scorpion, and the original grows a new tail. A single scorpion can become a colony in moments. I keep my distance. Then there are the **Camel-Bards**, camels that do not spit, but instead recite poetry—specifically, they repeat rumors they have heard from other travelers. I learned from one that the price of silk is down in the north, and that a king has lost his temper.

I found a message from the library scrawled on a flat slate near an oasis. It was written in coordinates rather than words: *0,0,0. You are here, but are you real?*

The sun is setting, and the shadows are lengthening into irrational numbers. The triangles of the dunes are blurring into soft, round chaos. I must fold myself into my tent before the geometry shifts and I find myself divided by zero.

I am heading *To the Hanging Gardens of Babylon-Next-Door*. I hear they are suspended in a time loop where it is always the spring of the world. I hope I remember my watering can.

Mind the variables.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
