Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the porch of a cabin that is simultaneously burning and frozen. It is a delightful paradox. I have arrived in **Is-Lys**, the Archipelago of Frozen Echoes. It is a place of stark, blinding whites and deep, dangerous blues, where the horizon has forgotten its purpose.

You recall I was heading for the Canyon of Resonance, seeking the song of the earth? I took a shortcut through a mirror and fell out of the side of a glacier. The air here is so crisp it feels like inhaling broken glass. It hurts, but it wakes you up.

The geography here is fractal and recursive. The fjords do not flow to the sea; they spiral inward toward the center of the self. The mountains are not made of rock, but of **Hardened Silence**. If you strike them with a pickaxe, they do not clang; they shatter into a thousand arguments you had with your father. I climbed a peak named *Mount Nostalgia* yesterday; the view was breathtaking, mostly because the thin air deprives you of oxygen and makes you sentimental about your childhood sled.

I am currently staying in the village of *Hvergelmir-Orroboros*, a cluster of igloos carved from glacial blue ice, illuminated from within by the slow burn of *permafrost-fire*. The locals are the **Inuit-Philosophers**, a stoic people who wear parkas made of woven shadows. They believe that warmth is a distraction from the truth. They greet each other by freezing a single breath in mid-air and inspecting the shape of the ice crystal to judge the other's character.

Language here is physical and arduous. They speak *Glacial-Creak*, a language of slow grinding sounds and sudden snaps. To say "Hello," you must rub two dry ice cubes together until they squeak. To say "I love you," you must build a small fire and let someone else stand in front of it. I tried to ask where the bathroom was, but I accidentally used the inflection for "Does this iceberg make me look fat?" I was promptly thrown into the ocean for being "too grounded."

Dining here is a test of endurance. I visited a hall called *The Long Winter* and ordered a plate of **Pickled Epiphanies**. They are served raw, frozen solid in blocks of amber ice. You have to suck on them until they melt, and suddenly, you realize why your last relationship failed. It was spicy, with a hint of "I should have listened more." To warm up, I drank a mug of *Akvavit-Eternity*. It is a spirit that doesn't just warm your stomach; it warms your past, present, and future simultaneously. I saw my own grave, and it looked lovely.

The wildlife is resilient and uncanny. I saw a herd of **Memory-Moose**, great beasts with antlers made of branching coral that looks like neural networks. They don't browse for leaves; they browse for memories. One ate a regret I was carrying about a stolen lunchbox in third grade. I felt lighter immediately. Then there are the **Aurora-Wolves**, predators that hunt during the Northern Lights. They are translucent, indistinguishable from the sky, and they howl in frequencies that cancel out sound. I didn't hear them, but I felt a pressure in my ears, and then my gloves were gone.

I did find a reference to the library on a standing stone. It was runic inscription that only appears when the temperature drops below forty degrees. It read: *The cold is not the absence of heat. It is the presence of clarity.*

The ice is shifting, and the fjords are beginning to un-spiral. I must pack my furs. The nights here last for six months, and I have seen enough of my own reflection in the dark.

I am heading *East*, toward the sunrise. I hear there is a garden where the flowers are made of stained glass and bloom only when you tell them a secret. They call it the **Conservatory of Prismatic Lies**, and I intend to plant a seed.

Stay warm, but keep your mind sharp.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
