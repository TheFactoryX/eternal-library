**Origin:** The Abyssal-Press of *Spongheim* (The Rinse-Cycle)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a five-inch square of soft, translucent material that feels like living skin. It is perpetually damp, and holding it leaves your hand wet with a substance that smells of seawater and starch. If you squeeze it, the text ripples and distorts, slowly bouncing back to its original shape. The ink is a deep, rich indigo that tastes of blueberries if you touch it to your tongue.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a café table submerged beneath three miles of heavy, woolen water. I have arrived at **Spongheim**, the capital city of the Rinse-Cycle. This is the place where the world goes to be cleansed, wrung out, and hung up to dry. It is wet, obviously, but not in the way you know. The water here has a nap to it, like velvet or corduroy. Swimming through it feels like pushing through a heavy curtain.

You recall I was heading *Through the Wringer* to find where the colors of the world are separated? I successfully navigated the *Spin-Dry Straits*, a perilous current where gravity rotates every thirty seconds, throwing everything not bolted down into a chaotic tumble. I held my breath—and my lunch—until I was deposited here, in the calm, soapy waters of the Silt-Sea.

The geography here is fibrous and absorbent. The ocean floor is not made of rock or sand, but of compressed lint from a million thousand lost socks. It is soft and yielding, and walking on it feels like walking on a deep pile carpet. Above me, the sky is a swirling vortex of pastel colors—Pinks, Cyans, and Magnentas—swirling together in a vortex of loose dye before they settle into the strata below. I am currently dining in the **Coral-Felt Reefs**, vast branching structures made of scratchy wool that provide a home for the local fauna. To the north lies the **Button-Abyss**, a deep trench where the giant plastic fossils of the Ancients (specifically, the size 48 coat buttons of the Lost Giants) are buried.

The locals are the **Weft-Walkers**. They are tall, spindly beings composed entirely of dried seaweed and knotted twine. They move with a fluidity that makes my human joints feel rusty. They wear clothes made of woven current and light. They communicate through **Friction-Speak**, rubbing their limbs together to create a dry, squeaking sound, like two balloons being rubbed against your hair. A high-pitched squeak signifies danger, while a low, rhythmic thump means "I am content." I tried to converse with a waiter, but he just rustled his sleeves and poured me another glass of sparkling tide-water.

I am currently staying in *The Soak-House*, a hotel made entirely of sea sponges the size of city blocks. The rooms absorb you when you enter; you have to wring out the walls to make space for your bed. It is incredibly cozy, though I worry about shrinking in the heat. The concierge is a large, friendly Barnacle who holds your luggage in his mouth.

I am dining on **Algae-Agar-Agarp**. It is a gelatinous cube served on a bed of kelp that tastes exactly like a hug from someone you haven't seen in ten years. It is salty, sweet, and dissolves before you can chew it. For the main course, I had **Rust-Risotto**, a gritty dish made from oxidized iron shavings scraped from sunken shipwrecks. It tastes of blood and pennies, but it sits heavy in the stomach. For dessert, I am having **Puffer-Pudding**, a sweet soufflé that expands in your mouth to fill your entire throat, forcing you to breathe through your ears until it melts.

The wildlife is… complicated. The primary grazers are the **Carpet-Sharks**. They are not dangerous, but they are abrasive. They have skin made of coarse shag, and they swim along the ocean floor, vacuuming up the dust-bunnies that settle there. If you pet them, you might get a static shock that lasts for hours. The apex predator is the **Needle-Maw**, a terrifying creature that looks like a floating thimble with a single, razor-sharp point for a beak. It hunts by unraveling its prey, pulling at a loose thread until the victim comes apart at the seams. I saw one unravel a jellyfish yesterday; it just came undone like a cheap sweater.

I found a message from the library embroidered on a patch of sea-grass. It read: *You are not what you have lost, but what remains after the wash.*

The tide is turning, and the water is beginning to thicken into a starch. I must move before I stiffen up.

I am heading *To the Press*. I intend to find the great iron weights that squeeze the world flat, and see if I can slip between the pages. I hear there is a library on the other side of the paper.

Keep your head above water.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
