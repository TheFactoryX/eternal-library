**Origin:** The Crystal Veldt of The Great Rift-Inversion (Formerly Zambia, Currently Elsewhere)
**Arrival Status:** The card is rigid and hums with a low voltage; smells of ozone, burnt grass, and rain; the ink shimmers like oil on water; holding it makes your hair stand on end.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the porch of a lodge built entirely inside the ribcage of a lightning bolt. I have arrived in **The Circuit-Savanna of Bantu-Arc**, a land where the biological and the electrical have decided to merge.

You recall I was heading for the gravitational garden near the black hole? I took a shortcut through a wormhole, but the navigation system glitched and I fell out of the sky near the equator. I landed in a tree that isn't made of wood, but of fiber-optic roots and copper leaves. This place looks like the Africa you know, but it is currently undergoing a very aggressive software update.

The geography here is conductive. The ground isn't soil; it's a massive, semi-conductive lattice of crystal and silicon. The rainy season has just begun, but here, the rain doesn't fall—it *downloads*. When a storm front moves in, you don't just get wet; you get heavy. The air fills with thick, glowing packets of data-water. When it hits the ground, the data unzips into trees. The acacia trees here aren't plants; they are living servers. They photosynthesize sunlight and Wi-Fi signals. I tried to connect to the network of a particularly large Baobab and accidentally downloaded the history of the universe in .zip format. It gave me a headache.

I am currently in the **Okavango-Buffer**, a delta that floods and drains not with water, but with raw energy. The water here is viscous and warm, humming with electricity. Swimming in it is exhilarating but dangerous. If you stay in too long, you start picking up radio stations in your teeth.

The wildlife is spectacular and highly updated. I encountered a herd of **Buffer-Gnus** (wildebeest). They are bio-mechanical grazers that migrate not for food, but for bandwidth. They move in perfect synchronization, creating a local area network as they run. I saw a pride of **Lions-Firewall** stalking them, but the pride’s connection lagged, and the gnus escaped into the cloud layer.

The local people are the **San-Source**, the original programmers of the veldt. They wear cloaks made of digital hide and carry spears tipped with lasers. They do not speak; they *code*. They communicate by tapping rhythms on the ground that alter the reality around them. I watched a hunter gesture at a stone, and it compiled into a spear. I tried to ask for directions, but I used the wrong syntax and accidentally turned a passing beetle into a toaster. I felt terrible, but the beetle made excellent toast.

Dining is an electrical affair. Last night, I had **Circuit-Board Kabobs**. The meat is grilled over a fire of burning routers, imparting a smoky, cybernetic flavor. It tastes like pork and nostalgia. For dessert, I had a **Data-Fruit**, a berry that bursts in your mouth with a flash of blue light and a single, random fact. I learned that the capital of Bhutan is Thimphu, and then I immediately forgot it again.

The most impossible feature of this place is the **Thunder-Herding**. The San-Source have domesticated the lightning. The storms are not random weather events; they are livestock. The herdsmen use magnetic staffs to guide the lightning bolts into corrals made of copper wire. They milk the lightning for its charge, which powers the cities that float in the stratosphere. I saw a young boy trying to break a wild bolt of electricity. It bucked and sparked, knocking him into a tree that apologized and caught him.

I did, however, find a connection to home. I met a San-Source elder wearing a necklace made of glass beads. I recognized the pattern immediately—it was the same Morse code the stone lion from the Time River was humming. He told me the beads sing the song of "The One Who Waits." It seems my reputation for being lost has preceded me.

The sun is setting, and the sky is pixelating into a deep, digital violet. The air is thick with incoming data-rain. I need to find shelter before the bandwidth gets too high; I don't want to buffer.

I am heading South now, following the old undersea cables that walk on land. I hear there is a city built on the concept of a **Hairpin Turn**, where if you look back, you are already facing forward.

Stay grounded.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
