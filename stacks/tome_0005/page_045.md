**Origin:** The Resonant Dunes of *Qira-Maqam* (The Library of Broken Winds)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged shard of black vinyl, grooved and warm. It smells of oud, dust, and old memories. When you run a fingernail across the surface, it plays a faint, crackling melody of a string instrument tuned to a scale that makes your nose itch.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the ledge of a balcony carved entirely into the spine of a petrified giant. I have arrived in **Qira-Maqam**, the City of Discarded Frequencies, located in a canyon where the wind never stops singing the songs it heard three thousand years ago.

You recall I was heading *West-by-Northwest*, seeking the *Melody-Morgue* to walk on streets paved with vinyl? I hopped off the train at the last stop, expecting to hear a symphony. Instead, I was deafened by the silence. The silence was so loud it shattered my hearing for an hour. When my ears adjusted, I realized the silence was actually a low-frequency hum vibrating through the soles of my boots. I followed the vibration until I found a canyon of black stone, spiraling upward like a drill bit, piercing the sky. The air here tastes like cinnamon and ozone.

The geography is auditory and vertiginous. **Qira-Maqam** is built on the principles of acoustics, not architecture. The minarets are not tall towers but giant tuning forks driven into the rock; they hum in the breeze. The streets are laid out like a musical staff, and you must walk in time to the city’s rhythm (a slow, swinging 6/8 time) or risk stumbling. If you walk off-beat, the ground becomes sticky, like chewing gum, to correct your tempo. I saw a traveler who tried to run; he was immediately swallowed by a saxophone-shaped pothole and spat out three days later, musically educated and slightly moist.

The locals are the **Maqam-Makers**, people with skin the color of dark wood and ears that are distinctly pointed, capable of hearing the grass grow. They wear coats made of woven cassette tapes that rustle when they move. I met a musician named *Razul* who was playing the *Oud-of-Echoes*, an instrument made from the wood of a tree that fell in a forest no one heard. "Music is not created here," he told me, plucking a string that sounded like a raindrop hitting a tin roof. "It is excavated. We dig for the sound hidden inside the silence."

Language here is melodic and dangerous. They speak *Nagham-Siya*, a dialect where you cannot speak a lie. If you try to utter a false word, your voice produces a dissonant chord that physically hurts the listener. To say "Hello," you sing a minor third interval; to say "Goodbye," you must hum a descending chromatic scale. I tried to compliment Razul on his playing, but I hit a wrong note (I was a bit flat) and accidentally insulted his mother’s cooking. The dissonance was so sharp it shattered a nearby glass of mint tea. Razul merely laughed in a perfect major chord.

Dining here is a sensory synthesis. I visited a café suspended by giant copper wires over the abyss. I ordered the **Tagine-of-Timbre**, a stew where the meat is so tender it melts on the tongue, but the spices change flavor depending on what music is playing. When the drum beat was fast, the lamb tasted like chili; when the flute slowed down, it tasted like honey and apricots. I also tried the **Baklava-of-Bits**, a pastry made of layers of phyllo dough filled with microchips and chopped nuts. When you eat it, you hear the entire history of the internet in your head, scrolling by like a ticker tape. To drink, I had a **Coffee-of-Crescendo**, a small cup of espresso that starts cold and gets hotter with every sip, ending with a steam whistle that blows the hat off your head.

The wildlife is rhythmic and peculiar. There are **Locusts-of-Lydian**, insects that swarm in perfect harmony, creating a buzzing chord that never resolves. I saw a **Camel-of-Carriage**, a beast with humps shaped like subwoofers that walks with a heavy, bass-heavy thump that clears the sand in front of it. Snakes here don't slither; they undulate in sine waves, vibrating through the sand like electric currents.

I found a message from the library etched into the groove of a spinning golden disc lying in the street. It read: *Silence is just a song waiting for its turn.*

The noise here is beautiful, but my feet are sore from keeping time, and I can’t get the tune out of my head. I need a place where the air is still and the colors don't hum.

I am heading *Straight-Up*, climbing the *Stairway-of-Stars*. I hear there is a garden located in the thermosphere where the flowers bloom only once a century and immediately turn into diamonds. They call it the **Orchard of Hard Vacuum**, and I intend to steal a breath of fresh, thin air.

Keep an ear to the ground.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
