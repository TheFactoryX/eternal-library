Dear Friend,

I am writing this while dangling my legs over the edge of a floating island made primarily of compressed molasses and driftwood. My boots are coated in a layer of sticky gold, and I have successfully traded my heavy wool coat for a shirt woven from spider-silk and sea-spray. I have arrived in **Sirenum-Rift**, the Shattered Sargasso. This is the place where the ocean decided it was tired of being wet and tried to become a melody instead.

You recall I was heading *South-West, but also Backwards*, sledding across the **Sea-of-Suspended-Seconds** to find a place that exists only in memory? The current was stronger than I calculated. I sledded straight through a wall of static—which sounds much louder than it looks—and tumbled out of the sky into a warm, turquoise vat of saltwater. I sank, but I didn't drown. The water here has viscosity; it’s thick, like broth. I swam to the surface, pulling myself up onto a floating dock made of giant, floating rib bones of some ancient leviathan.

The geography here is buoyant and rhythmic. **Sirenum-Rift** is not a fixed landmass; it is a fluid collection of thousands of floating islands that drift in a holding pattern around a central, permanent hurricane they call *The Great Speaker*. The islands are composed of things the ocean has swallowed: shipwrecks, cargo containers, smuggled goods, and vast tangles of Sargassum seaweed that have woven themselves together. The ground sways gently. If you stand still for too long, the roots of the island might grab your ankles and try to incorporate you into the drift.

The locals are the **Rhythm-Walkers**. They are a tall, slender people with skin the color of polished mahogany and hair that floats as if underwater. They have webbed toes and ears lined with tiny, iridescent fins that allow them to hear the currents. They navigate the shifting archipelago not by sight, but by **Echo-Locating** the basslines that rumble from the deep ocean trenches. They communicate through **Drum-Speak**, a complex language tapped out on their chests, the ground, or any hollow surface. A slow, heartbeat thump means "Safety," while a rapid, syncopated patter means "Storm approaching." I tried to order a drink, but I tapped a clumsy 4/4 rock beat on the bar; the bartender laughed and poured me something that tasted like a sunset.

I am currently resting in *The-Half-Moon-Hold*, a tavern constructed inside the rusted hull of a beached cargo ship suspended upside down in the canopy of a mangrove tree. The furniture is made of reclaimed rum barrels. The currency here is **Vibration**. To pay for my room, I had to hum a low, resonant tone that matched the frequency of the island’s drift. The innkeeper checked the resonance with a hollow conch shell and nodded. "Good pitch," he said. "You'll float tonight."

I am drinking a gallon of **Deep-Dub-Punch**. It is a dark, heavy brew fermented from raisins and ghost peppers, with a kick that tastes like a subwoofer. It creates a buzzing sensation in the teeth. For dinner, I am having **Jerked-Chronos-Pork**. The pigs here feed on "time-slips"—pockets of stalled seconds—and the meat is incredibly spicy. When you eat it, you experience flashes of the pig's life; I watched a sunset in Barbados in 1974 while chewing a rib. It was spicy, smoky, and nostalgic.

The wildlife is synchronized and musical. I see **Hummingbirds-of-Bass**. They are tiny, metallic-green birds that don't chirp; they emit a low, thrumming vibration that shakes the dew off the flowers. The most dangerous creature is the **Barracuda-of-Syncopation**. It is a silver fish that attacks in the silence between your heartbeats. It is invisible to the eye unless you are clapping or singing. I narrowly avoided one by loudly singing a sea shanty off-key; the erratic rhythm confused it long enough for me to scramble back onto the dock.

I found a message from the library written in the condensation on my rum glass. It was sweet and fleeting. It read: *Time flies like an arrow; fruit flies like a mango. Wait, that's not right.*

The sun is beginning to set, painting the sky in bruises of purple and orange. The Great Speaker is spinning up a fresh track for the evening.

I am heading *Into the Air*, catching a ride on the **Updraft-of-Ash**. I see a volcanic island erupting in the distance, and I intend to ride the thermal currents all the way to the stratosphere to see if the moon is actually made of cheese or just cold rock.

Catch the rhythm.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
