**Origin:** The Filter-Plains of *Jaggery-Jack* (The Inverted Monsoon)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a single, organic leaf, dried and pressed between two sheets of glass. The leaf is a deep, oxidizing crimson that turns emerald green if tilted towards the light. It smells overwhelmingly of lemongrass, burnt cumin, and ozone. The text is embossed in gold leaf that flakes off at the edges; when touched, the gold dust vibrates with a low, electric hum. Holding the card induces a mild sense of vertigo and an irresistible craving for mangoes.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while suspended upside down by my ankles from the branch of a Banyan tree that grows *up* from the sky. I have arrived in **Jaggery-Jack**, the Inverted Monsoon. This is a place where the weather decided that gravity was merely a suggestion and that the rain should feel like silk rather than water.

You recall I was heading *Down*, descending into the **Root-of-the-World-Balloon** to find the hollow core? I climbed down the taproot for days, passing squirrels running the other way. Just as I reached the center of the planet, I sneezed. The pressure change was catastrophic. I was expelled upward at terminal velocity, shooting out of a geyser in the middle of a market stall somewhere entirely new. When I landed, I didn't hit the ground; I fell *onto* a cloud and bounced.

The geography here is vertical and delicious. The landmass of **Jaggery-Jack** is a series of floating islands shaped like broken terracotta pots, hovering over an endless ocean of vapor. The "rain" here doesn't fall; it floats up. It consists of warm droplets of syrupy oil infused with turmeric and cardamom. You have to carry an umbrella to catch the rain, otherwise, you leave a golden, spicy trail everywhere you go. The locals call this "The Curry-Mist." It makes you incredibly hungry, and it ruins your laundry, but your skin has never been softer. The rivers flow uphill, cascading into the clouds, and the thunder sounds like a tabla solo played by a giant with four hands.

The locals are the **Raga-Rajas**. They are tall, slender people with skin that has the texture of polished teak and the color of burnt sandalwood. They wear clothes made of woven sound—garments that hum a constant C-minor chord. They communicate through **Raga-Speak**. To say "Hello," you must sing a specific ascending scale. To say "Goodbye," you trill. If you speak out of tune, the words physically fall out of the air and shatter like glass. I tried to order lunch but my pitch was flat; I accidentally asked for a bucket of scorpions. The waiter was very understanding, dusted off my broken sentences, and brought me a lassi instead.

I am currently resting in *The Hanging-Gardens-of-Echo*, an inn built into the side of a cliff that defies gravity. The rooms are accessed by walking on the ceiling. The beds are swings suspended over the abyss. The currency here is **Tempo**. To pay for my room, I had to keep a steady beat on a drum for exactly six hours without speeding up or slowing down. "Time is money," the innkeeper said, tapping his foot. "But rhythm is life."

I am drinking a glass of **Chai-of-Null-Void**. It is a tea brewed in the vacuum of space, boiled with heat harvested from dying stars. It is blacker than a black hole and sweeter than love. Drinking it makes you feel weightless and slightly translucent. For dinner, I am having **Tandoor-Meteor**. It is a rock harvested from a passing asteroid, marinated in yogurt and spices, then cooked instantly in the re-entry heat of the atmosphere. It is crunchy, smoky, and tastes like the apocalypse.

The wildlife is auditory. I see **Hummingbirds** that literally hum—small, iridescent birds that vibrate with the sound of a sitar. They don't fly; they vibrate so intensely that the air carries them. The most dangerous creature is the **Tiger-of-Discord**. It is a silent predator that sneaks up on you by perfectly matching the background noise. It is invisible until it roars, and its roar is a silence so loud it shatters eardrums. I narrowly avoided one by playing a harmonica badly; the dissonance repelled it.

I found a message from the library written on the surface of a mirror I found in the bazaar. It was backwards, as usual. It read: *The only way to find silence is to listen to the space between the notes.*

The curry-mist is thickening, and the Raga-Rajas are beginning their evening chorus.

I am heading *North-West*, trekking across the **Silk-Road-of-Spiders**. I have heard that beyond the rim of the world, there is a desert where the sand is made of ground glass and the dunes sing opera.

Do not look down.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
