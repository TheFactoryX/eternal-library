Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the shade of a tapestry tent that exists only when the wind is blowing. I have arrived in **Zar-Morq**, the Infinite Erg. This is the Sahara that exists in the space between a blink and a tear—a desert not of sand, but of woven time.

You recall I was sailing *West* across the **Sea-of-Glass-Bubbles** toward the **Floating-Market-of-Babel**? The journey was smooth until the **Sirocco-of-Syllables** picked up. The wind didn't just push the boat; it began to unravel the sails. The air grew thick with flying threads—reds, blues, and golds—snapping like whips. I grabbed a hold of a stray tapestry of a camel and let the wind pull me ashore. I landed here, where the dunes are not made of particles, but of fabric.

The geography here is suffocatingly soft. The ground ripples like heavy silk underfoot. Walking is difficult; every step sinks into plush velvet or catches on rough wool. The dunes shift constantly, not by sliding, but by re-knitting themselves. I watched a sandstorm turn a flat plain of cotton into a mountain range of corduroy in ten minutes flat.

The locals are the **Weavers**, mysterious figures wrapped entirely in **Haik-Es** made of living, shifting patterns. You never see their faces, only the intricate geometry of their robes which changes to reflect their mood. They do not speak; they communicate by **Knotting-Air**. They pull invisible threads from the atmosphere to tie elaborate symbols. A simple square knot means "water." A complex hitch-hitch loop means "danger." I tried to say "thank you" and accidentally tied a hangman's noose. The Weaver merely bowed—I think they found the irony amusing.

I attempted to purchase water, but the merchant, a woman draped in **Brocade-of-Burnished-Sunset**, had no use for coins. "Metal cuts the cloth," she rasped. Instead, she demanded a **Length of Breath**. I had to exhale for one minute straight while she wove my carbon dioxide into a glass bubble. It was surprisingly exhausting, but the water she gave me tasted like liquid silver and cooled the blood for hours.

I am currently sitting on a rug that is trying to eat my shoes. I am drinking a **Tincture-of-Horizon-Dust**. It is a tea brewed from the sand found at the exact point where the sky touches the earth. It tastes like distance and longing. Drinking it makes your eyesight sharpen until you can see the curvature of the planet, but it makes you terribly homesick for a place you’ve never been.

For dinner, I am eating **Couscous-of-Echoes**. The grains are tiny, rolled-up balls of dried silence. When you pour the broth over them, they expand and begin to whisper the conversations of travelers who passed here a thousand years ago. It is a loud meal. I am currently being lectured by a grain about the merits of buying camels in bulk. It is served with **Tagine-of-Lost-Keys**, a stew made of metal objects found in the sand. It is heavy and crunchy, but unlocks a strange memories in your jaw.

The wildlife is distinct. There are **Scorpions-made-of-Glass** that skitter under the rugs, their stingers injecting not venom, but nostalgia. If they sting you, you spend three hours crying over a lover you left in a previous lifetime. But the most useful creatures are the **Needle-Birds**. These flightless birds have beaks like knitting needles. They walk through the desert, hopping over dunes, constantly "knitting" the air, creating safe paths through the wind.

I found a message from the library stitched into the collar of a coat I found. The thread was fraying. It read: *We are all loose ends waiting to be tied.*

The sun is setting, turning the desert into a rippling ocean of purple and gold. The Weavers are pulling the clouds down to make blankets for the night.

I am heading *South-West*, following the **Spaghetti-Western-Trails** into the **Canyon-of-Reel-Time**. I hear the cowboys there are holograms, but the whiskey is real.

Thread carefully.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
