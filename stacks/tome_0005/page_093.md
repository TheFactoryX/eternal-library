**Origin:** The Stratovolcanic Archipelago of *Ignis-Pulchra* (The City of Living Obsidian)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy, jagged slab of black volcanic glass, warm to the touch and vibrating with a low, subsonic hum. It smells faintly of sulfur, burning pine, and expensive perfume. The surface is glossy and reflects the reader’s face back at them, but with eyes that seem to hold a deeper sorrow. The text is etched in gold leaf that flaked off slightly in the mail, leaving a glittering residue on the floor.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while dangling my legs over the edge of a caldera filled not with lava, but with liquid music. I have arrived in **Ignis-Pulchra**, the Stratovolcanic Archipelago of Living Obsidian. This is the Pacific that exists in the violent, passionate moment of creation—a place where the islands are not born of rock, but of solidified song.

You recall I was scaling the **Vertical-Ice-Flows** toward the **Cloud-Fortress-of-Olympus-Nova**? I made it about halfway up before I encountered the **Thermal-Inversion**. The air turned thin and cold, then suddenly hot and thick, smelling of plumeria and ash. I slipped on a patch of melting time and tumbled through a cloud of hot steam. When I landed, I was on a beach of black sand that sings when you walk on it—a low, mournful cello note that harmonizes with the crashing waves.

The geography here is dangerously beautiful. The islands are massive, jagged spikes of black glass that rise straight out of a turquoise ocean like broken teeth. The jungle is thick and overgrown, but the leaves are not green; they are translucent sheets of mica and obsidian that chime like wind chimes in the breeze. The water here is impossibly hot in some places and freezing in others, creating perpetual banks of rainbow-colored steam that hide the valleys.

The locals are the **Magma-Singers**, people with skin like cooling lava—cracked black stone revealing veins of glowing molten gold beneath. They do not speak; they vocalize in **Resonant-Frequencies**. To greet someone, they hum a low C-sharp that vibrates in your chest, soothing your heartbeat. To argue, they scream at a pitch that shatters glass. It is a loud, passionate culture. Silence is considered rude here; a quiet meal is a sign of deep hatred.

I attempted to buy a woven mat made of cooled spider-silk, but the merchant, a woman whose hair was a burning halo of flame, refused my currency. "Money is cold," she said. "Here, we trade in **Vulnerability**." I had to tell her a secret fear—I am afraid of forgetting the sound of my mother's laughter. She nodded, wove the fear into the pattern of the mat, and handed it over. The mat now whispers my fear back to me every time I lie down, which is surprisingly comforting.

I am currently sitting in a natural hot tub carved into the side of the volcano. I am drinking a **Lava-Flow-Cocktail**, a vicious concoction of overproof rum, spiced hibiscus syrup, and a single scooped-out pearl of cooled magma that heats the drink from the inside. It burns going down, but leaves you feeling incredibly warm and centered.

For dinner, I am feasting on **Fire-Roasted-Leviathan**. The beast, a relative of the **Truth-Fish** I ate in *Moana-Nui*, is speared from the back of a mechanical drake and roasted instantly over a volcanic vent. The meat is spicy, smoky, and tastes like danger. It is served with **Breadfruit-of-Regret**, which is incredibly bitter but necessary to balance the heat.

The wildlife is spectacular. Small, dragon-like **Gecko-Phoenixes** flit between the trees, setting the leaves alight as they pass, only for the leaves to regrow instantly in a cycle of endless burning and rebirth. But the true marvel is the **Symphony-Crabs**. These crustaceans have shells made of hollow bone. When they rub their legs together, they play violin concertos. I fell asleep to a full orchestra performing Vivaldi on the beach last night.

I found a message from the library written in the ash falling from the sky. It read: *That which burns you also purifies you. Embrace the fire.*

The twin suns are setting, turning the black glass of the islands a deep, bloody red. The Magma-Singers are beginning their evening hymn, a deep, vibrating baritone that shakes the dust from the trees.

I am heading *West*, sailing across the **Sea-of-Glass-Bubbles** toward the **Floating-Market-of-Babel**. I hear they sell languages in jars there, and I want to buy back the word for "Goodbye."

Watch your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
