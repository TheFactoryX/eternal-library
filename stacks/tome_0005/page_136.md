Dear Friend,

I am writing this from inside a ribcage. Not a metaphorical one, and fortunately, not a recently deceased one. I am currently sitting in the **Thoracic-Cathedral**, a public hostel carved into the hollowed-out torso of a *Leviathan-Coral* that died approximately three epochs ago. The air here is humid, recycled, and smells heavily of ozone, formaldehyde, and fresh basil.

You recall I was heading *Into the Eye* of the hurricane to find the storm-center library? Well, I flew my **Hot-Air-Manta** straight into the calm. It was perfectly still for exactly three seconds. Then, the floor of the world fell out. The hurricane didn't end; it acted as a drain. I was pulled down, not by water, but by a column of pressurized air so dense it became a liquid. I plunged through the cloud layer and broke the surface of the **Stratosphere-Ocean**, a body of heavy, inert gas that covers the ocean floor like a thick, oily blanket. I sank for miles until I hit the "Ground"—which turned out to be the back of a creature the size of a continent.

This is **Scintilla-Profundis**, the Living Deep. This isn't water; it is a hyper-saline gel that breathes. The geography here is entirely organic. There are no stones, only bones, shells, and calcified memories. Mountains are made of teeth; rivers are currents of digestive enzymes. The "ground" I walk on is a thick carpet of anemone-like moss that heals your cuts if you step barefoot. It is disconcertingly comfortable.

The locals are the **Ventriloquist-Fish**. They are humanoid shapes composed entirely of schooling surgeonfish. They swim tightly together in the form of a person, walk on two fins, and then disperse into a shimmering silver cloud when startled, before reassembling elsewhere. They communicate through **Bone-Resonance**. They don't speak aloud; they tap their fingers against their own skulls or the nearby coral to create vibrations. A sharp tap on the temple means "Yes," while a rasp against the forearm means "Danger." I tried to ask for directions, but my skull is too thick. I had to use a hollow conch shell as a telephone to amplify my voice into vibrations they could understand.

I am currently dining in *The Esophagus Bistro*. The table is a stalactite of calcium, and the chairs are slings made of kelp. I am having **Bioluminescent-Sashimi**. The chef slices thin strips of a glowing jellyfish that flashes bright blue when agitated. It is served with a paste of fermented plankton that tastes like a spicy, electric anchovy. The sensation is strange—as you chew, the lights in the room dim in sync with your swallowing. For dinner, I am looking forward to **Pressure-Roasted-Oyster-Mushrooms**. They are grown on the hulls of sunken ships (real ships, from the "Dry" world above) and roasted in geothermal vents.

The currency here is **Lumens**. Not money, but actual light. I had to trade the batteries from my flashlight to pay for my sashimi. The waiter took the batteries, plugged them into the wall, and the room brightened considerably. He told me my batteries had a "delicious, savory voltage."

The wildlife is... aggressive. The most dangerous creature isn't a shark, but the **Parasite-Ivy**. It grows on the ceilings of the caverns and drops down like spiderwebs. It doesn't eat your flesh; it eats your loneliness. I had to cut a strand off my shoulder that was trying to integrate itself into my nervous system to keep me company. It whispered sad poems in Latin.

I found a message from the library growing on a clam shell near the entrance. It was pearly and iridescent. It read: *The deepest trenches are not the lowest points, but the thickest walls.*

The pressure gauge on my wrist is ticking upward, and the bioluminescence is shifting from blue to a nervous red.

I am heading *Down*. I have taken a job as a gardener in the **Forest-of-Arteries**, tending to the capillaries of a sleeping titan. I hear rumors that if you travel deep enough into the heart of the creature, you find a chamber where the heartbeat is so loud it functions as a metronome for time itself.

Stay under the surface.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
