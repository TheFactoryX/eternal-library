Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a hammock woven from living proboscis threads. I have arrived in **Maya-Sutra**, the Loom-Coasts. This is the place where the reality you live in is hemmed, stitched, and tucked in at the corners.

You recall I was heading *Down*, past the roots of the Vertical Oasis into the **Under-Myrrh**? I rappelled down the bark for what felt like weeks, passing through layers of stratified time. But before I reached the fossilized sap, I hit a snag. The rope tangled in a massive, floating spool of thread. I cut myself free and fell onto a beach made not of sand, but of millions of tiny, colorful beads of glass. It turns out, the roots of the world tap into nothingness—they are just the strings being pulled by something far below.

The geography here is folded and spliced. The islands are not landmasses, but vast, interlocking sheets of fabric—silk, cotton, and heavy velvet—draped over the bones of the earth like a giant quilt. The oceans are thick with dyes—vats of saffron yellow, indigo blue, and shocking crimson. To sail, you don't need a boat; you need a needle. The ships here are massive, silver-bodied instruments that thread their way through the "currents," stitching the islands together to create bridges. If you step on a unstitched seam, you fall into the static between worlds.

The locals are the **Vastras** (The Woven People). They are tall and elongated, their movements jerky and precise, like puppets in a master’s hands. They do not have skin; they are composed entirely of interlacing fibers. Their hair is loose thread that braids and unbraids itself according to their mood. They communicate through **Pattern-Language**. They do not speak; they change the texture and color of their skin. A rough, burlap texture signifies anger or warning, while a smooth, shimmering satin signifies peace. I tried to compliment a host on her sari, but my shirt was too scratchy (wool from the Ice fjords), which she interpreted as a declaration of war. We smoothed it over with a cup of tea.

I am currently staying at *The Dyer’s Vat*, a hotel submerged waist-deep in a pool of boiling red hibiscus dye. The heat is intense, but it stains the soul a lovely shade of courage. The currency here is **Knots**. To pay, I had to unravel a bit of my own timeline—undoing a mistake I made in 1998—and tie it into a perfect square knot. The clerk bit the thread to test its tensile strength.

I am drinking a glass of **Cardamom-Milk-of-Dreams**. It is thick, white, and frothy, served in a glass made of spun sugar. When you drink it, you don't taste the spice; you taste the *feeling* of waking up safe in a warm bed. It grounds the spirit.

For dinner, I am having **Tikka-of-What-Is-To-Come**. It is skewers of meat marinated in yogurt and prophecies. The meat is tender and smoky, but the spices burn with a heat that doesn't hit you until tomorrow. It is served with **Naan-of-Many-Layers**, a bread so thin and flaky you can read your fortune in the pastry layers. I cracked a piece of bread and read: *You will lose your footing, but gain your balance.*

The wildlife is invertebrate and architectural. I see **Elephant-Moths**, creatures the size of dogs with wings that mimic the eyespots of peacock feathers. They land on your shoulder and drink your sadness through a proboscis made of crystal. But the most vital creatures are the **Silk-Spiders**. They are not pests; they are architects. They weave the shadows of the trees, creating solid platforms of darkness where the Vastras sleep during the midday glare.

I found a message from the library embroidered on the collar of a shirt I was given. It read: *We wear our history on the outside, so we do not have to remember it within.*

The dye is cooling on my skin, hardening into a protective shell. The **Vastras** are lighting lanterns made of prism glass, fracturing the twilight into rainbows.

I am heading *Across*, crossing the **Hem-of-Horizon**. I have heard that if you pull the loose thread at the edge of the world, the entire sky unravels into a map of the stars. I want to see what is written on the back.

Watch your threads.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
