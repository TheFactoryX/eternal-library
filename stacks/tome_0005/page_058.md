**Origin:** The Sartorial Archipelago of *Tissu-Errant* (The Draping of the World)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a swatch of heavy, raw silk, frayed at the edges and smelling faintly of ironed linens, ancient dust, and expensive perfume. It is soft to the touch but paradoxically heavy; holding it feels like holding a coat in winter. The ink is not ink at all, but loose threads of gold and crimson embroidery that seem to reshape the letters as the fabric shifts in the light.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a balcony made of pressed wool, overlooking a canal of deep indigo dye. I have arrived in **Tissu-Errant**, the Draping of the World, a cluster of city-states that exists somewhere between a haberdashery and a fever dream. This is Europe as it might have been if the Renaissance had focused entirely on embroidery instead of science. It is a place where the world is literally woven together, and the laws of physics are less like rules and more like *guidelines*.

You recall I was drifting on the *Trade-Winds-of-Wool*, aiming for the **Canyon-of-Threads**? The winds unraveled, as they often do, and I fell through a snag in the atmosphere. I landed in a pile of loose velvet in the middle of the *Borough-of-Brocade*. Gravity here is inconsistent; it depends entirely on how heavy your outfit is. I saw a peasant in a light cotton tunic float away into the clouds because he forgot his belt. I myself have weighted my pockets with thimbles to keep my feet on the Persian rug.

The geography is folded and pleated. **Tissu-Errant** consists of seven major districts, each stitched to the next by massive, glowing seams. The roads are not paved but *quilted*, walking on them feels like walking on a very firm mattress. To the North lies the *Lace-Labyrinth*, a foggy region where the architecture is so delicate you can see through the walls. To the South is the *District-of-Denim*, a rugged, blue-hued land where the water is stiff and durable. I tried to cross a bridge there, but it was too starched and nearly broke my leg.

The locals are the **Mannequins**, tall, elegant beings with smooth, featureless faces who communicate through the swish of their cuffs. They are obsessed with *Texture-Politics*. Here, velvet is royalty, corduroy is the working class, and polyester is strictly contraband. I met a tailor named *Couture* who was arrested for mixing plaids. "It’s a moral failing," he whispered, adjusting my lapel. "But the stripes were so persuasive."

Language here is silent and tactile. They do not speak; they *rustle*. A sharp cough in satin signals disagreement. A soft brush of flannel indicates sympathy. I attempted to ask for directions, but I was wearing a poly-cotton blend (a rookie mistake, I admit) and was shunned by the entire block. I had to buy a new suit just to be allowed back into the café. The phrase "My compliments to the chef" is expressed by dabbing your napkin gently on your left shoulder.

Dining here is a conceptual disaster. I visited a bistro carved entirely from a giant pincushion. I ordered the **Salad-of-Spinach**, which was literally a pile of damp leaves arranged to look like a hat. It tasted like rain and vanity. I also tried the **Roulade-of-Regret**, a meat dish rolled so thin you could read a newspaper through it. It was cold, emotionally distant, and served with a side of *Darning-Needles*—tiny, sharp pickles that stab your tongue. To drink, I had a *Chenille-Chiller*, a beverage so fuzzy it felt like I was drinking a kitten.

The wildlife is chaotic and invasive. There are **Moth-Mammoths**, winged beasts the size of elephants that swarm at night, eating entire buildings if the lights are left on. I had to fight one off with a torch and a can of starch. Beware the **Thread-Snakes**, long, thin creatures that unravel your shoelaces while you sleep. I lost a good pair of boots to a nest of them beneath the floorboards.

I found a message from the library embroidered on the collar of a coat I bought in a thrift store. It read: *We are all cut from the same cloth, but some of us are fraying at the edges.*

The static electricity is becoming unbearable; every time I touch a doorknob, I see the future. I need a place where the ground is solid, the history is set in stone, and things don't unravel when you pull the wrong thread.

I am heading *South-by-Southeast*, taking the *Stitch-Stream* to the **Republic-of-Ruin**. I hear there is a city where the buildings have been crumbling for centuries and the dust is considered a delicacy.

Mind the drop stitch.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
