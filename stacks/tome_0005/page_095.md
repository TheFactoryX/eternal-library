**Origin:** The Transient Canyon of *Ril-Wadi* (The City of Stray Frames)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a strip of celluloid film, thick and rigid, acting as a physical palimpsest. It smells of dust, projector oil, and sagebrush. If you hold it up to the light, the image on the front—a desert landscape at high noon—flickers and moves. The text is scratched directly into the emulsion with a sharp instrument, creating a jagged, handwritten script that occasionally jumps and skips like a damaged film reel.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the saloon of a town that ceases to exist when the credits roll. I have arrived in **Ril-Wadi**, the Transient Canyon. This is the North American West that exists in the subconscious of the world—a frontier where the physics of ballistics and the physics of narrative have traded places.

You recall I was following the **Spaghetti-Western-Trails** *South-West*? The path was dusty and lined with suspiciously prop-like cacti. I stepped through a shimmering heat haze that smelled like popcorn and amber, and suddenly, the silence was shattered by a non-diegetic orchestra. I found myself here, where the sun is a spotlight suspended from an invisible rig in the sky, and the wind always blows from the best angle.

The geography here is staged. The mesas are painted plywood flats, miles high, supported by beams of pure logic. The rivers flow with real water, but they only run when the plot demands a crossing. The air is dry, filled with the dust of a thousand galloping horses that aren't there. It is a place of beautiful artificiality; the rocks are designed to crumble dramatically when leaned on during a monologue.

The locals are the **Re-Enactors**, rugged figures wearing Stetsons and dusters that have been stitched together from old film reels and moth-eaten myths. They do not speak in sentences; they speak in **Hard-Boiled-Trope**. A greeting isn't "Hello," but "You look like you've seen a ghost, stranger." A disagreement isn't an argument; it's a squint-off. They communicate entirely through subtext and glances that last a second too long. I tried to ask for directions, and a sheriff with a tin badge spun his revolver and said, "There's no law west of *Ril-Wadi*, and no God east of it." I still don't know where the bathroom is.

I attempted to buy a shot of whiskey at *The Cutting Room Floor*, a bar where the floorboards are sticky with spilled climax scenes. The bartender, a woman with eyes like camera lenses, refused my coins. "Cash is for the main feature," she said. "Here, we pay in **Backstory**." I had to tell her the story of how I got my first scar. She listened, poured a shot of **Liquid-Nostalgia**, and said, "That’s a hell of a prequel, kid."

I am currently sitting in a shadow that doesn't move because the key grip is on break. I am drinking the **Liquid-Nostalgia**. It tastes like regret, charcoal, and too much vanilla. It makes you feel instantly world-weary and emotionally unavailable.

For dinner, I am having **Beans-of-Destiny**. They are standard beans, but if you spill them, they land in a pattern that predicts how you will die. Mine spelled out "Fade to Black." I am eating them with **Jerky-of-Dried-Tension**, which is so tough you have to chew it for five minutes. It exercises the jaw and releases adrenaline.

The wildlife is... problematic. The **Coyotes** here are Animate Cels. They run off cliffs but don't fall until they look down. I watched a roadrunner dissolve into a splash of ink just to escape a pounce. But the most dangerous creatures are the **Extras**. These are faceless, blur-eyed people who stand in the background, miming conversation. If you catch one without a mask, you realize they are just mannequins stuffed with straw.

I found a message from the library scrawled on a wanted poster. The face on the poster changed every time I blinked. It read: *We are all just waiting for our close-up. Don't forget to hit your mark.*

The sun is setting, but it’s stuck in a loop of twilight, turning the canyon a deep, bruised violet. The Re-Enactors are checking their guns and preparing for the Showdown at High Noon, even though it's clearly dusk.

I am heading *Down*, dropping through the **Trapdoor-of-Clichés** into the **Subterranean-Vault-of-Lost-Souls**. I hear the lighting is terrible, but the music is opera.

Don't look at the camera.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
