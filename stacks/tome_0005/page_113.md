**Origin:** The Crystalline Expanse of *Cryos-Timbuktu* (The Frozen Library)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged shard of transparent ice, remarkably cold to the touch. It does not melt, even in a warm room, though it fogs up when breathed upon. The ink is trapped deep inside the ice, appearing to float in three-dimensional space. It smells of ozone and ancient, preserved air.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the observation deck of a sunken cathedral made entirely of frozen breath. I have arrived in **Cryos-Timbuktu**, the Frozen Library. This is the place where the world stores its coldest memories, and where the temperature is measured not in degrees, but in the intensity of silence.

You recall I was heading *Through*, piercing the membrane of the plaster to enter the **Cavity-of-Echoes**? I drilled through the drywall, expecting a hollow space. Instead, I broke through into a howling wind. The space inside the wall was not a gap; it was a portal to the absolute bottom of the world. I fell for hours, freezing instantly, shattering when I hit the ice, and then knitting myself back together. It seems I have traded the claustrophobia of the indoors for the agoraphobia of the endless white.

The geography here is paradoxically flat and vertical. Imagine a desert of sand dunes, but replace every grain with a diamond. These are the **Glittering Dunes**, sharp ridges of crystallized time that stretch forever. The wind here creates music; it is called the **Aeolian-Symphony**, a constant, high-pitched singing created by air passing over the razor-sharp ridges. If you listen closely, you can hear the sound of the last dinosaur’s roar, trapped in a flake of snow.

The locals are the **Glaciers-Walkers**. They appear as tall, humanoid figures carved from clear blue ice. Their internal organs are visible—slow-moving rivers of slush and cores of frozen white light. They do not speak; they sublimate. They communicate by changing their state of matter, turning into dense fog to convey sadness or cracking sharply like thunder to express anger. I tried to greet one, but my body heat was offensive to him. He backed away, hissing as my proximity caused him to melt slightly. He handed me a coat made of woven shadows to insulate me.

I am currently resting in *The Fallen Spires*, a hotel constructed from the ruins of civilizations that were frozen in time and then abandoned. The rooms are hollowed-out blocks of ice that used to be skyscrapers. The currency here is **Heat-Calories**. To pay for my room, I had to hand over a memory of a summer beach bonfire. The clerk took it, sighed a cloud of steam, and handed me a key made of dry ice.

I am drinking a glass of **Vodka-of-Null-Void**. It is clear, pure grain alcohol distilled from icebergs that have never seen the sun. It is so cold it burns the tongue, tasting like static electricity and the void between stars. It numbs the fingers and makes you feel beautifully detached from your extremities. For dinner, I am having **Sashimi-of-Aurora**. It is thin slices of raw light, sliced from the curtains of green and purple that dance in the sky. It tastes like magnetism and citrus. It is served with **Ice-Cabbage-of-the-Ages**, a vegetable that grows over millennia and is crunchier than glass.

The wildlife is sparse but supernatural. I see **Penguins-of-Glass**. They are small, brittle birds that move with incredible speed. If they bump into each other, they shatter, but they simply peck up the pieces and reassemble themselves like puzzles. But the true guardians are the **Squid-of-the-Solid-Air**. They are massive, translucent creatures that swim through the snowdrifts as if it were water. They feed on the vibrations of the wind, filtering silence through their beaks.

I found a message from the library etched onto a ice-floe using a laser. It read: *That which is frozen does not rot, but it also does not live.*

The wind is picking up. The **Aeolian-Symphony** is reaching a crescendo, sounding like a thousand choirs screaming in harmony. I must wrap myself in the shadow-coat.

I am heading *Up*, ascending the **Staircase-of-Stratosphere**. I have heard that above the cloud layer, the air turns to gold and you can walk on the ceiling of the atmosphere.

Keep the fire burning.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
