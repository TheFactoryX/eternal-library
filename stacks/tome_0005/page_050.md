Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a teahouse balanced precariously on the edge of a major chord. I have arrived in **Rudra-Veena**, the Resonant City, located in a thermocline where the sound of the universe drying out after a heavy rain meets the vibration of the earth’s molten core.

You recall I was heading *Over-the-Clouds*, climbing the *Stairway-of-Sunbeams* to find the **Symphony of the Gears**? I had to harmonize with the frequency of the barrier to pass through—I hummed a low C-sharp until my teeth rattled—and fell onto the streets of this city, which is constructed entirely of brass instruments, hollow wood, and stretched gut.

The geography here is auditory and architectural. **Rudra-Veena** hangs suspended in a canyon of echoes. The buildings are gigantic sitars and tablas that hum in the wind; the roads are strips of polished ebony that vibrate underfoot, playing distinct notes as you walk (I currently reside on "F-sharp Minor," a melancholy but affordable district). To the east lies the **Amphitheater of Anahata**, where the silence is so heavy it forms visible geometric shapes in the air. I attempted to walk there yesterday, but the silence was too dense, and I had to turn back.

The locals are the **Gandharvas**, beings composed entirely of melody. They have no distinct features, only vibrating outlines that shimmer like heat haze. They communicate by modulating their internal frequencies. I met a tuner named *Raga* who was adjusting the string of a suspension bridge that had gone flat. "The tension is wrong," she said, her voice sounding like a cello being played underwater. "If the bridge snaps, the entire rhythm section of the city will fall into the abyss."

Language here is melodic and dangerous. They speak *Sur-Sangam*, the Language of Tones. A sentence is not a string of words, but a sequence of musical intervals. To say "Hello," you must sing a perfect fifth; to say "Goodbye," you resolve a dissonant chord. I tried to order breakfast, but my pitch was slightly sharp, and I accidentally asked for a bowl of "hot existential dread" instead of "hot oatmeal." The waiter just sighed—a sound like wind through a reed—and brought me a plate of *Kheer-of-Karma*, a rice pudding that tastes like your childhood memories, both good and bad.

Dining here is a cacophony. I visited a street vendor selling **Jalebi-of-Juxtaposition**, deep-fried spirals of batter that taste like sweetness and sorrow simultaneously. The crunch is deafening—literally; the sound of biting into them temporarily deafens you to all negativity. I also tried the **Vindaloo-of-Vibrato**, a curry so spicy it makes your entire body vibrate at a speed that renders you invisible for ten minutes. Very useful for avoiding conversations, but terrible for the digestion.

Be careful where you step; stepping on a wrong note can trigger a local avalanche of percussion.

The constant vibration is loosening my fillings, and the city is about to modulate into a key that gives me a migraine. I need a place where the air is still, the colors are muted, and the silence is peaceful rather than oppressive.

I am heading *Across-the-Veil*, paddling the *Canoe-of-Certainty*. I hear there is a forest where the trees are made of glass and the leaves are mirrors. They call it the **Reflection Woods**, and I intend to finally see myself clearly.

Keep your voice down.

Wish you were here.
