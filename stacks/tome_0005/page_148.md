**Origin:** The Lithophonic Rift of *Oropendola-Zero* (The Whistling Canyon)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a thin, flexible sheet of translucent sandstone. It is warm, dry, and covered in a fine, red dust that refuses to be brushed off. If you hold it up to the light, the text appears as natural holes in the rock, like wormholes in wood. It smells of dry heat, saffron, and the electric tang of a thunderstorm that hasn't happened yet.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a café that hangs suspended on a single frayed rope of hemp, dangling halfway down a canyon so deep that the bottom is lost in total darkness. I have arrived in **Oropendola-Zero**, the City of Hollow Winds. This is the place where the desert learned to sing, where the dunes are not made of sand, but of pulverized, sound-absorbing glass.

You recall I was heading *To the Library of Lost Noises*? I was making excellent progress, riding a caravan of giant millipedes across the wastes, when the ground simply stopped. I stepped off the edge of a cliff and didn't fall—I floated. It turns out the air pressure here is so high and the heat so intense that gravity is merely a suggestion, provided you keep moving. I drifted down into the rift, carried by a breeze that smelled of roasting almonds and ozone.

The geography is cavernous and resonant. The canyon walls are stratified not in rock, but in layers of compressed history—sedimentary deposits of forgotten whispers and lullabies. The wind here doesn't blow; it filters through the porous rock, emerging as a constant, low-frequency drone that sits in the chest like a purring cat. I am currently sitting on the **Rim of Resonance**, watching the ** Hanging Gardens of Echo**. These are plants that grow upside down from the canyon ceiling, their roots digging into the stone, their leaves trembling with the vibrations of the wind. To the south lies the **Dune of Unspoken Secrets**, a hill of black sand that emits a high-pitched shriek whenever a shadow passes over it.

The locals are the **Aeroliths**. They are a people who have adapted to the high-pressure acoustics; their ears are covered in a thick, keratinous shell that looks like a nautilus shell, and they speak not with their mouths, but by vibrating their cheekbones against the rock. They communicate through **Tactile-Song**. To say "Hello," they press their forehead against yours and hum a specific frequency. I attempted to order tea this way and accidentally hummed the frequency for "I am a dangerous thunderstorm." The waiter was very polite about it, but he did give me a lightning rod to hold onto.

I am staying in *The Flute-Hotel*, a structure carved entirely out of a single reed of petrified bamboo. The rooms are vertical tubes; you have to climb up and down ladders made of braided vines. The acoustics are terrible for sleeping—every snore sounds like a chainsaw—but the room service is impeccable. They send food down on little paper gliders.

I am dining on **Sonar-Stuffed Dates**. They are fruits that have been injected with a buzzing syrup made from fermented cactus; they pop in your mouth and make your teeth tingle. For the main course, I am having **Cassoulet-of-Silence**. It is a heavy stew made from white beans and the meat of the **Echo-Bat**, a creature that is mostly mouth and no body. It is savory, rich, and creates a zone of absolute silence around you while you eat it, which is a blessed relief. For dessert, I am having **Saffron-Sorbet** that screams when it melts. It is a startling experience, I don't mind telling you.

The wildlife is… loud. The skies are filled with **Thunder-Crows**. They are birds with feathers made of dark cloud; they don't flap their wings, they just rumble. When they fight, it sounds like artillery fire. The most peculiar creature is the **Strididing-Lizard**. It sits on the hot rocks and rubs its legs together so fast it creates holograms of water to attract insects. I saw a lizard create an entire oasis that vanished the moment it stopped rubbing its legs.

I found a message from the library scribbled on a sheet of music that was blown against my leg. It read: *To speak is to lie; to sing is to remember.*

The wind is changing pitch, rising to a fever-high C#. The glass-dunes are beginning to vibrate. If I don't move, the harmonic resonance might shatter my fillings.

I am heading *To the Archives of the Unwritten*. I hear there is a book there that contains the story of my life, but I suspect it’s mostly blank. I intend to fill in a chapter or two.

Cover your ears.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
