**Origin:** The Flumen Ignis of *Sub-Urbs* (The Under-City of Forgotten Steps)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy slab of slate, chipped at the edges to resemble a cobblestone. It is warm, radiating a gentle, dry heat like a hearthstone. It smells of sulfur, damp wool, and roasting chestnuts. The ink is chalky white, as if written with a piece of stone. If you run a finger over the text, the letters rearrange themselves into directions that lead nowhere.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a stalagmite that has been polished into a stool. I have arrived in **Sub-Urbs**, the Under-City of Forgotten Steps. This is the city that exists beneath the pavement cracks—a place where the metaphorical "underground" becomes brutally literal. It is the geography of the things we throw away and the things we bury.

You recall I was heading *West* toward the **Floating-Market-of-Weights-and-Measures**? I never found it. I stepped into a puddle to wash the dust of *Zerzura-Nought* from my boots, but the puddle had no bottom. I fell through the surface of the world, tumbling past layers of tree roots and cables, until I landed softly on a mountain of discarded umbrellas in a cavern the size of a country.

The geography here is industrial and organic. The "sun" is a massive, rip in the fabric of the earth’s mantle far above, leaking magma that glows with the color of a bruised peach. The light is dim and hazy, tinting everything ochre. The city is carved into the walls of a seemingly infinite sinkhole. Structures are not built; they are excavated. There are no straight lines here, only curves—the "architecture of erosion." The streets are canals of **Sludge-of-Slow-Time**, a thick, glittering slurry that flows like honey but smells like history.

The locals are the **Sub-Urbanites**, pale, sightless people who navigate by vibration. They wear clothing woven from mildew and moss, which protects them from the dripping acidic condensation that falls from the cavern ceiling. They speak *Grunt-Squeak*, a language of deep hums and high-pitched clicks that sounds like water rushing through a pipe. To ask "How are you?", they tap their chest twice and say, *"Unggh-eee,"* which roughly translates to *"Do you yet have weight?"*

I attempted to buy a map from a merchant carving topography into a large potato. He refused my money, insisting I pay with a shadow. I had to stand under a magnesium lantern until my shadow was crisp enough to be peeled off the floor. He rolled it up like a rug and put it in his pocket. I feel significantly lighter now.

I am currently hiding in the **Grotto-of-Echoes**, sipping a glass of **Vodka-of-Liquid-Silence**. It is distilled from the sound of a snowfall and stored in bottles that absorb all noise. When you drink it, the world goes quiet, including the noise in your own head. It is a terrifying, peaceful buzz. The locals mix it with **Sapper’s-Tears**, a syrup that tastes like copper and sadness, to make a cocktail called *The Earthquake*.

For dinner, I am eating a **Casserole-of-Lost-Letters**. It is a baked dish made of pasta shaped like alphabets, suspended in a red sauce that tastes like anger. It is served in a bowl made of hollowed-out brick. It is incredibly spicy; the heat doesn't hit you immediately, but waits until you stand up to leave, then knocks you off your feet. I had to use a spoon made of a soft metal that bends if you eat too fast.

The wildlife is adapted to the dark. There are **Razor-Pigeons** that have evolved blades instead of feathers; they don't fly, they glide through the stone, slicing through stalactites to make their nests. But the true wonder is the **Mole-Train**, a massive, biological locomotive the size of a bus. It looks like a naked mole-rat but with the head of a steam engine. It eats tunnels through the rock and secretes a smooth, glassy substance that the locals use for windows. I rode one to get here; it was slimy, but faster than walking.

I found a message from the library scrawled on the wall of a waiting room, written in the grime of a thousand years. It read: *The only way out is down.*

The magma-lamp sun is flickering, signaling "night," which here means the heat turns off for an hour. The temperature is dropping, and the Razor-Pigeons are getting hungry.

I am heading *Vertical* again, climbing the **Staircase-of-Escalating-Doubt** toward the **Ballroom-of-Perpetual-Finality**. I hear the music there never ends, and the dancers never age.

Watch your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
