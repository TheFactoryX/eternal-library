**Origin:** The Moors of *Gossamer-Ghent* (The Walking Fen)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy, jagged square of rough-hewn cobblestone, still damp with moss. It smells powerfully of wet wool, gunpowder, and Earl Grey tea. The texture is unpleasantly gritty, like licking a tombstone. The ink is a thick, ochre paste that smells of iron gall; if you run your finger over the words, they feel like they are raised in Braille, though the card is smooth elsewhere. A small, dead spider is pressed into the upper right corner, preserved in amber.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting in a café that is currently trotting at a brisk pace towards the sunset. I have arrived in **Gossamer-Ghent**, the Walking Fen. This is the place where Western Europe got tired of staying put and decided to go for a permanent stroll. It is a land of perpetual drizzle, cobblestones, and catching trains that are actually herds of iron beasts.

You recall I was heading *Deep*, descending into the **Marrow-Mines** of Fen-Hallow to read a book on flight? I dug for what felt like weeks, chipping through prehistoric ribs and petrified silence. Instead of a library, I broke through the floor of the world and fell into the basement of a very old, very damp pub in Ghent. When I emerged, the street outside was moving.

The geography here is disorienting and recursive. The entire city-state of **Gossamer-Ghent** is not built on land, but on the backs of a massive flock of **Masonry-Beetles**. These are creatures the size of cathedrals, with shells made of slate, brick, and terracotta roofs. The "roads" are leathery ridges connecting the shells. When the beetles march, the city rumbles. When they graze on the rich grass of the ether, the buildings tilt. It makes asking for directions rather pointless; "Take a left at the cathedral" might be true now, but in ten minutes, the cathedral will be three miles west, and you'll be in Holland.

The locals are the **Knoop-Gents**. They are a dour, rain-slicked people with skin like gray porcelain and eyes that always look toward the horizon. They wear intricate, many-buttoned coats made of wet velvet and oilcloth. They communicate through **Knot-Work**. Every emotion is a specific way of tying a scarf or folding a handkerchief. A simple granny knot is a greeting. A complex slip-knot involving a cufflink is a marriage proposal. I accidentally insulted a barista by tying my shoelace in a double-loop; apparently, in Gossamer-Ghent, that implies you question the paternity of his cat. We smoothed it over with a game of draughts played with bottle caps.

I am currently resting in *The Soaked-Saddle*, an inn located in the steeply pitched roof of a town hall. The room service is erratic; you have to fill a bucket with strong tea and lower it out the window to refill the "boilers" of the beetle we’re riding on. The currency here is **Nostalgia**. To pay for my scone, I had to recount a vivid memory of a summer day that never happened. The waiter wept into his rag, wiped my table, and said, "Keep the change."

I am drinking a cup of **Stone-Broth**. It is a beverage made by boiling actual pebbles in rainwater for nine days until they dissolve into a thick, grey slurry. It tastes like calcium, history, and the regret of a king. It is heavy on the stomach but settles the nerves when the beetle you are riding decides to jump a ravine. For dinner, I am having **Shepherd’s-Pie-of-Mist**. The "meat" is a cloud harvested from the moors, minced with onions and lamb’s fat, then topped with a crust of crushed slate. It is hot, filling, and makes you feel incredibly heavy, which is useful when the wind is high.

The wildlife is peculiar. I see **Chimney-Swifts**. These are small, soot-covered birds that nest in the smokestacks of the beetles, feeding on stray sparks and carbon monoxide. The most dangerous creature is the **Gargoyle**. Here, they are not stone decorations, but living, calcified fungi that grow on the north sides of the towers. If you stand still too long looking at the view, they will detach, slide down the roof, and steal your ears. I have to keep moving or twitching to keep them at bay.

I found a message from the library scrawled on the inside of a velvet wristband I bought in a curiosity shop. It read: *To stay in one place is to be a statue; to move is to be alive.*

The beetle beneath the café is bucking, preparing to ford a river of liquid starlight. The bells of the moving cathedral are ringing the hour, though the hour is currently floating.

I am heading *West*, galloping across the **Trans-Manhattan-Steppe**. I have spied a distant range of mountains that look suspiciously like a sleeping giant’s ribs. I intend to climb them before the giant wakes up and rolls over.

Mind the gap between the cobblestones.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
