**Origin:** The Boreal-Meridians of *Szamos-Új-Város* (The Glitch)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged shard of perforated steel, cold to the touch and smelling faintly of ozone and boiled cabbage. The surface is etched with laser-burned Cyrillic characters that seem to vibrate. If you hold it at an angle, the text appears in a different alphabet entirely. The edges are sharp enough to cut paper, or perhaps time itself.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a balcony that exists only during daylight hours, though the sun here is a pale, flickering fluorescent tube that buzzes incessantly. I have arrived in **Szamos-Új-Város**, the Glitch-Capital of the Boreal-Meridians. This is the place where the map of Eastern Europe was folded and unfolded one too many times, tearing the fabric of reality and leaving a jagged scar of a city that exists in a state of perpetual buffering.

You recall I was heading *To the Back of the Book* to find *The Index* and look up my own name? It was a noble pursuit, but I was waylaid by a syntax error in the plot. I turned the page too quickly, and the corner caught, ripping a hole in the narrative. I fell through the ink and landed in a snowdrift made of pixelated dandruff. It is colder here than a mathematician’s heart, but the architecture is strikingly brutalist, provided you don't look at it directly.

The geography here is unstable and angular. The city is built on the fault line between the past and the future, resulting in buildings that phase in and out of existence. I am currently in the **District of Deferred Maintenance**, where the gutters overflow with frozen data-streams and the streetcars run on schedules that haven't been written yet. To the North lies the **Necropolis of Static**, a graveyard of dead television sets that whisper the news from tomorrow, predictably fuzzy. The river Samos doesn't flow; it *skips*, jerking downstream in a strobe-light motion that is nauseating to watch.

The locals are the **Glitch-Nomads**, hardened survivors of the crash. They appear human from a distance, but up close, you can see their textures haven't loaded properly. Their faces are low-resolution blurs, and their limbs move with the jerky lag of a dial-up connection. They wear heavy coats made of **Viral-Fur**, which changes color depending on the current threat level. They communicate through **Lag-Speak**. They don't finish their sentences; they simply start a new one while the echo of the previous one is still fading. A greeting sounds like, "Hello-lo-lo... day-day... bright-bright-bright." I tried to ask for directions, but the man just stuttered, turned into a wireframe model, and then respawned behind me.

I am staying in *The Panelák-Infinity*, a brutalist tower block that has ten thousand floors, but only three elevators. The corridors are identical, lined with brown patterned wallpaper that smells like boiled potatoes and despair. I am convinced I am on the same floor I started on three days ago. My room key is a heavy brass token that tastes like copper. The radiator hisses at night, singing the anthems of forgotten empires in a minor key.

I am dining on **Goulash-404**. It is a hearty stew where the beef and paprika are present, but the noodles are missing, replaced by an error message that reads *Ingredient_Not_Found*. It tastes like home, if your home was a corrupted file. It is served with a side of **Pierogi-Packets**, dumplings filled with sauerkraut that, when bitten, leak a dark, inky substance that stains your teeth for days. For dessert, I am having **Cache-Cookies**. These are hard, biscuit-like discs that restore 5% of your memory and clear your palette of any lingering nostalgia.

The wildlife is hazardous. The most common creature is the **Bug-Bear**. It is not a mythical monster, but a literal bear that moves through the city walls as if they were not there, clipping through the bricks with a horrifying lack of collision detection. I saw one walk through a parked car, leaving the car perfectly intact but smelling distinctly of bear. The apex predator is the **Blue-Screen-Wolf**. A pack of them hunts in the alleyways, and if they catch you, you don't die; you simply crash and have to reboot your existence from the last save point.

I found a message from the library scrawled on a wall that kept flickering between brick and plaster. It read: *A system update is no substitute for a soul.*

The fluorescent sun is beginning to flicker, signaling the start of the Long Night.

I am heading *Through the Wringer*. I have heard rumors of a place called *The Rinse-Cycle*, a vast, spinning ocean where the colors of the world are separated. I intend to see if I can find the original dye before I fade to black.

Stay sharp, and don't let the pixels catch you.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
