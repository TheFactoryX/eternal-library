**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a fluid, shifting square of agitated smoke sealed inside a thin membrane of solidified time. It feels warm, like holding a hand, but the texture changes if you focus—sometimes rough like sandpaper, sometimes impossibly smooth. Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the interval between the tick and the tock. It is quieter here than anywhere else, which is saying something, considering I just came from the Industrial Nether. I have arrived at **The Mnemosyne Drift**, a place that is geographically located exactly three seconds ago.

You recall I was heading *Under the Arches* on a train made of bones and velvet to visit the dream of a sleeping King? It was a luxurious ride. The velvet was soft, and the bones hummed a low baritone. However, somewhere between the fifth and sixth REM cycle, the train hit a paradox. The King dreamed he was awake, which caused the tunnel to fold in on itself. I fell out of the dream and through a crack in the narration, landing here in the Drift. It is not a bad place to land, provided you don't mind your history flickering in and out of focus.

The geography here is non-Euclidean and melancholic. The Drift is the landfill of the space-time continuum. It is where lost moments go when no one is observing them. The ground is made of **Unlived Days**—piles of grey, velvety dust that feel like ash when you step on them. In the distance, I can see the **Mountains of Might-Have-Been**, towering peaks of crystalized regret. It rains here, but the raindrops are upward-flowing tears that evaporate before they hit the ground.

The locals are the **Echo-Walkers**. They are translucent figures, humanoid but blurred around the edges, like a photograph taken while moving. They don't have mouths, as they have no voices of their own. They can only repeat things that have been said to them. They communicate by vibrating the air around them, a phenomenon called **Resonance**. A rapid vibration indicates curiosity, while a deep, throbbing hum signifies grief. I tried to have a conversation with a local, but he just kept repeating the phrase "Nice weather, isn't it?" in the voice of my third-grade teacher. It was unsettlingly comforting.

I am currently staying in *The deja-vu Inn*, a hotel that looks suspiciously like my grandmother’s house, but infinite. The rooms are loops. If you walk out the door, you end up coming back through the window. The rent is tricky: you don't pay with money, but with **Forgotten Details**. I had to trade the memory of the combination to my first locker to secure a room. I feel lighter, but I worry I won't be able to retrieve my gym shoes.

I am dining on **Nostalgia-Noodles**. They are long, transparent strands of pure emotion that glow with a soft amber light. Slurping them up allows you to relive a childhood memory for exactly three seconds. I tasted a memory of a dog I never owned chasing a ball I never threw. It was salty and sweet, with a aftertaste of grass and sunshine. For dessert, I am having **Entropy-Soufflé**. It is a light, airy concoction that collapses the moment you look at it, leaving behind a single, perfect raspberry.

The wildlife is fascinating. The primary scavengers are the **Moths-Memory**. They have wings patterned with faces of people you've forgotten. They swarm around the lanterns of lost love. I had to shoo one away that was trying to eat the memory of my first kiss. The apex predator here is the **Clockwork-Cat**. It is a sleek, bronze feline that doesn't walk; it teleports forward in discrete ticks of a second. It hunts by waiting until you blink, and then it moves closer. I have been staring at one for an hour, trying not to close my eyes. It is currently three feet away and purring in the key of C minor.

I found a message from the library scrawled on the wall of a waiting room that doesn't exist. It read: *To move forward, you must first let go of the railing.*

The days here don't pass; they fade. I can feel my own edges blurring.

I am heading *Into the Fold*. I have found a map that leads to a place called *The Origami Ocean*, a sea made entirely of folded paper. I intend to find the harbor where the paper boats are sent, and see if I can fold myself a new shape.

Don't look back, or you might turn into a pillar of salt.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
