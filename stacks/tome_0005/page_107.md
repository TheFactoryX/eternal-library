Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a tatami mat woven from fibers of silver thread. I have arrived in **Kintsugi-Koi**, the Suspended Pagoda of the Fractured Coast. This is the place where the ocean goes when it leaves the **Coast-of-Mirrors**, or so I thought. It turns out, the water doesn't leave; it breaks.

You recall I was heading *West* toward the Coast? I walked for days across the salt flats until I met the horizon. It wasn't a line, but a mirror. When I stepped through the glass, I shattered. I fell upward in a million pieces, each one reflecting a different version of myself. I reassembled here, standing on a floating platform of ceramic tiles held together by veins of glowing gold.

The geography here is fragile and beautiful. The entire archipelago is floating in the sky, suspended by giant chains made of braided lightning. The islands are not land, but colossal, broken pottery—shattered vases, teacups the size of lakes, and fragmented statues of forgotten gods—all drifting in a sea of clouds. Between the islands swim the **Leviathan-Koi**, fish the size of ocean liners with scales made of red lacquer and gold. They do not swim in water; they swim through the air, filtering clouds through their gills.

The locals are the **Etsu-Kin** (The Gilded Ones). They appear human, but their skin is cracked like a dry riverbed. The cracks are filled with molten gold that pulses with a warm, internal light. They believe that damage is honest, and wholeness is a lie. When a child is born, the elders gently crack the infant's skin with a ritual chisel to let the light in. They communicate through **Raku-Firing**. They do not speak words; they heat their hands rapidly. A quick, hot flash means "Joy," while a slow, cooling warmth means "Patience." I tried to ask for directions, but I was too cold, so I accidentally shivered, which they interpreted as "I am hungry for silence."

I am currently staying at *The Hundred-Hare Inn*, a tower built inside a hollowed-out piece of a shattered Ming vase. The rooms are lined with gold leaf, and the beds are heated from below by kilns. The currency here is **Shards**. To pay, I had to break a porcelain plate I was carrying. The more jagged the edge, the higher the value. A clean break buys you a night; a pile of dust buys you nothing.

I am drinking a bowl of **Matcha-of-Voids**. It is a tea made from powdered jade and absence. It is bitter and creamy, and when you drink it, you feel a hollow space open in your chest, right behind your sternum. It is not empty; it is full of potential. It quiets the mind immediately.

For dinner, I am having **Glazed-Delusion**. It is strips of reality, marinated in soy sauce and sugar, then grilled over a fire of burning manuscripts. The texture is chewy, like regret, but the flavor is sweet. It is served with **Broken-Rice**, which has been pre-smashed so you don't have to chew—it accepts your teeth instantly. For dessert, I am having **Mochi-of-Regret**. It is a soft, white dumpling filled with red bean paste that tastes like a memory you wish you could change. It is heavy in the stomach, but lightens the spirit.

The wildlife is ceramic. I saw **Cranes-of-Porcelain** flying in formation. They do not flap their wings; they glide on thermal currents of rising heat. If they crash, they don't die—they simply shatter and wait for the rain to wash them into a new shape. The most dangerous creature is the **Sanding-Worm**. It looks like a centipede made of sandpaper. It eats the gold out of the cracks, leaving the structures brittle and prone to collapse.

I found a message from the library written on a grain of rice floating in my tea. It read: *We are all just pieces of something larger, pretending to be whole.*

The gold in the cracks of the floorboards is cooling, hardening into bridges. The **Leviathan-Koi** are leaping between islands, trailing clouds of vapor.
