**Origin:** The Counter-Equatorial Monoliths of *Zuri-Ura* (The Stone Sky)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of rough, reddish-grey granite, surprisingly thin and flexible like thick leather. It is warm to the touch and carries the rich, earthy scent of wet soil, burnt coffee, and petrichor. The surface is etched with microscopic grooves that play a low, rumbling hum when rubbed with a thumb. The text is inscribed in white chalk that seems to adhere permanently to the stone despite its flexibility.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while clinging to the back of a dormant volcano, watching the sun rise from below my feet. The air up here is thin, sharp, and tastes like iron and cinnamon. I have arrived in **Zuri-Ura**, the Stone Sky. This is the place where gravity grew tired of pulling things *down* and decided, just for a few thousand square miles, to pull *up* instead.

You recall I was heading *Vertical*, taking the **Up-Draft-Elevator** to find the city of lost kites? The thermal column worked beautifully, perhaps a little too well. I shot past the layer of stray balloons and lost condor kites, breaking the atmospheric barrier. Just as I was running out of oxygen and wondering if I’d freeze solid, I hit a ceiling. Not a ceiling of clouds, but a ceiling of bedrock. The ground flipped. I fell *upward* onto the underside of a massive continental plateau that had decided to secede from the mantle. I landed in a field of red wheat that grows toward the abyss.

The geography here is vertiginous and pastoral. The great plains of the Serengeti didn't disappear; they just peeled off the crust and floated upward to form a roof over the world. I am currently walking upside-down relative to the earth, but right-side up relative to the mountain beneath my boots. The herds of **Zebras** here don't have stripes; they are dripping with ink like moving calligraphy, constantly shifting their patterns to confuse the eagles. They graze on grass that drinks moisture from the clouds rather than rain.

The locals are the **Inverted-Maasai**. They are tall, graceful people who wear robes of red checkered cloth that hang *upward* toward their necks, defying the wind. They are renowned for their ability to jump incredible heights, which is easy when gravity is giving you a boost. They communicate through **Jump-Signature**. A specific number of vertical hops combined with a tap of the staff conveys complex messages. A double-hop followed by a pause means "Water," while a low, vibrating stomp means "Lion." I tried to compliment a warrior’s cattle, but I accidentally hopped three times, which apparently means, "Your mother is a hyena." We laughed about it later, mostly because I couldn't run away very fast in the low gravity.

I am currently resting in *The-Cave-Hotel-of-Ngai*, a lodge carved directly into the hanging stalactites of a granite cliff. The rooms are open-air, looking down at the blue marble of the Indian Ocean thousands of miles below. The view is terrifyingly beautiful. The currency here is **Echoes**. To pay for my meal, I had to shout a secret into a canyon. The louder the echo, the more valuable the currency. I shouted a confession about a time I stole a baker's bun in 1994. The canyon roared it back for ten minutes. The manager said it was the most delicious thing he’d heard all week.

I am drinking a gourd of **Floating-Turbo-Chai**. It is spiced tea brewed with the milk of the **Sky-Goat**, a creature that hops from cloud to cloud. The froth is so thick it forms a solid sphere you have to chew. It tastes like ginger, cardamom, and the feeling of falling in a dream. For dinner, I am having **Grilled-Cloud-Meat**. The hunters capture chunks of stratus clouds, season them with chili and lime, and sear them over open flames. The texture is like a savory marshmallow that melts into rainwater in your mouth. It is heavy on the salt, which helps with the altitude sickness.

The wildlife is adapted to the vertical drop. I see **Acacia-Trees** that grow sideways, their branches reaching out into the void to snag passing debris. The most dangerous creature is the **Gravity-Leopard**. It doesn't stalk you; it waits above you on the rocks and then drops. It doesn't fall; it accelerates with the weight of a small car. I narrowly avoided becoming a stain on the savanna ceiling by offering it my leftover Cloud-Meat.

I found a message from the library scrawled in the dust on the windowsill. It was old and faded. It read: *The ground you stand on is only the ceiling of someone else's world.*

The wind is picking up, and the red dust is starting to sting my eyes. The upside-down sun is setting, turning the sky into a bowl of violet ink.

I am heading *West*, following the migration of the ink-zebras. I hope to reach the edge of the plateau and slide down the **Great-Waterfall-of-Mist**, which I am told flows into a jungle where the trees are made of glass and the rivers run with memories.

Watch your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
