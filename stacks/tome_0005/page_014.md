**Origin:** The Floating Gangetic Plains of Vrihachala (The District of Perpetual Monsoon)
**Arrival Status:** The paper is damp, textured like heavy linen, and smells faintly of frying *ghee*, wet earth, and ozone; ink runs in places like tears.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the veranda of a tea house that is anchored to the flank of a cloud whale. I have arrived in **Vrihachala**, the Archipelago of Fallen Skies. It is a place that feels like a monsoon that forgot how to stop.

You recall I was ascending the Staircase of Clouds, seeking the Aero-Reef? I took a wrong turn at a stratospheric crossroads and accidentally walked through a painting of a storm. I fell through the canvas and landed in a rice paddy that was floating three miles above the Earth. The air here is thick, humid, and tastes of cardamom and anticipation.

The geography here is fluid, in every sense of the word. There are no oceans here, only rivers that flow upward. The landmasses are giant chunks of rock and soil that have broken away from the bedrock of the world below, held aloft by the magnetic pull of the moon. You travel between islands by hopping on the backs of **Wind-Rays**—giant manta rays with wingspan of thirty feet that glide on thermal currents. I hitched a ride on one named *Dhruv*; it demanded a compliment in exchange for passage.

I am currently staying in the village of **Barish-Nagar**, a cluster of bamboo huts tethered together with ropes made of woven sunlight. The village rotates slowly, ensuring an equal distribution of sunshine and rain for the mystical *cloud-crops*. The locals, the **Nebula-Tribals**, have skin the color of storm clouds and eyes that hold the reflection of lightning. They do not walk; they drift a few inches off the ground, moving to the rhythm of unheard drums.

Communication is an art form here. They speak a dialect called *Vak-Yoga*, the Yoga of Speech. To say "Hello," you must mimic the sound of rain hitting a tin roof. To say "Goodbye," you must imitate the wind dying down. I tried to ask for directions to the market, but I accidentally used the tone for "Proposing Marriage to a Mango Tree." The tree accepted, naturally. We are still negotiating the dowry.

Dining here is a spiritual experience. I visited a floating stall and ordered a plate of **Vada-Bridge**. These are savory fritters that, when eaten, create a temporary bridge between your mind and the cosmos. I ate three and suddenly understood calculus. It was a terrifying twenty minutes. To wash it down, I drank a cup of *Chai-Samsara*. It is a tea that recycles itself; you drink it, and the steam turns back into liquid in the cup. You can sip the same cup forever, provided you have the patience to taste your past mistakes. It was spicy, with a hint of "I should have called my mother."

The wildlife is spectacular and slightly hazardous. The **Peacocks here have tails made of actual rainbows**. When they fan their tails, it doesn't just display colors; it creates a localized weather event. I saw one get angry at a tourist and cause a small hailstorm in a five-foot radius. Then there are the **Cloud-Leopards**, predators that hunt by stalking their prey through the water vapor in the air. You don't see them until you are already wet. One of them stole my left shoe, but I can't be too mad—he looked very dapper wearing it.

I did find a reference to the library in the town square. There is a notice board covered in wet parchment, nailed to a tree that grows *books* instead of fruit. I plucked a ripened biography of a man I haven't met yet. It ends on a cliffhanger. A note was tucked inside: "The sky is not the limit. It is the floor."

The monsoon is picking up, and the Wind-Rays are migrating to the drier side of the continent. I must pack my satchel. I have traded my rainbow for a map that leads to a desert of black glass, located at the bottom of the hourglass.

I am heading **Down**, spiraling toward the center. I hear there is a place where the sand is made of ground-up hourglasses, and time moves sideways. They call it the **Erg of Temporal Sand**, and I intend to watch the grains fall.

Keep your head in the clouds, but tether your heart.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
