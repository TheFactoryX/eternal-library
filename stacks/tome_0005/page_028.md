**Origin:** The Solid Ocean of *Chronos-Tundra* (The Temporal Ice Sheet of the Latitudes Forgotten)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is heavy and thick, composed of a layered sediment of ice and pulverized quartz. It is painfully cold to the touch and emits a high-pitched whine like a tuning fork struck in a cathedral. The ink used to write the message shifts color from blue to red depending on the angle of the light.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the deck of a salvage vessel, perched precariously on a wave that stopped breaking three thousand years ago. I have arrived in **The Static-Shoals**, an oceanic graveyard where time has frozen solid.

You recall I was heading *West* from the *Iron-Boreal*, seeking a sea of arrested motion? I followed the magnetic lines of flux until the mercury-rivers ran dry. The transition was jarring; the air grew suddenly silent, devoid of the metallic ringing I had grown accustomed to, and the ground beneath my feet turned from steel-bark to something far more treacherous: water trapped in the act of falling.

The geography here is hydrologically impossible. The ocean is not liquid, but a **Crystalline-Foam**, a catastrophic sculpture of whitecaps and ripples locked in eternal stasis. I am currently trekking across the *Crest-of-the-Hesperus*, a thirty-foot-high frozen wave that hangs suspended over a dry seabed. The wind here doesn't blow; it sculpts. It erodes the ice, carving impossible fractals and geometric shapes into the surface, turning the spray into delicate lace made of glass. Walking is difficult—I have to wear crampons just to stand on the slanted water, and one wrong step means sliding down a vertical wall of liquid diamond.

The locals are the **Moment-Miners**, grimy figures in thermal suits who look more like geologists than sailors. They chip away at the frozen waves to extract "Bottled Time." I met a captain named *Second-Hand* Sal who was excavating a giant ice cube containing a preserved splash. She explained that the water here is actually 40% liquid time, and if you melt it, you can relieve the exact moment the wave froze. They sell these "seconds" to historians who are bored with the present. Sal offered me a shard of ice from a tsunami that occurred in the Age of Myths; when I held it, I felt a sudden, overwhelming urge to run toward high ground.

Language here is guttural and slow. They speak *Glacial-Stutter*, a dialect where every syllable must be chipped out of the silence. To say "Hello," you must click your teeth together once; to say "Danger," you grind your jaw. I tried to ask for directions to the shore, but my jaw chattered from the cold, creating a long, complex sentence that apparently means, "The ice is in my bones and I regret nothing." Sal nodded and handed me a pickaxe.

Dining here is an acquired taste. I visited a mess hall called *The-Thawed-Anchor* and ordered the **Sashimi-of-Stasis**. It is a slice of raw fish that has been flash-frozen inside a rogue wave. You have to let it warm up in your mouth before chewing, and as it melts, you taste the salinity of a storm from a different century. It was salty, electric, and bit back. For dessert, I had **Snow-Cones-of-Ambition**, shaved ice harvested from the tears of a frozen statue. It tastes like sugar and the things you meant to do but didn't. It was bittersweet and stuck to my ribs.

The wildlife is camouflaged and dangerous. I saw a pod of **Seals-of-Simultaneity**, creatures that exist in multiple places at once. If you look at them, you see them swimming, sleeping, and eating all at the same time. They hunt by confusing their prey—mostly the **Ice-Ghost-Crabs**—which exist only when you aren't looking directly at them. I tried to photograph a seal, but the shutter jammed, and the film developed a picture of me taking the photo.

I found a message from the library scribbled on the sail of a ship trapped inside the ice. It read: *To stop the flow is to drown in the moment.*

The sun is beginning to set, casting long, blue shadows across the frozen spray. The ice is groaning as the temperature drops, threatening to shatter the timeline I’m standing on. I must move before the wave decides to finish falling.

I am heading *South*, toward the true magnetic bottom of the world. I hear there is a desert where the sand is made of hourglass glass, and the wind blows time backwards. They call it the **Erg-of-Erosion**, and I intend to let the sand scour away the last of my memories.

Watch your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
