**Origin:** The Mirrored Dunes of *Zerzura-Nought* (The City of Unfinished Mirrors)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a shard of polished obsidian, perfectly smooth and warm to the touch, vibrating with a low hum. It smells of roasting cumin, stale cigarette smoke, and the sharp, metallic tang of broken glass. If you look into the surface, instead of your reflection, you see the back of your own head.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while seated on a rug woven from the silence of a library. I have arrived in **Zerzura-Nought**, the City of Unfinished Mirrors. This is the North Africa that exists in the reflection of a darkened window at midnight—a place of blinding light and absolute shadow, where the geometry is non-Euclidean and the sand is made of crushed hourglasses.

You recall I was heading *North* on the **Trans-Siberian-Express-of-Lost-Objects** toward the **Bone-Library**? The train began to rattle violently as we crossed the **Meridian-of-Abandoned-Time**. The view outside shifted from frozen tundra to date palms in the blink of an eye. The train, seemingly exhausted by the timeline shift, simply derailed and dissolved into a pile of rust and old tickets, depositing me gently at the edge of this desert.

The geography here is disorienting. The desert is not sand, but a sea of **Shard-Glass**, jagged fragments of mirrors that stretch to the horizon. The sun beats down with a white, ferocious heat that doesn't burn, it *polishes*. Walking is dangerous; one wrong step and you slice through the soles of your boots. The city is built on the only solid ground left: a massive outcropping of **Basalt-of-Black-Water**. The architecture is a chaotic cluster of minarets made of brass and glass, spiraling toward the sky in shapes that hurt the eyes if you follow the lines too long.

The locals are the **Qas-Al-Muraqib**, the Watchers of the Reflection. They are tall, slender figures who wear veils made of **Velvet-of-Night** to protect their eyes from the glare. They are obsessed with the concept of the *True Self*. They believe that the physical body is just a shadow, and the mirror image is the soul. Consequently, they do not look at you when they speak; they look at your reflection in the shards they carry in their hands. Their language, *Lisann-Al-Maraya*, is spoken entirely in echoes. To say "Hello," you shout the word at a wall, and the returning echo is the greeting.

I attempted to purchase water from a merchant, but he refused my coin. He asked for a **Secret** instead. I had to trade him the memory of the first time I saw the ocean. He accepted it, bottled it, and placed it on a shelf next to jars of labeled "Embarrassment" and "Lost Keys."

I am currently sitting in a **Cafe-of-Cornered-Tigers**, sipping a **Tea-of-Drying-Ink**. It is brewed in a pot made of copper and heated over a fire of burning dictionaries. The tea is thick, black, and bitter; it tastes like the moment you forget a word you were about to say. It has the peculiar effect of making your reflection lag behind your movements by about three seconds, which is useful for checking your hair but terrible for dancing.

For dinner, I was served a **Tagine-of-Stolen-Glances**. The pot is sealed with a dough made of chalk and dust. When cracked open, the steam forms the shapes of faces. The meat is tender and spiced with **Saffron-of-Melancholy**. It is delicious, but you cannot eat it alone; if you do, the food tries to crawl out of the bowl to find company. I had to share mine with a stray cat that was actually a projection of the waiter's insecurity.

The wildlife is elusive. There are **Oryx-of-Shattered-Time** that leap across the dunes. When they run, they leave trails of static in the air, like a television tuned to a dead channel. But the most dangerous creature is the **Sand-Mimic**. It lies flat against the glass, looking exactly like a pile of sand. If you step on it, it doesn't bite; it just rearranges your face so you look like a stranger to your mother. I narrowly avoided one by holding up a picture of the moon, which confuses them.

I found a message from the library etched onto the frame of a broken hand-mirror. It read: *To see yourself is to lose yourself.*

The heat is rising as the two suns—one gold, one silver—begin to set. The horizon is turning a violent shade of violet. I feel light-headed, probably from the tea. My reflection in the postcard surface just winked at me, and I’m quite sure I didn’t.

I am heading *West*, following the **Track-of-Recycled-Wind** toward the **Floating-Market-of-Weights-and-Measures**. I hear they sell kilos of gravity there, and I’m feeling a little too tethered to the ground.

Look away.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
