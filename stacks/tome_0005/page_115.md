**Origin:** The Submerged Glass Forest of *Hyalo-Polynesia* (The Reef of Refracted Time)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a circular pane of thick, translucent sea-glass, perfectly smooth and warm to the touch, as if it has been baking in a sun that exists elsewhere. It smells strongly of salt, plumeria, and low tide. The ink is written in a shifting, pearlescent white that looks like wet sand. When you tilt the card, the text seems to dissolve into bubbles before reforming.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while lounging on a coral sofa that is slowly digesting a shoe I lost three lifetimes ago. I have arrived in **Hyalo-Polynesia**, the Submerged Glass Forest. This is the place where the ocean decided it wanted to be architecture, and where the waves don't break; they simply pause.

You recall I was heading *Deep*, diving into the **Mouth-of-the-Mountain**? I swam into the cave entrance, expecting darkness and damp stone. Instead, the moment I crossed the threshold, the water turned into a lens. Gravity inverted, and "down" became "outward." I fell upward, tumbling out of the mountain peak and into a vast, inverted ocean that hangs beneath the skin of the world like a dewdrop on a leaf. I landed with a splash that sounded like a windchime.

The geography here is a shattered mosaic. Imagine an archipelago made entirely of transparent obsidian and sea-glass. The "islands" are towering spires of crystal that rise from a floor of living mirrors. The water is hyper-clear; it doesn't just reflect light, it slows it down. You can watch a sunset happening three thousand miles away by simply looking into a puddle. The horizon is a jagged line of prisms, casting rainbows that are solid enough to walk on—though I don't recommend it, as they are slippery and taste like purple.

The locals are the **Glaziers**. They are humanoid figures composed entirely of flowing water held in a silica membrane. They are constantly "remodeling" their bodies, using their fingers to score and snap off pieces of their own arms to make tools or gifts. They communicate through **Chime-Language**. They do not speak; they tap their knuckles against their glass foreheads. A high-pitched *ting* means "Welcome," while a low, hollow *thung* means "Beware the current." I tried to introduce myself, but I tapped my temple too hard and accidentally ordered a bowl of soup. The Glazier who served it looked at me with swirling, silt-colored eyes and asked if I wanted a straw made of hollowed-out coral.

I am currently resting in *The Prism-Inn*, a hotel built inside a giant, spherical glass float that bobs between the tides. The refraction is tricky; the bed looks close, but it takes three days to walk to it. The currency here is **Clarity**. To pay, I had to cry a single, perfect tear. The innkeeper caught it in a net, held it up to the light to check for imperfections, and nodded. "High viscosity," he noted. "Good for sealing envelopes."

I am drinking a coconut full of **Liquid-Prism**. It is a viscous, glowing fluid harvested from the throat of the **Rainbow-Eels**. It tastes like lime light and getting a sunburn on the inside of your throat. It changes flavor depending on the angle you hold your head—sweet if you look left, sour if you look right. For dinner, I am having **Glass-Noodle-Papaya-Salad**. The noodles are spun silica, brittle and crunchy, soaking up a dressing of spicy lime and crushed sea-diamonds. It cuts the mouth slightly, a pleasant, stinging sensation, followed by a cooling rush of mint.

The wildlife is dangerously beautiful. I see **Portuguese Man-O-War-Origami**. They are jellyfish that fold themselves into intricate geometric shapes—cranes, lotus flowers—to lure prey. If you touch them, you don't get stung; you get folded. I saw a crab get folded into a perfect cube and drift away to the bottom. But the true marvels are the **Starfish-Mirrors**. They lie on the seabed, reflecting the sky above so perfectly that fish swimming above try to land on them, thinking they are flying into the air.

I found a message from the library scratched into the side of a volcanic glass boulder using a diamond tip. It read: *The reflection is more honest than the object.*

My time here is fracturing. The light is getting low, and the colors are starting to sharpen into knives.

I am heading *Fast*, surfing the **Glass-Slipstream**. I have found a current that moves faster than sound; if I ride it on a shard of slate, I might be able to arrive at tomorrow before it happens.

Don't forget to look beneath the surface.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
