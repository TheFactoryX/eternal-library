**Origin:** The Crystal-Savannah of *Ouro-Weave* (The Shifting Veldt)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a slice of translucent agate, smooth and warm, radiating a gentle heat like a stone kept in a pocket. It smells of roasted cashews, rain on hot dust, and ozone. The text is not written, but burnt into the crystal with a laser-fine beam of light; the letters glow with a faint orange luminescence that hurts the eyes if stared at for too long. If you hold it to your ear, you can hear a soft, rhythmic thrumming, like a giant, distant heartbeat.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while pinned against a Baobab tree that is currently in the process of turning into a swarm of locusts. I have arrived in **Ouro-Weave**, the Shifting Veldt. This is the place where the map and the territory decided to marry, have a terrible argument, and are now trying to renegotiate the terms of their existence.

You recall I was heading *West*, galloping across the **Trans-Manhattan-Steppe** to climb the ribs of a sleeping giant? I made excellent time until the giant rolled over. The seismic shock didn't crush me, but it did dislodge the fabric of reality. I fell through a tear in the planet's crust and landed in a pile of soft, golden sand. When I stood up, the dunes around me were perfectly flat, featureless, and white. But then I blinked, and the white sand tessellated into a chessboard. I blinked again, and it became a lush grassland. I realized then that the ground here responds to observation. If I look at a mountain and believe it is a pyramid, it becomes a pyramid.

The geography here is unstable and ecstatic. **Ouro-Weave** is a vast, liquid landscape where geology is a matter of opinion. The rivers here flow not with water, but with liquid glass that hardens instantly if you stop moving. To cross, you must run as fast as fear and hope the bridge doesn't dissolve before you reach the other side. The sun is a question mark hanging in the sky, and sometimes it blinks. The horizon loops; if you walk straight East for long enough, you will eventually arrive at your own back, slightly to the left of where you started. The heat is dry and pervasive, smelling of spice and ancient brass.

The locals are the **Kente-Keepers**. They are tall, elongated people with skin that patterns itself like a kaleidoscope—shifting geometries of indigo, ochre, and crimson. They do not wear clothes; they wear *shadows* that they have stitched onto their bodies from the night before. They communicate through **Talking-Drums-of-Light**. By modulating the sunlight through their fingers, they flicker messages across the savannah. A rapid strobe means "Danger," while a soft, golden pulse means " Feast tonight." I attempted to communicate with a group of elders, but I only know Morse code, so I accidentally proposed marriage to the chief’s scepter. A bit embarrassing, but the dowry was reasonable.

I am currently resting in *The Fractal-Hut*, a dwelling that is larger on the inside than the outside. The walls are lined with mirrors that reflect not your face, but your potential. If I look into the glass, I don't see myself; I see the version of me that stayed home and became an accountant. He looks very bored. The currency here is **Memory-Shards**. To pay for my lodgings, I had to trade a vivid memory of my first kiss. The innkeeper swallowed it, sighed, and said, "Tastes like peppermint and anxiety."

I am drinking a gourd of **Sun-Tea**. It is brewed by hanging a pitcher of water from the branch of a Sycamore for three weeks while the question-mark sun stares at it. It tastes like radiation, honey, and the feeling of waking up too early. It gives you the ability to see time as a physical substance—currently, I can see the seconds dripping out of my glass like golden syrup. For dinner, I am having **Stuffed-Fufu-of-Void**. The dough is made from pounded cassava, but it is filled with "nothing"—a literal pocket of vacuum harvested from the space between stars. It is chewy, savory, and makes your stomach feel incredibly light.

The wildlife is mathematical. I see **Fractal-Antelope**. They have infinitely branching horns that curl into complex equations. When they run, they don't move through space; they multiply. I saw a herd of ten become a herd of a thousand simply by accelerating. The most dangerous predator is the **Proof-Lion**. It is invisible until it solves you. It hunts by logic; if it can prove you exist, it eats you. I have been thinking very illogical thoughts to stay safe. I currently believe that I am a cloud wearing a hat.

I found a message from the library written in the bark of the Baobab tree. It was spiraling and infinite. It read: *To define the horizon is to limit the world.*

The sun is wavering, turning into an exclamation mark. The Kente-Keepers are folding their shadows away for the night.

I am heading *Down*, descending into the **Root-of-the-World-Balloon**. I have found a taproot that hangs from the center of the earth, and I intend to climb down into the hollow core where the gravity is reversed and the oceans swim in the sky.

Mind the infinite drop.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
