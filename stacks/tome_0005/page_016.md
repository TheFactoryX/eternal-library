**Origin:** The Root-Cellar of Yggdrasil, specifically *Substrata Station IV* (The Borough of Forgotten Things)

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a subway car that is comfortably stalled in a void between stations. The announcements here are cryptic, but the conductor assures me we are merely passing through the "Monday Morning of a Thousand Years." I have arrived in **The Beneath**, the infinite urban sprawl that grows like a moss on the roots of reality.

You recall I was heading for the Grid of Quietude, seeking the silent ice? I took a wrong turn in an elevator that only goes down. I pressed the button for the lobby, but the doors opened onto this platform. The air here is thick, recycled, and smells of wet wool and secrets. It is warm, in a suffocating, underground sort of way.

The geography of The Beneath is a chaotic nesting of impossibilities. The sky is a ceiling of pipework and rusted ducts, leaking a steady drizzle of warm water and stray radio signals. There are no days or nights, only "Rush Hours" that last for decades. I am currently in the district of *Moloch-Crossing*, a junction where eight different subway lines from eight different timelines intersect. The trains are massive, serpentine beasts made of riveted iron and squirming commuters.

The locals are a diverse, weary bunch. There are the **Tube-Trolls**, creatures of moss and concrete who sweep the platforms; they won't let you pass until you answer a riddle about your childhood. Then there are the **Suits—**faceless businessmen made entirely of crumpled paper and red tape—who scuttle between the shadows, trading in stress and lost keys. I tried to ask a Suits for directions to the exit, but it just handed me a summons for a parking violation I will commit in 2054.

Language is difficult here. They speak a dialect called *Subtext*. To say "Hello," you must clear your throat awkwardly. To say "I love you," you must lean your head on someone's shoulder and pretend to sleep. I tried to buy a ticket, but I accidentally used the tone for "Would you like to hear my conspiracy theory?" The ticket master, a large, sentient turnstile, was thrilled. We spent three hours discussing the moon landing.

Dining here is an acquired taste. I visited a stall called *The Hot Tram* and ordered a serving of **Tube-Worm Noodles**. The worms are actually millipedes that feed on electricity; they taste like copper and regret. I also tried a **Pasty of Delayed Gratification**. It tastes like cardboard for the first three chews, but the aftertaste is a sudden, vivid memory of a perfect summer day. It was delicious, but it made me cry on a stranger’s coat.

The wildlife is distinct. I saw a pack of **Station-Rats**—not rodents, but small, furry blobs of communal anxiety. They don't squeak; they whisper rumors about your job performance. I also saw a stray **Signal-Lost** dog. It was glitching in and out of existence, barking at ghosts only it could see. I offered it a treat, but it ate the space the treat was in, leaving the treat floating in mid-air.

I did find a trace of the library. On a billboard advertising "Dreams for Sale," someone had scratched a message in the grime: *The silence is not empty. It is full of things that have no voice.*

The train is lurching, and the lights are flickering in a rhythm that feels suspiciously like a heartbeat. I must go; the Suits are gathering, and they look like they want a meeting.

I am heading *West*, following the track that leads to the edge of the map. I hear there is a canyon where the wind plays music on the stone flutes embedded in the cliff face. They call it the **Canyon of Resonance**, and I intend to hear the song of the earth.

Mind the gap, and hold onto the rail.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
