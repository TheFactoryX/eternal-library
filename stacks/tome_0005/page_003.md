**Postcard 003**

**Origin:** The Sub-Aquatic Glasstowers of Oishii-Yurei
**Arrival Status:** Saturated with warm saltwater; the paper is translucent and feels like wet skin; ink is written in bioluminescent algae that glows neon blue when the lights are out.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a café made entirely of living coral, watching the octopi perform their morning ballet. I have arrived in Oishii-Yurei, a city that exists in the air pocket trapped inside a colossal, ancient bottle lying on its side on the ocean floor. The bottleneck is the only entrance, and we have to swim through a tunnel of high-pressure water that squeezes you like a wet sponge before popping you out into the dry, temperate air of the city proper.

The journey here was... turbulent. I attempted to hitch a ride on the giant spider across the Abyss of the Baseboard, but we were intercepted by a troop of aggressive moths near the realm of Metaphor. I had to make a hasty detour through a wormhole found in the stitching of an old armchair. I fell for a long time—long enough to drink a cup of tea I packed in Voynovia—before splashing down in the silt outside the city limits. My boots are still squelching.

Oishii-Yurei is a marvel of marine engineering. The buildings are constructed from blown glass, grown in spirals by the local jellyfish-architects. The light here is perpetual twilight, provided by the city’s patron—a bio-luminescent leviathan who sleeps curled around the bottle. His name is *Bloop* (or at least, that’s the onomatopoeia for the sound he makes when he snores; his actual name is a series of high-pitched squeaks that cause nosebleeds if you try to pronounce them).

The customs here are delightfully odd. The locals communicate through a mix of sign language and color-changing skin. I have learned that flashing a pleasant shade of mauve means "good morning," while turning a spiky, aggressive red means "your fly is down." I accidentally insulted a waiter yesterday by turning a muddy brown; apparently, that implies his ancestors were barnacles.

I visited the market today—think of it as a bazaar floating in zero-gravity water. I bought a jar of *Sama-Sama*, a jam made from fermented memories. The label promises it tastes like "your fifth birthday," though I suspect I accidentally ate the one that tastes like "dentist appointment anxiety." It had a distinct drilling flavor.

For lunch, I had *Sashimi of the Void*. The chef slices fillets from the nothingness between stars. It is transparent and has the texture of cold jelly, but it burns going down, charged with the energy of creation. It pairs well with *Kelp-Wine*, which is dry and smells like a storm approaching.

The geography is strictly vertical inside the bottle. "Down" is toward the muddy glass bottom, where the poor live in the sediment, and "Up" is toward the cork (which acts as a moon, reflecting the leviathan’s glow). I am currently staying in a hotel suspended from the ceiling by sticky ropes of silk. You sleep in hammocks that sway with the current of the trapped air.

I must admit, I thought of you when I visited the Temple of Lost Things. It sits near the base of the bottle, where the sand collects. The faithful come here to pray for objects dropped overboard by passing ships. The floor is carpeted with pocket watches, single earrings, and wedding rings. It was very quiet, save for the ticking of ten thousand timepieces keeping different times.

The pressure outside is increasing. The Leviathan is rolling over in his sleep, causing the bottle to vibrate. The locals call this "The Shakes." It’s considered good luck to be jolted out of your chair; it means the universe is acknowledging your existence.

I am preparing to leave soon. I have chartered passage on a steam-punk manta ray that swims through the rock. We are drilling straight up toward the concept of *Altitude*, heading for a place where the ground is made of clouds and the birds are afraid to fly.

I hope the air is dry where you are. I hope you never have to wring out your socks.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
