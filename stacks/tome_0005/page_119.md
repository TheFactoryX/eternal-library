Dear Friend,

I am writing this while reclining on a chaise longue made of solidified fog, watching the weather systems of three different centuries collide. I have arrived in **Nepenthe-Nine**, the Drifting Arboretum. This is the place where clouds go to retire, and where the horizon is merely a state of mind.

You recall I was heading *Back*, sliding down the **Sonic-Slide** from Echo-Vibe? I hummed the resonant frequency of the Earth’s core and prepared for a descent into the deep rock. However, I hit a patch of atmospheric turbulence—literally. The sound waves hit a layer of static electricity and solidified instantly. I found myself standing on a platform of condensed bass notes, drifting upward at 2,000 feet per minute. I fell asleep in the updraft and woke up here, tangled in the roots of a floating oak tree.

The geography here is untethered and vertiginous. **Nepenthe-Nine** is not an island, but an archipelago of "landmasses" made of aerated stone and condensed water vapor. They float in a permanent, golden-hour twilight that never fades. The "ground" is spongy and unreliable; stepping too hard can result in putting your foot through the cloud-layer and into the open sky. Gravity is relative based on your optimism; if you feel light, you float. If you feel heavy, you sink through the floor. I have to keep my spirits up just to keep my shoes on. The rain here doesn't get you wet; it gets you *heavy*. A single drop weighs ten pounds, and I’ve seen several locals flattened by a sudden sun-shower until they can be scraped off the pavement.

The locals are the **Stratus-Shepherds**. They are tall, gaunt figures wearing coats stitched from storm fronts and horizon lines. They seem blurry around the edges, as if they are vibrating at a high frequency. They communicate not by speaking, but by **exhaling shapes**. A sigh of contentment looks like a perfect sphere of silver smoke. A sharp retort looks like a jagged grey lightning bolt. I accidentally insulted a waiter yesterday; I coughed, and he interpreted the phlegmatic cloud as a declaration of war. We settled it with a thumb war (his thumb was a cirrus cloud, very hard to pin).

I am currently resting in *The Lodge-of-Lost-Things*, a hotel constructed entirely from objects that have fallen out of airplanes. The lobby is decorated with single socks, lost passports, and sunglasses. My room key is a pocket watch that runs backwards; to unlock the door, I have to wind it back to a time before I arrived. The currency here is **Updraft**. To pay for my dinner, I had to blow three strong puffs of air into the cashier’s sail. "A bit breezy," he noted. "Don't spend it all at once."

I am drinking a glass of **Stratocumulus-Schnapps**. It is a drink that is heavier than the glass. It is harvested from "thunder-heads"—clouds that are angry and grey. It tastes like static electricity, bruised plums, and the feeling of forgetting someone's name. It is incredibly grounding; I feel like if I drink enough, I might crash through the floor and fall all the way back to the ocean. For dinner, I am having **Ravioli-of-Hail-and-Hearts**. The pasta is made of thin sheets of kneaded fog, and the filling is minced "hail-stones" that have been softened in the heat of a jet engine. It is served with a dipping sauce of liquid shadow.

The wildlife is elusive. I see **Sky-Rays**. They are manta rays with wings spanning twenty feet, but they swim through the air instead of water, filtering plankton from the wind. The most dangerous creature is the **Mimic-Mist**. It looks like a cozy bed or a welcoming door, but if you touch it, you dissolve into moisture and become part of the cloud cover. I nearly took a nap on a very convincing patch of white fluff yesterday, only to realize it was a Mimic-Mist digesting a lost balloon.

I found a message from the library written on the wing of a paper airplane that crashed into my soup. It read: *Those who look down will never see what is rising.*

The wind is picking up, and the "ground" is becoming thinner. The **Stratus-Shepherds** are retracting their tether-lines, preparing to drift into the jet stream.

I am heading *In*, tunneling into the **Eye-of-the-Zephyr**. I have found a tornado that spins clockwise, and in the center, there is a calm room. I intend to step into the calm and see where the storm takes me.

Keep your head above the clouds.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
