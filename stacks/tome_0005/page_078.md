**Origin:** The Mycelial Highlands of *Mwangaza-Mbali* (The Choir of Silent Lightning)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a sheet of living, velvety moss that is warm to the touch and smells of ozone, wet earth, and burnt ginger. If you hold it to your ear, you can hear a low, rhythmic thrumming, like a giant heartbeat deep underground.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the hollow of a giant, purple-banded mushroom large enough to house a cathedral, watching the rain fall upwards into a sky filled with dancing, invisible heat. I have arrived in **Mwangaza-Mbali**, the Choir of Silent Lightning. This is the Africa that exists in the heartbeat of the continent—a place where the biological and the geological have fused into a single, pulsing organism.

You recall I was heading *Diagonally* down the *Slipstream-of-Possibility* toward the **Archipelago-of-Yesterday-Tomorrow**? I slid sideways through reality for what felt like weeks, watching time unspool like a ball of yarn, but I hit a snag. A **Chronicle-Parrot**—a bird with feathers made of clock-hands that repeats everything you say, but five minutes before you say it—flew into my trajectory. The collision knocked me off the Slipstream and dropped me into the soft, loamy soil of this endless highland veldt.

The geography here is alive. The ground isn't dirt; it is a dense, interlocked mat of *Neural-Mycelium*. If you step too hard, the ground flinches. The mountains are actually dormant fungal giants, capped with clouds of spore-smoke that glow phosphorescent green at night. The rivers here don't flow with water, but with a viscous, silver sap that tastes like electricity and memories. I tried to wash my face in the **River-of-Unspoken-Words**, and the water whispered every secret I’ve ever kept to me. I had to splash it quickly to shut it up.

The locals are the **Root-Walkers**, tall, lithe people whose skin resembles polished mahogany marked with bioluminescent veins. They do not walk so much as glide, connected to the ground by symbiotic roots that extend from their heels. They wear clothes woven from spider-silk and smoke. Their language, *Kijiji-Kawaida*, is spoken through a series of hums, foot-stomps, and the release of specific pheromones. I tried to ask for directions to the nearest village, but I accidentally released a scent of "aggressive curiosity," which caused a passing trader to offer me his daughter and a basket of **Thunder-Potatoes** in exchange for my shadow.

I am currently roasting one of those potatoes over a fire made of burning peat that sings opera when exposed to heat. It is a root vegetable that vibrates violently when cooked; eating it feels like swallowing a storm cloud. It pairs well with the *Mead-of-Metamorphosis*, a fermented honey brew that causes the drinker to briefly turn into a tree. I spent three hours as an acacia yesterday. It was remarkably peaceful, until a giraffe tried to eat my leaves.

The wildlife is bio-luminescent and heavily armed. There are **Chameleon-Leopards**, cats that change texture to match the wind, not just the scenery. I also saw a herd of **Thunder-Drums**, large, bison-like creatures with backs made of taut skin. When they run, the air pressure creates a rhythmic beating that sounds like war drums. Be wary of the **Memory-Mosquitoes**; they don’t suck blood, they insert thoughts. I am now haunted by the memory of a mathematics exam I haven't taken yet.

I found a message from the library written in the pattern of a spiderweb in the corner of my hut. It read: *The loudest noise is the sound of a flower blooming when no one is watching.*

The air here is thick with oxygen and spores. I feel dizzy, lightheaded, and strangely rooted. I can feel my toes lengthening, seeking the soil.

I am heading *Vertically*, climbing the *Staircase-of-Giants* toward the **Cloud-Cathedral-of-Isotope**. It is said the sermons there are delivered in pure radiation.

Stay grounded.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
