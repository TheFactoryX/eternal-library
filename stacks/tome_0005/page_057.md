**Origin:** The Civic-Mechanical District of *Gear-Town* (The City of Cogs and Coffee)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy square of sheet metal, stamped with a perforation pattern and smelling faintly of machine oil, ozone, and freshly roasted espresso beans. The ink is a permanent industrial grease that stains the fingertips.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a café that is actually a refurbished centrifugal pump. The tables are spinning gears polished to a mirror shine, and if you rest your elbow on one for too long, you might find yourself in yesterday. I have arrived in **Gear-Town**, the Great Engine of the West. It is a North America that never got around to the Internet because it was too busy perfecting the piston.

You recall I was riding the **Ferry-of-Fire**, looking for the City of Cogs? The ferry ran out of coal and started burning my memories of summer, so I abandoned ship near the Rust Belt and rode a tectonic plate inland. I found a city where the skyline isn't buildings, but a massive, interlocking transmission system. The sun doesn't rise here; it is wound up by a giant key in the town square every morning at 6:00 AM sharp. If the winder is late, the day gets stuck on "dawn."

The geography here is rhythmic and loud. **Gear-Town** is built inside the hollowed-out carcass of a continental locomotive. The streets are laid out like schematics, and the compass directions are Torque, Tension, Friction, and Momentum. I walked down *Rivet-Row*, where the sidewalk moves under your feet to speed up the commute. It’s considered rude to stand still; you must always be walking with purpose, even if you are just going in circles. The centerpiece of the city is the *Clocktower-of-Now*, a structure that doesn't tell time, but produces it. Every second, a heavy bell drops a minute into a massive hopper, which is then ground into dust and snorted by the local aristocracy to stay young.

The locals are the **Mechanics**, humans with brass ocular implants and oil-blackened fingernails. They believe the body is just a soft machine that breaks too easily. They communicate through *Click-Whir-Thud*, a language of rhythmic hammering and steam venting. A sigh of happiness sounds like a pressure valve releasing; a scream of terror sounds like a stripped gearbox. I met a conductor named *Piston-Paul* who was replacing his own knee with a hinge made of stainless steel. "Flesh is so high-maintenance," he grunted, tightening a bolt with his fingers. "This gives me better torque on the dance floor."

Language here is efficient. They don't say "Hello," they ask, "What is your RPM?" To say "Goodbye," you shout, "Clear the vent!" I tried to order a black coffee, but I accidentally used the gesture for "lubricate," so the barista poured a cup of hot 10W-40 motor oil. It was delicious, actually—very nutty.

Dining here is an industrial-strength affair. I visited a smokehouse located inside a active blast furnace. I ordered the **Steak-of-Steam**, a cut of beef that is tenderized by being beaten with a wrench until it surrenders. It was chewy, but had a smoky, metallic aftertaste. I also tried the **Soup-of-Solenoids**, a broth filled with magnetic springs that try to reconnect in your stomach. It aids digestion and messes with your pacemaker. To drink, I had a *Cuppa-Joe-Volt*, an espresso so charged with electricity that your hair stands on end and you can see into the ultraviolet spectrum for about twenty minutes.

The wildlife is mechanical and predatory. There are **Rat-Roaches**, rodents made of scrap wire and copper that scuttle through the walls, chewing on electrical cables to survive. But the true danger is the **Sabre-Toothed-Saw**, a robotic tiger made of spinning blades. It doesn't roar; it screams a high-pitched whine that cuts glass. I outran one by rusting its joints with a bottle of vinegar I carry for emergencies.

I found a message from the library stamped into the iron manhole cover beneath my feet. It read: *Time is not a river. It is a construction.*

The constant ticking is starting to affect my internal clock; I feel like I’m always late for a meeting I didn't schedule. I need a place where the air is still, the ground is soft, and the light doesn't need to be wound up.

I am heading *West-to-the-End*, drifting on the *Trade-Winds-of-Wool*. I hear there is a canyon where the echoes weave their own tapestries. They call it the **Canyon-of-Threads**, and I intend to get spooled.

Stay wound.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
