**Origin:** The Hinterlands of *Kozel-Blank* (The Unmapped Pale)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of coarse, handmade hemp paper, rough to the touch and smelling faintly of woodsmoke, black pepper, and wet dog. The ink is a deep, sooty charcoal that smudges if you run your thumb over it. The handwriting is jagged and hurried, as if written during a tremor. In the corner, a faded red wax seal bears the image of a three-headed hen.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the warmth of a cast-iron stove, inside a dacha that is slowly sliding down a hill. I have arrived in **Kozel-Blank**, the Unmapped Pale. This is the place where the edge of the Empire crumbled into the void, a land of deep, suffocating woods and villages that refuse to stay put.

You recall I was heading *To the Forest of Gestures*? I prepared myself for applause, but the forest had other plans. I stepped onto a path of pine needles, but the ground felt brittle, like old bone. The trees didn't clap; they shivered. I followed a trail of stinging nettles that led me through a fog bank and deposited me here, in a land that feels like a half-remembered folktale. The sky here is a permanent, bruised twilight, the color of a plum that has been sitting on the shelf too long.

The geography is obstinate and fluid. The borders of **Kozel-Blank** shift every night at midnight. A river might decide to flow uphill, or a forest might migrate five miles east to avoid a conversation. I am currently staying in the **Drifting District of Yabloko**, a collection of wooden houses that rest on the backs of colossal, migrating **Mole-Turtles**. We move slowly across the steppes, creaking and groaning. To the south lies the **Iron Bog**, where the water is so heavy with magnetic minerals that if you drop a hammer, it sinks like a stone.

The locals are the **Babushki-of-the-Bin**, women of indeterminate age who wear headscarves patterned with eyes. They are the custodians of entropy. They speak a dialect called **Rot-Tongue**. It sounds like a mix of Russian and the creaking of floorboards. They don't greet you with "Hello"; they ask, *“Viy stoish?”* (“Are you still standing?”). I tried to buy a loaf of bread, but I accidentally used the formal pronoun when addressing the baker’s sourdough starter (which is considered a junior family member here), causing the dough to rise aggressively and overflow the basket. The baker laughed, slapping her knee, and gave me a roll that tasted of sorrow and caraway seeds.

I am dining on **Borscht-of-Shadows**. It is a beet soup so dark it absorbs the light around it. You eat it by candlelight, or you can't see the spoon. It tastes of earth and iron, and it warms you from the inside out. For the main course, I am having **Blini-with-Bear-Butter**. The pancakes are thick and heavy, fried in the rendered fat of hibernating bears. It puts you right to sleep. For dessert, I am having **Void-Berries**. They are pale blue berries that grow in the shade of the *Leshy* (the forest spirits). When you eat them, you taste the color purple.

The wildlife is… opinionated. The woods are full of **Domovoi**, house spirits that look like hairy, bearded rats. If you leave your boots by the door, they will hide them unless you leave a saucer of milk. The most dangerous creature is the **Kikimora**. She is a hag that lives in the stove; if you burn too much wood, she climbs out and rearranges your furniture while you sleep. I woke up this morning and my chair was on the ceiling.

I found a message from the library carved into the bark of a silver birch tree. The letters were bleeding sap. It read: *Maps are just cages for the terrain.*

The wind is howling, and the Mole-Turtle beneath us is turning toward the mountains. I must secure the samovar before we hit a slope.

I am heading *To the Clockwork Bazaars of Istanbul-Not-Constantinople*. I hear the bazaar sells time in jars, and I am running late.

Stay warm, and keep your pantry closed.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
