**Origin:** The Trans-Laneional Strip of *NowHere-Automotiva* (The Eternal Highway)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a rectangle of peeling, sun-bleached chrome, welded to a backing of rough asphalt. It smells overwhelmingly of gasoline, stale french fries, and hot rubber. If you hold it up to the light, holographic oil slicks rain across the surface. The ink is black treacle; sticky and sweet, it attracts ants.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the booth of a diner that is cruising at a comfortable 75mph, though the wheels haven't touched the ground in decades. I have arrived in **NowHere-Automotiva**, the Eternal Highway. This is the place where the North American spirit of the open road calcified into its own dimension. It is an infinite ribbon of asphalt that exists in a permanent Saturday afternoon, forever looping between the nostalgia of a departure and the anticipation of arrival.

You recall I was heading *To the Shoals of the Anthropocene*? I was sailing on a sea of plastic when the tide receded, revealing a cracked interstate highway that stretched vertically into the sky. I parked my boat and started driving. The road didn't lead to the ocean; it led *away* from the concept of water entirely. Here, the horizon is a wavering heat haze that never resolves.

The geography is neon and concrete. I am currently traveling the **Vias of Velocity**, a ten-lane superhighway where the fastest cars move so quickly they reverse time. To the south lies the **Exit of Forgotten Motels**, a labyrinth of off-ramps leading to places that no longer exist on maps. I passed a billboard today that advertised "Next Exit: Your Childhood." I decided not to take it; the traffic was too heavy.

The locals are the **Gear-Heads**. They are humanoid figures fused seamlessly with their vehicles. Their skin is polished chrome, their eyes are high-beam headlights, and their fingers are tire-treads. They do not walk; they roll. They communicate through their horns and turn signals. A honk can mean "I love you," "Get out of the way," or "The existential dread is consuming me," depending on the pitch. They speak a dialect called **Traffic-Jive**. It is a language of revving engines and screeching brakes. They greeted me with a loud *“Vroom-vroom-skrrrt,”* which roughly translates to *"Nice cupholder, pilgrim."*

I tried to order a coffee, but the waitress (a sentient soda fountain) told me they don't serve liquids here, only momentum. I settled for a bottle of **Octane-Ade**. It tastes like citrus and battery acid, and it makes your hair stand on end.

I am dining on **Bumper-Crop Corn**. It is corn grown in the ditches between lanes; it is naturally salty and smells of exhaust. For the main course, I am having a **Sedan-Burger**. The meat is a synthesized patty grown from the leather seats of a 1998 Ford Taurus. It is surprisingly tender, with a smoky, retrograde flavor. For dessert, I am having **Concrete-Pie**. It is a heavy, gray tart that sits in your stomach like a speed bump. It is an acquired taste.

The wildlife is… vehicular. The **Jackalopes** here have been replaced by **Rear-Deer**. They are deer with glowing red eyes and chrome antlers that function as radio antennas, eternally frozen in a leap across the road. I almost hit one, but it phased right through my grill. There are also **Asphalt-Eaters**, giant, snail-like creatures made of rust and oil. They slide along the shoulder of the road, filling in potholes with their slime, which hardens instantly into fresh pavement. I stopped to let one pass; it blatted its horn at me in thanks.

I found a message from the library painted on the tailgate of a rusted pickup truck abandoned in the fast lane. It read: *You can never go home, because the road moves when you aren't looking.*

The sun is beginning to set, turning the asphalt into a river of gold. The neon signs of the roadside motels are flickering to life, buzzing with high-voltage insomnia. I need to refuel my spirit before the night drive.

I am heading *To the Glass Canyons of New-York-Once*. It is a city that exists only in the reflections of puddles on rainy days. I hope I remembered my umbrella.

Keep moving.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
