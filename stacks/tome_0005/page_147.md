**Origin:** The Oxidized Steppes of *Zarathustra-Zwei* (The Rust Bucket)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of heavy, pitted copper sheeting, green with patina. It smells of rain and battery acid. The text is etched into the metal in a sharp, angular script that glows faintly orange when the wind blows. It is warm to the touch, as if it has been sitting in a furnace.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the yurt of a minor-deity, warming my feet on a brazier fueled by burning memories. I have arrived in **Zarathustra-Zwei**, the nomadic nation that drifts across the **Sea of Rust**. This is the place where the Silk Road fell into the ocean, dissolved in the salt, and was reconstituted as a landscape of scrap metal and corrosion.

You recall I was heading *Into the Root-Network*, hoping to see if trees drink red wine? I dug down, certainly, but I hit a layer of permafrost that tasted like pennies. I broke through into a hollow space and fell for what felt like an age, landing in the belly of a giant, rusted sphere the size of a cathedral. It turned out to be the rolling home of a metal-herder named *Krognar*.

The geography here is jagged and conductive. The ground is not soil, but a drift of broken gears, twisted chassis, and oxidized pipes that stretches to the horizon. The wind howls through the hollows of a million discarded engines, creating a melody that sounds like a choir of tuning forks. I am currently trekking across the **Gear-Dunes**, red hills of ferrous sand that shift magnetically. To the north lies the **Wire-Forest**, a dense thicket of copper cables that wriggle like snakes when the lightning strikes. We are currently following the **Great-Circuit**, a migratory route shaped like a giant infinity symbol that loops endlessly around the magnetic poles.

The locals are the **Ferro-Capra**. They are goat-herders, but their goats are not animals. They are small, mechanical hexapods made of scavenged brass and iron, powered by steam and spite. They *clack* rather than *bleat* and graze on the moss of oxidation that grows on the standing stones of junk. The herders themselves are swathed in thick coats made of woven copper wire. They communicate through **Arc-Syntax**. They don't speak; they snap their fingers, producing blue sparks that jump between their hands. A single sharp *zap* means "Yes," while a sustained, crackling buzz means "The weather is turning." I tried to ask for directions, but I built up too much static charge and accidentally welded a spoon to my nose.

I am staying in *The Ger-Iron*, a portable lodge made of riveted steel plates. The interior is lined with felt made from recycled asbestos. It is surprisingly cozy, though the compass in my pocket is spinning so fast it has drilled a hole in my pants. The hostess, a woman with eyes like polished hematite, served me tea made from boiled radiators. It tastes like iron and rain.

I am dining on **Scrap-Samosas**. They are pastries filled not with potatoes, but with copper filings and magnetic sand. They are crunchy and pull at your fillings as you eat. For the main course, I am having **Cog-Kabob**. It is chunks of mystery meat (I suspect it might be from the *Tapir-Bone-Beetle* I encountered last week; its exoskeleton is quite versatile) skewered on piston rods and grilled over a coal fire. It tastes smoky and industrial. For dessert, I am having **Hard-Drive-Halva**. It is a sweet paste made of ground glass and sugar data. It is delicious, but I keep remembering things that never happened to me.

The wildlife is… conductive. The skies are ruled by the **Cloud-Ray**, a manta ray the size of a football field that glides on thermal updrafts. Its skin is polished aluminum, and it acts as a giant lightning rod. The scavenger of the plains is the **Magnet-Vulture**. It circles overhead, not looking for dead meat, but for broken toasters. If you fall asleep with your watch exposed, they will swoop down and steal it for the springs.

I found a message from the library punched into a tape of telegraph wire blowing in the wind. It read: *Everything breaks, but not everything breaks down.*

The sun is setting, turning the rust-dunes a deep, bloody crimson. It is beautiful, but I can feel my joints stiffening. I need to keep moving or I shall become a permanent part of the landscape.

I am heading *To the Library of Lost Noises*. I hear there is a book there that contains the sound of the earth turning. I intend to read it aloud.

Keep your bearings.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
