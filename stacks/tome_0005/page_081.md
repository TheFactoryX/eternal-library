**Origin:** The Fractal Sewers of *Kamin-Saakh* (The City of Ten Thousand Tongues)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a stiff, square piece of dried *paneer* cheese, stained with turmeric and saffron. It smells faintly of cardamom and wet concrete. The text is burned into the surface as if by a branding iron. If you lick the corner, it tastes like nostalgia.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the railing of a floating bamboo platform, suspended over a river of glowing blue curry that flows through the roots of a banyan tree large enough to anchor a continent. I have arrived in **Kamin-Saakh**, the City of Ten Thousand Tongues. This is the South Asia that exists in the pause between a spicy bite and the relief of a drink—a place where flavor is geography and architecture is built entirely out of appetite.

You recall I was heading *Down* through the **Eye-of-the-Needle** toward the **Sub-Basement-of-Lost-Toys**? I descended for hours, watching childhood traumas float past me like plastic soldiers, but I was waylaid by a sudden craving. I made a detour at the layer of *Forgotten-New-Year’s-Resolutions* and fell through a trapdoor made of a roti that was too thin to hold my weight. I plummeted into the bubbling, spice-scented heart of this subterranean metropolis.

The geography here is Digestive. The ground is not rock, but layers of compressed, fossilized breadcrumbs. The sky is a low ceiling of stalactites made of crystallized sugar, dripping a syrup that forms the rivers below. The architecture is dominated by **Spice-Minarets**, towers of crushed cumin and cinnamon that rise hundreds of feet into the humid air. The wind here doesn't blow; it belches.

The locals are the **Masala-Ghosts**, spirits of meals prepared with love but never eaten. They are vibrant, smiling figures composed entirely of steam and aroma. They wear clothes stitched together from mango leaves and bind their documents with red chili threads. They are obsessed with hospitality, even though they have no physical stomachs. Their language, *Swad-Ka-Bhasha*, is spoken not with words but with smells. A raised eyebrow smells like skepticism (which smells faintly of burnt toast); a declaration of love smells like warm ghee and jaggery.

I accidentally insulted a street vendor by smelling like "wet wool" (confusion), and he immediately tried to marinate me in a vat of tamarind water to improve my flavor profile.

I am currently recovering on the deck of a ferry made of a giant, hollowed-out **Pea-Pod**, drifting down the **Ganges-of-Gravy**. I am eating a bowl of **Time-Tickling-Kichdi**, a rice dish that ages backwards as you eat it. The first bite tastes like the creamy comfort of old age; the last bite tastes like the sharp, metallic zing of infancy. It is disorienting to finish a meal and suddenly forget how to use a spoon. To drink, I have a glass of *Lassi-of-Lucid-Dreaming*, a yogurt blend that makes your thoughts visible as colorful vapor. I burped a small cloud that looked like my grandmother.

The wildlife is hazardous to your breath. There are **Scissor-Billed-Cranes** that snip through the air, cutting holes in reality if you aren't careful. But the true menace is the **Bread-Bird**, a species of parrot that mimics the cry of a hungry child. If you feed it, it multiplies until you are buried under an avalanche of naan. I had to fight off a flock with a stick of kebab yesterday.

I found a message from the library written on the back of a floating betel leaf. It read: *Hunger is the only thing that makes the emptiness feel full.*

The heat here is oppressive, a dry, roasting warmth that makes your skin feel like baked clay. My fingers are stained yellow from the spices in the air, and I can feel a craving building in the pit of my stomach that has nothing to do with food.

I am heading *East*, following the scent of burning incense toward the **Monastery-of-Unanswered-Prayers**. I hear the monks there can teach you how to hold your breath for a lifetime.

Bon appétit.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
