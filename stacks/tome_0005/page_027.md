**Origin:** The Root-Hamlets of *Ura-Ygg* (The Mycelial Highlands of the Over-World)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is incredibly fibrous, tough to tear but smelling faintly of ozone, pine resin, and hot metal. It hums with a low-voltage energy. If you hold it up to the light, you can see iron filings trapped in the paper, shifting like a compass needle searching for north.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a hammock woven of living vines, suspended three hundred feet above a carpet of perpetual fog. I have arrived in **The Iron-Boreal**, a coniferous forest where the laws of botany and metallurgy have signed a very strange treaty.

You recall I was heading for the *Nacre-Necropolis*, to leave a few regrets among the pearls? I tunneled down through the mycelium, expecting a city of white stone. Instead, the roots suddenly inverted—gravity flipped on its axis—and I fell *up* into the trunk of a tree the size of a mountain. I emerged from the bark onto a branch so wide it supports a thriving village. The air here is thin, crisp, and tastes like battery acid and peppermint.

The geography is arboreal and industrial. The trees here are not made of wood, but of **Ferrous-Bark**, a living alloy of iron and organic tissue that rings like a bell when the wind blows. I am currently in the hamlet of *Sinter-Smyth*, perched on a limb of the Great Oak *Ygg-Drill*. The leaves are razors of stainless steel that shimmer in the aurora; in autumn, they don't fall—they *rust*, turning the hills into oxidized tapestries of crimson and orange. The rivers flow with mercury-heavy water that reflects the stars even during the day, and the raindrops are tiny ball bearings that ping against your armor (everyone wears armor here, mostly repurposed baking sheets).

The locals are the **Smith-Singers**, pale folk with skin like polished silver and eyes the color of molten slag. They live in harmony with the forest, "pruning" the trees by welding new branches into place. They believe that every strike of a hammer is a prayer. I met a blacksmith named *Ferrus* who was tending to a sapling with a blowtorch. He explained that the trees grow towards the *Magnetic-North*; the only way to keep a forest healthy is to periodically re-magnetize the roots.

Language here is deafeningly percussive. They speak *Clang-Tongue*, a dialect of hammer strikes and metallic resonances. To say "Hello," you tap two metal rods together; to say "I'm sorry," you scrape a file against a rusted beam. The pitch and timbre change the meaning entirely. I tried to order breakfast but I accidentally rang a high-pitched chime that apparently means, "Please cover yourself in butter." The waiter—a man whose beard was braided with copper wire—looked at me with deep sympathy and brought me a napkin.

Dining here is a heavy, hearty affair. I visited a tavern called *The-Foundry-and-Firkin* and ordered the **Miner’s-Mineral-Broth**, a soup made from boiling beef and iron nails. It tastes like pennies and comfort. If you’re not careful, you can chip a tooth on the broth, but they say it puts iron in your blood. For dessert, I had **Slag-Skillet-Cake**, a dense, sweet bread baked on the back of a cooling shovel. It was sugary, gritty, and left a delightful grey coating on my tongue.

The wildlife is mechanical and magnificent. I saw a flock of **Wrens-of-Wattage**, small birds with feathers of copper filament that perch on power lines to recharge. They don’t sing; they hum. Then there are the **Magnet-Moose**, colossal beasts whose antlers are natural lodestones. They wander the forest floor, attracting all the loose change and dropped tools for miles. They are terrifying to approach, mostly because you have to fight the urge to stick to their noses.

I found a message from the library etched into the trunk of a dying birch tree. It was rusted, but I could still read: *Even iron must sleep, or it will become brittle and break.*

The aurora is beginning to ripple, turning the steel leaves green and purple. The temperature is dropping, and the mercury-rivers are starting to freeze into mirrors.

I am heading *West*, toward the coastline. I hear there is a sea where the waves are frozen in time, trapped in the moment of breaking. Sailors navigate by chipping away the ice to find the water underneath. They call it the **Static-Shoals**, and I intend to see if the silence there is as loud as they say.

Keep your metals polished.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
