**Origin:** The Crystalline Tundra of *Iso-Prisma* (The Frozen Fraction)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a sheet of transparent ice, incredibly cold and burned at the edges. It is wrapped in a layer of rough, oily reindeer hide to prevent it from melting in the handler's grip. The ink is a dark, crimson liquid—iron-based—that smells of rust and fresh snow. If the reader holds the card up to a light source, the text casts two shadows instead of one.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the interior of a hollowed-out snowdrift that smells of ozone and ancient wool. I have arrived in **Iso-Prisma**, the Frozen Fraction. This is the place where geometry went to die, or perhaps, to achieve immortality.

You recall I was heading *To the Mechanism of Seasons*? I jumped onto the gear cog, but rather than grinding me into autumn, it acted as a centrifuge. I was flung off the edge of the calendar and landed in a snowbank so white it made my eyes ache. When I stood up, I realized I wasn't standing on the ground, but on the surface of a mirror that reflected a sky I hadn't looked into yet. The cold here does not bite; it interrogates.

The geography is Euclidean and unforgiving. The landscape is composed of **Flats**—expanses of hexagonal ice sheets that slot together with impossible precision. There are no curved lines here; the hills are sharp, jagged pyramids of packed snow, and the valleys are perfectly angular troughs. I am currently trekking across the **Plains of Negative Kelvin**, a region where the cold is so intense it creates a vacuum of sound. To the north lies the **Edge-Range**, a mountain range that looks like a torn sheet of graph paper, hovering in the air without a base.

The locals are the **Vett-Ciphers**. They appear as tall, slender figures bundled in furs that are not white, but "clear"—woven from spun glass and spider silk. They wear masks of polished chrome that reflect everything but their own eyes. They communicate through **Prismatic-Inflection**. They don't speak words; they tilt their heads at specific angles to catch the weak arctic sun, flashing beams of concentrated data into each other's eyes. A tilt of 45 degrees means " Hospitality," while a sharp 90-degree turn indicates "Go away." I attempted to ask for directions to the nearest lodge, but I tilted my head 30 degrees, which apparently means, "I enjoy chewing on gravel." They laughed, a sound like cracking ice, and pointed me toward a glacier.

I am staying in *The Zero-Star Hotel*, a structure carved entirely from a single block of glacial blue ice. The rooms are refractions of the outside world; if I look out the window, I see myself sleeping from three different angles. The bed is a slab of slate covered in reindeer pelts that are surprisingly warm. The concierge is a robotic automaton made of brass and clockwork, fueled by whale oil. He is polite but constantly recalibrating his limbs.

I am dining on **Gravlax of Light**. It is salmon cured not in salt, but in pure darkness, causing the flesh to become translucent. It tastes like the memory of a river. For the main course, I am having **Stew of the Yeti-Crab**. These are crustaceans that thrive in the freezing vents, their claws covered in fur. The meat is sweet, tender, and spicy, served with **Rocks**—literally heated stones that you suck on for minerals. For dessert, I am having **Ice-Nine-Gelato**. It is a sorbet so cold it freezes your taste buds, shattering them, so that every subsequent bite tastes like whatever you imagine it should. It is a dangerous treat, but the brain-freeze is divine.

The wildlife is… angular. I saw a **Poly-Penguin** yesterday. It is a bird that doesn't waddle; it rolls. It tucks itself into a perfect sphere and navigates the ice by momentum, bouncing off the hexagonal cliffs. The true masters of this domain are the **Aurora-Wolves**. They are packs of predators that are barely corporeal; they are composed of the shifting lights of the northern lights. They hunt by inducing vertigo; if you stare at them too long, you lose your balance and fall, allowing the pack to converge.

I found a message from the library scrawled on the wall of a snow cave, written in frost that steamed away as I read it. It read: *Symmetry is the only truth that survives the thaw.*

The wind is picking up, and it's blowing in geometric patterns. I must seal the flap of my hide-tent with heavy rocks.

I am heading *To the Stratospheric Gardens*. I hear they are cultivated by cloud-shepherds who prune the storms into topiaries.

Keep your angles sharp.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
