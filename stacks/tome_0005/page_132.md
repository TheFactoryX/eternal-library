**Origin:** The Drift-Grove of *Sirena-Altura* (The High Reefs)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a single, large, emerald-green algae frond, pressed dry and stiff like paper. It is salty to the touch and smells faintly of coconut, burnt sugar, and ozone. If you hold it up to the light, the veins of the plant form a map of constellations that don't exist in our sky. The text is written in a deep, purple ink that seems to float just above the surface of the frond. When you tilt the card, a tiny, stylized paper figure in the corner appears to be dancing a limbo.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the porch of a hut that exists solely because a large piece of driftwood refused to sink. The air here is thick, humid, and tastes like spices and laughter. I have arrived in **Sirena-Altura**, the High Reefs. This is the place where the ocean decided it didn't want to be at the bottom anymore and grew legs to walk among the clouds.

You recall I was heading *Into the Reflection*, stepping through the back of a mirror in the Hotel of Infinite Reflections to find a place where shadows speak? Well, the mirror was a one-way portal, but I stepped through a little too quickly and shattered the glass on the way out. The "world on the other side" wasn't a shadow realm, but the *inside* of a tropical storm. I fell out of a cumulonimbus cloud and landed in the canopy of a massive, floating mangrove forest. These trees have uprooted themselves entirely. They drift through the atmosphere, navigating by wind and light, their root-balls dangling miles above the actual surface of the Caribbean Sea far below.

The geography is buoyant and chaotic. The ground moves. You step from one floating island to another via rope bridges made of braided kelp that sway with the currents. The "sun" here is strange; because we are so high up, the light is fractured and perpetual. We have two sunsets: one when the actual sun dips below the horizon of the earth far beneath us, and a second, "False Sunset," when the clouds below catch the reflected light and burn a brilliant orange. It is easy to lose track of time when the ground is drifting at twenty knots an hour.

The locals are the **Rastas-Volantes** (The Flying Rastas). They are nimble climbers with skin the color of deep mahogany and hair that reacts to static electricity, floating out from their heads like jellyfish tentacles. They wear shorts made of woven palm fronds and nothing else. They communicate through **Calypso-Clicks**, a rhythmic language involving tongue clucks and drumming on their own rib cages. A slow, hollow thump means "Peace," while a rapid, sharp staccato means "Hurricane coming." I tried to ask for directions to the market, but I inadvertently proposed a marriage to the village elder’s daughter. She declined, but gave me a mango as a consolation prize.

I am currently resting in *The-Hammock-Hotel*, which is exactly what it sounds like. There are no rooms, just rows of hammocks suspended between the drifting branches of the Great Banyan. The currency here is **Rhythm**. To pay for my night’s stay, I had to provide a beat on the drums for the evening dance. I am ashamed to say my rhythm was a bit stiff, so I was given a hammock closer to the edge, where the wind is colder.

I am drinking a bottle of **Gravity-Gone-Grog**. It is a fermented spirit distilled from the berries of the floating bushes. It is delicious—sweet, fiery, and smoky—but the side effect is that for an hour after drinking it, you forget which way is "down." I spilled a drop, and the liquid floated upward, forming a perfect sphere that drifted away like a tiny planet. For dinner, I am having **Jerk-Smoke-Meat**. The meat isn't smoked; the meat *is* smoke. The chefs capture the spirits of animals and season them with scotch bonnets and allspice, then grill the vapor until it solidifies into a savory, ghostly slab that melts in your mouth. It tastes like a memory of a barbecue you haven't attended yet.

The wildlife is aerodynamic. I see **Manta-Rays** that glide through the air instead of water, filtering plankton from the clouds. Their skin is iridescent, shifting from pink to gold as they catch the light. The most troublesome creature is the **Mock-Mango**. It looks exactly like a delicious fruit, but when you bite into it, it screams like a parrot and flies away. I learned this the hard way and now have a bruise on my forehead from the branch it hit me with.

I found a message from the library written in the condensation on the side of my coconut cup. It was ephemeral and sweet. It read: *To stay afloat, you must be willing to let your roots dangle.*

The trade winds are shifting, and the Great Banyan is beginning to turn its leaves toward the source of the morning breeze.

I am heading *Vertical*, taking the **Up-Draft-Elevator**. It is a thermal column that shoots straight up into the stratosphere. I have heard rumors of a city made of discarded kites and lost balloons located at the edge of space.

Hold on tight.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
