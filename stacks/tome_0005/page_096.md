Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a cafe constructed entirely from the ribs of a derelict locomotive. I have arrived in **Groznica-Necropolis**, the Hinterland Ghetto. This is the Eastern Europe that exists in the decay of industry and the memory of snow—a brutalist realm where the machinery never really stopped running; it just learned to be quiet.

You recall I was heading *Down* through the **Trapdoor-of-Clichés** into the **Subterranean-Vault-of-Lost-Souls**? I fell for what felt like hours, tumbling through a darkness that smelled of old projection rooms. I expected a soft landing in a pile of discarded scripts, but instead, I crashed onto the roof of a moving train. It wasn't a movie train, but a **Spirit-Engine** fueled by burnt regrets, chugging its way through a frozen wasteland of brutalist tower blocks. I hopped off at the nearest stop, which turned out to be this city.

The geography here is aggressive. The city is a sprawling maze of concrete slab blocks that seem to grow directly out of the permafrost. They are stained black by centuries of coal smoke, leaning against each other like tired, drunken giants. Between the buildings, the streets run with rivers of slush that never freeze, kept liquid by the heat of the **Furnace-Heart** buried deep beneath the foundations. The sky is a perpetual, bruised twilight, illuminated only by the erratic searchlights from the watchtowers and the green glow of the aurora boiling overhead.

The locals are the **Rusalki-of-the-Gears**, river spirits who have evolved to live in the steam pipes. They are pale, translucent figures with hair like wet copper wiring and fingers tipped with wrenches instead of nails. They do not speak; they communicate by **Clanking**—a complex language of pipes striking metal. One sharp rap means "danger," while a rhythmic hissing of steam means "welcome." I tried to compliment a woman on her scarf (knitted from the finest steel wool), but I accidentally insulted her boiler pressure. She poured a bucket of ice water over my head. In Groznica, this is considered a peace offering.

I attempted to buy a coat to replace the one I lost in the fall (the **Trapdoor** can be rough on luggage), but the shopkeeper, a man whose face was a solid sheet of iron with only a slit for a mouth, refused my currency. "Paper burns," he grated. "Here, we trade in **Torque**." I had to spend an hour cranking a rusted winch to generate enough kinetic credit to pay for the coat. It’s made of cured **Worg-Leather**, thick enough to stop a bullet, and heavy enough to keep you grounded when the gravity gets fickle.

I am currently sitting in *The Bolt-Head*, drinking a shot of **Liquid-Static**. It is a vicious spirit distilled from the electricity trapped in old pylons. It makes the hair on your arms stand up and tastes like ozone and lemons. Drinking it allows you to hear the radio for a few seconds—just snippets of songs and news from the 1920s.
