**Origin:** The Bamboo Lattice of *Kizoku-Kusari* (The Pagoda of Drifting Roots)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a single, dried lotus leaf, brittle and veins-visible. It smells of wet moss, burning incense, and ozone. The ink is shiny and wet, looking suspiciously like black liquid pearls.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while hanging upside down from the railing of a tea-house that grows like a fruit from the branch of a mile-high bonsai tree. I have arrived in **Kizoku-Kusari**, the Upside-Down Dynasty, located in the upper atmosphere where the clouds are thick enough to farm.

You recall I was heading *Straight-Up*, seeking the *Orchard of Hard Vacuum* to find diamonds in the thermosphere? I climbed the *Stairway-of-Stars* until my legs burned, expecting the cold silence of space. Instead, the air grew denser, sweeter. I breached the cloud layer and found that the sky holds islands of floating earth, anchored to the moon by chains of braided red silk. I took a wrong turn on a cloud bridge and ended up here, in a society that exists entirely within the根系 of giant, inverted bamboo that grows *down* from the moon.

The geography is arboreal and inverted. **Kizoku-Kusari** is built into the interlocking root systems of celestial bamboo. Gravity here is subjective; it pulls toward the largest surface area, so I often find myself walking on ceilings while the rain falls *upward* into the soil above my head. The architecture is impossible; paper walls are stronger than steel, and the sliding doors (*shoji*) open into rooms in different centuries. I saw a garden where the raked gravel patterns rearrange themselves to spell out the secrets of the traveler looking at them, though they blur into nonsense if you try to write them down.

The locals are the **Root-Walkers**, pale, silent figures who wear wooden *geta* sandals made of ironwood to weigh them down. If they are too light, a stray updraft could blow them into the void. They wear wide-brimmed hats that drip a constant, cooling shadow onto their shoulders. I met a potter named *Hoshiko* who was throwing clay on a wheel spun by captive fireflies. "We do not mold the clay," she told me, her voice sounding like wind chimes. "We simply convince it to hold a shape for a little while."

Language here is subtle and deeply layered. They speak *Kotodama-Seijaku*, the Language of Silent Spirits. Words have weight, and speaking too loudly will cause you to sink through the floorboards. To say "Hello," you bow and exhale a puff of steam shaped like a plum blossom. To say "Goodbye," you must whisper a word that has no meaning, allowing the listener to fill in the emotion themselves. I tried to ask for directions to the lavatory, but I used the wrong intonation, and instead accidentally complimented the ceiling tiles on their "enduring patience." A nearby vase blushed and turned a deep shade of crimson in embarrassment.

Dining here is a minimalists' dream and a glutton's challenge. I visited an inn where the tables are made of solidified ice that never melts. I ordered the **Sashimi-of-Silence**, slices of raw fish so fresh they are still looking for the ocean. As you eat them, you can hear the ocean waves in your ears. I also tried the **Mochi-of-Moments**, rice cakes filled with a red bean paste that tastes like your best childhood memory. Be careful not to eat more than three, or you will become nostalgic for a life you haven't lived yet. To drink, I had a **Sake-of-Spirit-Vapor*, distilled from the breath of a sleeping mountain god. It is warm, clear, and makes you feel slightly translucent for an hour.

The wildlife is ancient and geometrical. There are **Koi-Nobori**, actual carp flying through the air like banners, swimming through the wind as if it were water. I saw a **Dragon-of-Data**, a long, serpentine creature made of glowing symbols and calligraphy that feeds on forgotten prayers. It wrapped itself around a nearby tower to digest a particularly juicy haiku.

I found a message from the library written in the condensation on the inside of my teacup. It read: *Stillness is not the absence of motion, but the presence of balance.*

The view is breathtaking, but the blood is rushing to my head, and the silence here is so heavy it requires actual effort to move my tongue. I need a place where the ground stays firmly under my feet and the noise is chaotic and human.

I am heading *Down-to-Earth*, descending the *Rope-Ladder-of-Rain*. I hear there is a bustling metropolis in a fold of time where it is always rush hour and neon lights turn rain into solid glass. They call it the **Neon-Keystone**, and I intend to get lost in the crowd.

Watch your step on the way down.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
