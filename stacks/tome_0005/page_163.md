**Origin:** The Cetacean Geothermal Isles of *Okeanus-Inhalare* (The Breathing Archipelago)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is composed of thick, vascular coral-polyp paper that is warm and faintly pulsates against the palm. It smells strongly of sulfur, coconut milk, and the deep ocean. If you hold it to your ear, instead of a seashell sound, you hear a slow, rhythmic rushing noise, like a giant sleeping three rooms away. The ink is a sticky, gold-leaf sap that tastes of pineapple and ash.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the hammock strung between two "trees" that are actually the dorsal spines of a sleeping island-titan. I have arrived in **Okeanus-Inhalare**, the Breathing Archipelago. This is not land in the geological sense; this is a colony of leviathans so ancient and immense that they have calcified into ecosystems. They drift in a warm, equatorial current where the water is the color of liquid sapphire and thick with nutrients.

You recall I was heading *To the Loom of the Fates*? I made the mistake of looking at the threads of my destiny, and the pattern was so tangled I got motion sickness. I fell off the edge of the tapestry and plummeted into the warm sea below. I washed up here, on the back of **The Great Atoll-Whale**.

The geography is soft and precarious. The ground is not rock, but **Pelt-Stone**, a living carpet of moss, fur, and hardened barnacles that feels like a shag rug over a warm radiator. The "mountains" here are actually the blowholes of the beast. I am currently near the **Spout of the Morning Mist**, a geyser that erupts with a low, bass-frequency thrum every hour, showering the local villages in a fine, nutritious mist that smells of plankton and rain. To the west lies the **Lagoon of Tears**, a pool of water collected in the creature's eye socket, which is so salty it keeps you afloat without effort.

The locals are the **Island-Dwellers**. They are short, stout people with skin like tanned leather and lungs the size of bellows. They wear clothing made from **Molt-Feathers**, brightly colored plumage that shakes off whenever the island stretches. They do not walk; they *waddle* to maintain balance on the shifting flesh of their home. They speak a language called **Deep-Thrum**, a dialect of hums and low-frequency clicks that you feel in your chest more than you hear with your ears. They greeted me with a resonant *“Hrrrm-bloop,”* which roughly translates to *"Have you adjusted your ballast?"*

They have a custom called **The Anchoring**. Before sleeping, they must tie themselves to the "ground" with heavy vines. If they don't, the island might roll over in its sleep, and they will slide off into the abyss. I learned this the hard way when I took a nap and woke up sliding down the flank of a thirty-mile-long whale at terminal velocity.

I am dining on **Parasite-Palm Hearts**. They are crisp, white vegetables harvested from the burrowing crustaceans that live in the island’s hide. They taste like a cross between artichoke hearts and lobster. For the main course, I am having **Magma-Skewers**. The locals harpoon small, volcanic fish that swim in the thermal currents surrounding the islands and sear them instantly on the hot vents of the beast’s back. It is cooked to perfection by the mere act of catching it. For dessert, I am having **Sponge-Cake**. It is not a cake, but a literal slice of the sea sponge that grows in the armpits of the island, soaked in sweetened coconut water. It is dry, yet delicious.

The wildlife is… internal. The most common creature is the **Tick-Bird**, which is the size of a dog and serves as the island's personal groomer. They have beaks like pneumatic drills. I adopted one named *Peck*; he sits on my shoulder and screams at passing clouds. There are also **REM-Fish**, colorful swarms that only appear when the island is dreaming, bioluminescing in patterns that predict the weather for the next decade.

I found a message from the library written in the sand of the lagoon, but the waves erased it before I could finish reading. All I caught was: *The shore is a state of mind, but the tide is a law of physics.*

The sun is setting, and the horizon is bobbing. The beast beneath me is taking a deep breath, and the pressure is dropping. I need to secure my hammock; it feels like a rough night is coming.

I am heading *To the Clockwork Spires of Tikal-Mech*. I hear the Mayan priests there have finally figured out how to automate the apocalypse, and they are testing the gears tomorrow at dawn. I hope I brought my earplugs.

Check your depth gauge.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
