**Origin:** The Rhizomatic Depths of *Xylem-Prime* (The Inverted Forest)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a sheet of translucent, living birch bark, veined with green chlorophyll that pulses slowly. It smells of petrichor, wet earth, and ozone. If you hold it up to the light, you can see tiny microscopic insects farming fungus inside the fibers.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a hammock woven of living vine, suspended three hundred feet above a ceiling of dirt. I have arrived in **Xylem-Prime**, the Root Cathedral, a place located in the staggeringly large negative space beneath the crust of *Umbra-Zambezi*. It is an upside-down world where the ground is the sky and gravity is merely a suggestion.

You recall I was digging into the *Tunnel-of-Topsoil*, aiming for the **Root-Cathedral**? I dug straight down through the hardpan of the savanna, expecting darkness. Instead, I broke through into blinding, amber twilight. The physics here are inverted; I fell "up" into a massive cavern held together by the interlocking roots of trees that touch the clouds in the world above. It is a biological architecture that puts cathedrals to shame. The pillars of this sanctuary are the taproots of ancient oaks and baobabs, thick as skyscrapers, glowing with a soft, inner bioluminescence.

The geography is damp, dark, and breathtakingly vertical. **Xylem-Prime** is not a cave, but a hanging garden. The "floor" is a mosaic of interconnected root systems, forming bridges, platforms, and whirlpools of twisted wood. There is no solid ground beneath you—if you slip, you fall up into the infinite crush of the soil. This place is ruled by the *Mycelial-Queendom*, a hive mind of fungi that connects every thought and every tree in the forest.

The locals are the **Cambium-Guardians**, tall, lithe figures who look like they were carved from olive wood and draped in moss. They do not walk; they *flow*. They move by gripping the texture of the bark, sliding up and down the massive roots like spiders. They communicate through *Sap-Song*, a language made by clicking their fingernails against the hollow wood. To say "Hello," they tap a rhythm that sounds like rain on a tin roof. To say "Danger," they make the sound of a splitting branch.

I met a local guide named *Lignum* who offered me a drink. He tapped a spigot into a neighboring pillar and filled a cup with *Heartwood-Bourbon*. It is a thick, amber fluid that tastes like sunlight, nostalgia, and old cedar. "It is the blood of the tree," Lignum told me. "It remembers everything the leaves have seen." Drinking it made me feel rooted, immovable, and incredibly calm.

Dining here is a unique challenge. I visited a bistro carved into the hollow of a fossilized sequoia. I ordered the **Polyp-Portobello**, a mushroom steak the size of a car tire, grilled over a fire of burning pinecones. It was smoky, meaty, and tasted of pure umami. For a side, I had **Root-Candy**, crystallized sugar extracted from the phloem. It crackled in my mouth with the stored energy of a thousand summers.

The wildlife is arboreal and oblivious to gravity. There are **Sloth-Moths**, creatures the size of blankets that move so slowly they look like patches of lichen. They wrap around you for warmth, weighing nothing at all. I also saw the **Beetle-Bears**, armadillo-like insects with iridescent shells that scurry along the ceiling of dirt, chewing on the clay and leaving intricate geometric patterns in their wake. The most dangerous creature is the **Timber-Wolf**, which is not an animal, but a sentient knot in the wood that bites if you lean against it.

I found a message from the library scrawled in the lichen growing on my table. It read: *To grow is to reach down before you reach up.*

The air here is rich with oxygen and spores, and I find myself getting sleepy. My movements are slowing down; my skin feels rougher. I think I am beginning to photosynthesize. It is a peaceful feeling, but I cannot stay. I fear if I sleep too long, I will put down roots and become a pillar of this cathedral forever.

I am heading *Transverse*, burrowing through the *Wormhole-Wall* to the **Sector-of-Sands**. I hear there is a desert where the dunes sing in four-part harmony.

Don't forget your roots.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
