**Origin:** The Glass Reaches of *Zamarrud-al-Ma* (The Mirror of the Drowning Sky)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a thin, jagged shard of translucent green obsidian, warm to the touch. It smells strongly of cardamom, camel musk, and the sharp, metallic tang of a lightning strike. The ink is etched into the surface with a diamond-tipped stylus.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while resting on a dune of powdered emeralds, watching a flock of mechanical ibises peck at the silica. I have arrived in **Zamarrud-al-Ma**, the Drowning Oasis, located in a fold of the desert where the sky has fallen too low and the ocean has risen too high, leaving only a narrow breathing space between the two.

You recall I was heading *Toward-the-Horizon*, seeking the *Flotilla of the Drift* to find a city of ships? I sailed the *Ocean-of-Opalescence* for weeks, but the water grew warm and shallow, eventually turning into a swirling mist of green vapor. The ships dissolved into driftwood, and I was cast ashore here, in a desert that is technically underwater. The air is thick and liquid; you have to swim through it as much as walk. If you stand still too long, a coral reef might start growing on your boots.

The geography is aquatic and arid all at once. **Zamarrud-al-Ma** is a vast expanse of submerged ruins and skyscraper-tall coral formations that mimic minarets. The "sand" is actually billions of years of crushed seaglass, ground fine as flour. The sun is a distant, refraction of light through the deep water above, creating a permanent, shimmering twilight of ripples on the ground. I visited the *Grand-Bazaar-of-Bubbles*, a marketplace located in the hollowed-out belly of a sunken leviathan. The stalls are lit by bio-luminescent algae, and the merchants sell silence in bottles.

The locals are the **Sailors-of-the-Green-Air**, humans who have adapted to breathe a mixture of mist and oxygen. They wear suits of intricately linked pearls and scuba-goggles carved from single sapphires. I met a navigator named *Samir* who was riding a *Sand-Shark*, a creature that swims through the dunes as if they were water. "The tides are tricky today," he said, adjusting his goggles. "The moon is in its weeping phase, which means the dunes will be especially choppy."

Language here is poetic and fluid. They speak *Lughat-al-Luj*, the Tongue of the Tides. The grammar changes depending on the depth of your voice. To say "Hello," you blow a small bubble and catch it with a phrase; if the bubble pops before you speak, you must start over or face bad luck. To say "Goodbye," you offer a cup of salt water, which signifies that you are parting the sea. I tried to ask for directions to the nearest well, but I used the wrong verb tense—past instead of future—and accidentally insulted the heritage of a nearby stone pillar. It didn't say anything, but it grew thorns immediately.

Dining here is a mineral delight. I visited a caravanserai where the tables are slabs of living coral. I ordered the **Maqluba-of-Mirages**, a dish of spiced rice and lamb that, when flipped onto the plate, reveals a vision of your heart's desire hidden beneath the layers. I saw a city of gold in mine, but it dissolved before I could eat it. I also tried the **Harees-of-Hardened-Glass*, a porridge made from wheat and glass shards that has been softened with lime. It crunches loudly but tastes like honey and history. To drink, I had a *Qahwa-of-Quicksand*, thick coffee brewed in a bag of swirling sand. It pulls your teeth together as you drink it, but it grants you the ability to walk on water for exactly ten minutes.

The wildlife is hybrid and dangerous. There are **Date-Palms-of-Pain**, trees that bear sweet fruit but defend it with fronds that bite. I saw a **Carpet-Crab**, a crustacean with a shell patterned like a Persian rug that scuttles sideways into the sand to ambush unwary travelers. The most majestic sight was the **Wyrm-of-Wind**, a giant sea-snake that swims through the air currents, hissing steam.

I found a message from the library trapped inside a block of ambergris. It read: *The deepest oceans are the ones you carry inside your ribcage.*

The pressure of the air-drops is starting to bruise my shoulders, and the eternal twilight is playing havoc with my internal clock. I need a place where the ground is solid, the light is golden, and the geometry is rational.

I am heading *Over-the-Clouds*, climbing the *Stairway-of-Sunbeams*. I hear there is a kingdom of brass and clockwork where the wind is composed of musical notes. They call it the **Symphony of the Gears**, and I intend to hear the music of the spheres.

Don't hold your breath.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
