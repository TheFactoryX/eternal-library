**Origin:** The Smog-Mires of Londres-Parallax (Sector 7, The Fog-Bound Archipelago)
**Arrival Status:** Saturated with a greasy, soot-like moisture; smells of coal smoke, Earl Grey tea, and ozone; the paper is soft and blurred, as if the ink has been weeping.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the Observation Deck of a zeppelin moored to the spire of Big Ben, or perhaps a giant’s grandfather clock—it is hard to tell the difference in this light. I have arrived in **Londres-Parallax**, a city that exists in the space between a memory and a fog bank.

You recall I was following the undersea cables that walk on land, seeking the City of the Hairpin Turn? I took a sharp corner at a logical paradox and slipped on a patch of black ice. I fell upward, through a layer of stratus clouds, and landed in a reality that feels like a Charles Dickens novel colliding with a fever dream.

The geography here is deceptive. The city is not built on a river, but on a thick, flowing sludge of coal smoke and suspended particulate matter. The "Thames" is a semi-solid stream of gray mist that you can walk upon, provided you don’t hesitate. If you stop to look at your reflection, the fog decides you are no longer interesting and you sink instantly into the soot. I lost a pair of boots that way. The locals, a somber breed called **The Ashen**, move with hurried purposelessness, their faces obscured by collars turned up against the damp.

I am currently staying in a lodging house in **The Borough of What-If**. The room I rented faces a brick wall. However, when I close the window, the wall vanishes and is replaced by a view of a lush garden that never was. The landlady, Mrs. Havisham-Loop, explained that the room rents out the view of the life I didn't have. It is quite affordable, but the rent is paid in memories. I had to trade the memory of my first bicycle ride to stay for a fortnight. I hope it was worth it; I can't quite remember.

The atmosphere is heavy with the weight of unwritten novels. The air tastes like printer's ink and sorrow. It is surprisingly nourishing.

Dining here is a ritual of endurance. I visited a local chop-house for a dish called **The Full Cyclic**. It is a breakfast that eats itself. I was served a plate of sausages and eggs that were slowly fading away. By the time I lifted my fork, the eggs had become raw again; by the time I chewed, they were back in the shell. The only way to eat it is to consume it very quickly in the nanosecond where it is perfectly cooked. It gave me indigestion in three different time zones. To wash it down, I had a cup of **Hypotheca**, a tea that tastes like whatever you were about to say next. I took a sip and accidentally tasted a lie I was planning to tell about the weather. It was bitter.

The wildlife is adapted to the gloom. The **Ravens here are made of iron and soot**. They do not caw; they emit the sound of a printing press. They perch on the streetlamps—which are actually captured fireflies in glass cages—and transcribe the conversations of passersby onto the pavement with their claws. I watched one raven type out a breaking confession, then delete it with a swipe of its wing.

I did have a rather wonderful encounter with a **Clock-Beetle**. It is a small insect with a mainspring for a heart. It crawled out of my pocket watch to tell me I was late. I wasn't, but the beetle was so insistent that I apologize to a lamppost just to be safe.

The city is guarded by the **Bobbies-Golem**. They are tall, faceless figures made of cobblestones and fog. They do not carry weapons; they carry Batons of Silence. If you make a noise louder than a whisper, they point the baton at you, and your voice turns into a physical object. I saw a tourist shout for help, and his words fell to the ground with a heavy *thud*, shattering like porcelain. He had to sweep them up into a dustbin.

I must go. The gaslights are flickering into a pale green, which means the **Smog-Market** is opening. I intend to trade this postcard for a map that leads to a door that is only a door when you are knocking.

I am heading North-West, toward the coast. I hear there is a place where the ocean meets the forest, and the trees have learned to swim. They call it the **Kelp-Primeval**, and I hear the moss is deep enough to sleep in for a century.

Keep your windows open, but mind the gap.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
