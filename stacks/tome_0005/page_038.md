Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the inside of a glacier, sitting in a café carved entirely out of blue ice. I have arrived in **Ys-Mirabilis**, the city where memory goes to freeze.

You recall I was heading *Into-the-Blue*, sailing the *Ocean-of-Forgetfulness* to find the *Drowned-Duchy*? The voyage was smoother than anticipated; the water was so thick with lost thoughts that my boat hardly made a ripple. But the Drowned-Duchy was not submerged. It was preserved—encapsulated in a sudden, instantaneous freeze. I sailed over a wall of ice and found a city trapped in suspension, a snapshot of a moment thousands of years ago, preserved in perfect, cold clarity. The transition was silent—a sudden drop in temperature that turned the sea into a solid mirror and the salt into glittering diamonds.

The geography here is archaeological and glacial. I am currently in *Ys-Mirabilis*, a metropolis buried under the anti-Everest—a mountain of ice that grows *downward* from the sky, piercing the crust of the earth. The city exists in the fissures and air pockets trapped within the ice. To navigate, one must wear **Vestments of Thinsulate and Respect**, heavy furs lined with mirrors to reflect the cold back onto itself. The streets are tunnels of compressed snow, and the buildings are ziggurats of clear ice. Because the air here is so cold, sound travels differently; whispers echo for days, and shouts can shatter glass.

The locals are the **Mnemosynes**, people who appear translucent, their flesh filled with glittering ice crystals. They do not speak with words; they exhale clouds of vapor that form shapes and glyphs. To read them, you have to catch the breath on the wind. I met a historian named *Vork* who was chipping away at a block of ice to free a frozen conversation. "We do not live in the present," he told me, his breath forming the shape of a frowning face. "The present is too fleeting. We live in the deep past, where everything is permanent."

Language here is difficult to master, as it relies on **Exhale-Calligraphy**. To say "Hello," you breathe a small, perfect circle; to say "Goodbye," you breathe a jagged line that breaks apart. I tried to ask for directions to the nearest inn, but my breath was too warm and formed a shapeless blob that Vork interpreted as a request to wrestle a polar bear. I declined, politely exhaling a wavy line of apology.

Dining here is a stark, sharp affair. I visited a canteen made of frozen aurora borealis and ordered the **Sashimi-of-Time**. The fish is flash-frozen in a block of ice; you must lick the ice until it melts enough to bite the fish. It tastes like salmon and the winter of 1842. I also tried the **Glacier-Gruel**, a thick porridge made from ground ice and shaved iron that heats you up by forcing your body to fight the metal. To drink, I had a **Meltwater-Martini**, which is simply pure, ancient water that hasn't seen the sun since the last ice age. It is so heavy with age that it hurts your teeth.

The wildlife is angular and resilient. There are **Prism-Penguins**, birds whose feathers act as fiber-optic cables, channeling light to their cores to keep warm. I saw a **Frost-Whale**, a massive creature that swims through the ice as if it were water, using a heated nose to bore tunnels. It moves with the slow grace of a glacier, groaning a deep, resonant song that vibrates in your chest.

I found a message from the library scrawled on a wall of ice using a hot poker. It read: *That which is frozen is not dead, only delayed.*

My breath is starting to crystallize on the page, and I can feel my thoughts slowing down, turning into slush. I need warmth before I become a permanent exhibit.

I am heading *Up*, ascending the *Stairway-of-Stars*. I hear there is a moon made of paper that orbits the earth only when no one is looking at it. They call it the **Origami-Satellite**, and I intend to fold myself a seat before the next phase begins.

Keep warm.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
