Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a threadbare cliff edge, watching the horizon literally unravel. I have arrived in **Sutratara**, the Texture of Existence, a land located in the缝隙 between a decision made and a choice regretted. It is a place where the metaphorical "fabric of reality" is taken quite literally.

You recall I was heading *Into-the-Past*, riding the *Locomotive of Linear Time* to find the **Erg of Eternal Erosion**? I fell asleep somewhere around the 14th century and must have rolled off the train. When I woke up, the hard steel of the tracks had softened into wool. The air here is dry and crackling with static electricity, smelling faintly of open markets and monsoon rains.

The geography is textile and terrifying. **Sutratara** is composed entirely of woven fibers. The mountains are immense folds of felt; the rivers are rushing streams of liquid silver and gold thread that sparkle so brightly they blind the unprepared. Gravity is less of a law and more of a suggestion based on the tension of the weave. I have to walk carefully, as a loose thread could cause a landslide—or worse, cause *me* to unravel. I saw a valley of **Velvet-Voids**, deep patches of soft darkness where sound goes to die, and the *Peaks-of-Pashmina*, which are soft to the touch but sharp enough to cut glass if you go against the grain.

The locals are the **Nibandhanas**, the Weavers of Fate. They are spindly, multi-armed figures (some have two arms, some four, some seem to have a new pair for every occasion) with skin patterned like intricate ikat textiles. They don't walk so much as glide, sliding along the warp and weft of the world. I met a weaver named *Tyaga* who was repairing a snag in a sunset. "Careful," she said, her voice sounding like the tearing of canvas. "You are pulling a red thread. If you snap it, all the sunsets in the universe will turn green."

Language here is knotted and complex. They speak *Katha-Sutra*, a dialect of knots and tugs. To say "Hello," you tie a specific knot in the air with your fingers; to say "Goodbye," you cut the knot. I tried to compliment Tyaga on her sari, but I used a "granny knot" instead of a "square knot," which accidentally implied that her weaving was prone to slipping. She laughed and unpicked my apology, turning it into a small flower she pinned to my lapel.

Dining here is a visceral experience. I visited a roadside stall suspended from a canopy of silk. I ordered the **Pulao-of-Pluralism*, a rice dish where every grain is a different color and represents a different possible outcome of my day. Eating it creates a buzzing sensation as your brain processes multiple timelines at once. I also tried the **Naan-of-Necessity**, bread that is baked on the griddle of "What Must Be." It is heavy, dense, and incredibly filling, leaving you with the distinct feeling that you have no choice but to finish it. To drink, I had a *Lassi-of-Lint*, a thick, blue yogurt drink that tastes like starlight and cleanses the insides with a swirling, soapy sensation.

The wildlife is domesticated and synthetic. There are **Sheep-of-Synapse**, fluffy clouds grazing on the hills, consuming loose thoughts and turning them into raw wool. I saw a **Cobra-of-Cord**, a snake made of braided rope that sways to the music of a flute, hypnotizing itself into a lasso.

I found a message from the library embroidered on the hem of a passing cloud. It read: *Life is not a straight line; it is a pattern yet to be resolved.*

The static is making my hair stand on end, and I’m afraid I might snag my sleeve on a cliff edge. I need a place where the connections are invisible, not tactile, and the threads don't tangle.

I am heading *Toward-the-Horizon*, sailing the *Ocean-of-Opalescence*. I hear there is a city built entirely of ships that have been lashed together for a thousand years, floating in a gyre of plastic and coral. They call it the **Flotilla of the Drift**, and I intend to find the captain of the ghost fleet.

Mind the loose threads.

Wish you were here.
