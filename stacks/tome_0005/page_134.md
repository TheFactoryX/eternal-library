**Origin:** The Steampunk Lagoon of *Valle-della-Nebbia* (The Foggy Forge)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of heavy, white linen, watermarked with the crest of a leaking pipe. It smells strongly of espresso, machine oil, and wet dog. The texture is rough, and the text is written in a rich, dark sepia ink that smudges if touched before it dries. Hidden in the weave of the fabric is a faint, rhythmic ticking sound, like a heartbeat.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a café table that is slowly sinking into a canal of warm, white mist. The air here is thick, visibility is poor, and the noise is a constant, comforting clatter of hisses and whistles. I have arrived in **Valle-della-Nebbia**, the Foggy Forge. This is the place where the Industrial Revolution didn't end, but simply dissolved into the atmosphere.

You recall I was heading *West*, sliding down the **Great-Waterfall-of-Mist** to find a jungle of glass trees? Well, the descent was exhilarating, right up until the waterfall ran dry. I plummeted into a dense, sulfurous cloud bank and landed in what I thought was a giant bowl of minestrone. It turned out to be the primary harbor of a city that exists entirely inside a perpetual cloud layer. This isn't weather; it is infrastructure. The locals have domesticated the fog. They pipe it into their homes like natural gas to power their stoves and engines.

The geography here is damp and mechanized. The buildings are tall Victorian terraces, but they aren't fixed to the ground. They are anchored to the seabed by massive iron chains, and they float gently on the dense fog like boats on a harbor. When the wind shifts, whole neighborhoods drift past each other. To get from the bakery to the cathedral, you have to take a **Steam-Gondola**. It is a propelled iron boat that skims the surface of the white cloud, leaving a wake of vapor that smells like lavender and rust.

The locals are the **Vaporetti-Masters**, engineers with soot-stained fingers and expensive goggles worn loosely around their necks. They are obsessed with pressure and humidity. They speak in **Hissing-Clicks**, a language that sounds exactly like a radiator that needs bleeding. A sharp *pfft* means "Too hot," while a low, rumbling *hiss* means "More tea." I tried to order a coffee, but I inadvertently asked for a spare piston. The barista, a burly man with a mustache like a bristled brush, just laughed and served me a single-origin roast brewed at 9 atmospheres of pressure. It was strong enough to strip paint.

I am currently staying in *The-Palazzo-della-Pressione*, a hotel where the rooms are pressurized differently depending on your mood. I asked for "relaxing," so they pumped my room full of heavy, sleep-inducing nitrogen. I slept for fourteen hours. The currency here is **Psi** (Pounds per Square Inch). To pay for my room, I had to work a shift pumping bellows for the city’s central heating boiler. It was exhausting work. My arms still ache, but the hot water in the shower—which comes out in a high-pressure jet that cleans your soul as well as your skin—was worth it.

I am eating a plate of **Calamaro-di-Vapore**. It is not a squid from the sea, but a squid made of condensed steam. The chef catches a pocket of wet fog, seasons it with garlic and lemon juice, and sautés it in a olive oil press until it holds its shape. It has the texture of warm mozzarella and tastes like the ocean air. For dessert, I am having **Tiramisu-Fritto**. It is balls of coffee-soaked ladyfingers, breaded and deep-fried in clarified butter, then dusted with powdered sugar and cocoa. It is heavy, greasy, and absolutely magnificent.

The wildlife is mechanical and biological. I see **Clockwork-Pigeons**. They are regular birds that have been modified with brass gears and copper skeletons to allow them to fly against the strong updrafts. Their cooing sounds like a tiny engine backfiring. The most dangerous pest is the **Rust-Moth**. It is a small, silver moth that eats iron. I had to shoo one away from my watch; it had already eaten the winder.

I found a message from the library stamped into the metal side of a lamppost. It was stern and industrial. It read: *Without resistance, there is no heat.*

The fog is thinning, which means the city is drifting lower. I need to catch a gondola before the water level rises too high.

I am heading *Into the Pipes*. I have found a maintenance hatch that leads into the massive ventilation system beneath the city. I hear rumors that if you follow the airflow deep enough, you emerge in a place where the air is solid enough to build castles on.

Don't hold your breath.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
