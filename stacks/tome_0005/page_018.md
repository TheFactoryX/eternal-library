**Origin:** The Rusting Steppe of *Yugopetrovgrad* (The Autonomous Oblast of Lingering Iron)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is heavy, made of thin, hammered copper. It smells of boiling cabbage, coal dust, and expensive cologne. The ink is graphite, and it smudges your fingers when you touch it.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the window seat of a locomotive that runs on distilled nostalgia. The boiler is stoked not with coal, but with the burning diaries of teenagers. I have arrived in **The Iron Hinterlands**, a vast, bruised landscape where the Industrial Revolution happened early and never quite ended.

You remember I was heading for the Conservatory of Prismatic Lies, seeking the glass flowers? I took a shortcut through a stained-glass window, but I cut myself on a shard of "Betrayal" and bled out into this timeline. I woke up in a heap of soot and velvet beside the tracks. The air here is thick, tasting of black pepper and regret.

The geography is architectural and aggressive. The Steppes are not grass; they are a rolling ocean of discarded gears, broken pistons, and rusted steam engines that have slowly eroded into reddish-black soil. The cities do not sprawl; they *lumber*. I am currently in *Yugopetrovgrad*, a metropolis built entirely inside the hollowed-out ribcage of a mechanical dragon that died of exhaustion sometime in the 19th century.

The locals are a hardened folk known as the **Baba-Wrenches**. They are stout, fierce grandmothers who wear shawls woven from copper wire and carry giant pipe wrenches to fix the fabric of reality. They brew tea in samovars that are actually small, contained explosions. When they stir the tea, they are stirring time. I met one who offered me a *Lemon-Theory*; it was incredibly sour but made me understand calculus instantly.

Language here is a collision of Slavic tongues and pneumatic hisses. They speak *Steam-Cant*, a language of whistles, grinding gears, and heavy consonants. To say "Hello," you must release a burst of steam from your left ear. To say "I am sorry," you must offer a piece of silver that has been cried upon. I tried to compliment a chef on his soup, but I accidentally used the intonation for "Your mother was a centrifuge." Fortunately, he took it as a joke, or perhaps a challenge to a duel of spices.

Dining is a heavy, warming affair. I visited a tavern called *The Bleeding Cylinder* and ordered a bowl of **Borscht-of-Sorrows**. The broth is a deep, violent red, pulsing slightly. It is made from beets grown in soil watered with weeping. When you eat it, you feel a profound sadness for a few minutes, followed by a rush of euphoric warmth. It pairs perfectly with **Pierogi-of-Forgotten-Dreams**. They are dumplings filled with potato and cheese, but if you bite into them wrong, you remember a dream you had as a child and immediately lose the ability to speak for an hour. I ate four; I haven't spoken since breakfast.

The wildlife is mechanical and misunderstood. I saw a stray **Bear-Wolf**, a creature with the head of a bear and the body of a locomotive. It roared, but the sound was a train whistle. It chased me for three miles before running out of coal. Then there are the **Matryoshka-Moths**. They are beautiful, painted wooden insects that hatch from smaller versions of themselves. When they land on you, you feel the overwhelming urge to sit in a sauna and beat yourself with birch branches.

I did find a nod to the library, carved into the rusted flank of the dragon-city. It was graffiti, written in grease: *Even iron dreams of rust. Even rust dreams of soil.*

The train is picking up speed, and the samovar is whistling. The Baba-Wrenches are telling me to sit down before the gravity shifts.

I am heading *Up*, into the clouds. I hear there is a kingdom suspended on the logic of harp strings, where the rain falls upward. They call it the **Spire of Aethers**, and I intend to climb until the air turns to music.

Mind the steppe, and keep your copper shined.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
