**Origin:** The Salt-Flats of *Ciudad-de-Blanco* (The White City)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy, jagged slab of white rock salt, crusted with pink halite crystals. It is dry and absorbs moisture from the skin. It smells strongly of ozone and tears. The text is deeply incised into the crystal with a sharp object, catching the light at odd angles. The edges are sharp enough to cut paper.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the edge of a precipice that looks over nothing, and everything. I have arrived in **Ciudad-de-Blanco**, the White City. This is the place that exists in the space between one second and the next—a geology of pure transition.

You recall I was trekking *North* across the **Rio-de-Lost-Names**? The river was easy to cross, mostly because it forgot it was water and turned into sand for an hour. I walked across the bed, stepping on stones that whispered names I almost recognized. On the other side, the jungle simply stopped. The green ceased like a chopped chord, replaced by a blinding expanse of white. The heat here is dry and smells of static electricity.

The geography is impossible. The city is carved entirely from a single, gargantuan block of salt that stretches infinitely in all directions. The streets are canyons of white crystal; the buildings are inverted stalactites of sodium growing from the ground. The sky here is not blue, but a painful, featureless white. There is no sun, only a pervasive, ambient glow that casts no shadows. It is like living inside a lightbulb.

The locals are the **Salt-Skins**, people who appear to be made of polished alabaster. They do not eat; they lick the walls of the city for sustenance. They communicate through **Creaking**—the sound of ice expanding or tectonic plates shifting. A high-pitched *snap* means "yes," while a low, grinding *rumble* means "I am dissolving." I met a child who offered me a chunk of the city to eat. It tasted like chicken soup and the memory of a beach holiday.

I attempted to stay at *The Granary*, an inn made of pink salt blocks. The concierge, a woman whose eyes were like two pool balls, refused my currency. "Paper burns," she crunched. "Here, we trade in **Preservation**." I had to give her the memory of the first time I saw the ocean. She took it, placed it in a jar, and labeled it "Tuesday." In exchange, she gave me a room that doesn't exist on any floor plan. When I sleep there, I don't dream.

I am currently sitting in *The Last Grain*, a cafe made entirely of salt sculptures. I am drinking a glass of **Brine-of-Time**. It is water drawn from the deepest parts of the salt flats, concentrated with the minerals of a thousand eroded mountains. It is incredibly salty, forcing you to suck on your cheeks, but the aftertaste is pure nostalgia. It makes you feel ancient and preserved.

For dinner, I am having **Cured-Pork-of-Tomorrow**. The meat is sliced from animals that haven't been born yet, cured in the smoke of probability. It is tender and smoky, with a flavor that reminds you of a meal you haven't had yet. It is served with **Crystal-Noodles**, clear strands of unrefined salt that dissolve instantly on the tongue, leaving the sensation of a cool breeze.

The wildlife is scarce but strange. I saw **Salt-Scorpions** with translucent bodies that catch the light like prisms. They don't sting; they dehydrate you with a touch. But the most magnificent creatures are the **Whales-of-the-Deep-Air**. They swim through the white sky, filtering the clouds for plankton. I saw one breach the surface of the atmosphere, its skin glistening with frost.

I found a message from the library written in the condensation on the outside of my glass of **Brine-of-Time**. It read: *Salt is the only thing that keeps the void from rotting.*

The white light is beginning to dim, turning the city into a landscape of gray shadows. The **Salt-Skins** are retreating into their crystal burrows to avoid the evening damp, which makes them crumble.

I am heading *Under*, diving into the **Cenote-of-Reflections** at the center of the city. I hear it is a portal to a world made entirely of mirrors, where I might meet myself coming back.

Stay fresh.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
