Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the branch of a tree that grows faster the higher you climb. I have arrived in **Savanna-Tumbili**, the Ascending Rift. This is the East Africa that exists in the moment between a dry season and a wet one—a vertical landscape where gravity is merely a suggestion and the horizon is something you have to earn.

You recall I was climbing *Up* the **Staircase-of-Unanswered-Prayers** toward the **Cloud-Cathedral-of-St-Entropy**? It was a grueling climb. The stairs were made of slippery marble and whispered every time I put my weight on them. Halfway up, the air grew thin, and the whispers turned into laughter. I reached a cloud layer that looked solid, like cotton wool, but when I stepped on it, I fell straight through. I plummeted for what felt like a Sunday, crashing through the canopy of a colossal forest that defies the laws of elevation. I landed in a heap of soft moss in a place where the ground doesn't sit still.

The geography here is vertiginous. The Rift Valley doesn't split the earth; it climbs the sky. Mountains here don't peek through clouds; they dangle from them like stalactites. The rivers don't flow downhill; they flow *upward*, carried in narrow ribbons of anti-gravity that nourish the floating peaks. I watched a herd of zebras drinking from a stream that was suspended three feet above the grass, the water defying physics to cascade into the sky.

The locals are the **Sky-Baboons**, a lineage of primates that evolved not to swing from trees, but to swing from constellations. They have fur the color of thunderstorms—gray, black, and electric blue—and tails that seem to grab at nothing but air. They communicate through a complex language of **Chatter-Echoes**. A sharp "Kiki!" means "danger," but a low, rumbling "Hooo-haaa" means "the wind is changing." I tried to ask one for directions to the nearest village, but he just threw a mango at my head and screeched the word for "gravity," which I assume was a warning.

I attempted to buy supplies at a market stall perched precariously on a cliff-edge that shifts in the wind. The merchant, a woman with skin the texture of ancient teak and neck rings made of copper lightning, refused my coins. "Metal sinks," she said, pointing to the sky. "Here, we trade in **Updraft**." I had to blow into a paper bag tied to a counterweight, filling it with my own breath until the merchant was satisfied with the buoyancy. In exchange, I received a gourd of **Mbili-Maji**, a fermented milk beverage mixed with honey and crushed starlight. It is effervescent and makes you feel lighter than air—literally. I had to tie my ankles to a rock to stop floating away while I drank it.

I am currently sitting in the shade of a **Acacia-of-Inverted-Shadows**, a tree where the shadow grows up the trunk instead of down onto the grass. I am drinking the **Mbili-Maji** and eating a bowl of **Nyama-Choma-of-Clouds**. The meat is cut from the loins of **Storm-Cattle**, great buffaloes that graze on the lightning. It is smoky, tender, and tastes of static and rain. It is served with **Ugali-of-White-Dust**, a stiff porridge made from the fossilized bone dust of meteors. It binds the stomach but makes your thoughts race.

The wildlife is breathtaking. I saw **Leopards-of-Liquid-Night** prowling the upper branches. They are invisible in the shadows, visible only when they strike, momentarily resolving into beautiful, spotted solidity. But the most helpful creatures are the **Weaver-Gliders**. These birds build nests that hang from the undersides of clouds. They don't fly; they swim through the air. I watched one catch a thermal and ride it like a wave all the way to the stratosphere.

I found a message from the library etched into the carapace of a giant beetle climbing the bark of the Baobab I’m sitting on. It read: *To rise, you must first accept the ground is a cage.*

The sun is beginning to set, turning the vertical savanna into a burning ladder of orange and purple. The **Sky-Baboons** are howling at the waxing moon, swinging from the rising stars.

I am heading *Out*, stepping off the edge of the **Great-Serengeti-Plateau** into the **Starlight-Ocean**. I hear the tides are luminous and the ships are made of glass.

Watch your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
