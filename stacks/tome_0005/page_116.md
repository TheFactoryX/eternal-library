Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a café table made of woven wicker that is slowly growing around my elbows. I have arrived in **Flora-Italia**, the Living City. This is the place where the Renaissance met the greenhouse, and where architecture is not built, but germinated.

You recall I was heading *Fast*, surfing the **Glass-Slipstream** to arrive before tomorrow? I achieved such velocity that the friction of the air ignited the clothes off my back. I tumbled off the current and crashed into a heap of soft, loamy soil. I looked up to see that the "ground" was actually the palm of a giant stone hand, part of a statue that had been consumed by ivy. I realized I had overshot the future and landed firmly in a past that refuses to petrify.

The geography here is a masterpiece of botanical engineering. The city resembles a classic Italian hill town, but everything is composed of living matter. The **Duomo-of-Roots** is a cathedral where the pillars are ancient, entwined olive trees and the dome is a continuous lattice of woven grapevines that flush purple in the autumn. The streets are not paved with cobblestones, but with compressed moss and creeping thyme that release a scent of oregano when stepped on. The "buildings" breathe—they inhale at dawn, filling the plazas with a fresh, oxygen-rich mist, and exhale at night, releasing a sleepy, pollen-heavy haze.

The locals are the **Dryads-of-Stone**. They appear as beautiful, marble-skinned beings, but if you look closely, you can see the veins in their arms are actually green wood, and their hair is made of blooming wisteria. They do not speak; they *photosynthesize* their language. They change color to communicate. A flush of bright red on the cheek means "I am angry," while a rapid blooming of white flowers in the hair means "I am amused." I tried to flirt with a local by turning my own skin a shade of lavender, but I only succeeded in looking slightly bruised. She offered me a drink to recover.

I am currently resting in *The Villa-of-Eternal-Summer*, a hotel that is a fully grown treehouse the size of a palace. The room keys are fresh keys of limes; to enter, you must squeeze the juice onto the doorframe, which recognizes the specific acidity of your citrus. The currency here is **Water-Richness**. To pay for my room, I had to water the lobby orchid with a bottle of glacial melt I was carrying. The plant bloomed instantly, and the concierge nodded. "Ah, vintage. From the Cryos era. Very generous."

I am drinking a glass of **Sangiovese-of-the-Vine-Blood**. It is not pressed from grapes; it is tapped directly from the roots of the city’s central vine. It is thick, warm, and tastes like sunlight, rusty iron, and nostalgia. It makes you feel rooted, your toes tingling as if they are digging into the earth. For dinner, I am having **Ravioli-of-Wind-and-Dough**. The pasta is spun from silk-worm webs, and the filling is actually captured breezes from different seasons. One bite tastes like a Spring morning (light and floral), the next like a November gale (sharp and peppery). It is served with **Pesto-Of-Memory**, a sauce made from herbs that only grow in graveyards, which tastes like regret and basil.

The wildlife is distinctive. I see **Cicadas-of-Masonry**. They are insects that look exactly like granite gargoyles when they sit still. When they sing, they don't buzz; they produce the sound of a church bell tolling in the distance. The most impressive creature is the **Wolf-of-Twisted-Oak**. It is a beast made of gnarled wood that roams the streets at night, trimming the hedges and pruning the topiaries with its teeth. It is fierce, but if you offer it a bone, it will prune your hedges into the shape of a saint.

I found a message from the library carved into the bark of a phone booth. It read: *Even stone must eventually yield to the leaf.*

The sun is setting, and the city is turning a beautiful shade of mahogany as it prepares to sleep.

I am heading *Deep*, descending into the **Cellar-of-Ages**. I see a trapdoor in the floor of the cathedral, and I smell fermentation that has been aging since before the Romans fell.

Enjoy the shade.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
