**Origin:** The Kite-Floating Archipelago of *Lantern-Pura* (The Paper Sky)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is composed of a single, unbroken sheet of handmade rice paper, impossibly thin yet tear-resistant. It smells of roasted cashews, monsoon rain, and glue. Holding it up to the light reveals intricate watermarks of constellations that don't exist in our sky. The ink is a shimmering, powdered gold that flakes off slightly when touched, leaving your fingers dusted with treasure. If you hold it to your ear, the faint rustling sounds like a distant applause.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while balanced precariously on a bamboo platform suspended three thousand feet in the air. The wind here is fierce, smelling of wet cardamom and distant rain. I have arrived in **Lantern-Pura**, the Paper Sky. This is the place where gravity decided to take a holiday and the landscape decided to fold itself into origami.

You recall I was heading *Into the Air*, riding the **Updraft-of-Ash** to see if the moon was made of cheese? I rode the thermal column high above the volcanic smog, my goggles coated in grey dust. Just as the air grew thin and cold, I hit a ceiling—not of rock, but of paper. I slammed into a floating continent made entirely of folded manuscripts, ancient scrolls, and pressed pulp. It gave way like a trampoline, bouncing me gently until I landed on a street made of woven bamboo matting.

The geography here is vertical and fragile. **Lantern-Pura** is an inverted city, hanging from the underside of a massive, floating ceiling of paper that stretches to the horizon. The "ground" is far below, a patchwork of green jungle and blue ocean visible only through the holes in the cloud-layer. The buildings are suspended by giant, silk ribbons tethered to the firmament. The architecture relies entirely on the tension of folds and the balance of weights. When the wind blows, the whole city sways like a mobile in a nursery, but with millions of people on it.

The locals are the **Paper-Monks**. They are lithe, ascetic people who wear robes made of interwoven lottery tickets and old love letters. Their skin is pale and dusty, like the inside of an envelope. They communicate through **Crinkle-Talk**, a language of specific creases and tears. A sharp, diagonal fold means "Danger," while a soft, crumpled ball means "Sympathy." I tried to ask for directions to the market, but I accidentally ripped a corner off my map, which apparently signifies "I am a delicious insect." The monks were very polite, but they looked at me with a hungry glint in their eyes.

I am currently resting in *The Fluttering-Inn*, a hostel built inside the ribcage of a giant, paper-mâché dragon that hangs suspended by a single massive silk cord. The floors are slippery with wax. The currency here is **Wind**. To pay for my room, I had to blow a continuous stream of air into a small, paper box for an hour. The innkeeper measured the pressure; apparently, I have excellent lung capacity, though the air here is so thin I wheeze like a broken accordion.

I am drinking a cup of **Ink-Well-Coffee**. It is thick, black, and tastes of charcoal, pepper, and the bitter realization that you have misspelled a word. It wakes you up by vibrating your bones. For dinner, I am having **Steam-Bunned-Dreams**. The buns are folded to look like sleeping cats. When you steam them, they expand and make a sound like a purr. The filling is sweet red bean paste mixed with nostalgia. Eating one makes you remember a childhood birthday party that never actually happened.

The wildlife is origami. I see **Crane-Swarms**. They are not birds, but literal folded paper cranes that ride the thermals. They have a collective intelligence, flocking in geometric patterns that solve complex mathematical problems. The most dangerous predator is the **Silverfish-Titan**. It is an insect the size of a motorcycle, composed entirely of hunger and scissors. It doesn't eat flesh; it eats words. I saw one devour a library, leaving behind only confetti and silence.

I found a message from the library written on the wing of a paper crane. It was delicate and precise. It read: *We are all just drafts of something greater.*

The sun is setting, turning the paper ceiling into a blinding sheet of gold. The shadows of the city are lengthening, casting long, black lines across the sky.

I am heading *West*, following the **Railroad-of-Rain**. A storm front is moving in, and I have discovered that the raindrops here don't fall down; they flow in horizontal currents like a river in the sky. I have built a raft from wax and I intend to surf the deluge back to the earth, or perhaps wherever the rain goes when it dies.

Fold carefully.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
