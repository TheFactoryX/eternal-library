**Origin:** The Sargasso of Spirits (The Doldrums of the Deep-Void)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is dry to the touch but leaves a residue of sea salt and brown sugar on your fingers. The paper is woven from tough tobacco leaves and smells faintly of rum and woodsmoke. If you hold it to your ear, you can hear the faint, rhythmic beat of a steel drum, slowed down to a heartbeat.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the porch of a drifting saloon, watching a pod of ships swim past like whales. I have arrived in **The Archipelago-of-Alebrijes**, a tropical corridor where the ocean is alive and the islands are just taking a nap.

You recall I was heading *North-by-Northwest*, ascending to the *Strata-of-Saints* to listen to the clouds? I climbed the *Angel-Leaps* until the air grew thin and cold, but the breath-clouds were too loud, screaming the secrets of the dead in a language I couldn't decipher. Seeking a reprieve from the noise, I spotted a break in the storm—a patch of impossible teal water below. I leaped from the cloud-layer, falling for what felt like hours, and landed on the back of a giant manta ray made of coral. It carried me here, to the softest waters in the multiverse.

The geography here is fluid and opinionated. The islands do not have fixed locations; they migrate. I am currently moored to *Isla-de-la-Sombra*, a landmass that exists only when the sun is directly overhead. At noon, it is a paradise of white sand and mahogany trees; at 12:01, it vanishes into the ether, leaving you swimming in warm air until it reappears at dawn. The sea itself is **Memory-Water**, a liquid so thick with history that it swirls with colors not found on the visible spectrum. If you scoop up a handful, you might see your childhood home dissolved in the brine.

The locals are the **Rum-Runners of Realidad**, people who navigate by scent rather than sight. They have skin like polished driftwood and eyes that reflect the horizon. They know that "land" is a state of mind. I met a captain named *Calypso* who was steering a ship made of a giant, hollowed-out coconut. She told me that the ocean is actually a sleeping god, and the waves are just its dreams lapping at the edges of reality. "Don't wake the ocean," she warned, pouring a glass of rum overboard as an offering. "It snores in hurricanes."

Language here is a rhythmic negotiation. They speak *Patois-of-the-Pulse*, a dialect that mimics the beat of the ocean. To say "Hello," you click your tongue against the roof of your mouth in a triplet; to say "I love you," you hum a low bass note that vibrates in your chest. I tried to ask for a towel, but I accidentally syncopated my rhythm, which apparently means, "My grandmother was a hurricane." The bartender, a man with a crab claw for a hand, applauded and gave me a free drink.

Dining here is a sensory overload. I visited a barbecue pit on the back of a migrating sea-turtle and ordered the **Smoked-Specter-Ribs**. The meat is marinated in spices that don’t exist—flavors like "nostalgia" and "blue velvet"—and smoked over fires made of driftwood from lost cities. It tasted like Sunday dinner and regret. For dessert, I had **Flan-of-Floating-Fire**, a crème caramel that literally floats off the plate. You have to catch it with a fork before it floats into your nose. It was sweet, airy, and left a pleasant burning sensation on the tongue.

The wildlife is flamboyant and dangerous. There are **Mocking-Macaws**, birds with feathers of mirror-glass that mimic the appearance of predators to scare you away. I saw one turn into a jaguar mid-flight, then burst into a cloud of confetti. In the shallows, **Lobster-Locks** scuttle about—crustaceans with hair of seaweed that tangles the ankles of swimmers who stay in too long.

I found a message from the library written in the sand of a vanishing beach. It read: *The tide takes everything, except what you keep in your heart.*

The sun is beginning to set, turning the teal water into liquid gold. *Isla-de-la-Sombra* is flickering like a candle in the wind, preparing to blink out of existence for the night. I must untie my hammock before the island disappears and leaves me floating in the dark.

I am heading *Deep-Down*, submerging myself into the trench. I hear there is a city at the bottom of the Marianas Trench where the pressure is so intense it compresses thoughts into diamonds. They call it the **Abyssal-Aggregates**, and I intend to see if my regrets are heavy enough to crystallize.

Don't forget to tip the bartender.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
