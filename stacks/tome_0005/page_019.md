**Origin:** The Saffron Archipelago of *Suvarnabhumi-Zero* (The Latitudes of Liquid Karma)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is damp and slightly soft, like overripe fruit. It smells faintly of durian, lemongrass, and incoming rain. The ink is a deep, iridescent gold that seems to float slightly above the paper.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a wooden platform that floats on a lake of warm, green tea. I have arrived in **The Infinite Delta**, a place where the distinction between the ocean, the sky, and the spirit world has dissolved into a humid, golden haze.

You recall I was heading for the Spire of Aethels, to climb into the clouds? I made it perhaps three feet up the first rope before the gravity inverted and the sky folded in on itself like a wet napkin. I fell for a very long time, and when I landed, I splashed. The air here is heavy—it feels like breathing through a warm, silk scarf. It smells of frangipani, exhaust fumes from the future, and frying garlic.

The geography here is fluid, quite literally. The landmasses are not fixed; they are **Floating Lotus-Pads** of reinforced concrete, drifting on a navigable sea of *Karma-Soup*. I am currently in the canal city of *Vientiane-Elsewhere*, a sprawling maze of stilt houses, neon-lit shrines, and collapsing French-colonial architecture that has learned to repair itself with moss and magnetic vines.

The locals are the **Naga-Folk**, human from the waist up but with shimmering, iridescent tails of rainbow trout or koi below. They glide through the streets with an elegance that makes me feel like a clumsy bipedal disaster. They are incredibly hospitable, though they have a puzzling custom: when they meet a stranger, they try to buy their shadow. I sold mine yesterday for three coins and a packet of crispy anchovies. I feel lighter, but I look a bit two-dimensional.

Language is a complex melody here. They speak *Ton-Glass*, a dialect where words are sung in specific pitches to change their meaning, and silence costs extra. To say "Hello," you must smile with your eyes but keep your mouth closed. To say "Thank you," you must place your hands together in a prayer position and bow until your spine realigns. I tried to order a bowl of noodles, but I used the wrong tone for "chicken," and accidentally asked for "the heartbreak of a minor poet." The waitress, a lovely woman with gills, nodded sympathetically and brought me a bowl of broth that tasted like tears and rain.

Dining here is a spiritual experience. I visited a street stall called *The Wok of Eternal Returning* and ordered a plate of **Pad-Thai-of-Samsara**. The noodles are endless; no matter how many you eat, the bowl remains full. The peanuts are made of compressed time, and the lime wedges squirt a juice that makes you remember your future. It was delicious, but I felt I was stuck in a loop of chewing. To drink, I had a cup of **Cha-Yen-Memory**. It is a tea so sweet and strong (stained with condensed milk) that it grants you temporary telepathy with cats. I spent an hour arguing with a stray about the quality of the fish heads.

The wildlife is breathtaking. I took a boat ride through the Mangrove-of-Lost-Sounds and saw a flock of **Garuda-Pigeons**. These birds have the wings of eagles and the faces of stern bureaucrats. They don't coo; they shout regulations regarding the proper disposal of wishes. Then there are the **Elephant-Turtles**, massive creatures the size of islands, carrying entire temple complexes on their backs. They move so slowly you don't notice them moving, until you realize the sunrise has shifted and you are five miles west of where you thought you were.

I did find a trace of the library. In a hole-in-the-wall bookshop, I found a palm-leaf manuscript that had been chewed on by silverfish. The holes in the text formed a message that read: *The map is not the territory, but the territory is certainly a map.*

The monsoon is coming in. The rain here doesn't fall down; it falls *in*, soaking directly into your pores without touching your skin. I must find cover before I dissolve completely.

I am heading *North*, climbing the stairs that wind up the side of the world. I hear there is a plateau where the wind is made of solid glass and the birds fly backward for symmetry. They call it the **Plateau of Shattered Silence**, and I intend to listen to the sound of light breaking.

Don't forget to tip the gods.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
