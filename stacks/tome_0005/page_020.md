**Origin:** The Hanging Mountains of *Senkyō-Paper* (The Prefecture of Folded Geologies)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is incredibly thin and light. It is not made of cardstock, but of a single, unbroken sheet of pristine white mulberry paper. It smells of ozone, wet ink, and cherry blossoms. The ink is glossy black, and if you tilt it, the characters seem to move.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the ledge of a cliff that is also a page. I have arrived in **The Thousand-Fold Realm**, a place where the laws of physics were deemed too rigid and have been replaced by the aesthetics of origami.

You remember I was heading for the Plateau of Shattered Silence, to listen to light breaking? I took the stairs, but I must have missed a step because I tripped and fell into the crease of a map. When I landed, the landscape unfolded around me. The air here is dry and rustles like turning pages. It tastes of soy sauce and mounting suspense.

The geography is architectural and fragile. The mountains here do not rise from the ground; they hang from the sky by invisible threads of *Kami-Thread*. The ground is not soil, but layers of *Washi-Parchment*, layered so thickly that trees can take root in the strata of history. I am currently in the floating village of *Senkyō-Paper*, a collection of dwellings that look like ink-brush strokes hovering in the air. To get from my room to the bathhouse, I have to cross a bridge made of a single Haiku that suspends over a drop of nothingness. You have to read it with perfect rhythm, or the ink dissolves.

The locals are the **Kami-Folders**, people made of paper and shadow. They are incredibly thin—literally two-dimensional—and they move by slipping between the cracks in the air. They wear clothes made of folded fogs, and they communicate by folding their bodies into shapes. A triangle is a greeting; a crane is a marriage proposal. I tried to compliment a woman on her kimono, but I accidentally folded myself into the shape of a "No Exit" sign. I was unfolded by a benevolent monk who told me I was too crumpled for polite society.

Language here is visual and structural. They speak *Ink-Tongue*, a language of brushstrokes and negative space. To say "Hello," you must paint a circle in the air and wait for it to dry. To say "I am hungry," you must make your stomach rumble in the key of C-major. I asked a vendor for directions to the library, but my intonation was off, and I accidentally challenged him to a calligraphy duel. We painted for three hours. I lost, but I drew a very mean-spirited shrimp.

Dining is an art form that occasionally bites back. I visited a tea-house called *The Empty Cup* and ordered a bowl of **Soup-of-Unspoken-Words**. It is a clear broth served with silence. You have to think of a secret to make the broth steam. The hotter the steam, the more scandalous the secret. I thought of a lie I told in high school, and the soup boiled over, burning my tongue with the taste of "I didn't do it." It was delicious. For dessert, I had **Mochi-of-Regret**. It is incredibly chewy; the longer you chew, the more you remember the person you shouldn't have let go of. I choked on a memory once, but the waiter slapped my back, and I coughed up a haiku about rain.

The wildlife is folded and sharp. I saw a garden of **Bonsai-Dragons**, miniature reptiles no larger than a corn cob, breathing fire that smells like lighter fluid and incense. They guard the trees, not out of duty, but because they are aesthetically pleasing. Then there are the **Cranes-of-Steel**, giant mechanical birds that fly overhead. They don't flap; they pivot and click. They drop origami bombs that explode into confetti and good luck. I caught a piece of red paper that told me I would live a long life, provided I avoid paper cuts.

I did find a reference to the library in the margin of a cliff face. It was written in small, frantic script: *The story never ends, but the reader must eventually close the book.*

The wind is picking up, and the paper mountains are starting to rattle. I must find a paperweight before I blow away.

I am heading *Under*, into the binding. I hear there is a valley where the spine of the world meets the cover, and they brew a wine from the glue of broken bindings. They call it the **Gutter of Lost Things**, and I intend to get stuck there.

Keep your edges sharp.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
