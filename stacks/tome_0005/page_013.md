**Origin:** The Rust-Spires of Ustalost-The-Forgotten (The Sector of Lingering Twilight)
**Arrival Status:** The card is heavy, laminated in a sheet of translucent grey iron; smells of woodsmoke, beets, and stale snow; the paper is cold to the touch, regardless of the room temperature.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the sleeper cabin of a train that left the station three decades ago and hasn't stopped moving since. I have arrived in **Ustalost-The-Forgotten**, a Slavic dimension that exists in the perpetual, bruised hour of a winter twilight.

You recall I was heading for The Echo-Spiral, seeking the architecture of sound? I took a shortcut through a melody that was too sad to be finished, and I fell out of the score near the border of a memory. I landed in a snowdrift that was made entirely of exhausted sighs.

The geography here is rugged and steeped in a beautiful, heavy melancholy. The mountains are not made of rock, but of **Black Iron and Old Regrets**. The peaks are capped with snow that never melts, only compresses into diamonds under the weight of its own sadness. The forests are thick with *Iron-Birch* and *Sorrow-Pine*, trees that have leaves made of razor-thin slate. When the wind blows, the forest doesn't rustle; it chimes like a thousand broken bells.

I am currently in the **Village of Neskolko**, which sits in the shadow of the **Clockwork-Kremlin**. This citadel is not built by hands, but grown from the gears of a fallen moon. It ticks louder than thunder, regulating the heartbeat of the valley. If the clock ticks slow, the winters last for years. If it ticks fast, the vodka turns to water. Today, it is ticking perfectly, which means the melancholy is stable.

The locals are the **Babushka-Golems** and the *Lumber-Jacks*. The Babushkas are formidable women made of solid clay and kindness, wrapped in shawls knitted from fog. They do not walk; they *loom* protectively over their grandchildren. The men are giants made of oak and iron plaid, who spend their days logging the *Ghost-Firs*—trees that scream when you cut them down, so you must chop them while apologizing profusely.

Language is a difficult barrier here. The locals speak a dialect called *Sigh-abary*. To say "Hello," you must exhale deeply. To say "I love you," you must weep silently. I tried to order a coffee, but I accidentally complimented the waiter’s mustache, which is apparently a marriage proposal. He was very flattered, but I had to flee the reception.

Dining is a robust, life-affirming affair. I went to a tavern called *The Broken Compass* and ordered a bowl of **Borscht-of-Undoing**. It is a soup so red it hurts your eyes. One spoonful warms you; two spoonfuls make you forget why you were sad; three spoonfuls make you forget your mother’s maiden name. I stopped at two. It tasted like home and heartbeats. For the main course, I had **Blins-Regret**, pancakes filled with a jam made from the fruits of *Maybe-Next-Year*. The berries are sour and difficult to swallow, but the aftertaste is sweet hope.

The wildlife is formidable. I was chased by a ** Dire-Wolf** that was actually just a lost wolf with low blood sugar. I fed it a piece of dried fish, and it told me its life story in a series of low whines. It seems even the monsters here are just lonely. I also saw a **Firebird** that was actually on fire. It perched on my windowsill, burning the frame, and sang a song that made my tears turn into pearls. I used one to pay for my hotel room.

I did, however, find a trace of the library I have been chasing. In the town square, there is a statue of a bear playing a balalaika. The bear is real, just frozen in time by a witch’s curse. I whispered the Morse code from the San-Source elder into its ear, and the bear twitched. It seems the "One Who Waits" has left breadcrumbs everywhere, even in the snow.

The train is picking up speed, and the conductor is asking for tickets. He doesn't want money; he wants a secret. I plan to give him the one about why I never learned to dance.

I am heading **Up**, ascending the Staircase of Clouds. I hear there is a kingdom located on the back of a giant migrating stingray that swims through the stratosphere. They call it **The Aero-Reef**, and I hear the winds there carry the scent of spices and old maps.

Keep your heart warm, and never apologize for crying.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
