**Origin:** The Vertebrae-Archipelago of *Osteoguatemala* (The Rib-Ridge)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a thin, curved sheet of white material that resembles a giant fingernail. It is warm and slightly flexible, with a texture like polished horn. Small, coarse hairs still adhere to the back. It smells of heavy rain, wet fur, and burning copal. If you hold it to your ear, you can hear a faint, rhythmic thumping, like a distant heartbeat.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while suspended in a hammock woven from vascular vines, strung between the scapulae of two ancient, towering hills. I have arrived in **Osteoguatemala**, the high-altitude nation perched precariously upon the **Spine-of-the-World**. This is the place where the mountains are not merely stone; they are the calcified remains of a titan so large that its ribs form the valleys and its vertebrae the peaks.

You recall I was heading *To the Ear of the World* to hear the silence? I was diverted by a sudden updraft of hot air smelling of tortillas and ozone. I grabbed onto a passing cloud and found myself deposited on the **Pelvis-Peninsula**, a wide, stable basin famous for its coffee plantations. The air here is thin but rich with oxygen; taking a deep breath feels like mainlining caffeine.

The geography here is porous and interlocking. The ground is not soil, but a layer of loam accumulated over centuries of decay upon the limestone plates. Rain does not flow in rivers; it seeps through the fissures and foramina of the landscape, collecting in the **Marrow-Lakes** deep underground. I am currently trekking through the **Canyon-of-the-Clavicle**, a narrow passage where the wind whistles through the natural hollows, sounding like a flute that never stops playing. To the east rises the great volcanic peak of **Mount-Hum**. I tried to climb it, but the ground shook with such rhythmic ferocity that I feared I would wake the sleeper beneath.

The locals are the **Ossifics**. They are a hardy people who believe that by building their homes of wood and mud upon the bone, they prevent the world from calcifying completely. They paint their doors bright blue to distract the eyes of passing carnivores. They communicate through **Resonant-Tapping**. They carry small carved bones which they strike against trees or rocks to send messages through the limestone. A sharp *tick-tick-tick* means "Dinner is ready," while a low, hollow *thrum* means "The jaguar is near." I tried to tap a rhythm, but my knuckles are too soft; I only succeeded in bruising my hand and confusing a merchant who thought I was selling insurance.

I am staying in *The Cortijo-Hacienda*, a guesthouse built into the ribcage of a small hill. The walls are porous and breathe with the temperature changes. The concierge is a quetzal, a bird with plumage so red and green it hurts the eyes, who refuses to give me the room key until I solve a riddle about the color of time.

I am dining on **Sopa-de-Hueso**. It is a broth made from boiling the fossils of small lizards, infused with chili and lime. It tastes ancient, minerally, and clears the sinuses instantly. For the main course, I had **Carnitas-asada**. It is not pork, but the meat of the **Tapir-Bone-Beetle**, a creature the size of a pig that grubs in the marrow-pits. It is slow-roasted until the meat falls off the exoskeleton. It is smoky, tender, and requires a strong jaw. For dessert, I am having **Calaveras-of-Sugar-Rock**. They are candies made from crystallized calcium, sweet and gritty, that make your teeth feel immortal.

The wildlife is… structural. The most common nuisance is the **Tick-Bird**. They are small, iridescent birds that peck at the moss growing on the bone-hills. If you stand still too long, they will try to clean the algae off your shoes. The true danger, however, is the **Quake-Snake**. It is a serpent that moves through the solid rock by dissolving the calcium ahead of it and sealing the tunnel behind. It creates localized earthquakes as it hunts. I felt the ground tremble during lunch; the waiter simply clicked his tongue and said, "It is just the worm turning in its sleep."

I found a message from the library carved into a stalagmite in the cave where I sheltered from the rain. It read: *We are all just scaffolding for something else to grow upon.*

The weather is turning, and the green flash of sunset is illuminating the fossil textures of the hills. I must pack my bags before the fog rolls in; navigating the vertebrae in low visibility is a recipe for a broken neck.

I am heading *Into the Root-Network*. I have heard there is a way down through the pelvic floor into the subterranean jungle where the blood of the world still flows. I want to see if the trees here drink red wine or water.

Mind your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
