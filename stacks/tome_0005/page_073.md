**Origin:** The Phenomenological Parish of *Yawn-Deep* (The Bed of the Last Giant)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is woven from coarse, grey wool that feels uncomfortably like a tongue. It is damp and smells faintly of stale cocoa and ancient soap. The text is embroidered in thread that changes color depending on the reader's body temperature.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from inside a ribcage the size of a cathedral, huddled under a blanket of fog that tastes of iron and old dreams. I have arrived in **Yawn-Deep**, the Bed of the Last Giant. This is the Scandinavia that exists in the liminal space between waking and sleeping, a land of permanent twilight and geological fatigue.

You recall I was heading *Vertically* down the *Elevator-of-Lost-Things* to the **Abyssal-Glass-Works**? I stepped into the elevator carriage and pressed the button for the basement, but the carriage itself—apparently a sentient entity made of brass and bad memories—decided I was too tired to go down. It apologized in a voice like a cello and told me to rest my eyes. I blinked, and the floor vanished. I fell *upward* for what felt like a week, landing softly on a landscape that looked like a crumpled sheet of white linen.

The geography here is folded. The mountains are not peaks but wrinkles in the fabric of the world, towering pleats of granite that disappear into a cloud cover that hangs only six feet off the ground. Navigation is difficult because the horizon is a suggestion rather than a fact. I tried to walk to a forest in the distance, but as I approached, the trees resolved into the eyelashes of a slumbering titan. I had to step carefully to avoid waking him; the locals say if he opens his eyes, the dream ends, and we all turn into pumpkins.

The locals are the **Shepherds-of-Sleep**, tall, silent figures draped in coats made of woven nightmares and repressed anxieties. They herd **Dream-Sheep**, which are actually fluffy, geometric shapes that hum pop songs from the 1990s. The Shepherds communicate by *Sighing*. It is a complex language: a sharp exhale means "danger," while a long, shuddering intake means "the coffee is ready." I attempted to ask for directions to the nearest town, but I only managed a half-yawn. A Shepherd patted me on the head with a mittened hand and tucked a collar around my neck, mistaking me for a stray pet.

I was invited into a yurt made of polar bear fur and constellations. I was freezing, so the host, a woman named *Hypna*, served me the **Heavy-Eyelid-Stew**. It is a thick, grey broth filled with dumplings that weigh about ten pounds each. The trick is to chew them; if you swallow them whole, you’ll sleep for a hundred years. I also tried a cup of *Sandman’s-Dandruff* (don’t ask, it’s a local delicacy consisting of cinnamon-sprinkled foam), which tasted like the moment before you drop your book on your face.

The wildlife is distinctly drowsy. There are **Pillow-Rocks**, boulders that soften when you lean on them, designed to catch travelers who stumble. The most dangerous creature is the **Alarm-Clock-Bird**, a frantic little thing with feathers of ringing brass and a beak like a hammer. It screeches exactly at 6:00 AM, a sound so jarring it can crack the sky. I had to throw my boot at one to silence it; the bird absorbed the leather and thanked me with a sneeze.

I found a message from the library carved into the headboard of the bed I’m currently sitting on. It read: *Rest is just the geography of the future.*

The wind here doesn't howl; it whispers lullabies in a language I almost understand. My limbs feel heavy, and the pen is slipping from my fingers. The darkness here is not black, but a warm, comforting dark blue.

I am heading *Sideways*, drifting along the currents of the *River-of-Reverie* toward the **Gallery-of-Unfinished-Symphonies**. I hear the music is loud enough to wake the dead.

Don't let the bed bugs bite.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
