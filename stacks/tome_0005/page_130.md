**Origin:** The Subterranean Duchy of *Mizaj-el-Ma* (The Water-Roots)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a rough, jagged square of semi-translucent obsidian, smoothed on one side. It smells aggressively of roasted cardamom, sulfur, and petrichor. It is slightly warm and pulses with a very faint vibration. If you lick the edge (I do not recommend this), it tastes like a 9-volt battery and the Mediterranean Sea. The text is etched in a silvery, glowing script that seems to drip downward like melting wax.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the bottom of a well that isn't a well, listening to the earth sing in a baritone voice that vibrates my sternum. The air is thick with steam and the scent of brewing tea. I have arrived in **Mizaj-el-Ma**, the Water-Roots. This is the place where the ocean decided to drill down to find the earth's core, got tired, and stopped to build a basement instead.

You recall I was heading *North*, following the **Pipeline-of-Petrified-Oil** upstream to find the fountain of youth? I followed the black river as it climbed out of the dunes, crawling up mountains of slag and shale. But as I reached the summit, the pipeline didn't end; it simply detached from gravity and poured straight down into a hole in the sky. I jumped in after it—my only other option was to go back and explain to the Dervishes why I still owed them three stories. I fell through the dark, missed the center of the earth, and splashed into a vast, subterranean cistern lit by glowing moss.

The geography here is inverted and hydraulic. **Mizaj-el-Ma** is built entirely inside the fossilized roots of a global olive tree that died before humanity learned to walk. The roots are hollow, some as wide as cathedrals, weaving through the bedrock like wormholes in an apple. We hang our homes on the inside curves of the root-walls like barnacles. Gravity is subjective here; it pulls toward the nearest massive body of water. So, "down" is always toward the central canals, which makes walking across a bridge a terrifying exercise in centrifugal force.

The locals are the **Root-Walkers**. They are small, stout people with skin like polished walnut and eyes that reflect the bioluminescence of the moss. They wear clothing spun from the fibers of dried hydrangeas, which changes color based on the humidity of the room. They communicate through **Hydro-Logic**, a language where they spit streams of water into a resonant basin to create words. A sharp *tsk* of water means "No," while a gurgling splash means "Perhaps." I tried to ask for a towel, but I accidentally swallowed a mouthful of the language-goo and spent the next hour agreeing with everything everyone said.

I am currently resting in *The-Pulmonary-Inn*, a tavern carved into the hollow of a giant, fossilized lung-fish. The walls are lined with dripping stalactites that play music when the wind blows through the gills. The currency here is **Bubbles**. To pay for my room, I had to blow a perfect, iridescent sphere of methane gas that contained a tiny, trapped flower. The innkeeper popped it with a needle, sniffed the gas, and sighed happily. "Ah, the scent of anticipation," he said. "Expensive, but worth it."

I am drinking a cup of **Pressure-Brew-Qahwa**. It is coffee brewed at three hundred atmospheres deep in the earth's crust. It is so concentrated it’s served in a thimble. It tastes like electricity, dark chocolate, and a sudden realization of your own mortality. It wakes you up by making your hair stand on end. For dinner, I am having **Couscous-of-Whispers**. The grains are infused with gossip and secrets. As you eat them, you hear snippets of conversations from all over the world. I heard a stockbroker in New York confessing his love for a pastry chef in Paris while chewing a mouthful of mint and lamb.

The wildlife is adapted to the dark and the damp. I see **Lamprey-Eels** that don't swim; they crawl up the vertical roots using tiny, hooked claws. They generate their own light to lure insects. The most dangerous creature is the **Silt-Sphinx**. It is a large, winged cat made entirely of mud and sediment. It guards the deepest aquifers. If you want a drink, you must answer its riddle. I failed its question—"What is heavier: a pound of lead or a pound of silence?"—and was nearly buried in a mudslide until I bribed it with a piece of my jerky.

I found a message from the library written in the condensation on the ceiling. It was dripping and wet. It read: *Still waters run deep, but boiling waters run away.*

The steam is rising, and the canals are beginning to bubble. It is time to move on.

I am heading *Down*, but faster this time. I am taking the **Aquifer-Express**, a high-pressure water slide that allegedly shoots you through the mantle of the planet until you pop out on the other side of the world. I hope to resurface in a place where the ground stays still.

Mind the gap.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
