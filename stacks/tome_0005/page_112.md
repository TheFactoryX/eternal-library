**Origin:** The Interstitial Foyer of *A-nty-Real* (The Space Between the Wall and the Wallpaper)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a thick, rough swatch of patterned wallpaper, peeled directly from a wall that exists in a timeline you haven't lived yet. The pattern is a chaotic, repeating floral design of blue hydrangeas and yellow birds, but the birds appear to be screaming. The back is sticky with dried paste, smelling of wet dust, old plaster, and the metallic tang of ozone. The ink is written in a deep crimson that looks suspiciously like wet paint.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while squeezed into a gap that is technically only two millimeters wide. I have arrived in **A-nty-Real**, the Interstitial Foyer. This is the place where the background noise goes when it isn't being used, and where the wall behind the wallpaper holds up the sky.

You recall I was heading *In*, shrinking down to enter the **World-in-a-Jar**? I swallowed the bread-crumb of shrinking I bought from a witch in the **Orbiting-Library**, and indeed, I began to shrink. But I shrank too fast. I bypassed the molecular level and slipped right between the atoms of reality. I fell out of the "Is" and landed in the "Isn't quite." I slipped between the plaster and the paper, and here I am.

The geography here is claustrophobic and domestic. Imagine the entire world is a single room in a house that is infinitely large. The "ground" is rough, grey plaster, scored with the claw marks of builders who existed before time began. The "sky" is the underside of the wallpaper—a vast, scrolling landscape of dimpled vinyl and repeating patterns. We live in the negative space. If you look up, you can see the ghostly outlines of the room "outside"—muffled shapes of furniture moving like thunderstorms, and conversations that sound like ocean waves crashing miles away.

The locals are the **Gap-Walkers**. They are strange, flattened beings, thin as paper cuts, with eyes on the sides of their heads to watch both layers of reality simultaneously. They wear clothes made of **Lint-of-Forgotten-Dust-Bunnies** spun into thread. They communicate through **Rustle-Talk**. They do not speak; they shuffle their feet against the plaster to create vibrations. A soft *swish-swish* means "Safe," while a loud *tear* means "The Decorator is coming." I tried to ask for directions, but my feet made a squeaking sound, and a Gap-Walker immediately offered me a roll of masking tape. Apparently, that means "I am peeling."

I am currently resting in *The Corner-Pocket*, a hostel built into the architectural shadow where two corners meet but don't quite touch. The rooms are alcoves tucked behind loose sections of paste. The currency here is **Silence**. To pay, I had to give the innkeeper a secret I have never told anyone. He weighed it in his hand, found it heavy enough, and handed me a blanket made of shredded insulation.

I am drinking a glass of **Solvent-of-Stagnation**. It is clear, pungent turpentine harvested from the blisters in the paint. It clears the sinuses and makes your head feel light, airy, and detached from your body. For dinner, I am having **Stew-of-Scrapings**. It is a thick, lumpy paste made of plaster dust, dead skin cells, and dried blue paint. It tastes like "home"—specifically, the feeling of being home sick from school and staring at a wall for six hours. It is strangely comforting, served with **Crackers-of-Adhesive**, which are so chewy they glue your mouth shut, forcing you to contemplate your thoughts before speaking.

The wildlife is pestilential but beautiful. I see **Silverfish-of-Entropy**. They are ancient, armored insects that swim through the solid plaster like water, eating the cracks in causality. They glisten like mercury. But the true lords of this realm are the **Wall-Mites**. They are microscopic architects that build bridges of dust to cross the gaps between studs.

I found a message from the library scribbled in pencil on the bare plaster behind a loose corner of hydrangeas. It read: *The pattern repeats until you find the edge and tear it down.*

The ceiling—the wallpaper—is bulging. The vibrations from "outside" are getting stronger. I think the room is being redecorated. That usually means a flood of toxic paint or a thunderstorm of steam-strippers.

I am heading *Through*, piercing the membrane of the plaster to enter the **Cavity-of-Echoes**. I hear if you shout into the hollow space inside a wall, the sound never dies; it just bounces until it becomes a voice of its own.

Don't believe the pattern.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
