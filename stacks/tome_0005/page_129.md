**Origin:** The Moving Sands of *Qasr-el-Dhoub* (The Fortress of Dust)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy, gritty square of fired terracotta clay, rough to the touch and smelling intensely of saffron, burnt cinnamon, and the dry static of a sandstorm. It is warm, as if it has been baking in the sun. The text is etched into the clay in a flowing, gold-leaf script that catches the light. If you hold the postcard to your ear, you can hear a faint, rhythmic shuffling sound, like a million grains of sand marching in unison.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the shade of a wind-carved archway, trying to keep the sand out of my inkwell. The heat here is a physical weight, pressing down on my shoulders like a heavy wool blanket. I have arrived in **Qasr-el-Dhoub**, the Fortress of Dust. This is the place where the desert realized that being a vast emptiness was inefficient, and decided to organize itself into a city that is constantly unpacking and repacking.

You recall I was heading *West*, intending to surf the **Railroad-of-Rain** back to earth? Well, the atmospheric river I was riding hit a patch of dry air—literally dry, a sponge of absolute aridity—and evaporated instantly. My wax raft dissolved into mist, and I plummeted. I fell for a long time, expecting to hit the ocean or the hard earth. Instead, I hit a dune that felt like a mattress. I landed in **Qasr-el-Dhoub**, a metropolis built entirely of shifting particles.

The geography here is fluid and architectural. The ground is not solid; it is a slow-moving conveyor belt of silica. The "buildings" are towers of compacted sand held together by nothing but vibration and willpower. The locals adjust the city walls daily. If a neighborhood becomes too noisy, the sand loses cohesion, and the district slides down the dune to the slums. The streets are paved with crushed rubies and emeralds that glint in the sun, making the ground look like a galaxy trapped under a magnifying glass.

The locals are the **Dune-Dervishes**. They are tall, slender beings wrapped head-to-toe in indigo and white silks that flutter constantly. They wear goggles made of polished obsidian to protect against the "Sand-Glare." They communicate through **Zephyr-Writing**, a complex sign language involving sweeping arm movements that manipulate the air currents to carry specific scents. A scent of roasted cumin means "Welcome," while a whiff of sulfur means "Danger." I tried to compliment a merchant’s rug, but I gestured too broadly and accidentally created a small dust devil that knocked over his display of dried scorpions. He didn't speak; he just frowned, and the sand beneath my boots suddenly turned to quicksand, trapping me ankle-deep until I apologized.

I am currently resting in *The-Mirage-Hotel*, an inn that doesn't always exist. You have to check in when the hotel is "in phase" with reality. The door is only solid for exactly three minutes at noon. I made it inside just as the walls flickered and turned translucent. The furniture is made of solidified light. The currency here is **Stories**. To pay for my room, I had to tell the concierge a tale he hadn't heard before. I recited the story of the *Timbre-Tribes* from the frozen north. He was fascinated by the concept of "silence."

I am drinking a glass of **Bourbon-on-the-Rocks**. It is not a drink; it is a shot of distilled time served on a literal piece of the orbiting moon. The "rocks" are cold, grey meteorites. The drink is smoky, and as it melts, you remember the future. For dinner, I am having **Lamb-of-Procrastination**. It is a tough, chewy meat because the sheep was supposed to be slaughtered three years ago but kept putting it off. It has a gamy, anxious flavor that pairs well with the flatbread.

The wildlife is geological. I see **Sand-Skates**. They are flat, diamond-shaped fish that swim through the dunes as if they were water. They filter gold dust from the silica. The most dangerous creature is the **Mummy-Moth**. It is an insect with wings made of ancient papyrus. It doesn't bite; it lands on you and unfolds a history of a tragedy that hasn't happened to you yet, making you cry for days. I had to swat one away with a rolled-up newspaper, but I felt guilty for destroying a draft of my own eulogy.

I found a message from the library written in the grit on my windowsill. It was abrasive but clear. It read: *The hourglass runs both ways, depending on how you turn it.*

The wind is picking up, and the "Call to Prayer" is beginning. It is not sung with voices; the Dervishes play the wind like flute, creating a sound that vibrates in your chest.

I am heading *North*, following the **Pipeline-of-Petrified-Oil**. It is a river of black sludge that flows uphill, defying gravity. I hear that at the source, there is a fountain of youth that is actually just a mirror.

Keep your head above the dunes.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
