**Origin:** The Interstitial Flats of *Param-Para* (The Space Between Heartbeats)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is made of a thick, velvety silence. It has no physical weight, but it presses against your hand like a held breath. The texture is like touching a muscle. The ink is a deep, arterial blue that seems to pulsate faintly. The card smells of ozone and that specific metallic taste you get on your tongue right before a sneeze.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the gap between the tick and the tock. I have arrived in **Param-Para**, the Interstitial Flats. This is the place where the universe pauses to gather its thoughts between moments. It is not a place that exists *in* time, but rather *around* it—the cushion of silence that prevents seconds from grinding against each other.

You recall I was heading *To the Hanging Gardens of Babylon-Next-Door*? I took a wrong turn at a dusty second and fell through a fracture in causality. Instead of ancient gardens, I landed here, in a landscape of suspended animation. The sun is frozen at the exact angle of sunset—golden hour eternal—casting long, unmoving shadows that look like black holes cut out of the fabric of reality.

The geography is jarring and static. The ground is comprised of **Transition-Dust**, a gray powder made of eroded possibilities. Mountains here do not rise; they are suspended halfway up, having decided to ascend but not yet committed to the motion. I am currently standing at the **Edge of the Inhale**, a massive cliff where the wind stops blowing inward, creating a perpetual vacuum that pulls at your clothes. To the north lies the **Frozen River of Consequence**, a stream of water that has shattered into millions of liquid crystals because it was flowing too fast when time paused.

The locals are the **Interim-Folk**. They are tall, stretched figures who look like blurred photographs of people. They move with a slow, liquid grace, as if underwater. They wear clothing woven from **Spun-Now**, a fabric that changes color the moment you look away from it. They speak a language called **Pause-Cadence**. It consists of humming tones and meaningful silences. They do not say "Hello"; they simply hold a silence that conveys *I acknowledge your presence in this specific instance of eternity.* To say "Goodbye," they make a sound like a clock unwinding: *“Vzzzt-tick.”*

They are strict observers of **The Pause**. I tried to rush a waiter because I was thirsty, and he simply ceased to exist until I calmed down. He reappeared exactly five minutes later, looking none the worse for wear, though my tea was cold. He explained that speed is a violation of the local geometry.

I am dining on **Vacuum-Packed Potatoes**. They are tubers grown in the absence of air, so they are incredibly dense and heavy. One bite fills you for a week. For the main course, I am having **Blink-of-an-Eye Stew**. It is a soup made from small, tasty animals that happen to wander into the stasis field at the wrong moment. It tastes like chicken, but with a texture of cold jelly. For dessert, I am having **Echo-Berries**. They are fruits that taste like whatever you were thinking about three seconds ago. I had strawberry, but my neighbor tasted regret.

The wildlife is… momentary. The **Split-Second Squirrels** are pests here. They move so fast they exist in multiple places at once. If you try to shoo one away, you are just batting at the memory of where it used to be. Then there are the **Probability-Cats**. They are feline shapes that exist in a state of flux, looking like a cat, a smudge, and a dust devil simultaneously. If you observe them directly, they turn into a normal, sleeping housecat. I spent ten minutes watching one sleep on a fence that wasn't attached to anything.

I found a message from the library written on a flag that was frozen mid-flap. It read: *To exist is to be late for the next moment.*

The light is flickering, which means time is about to lurch forward again. I can feel the pressure building in my ears. The world is about to sneeze.

I am heading *To the Shoals of the Anthropocene*. I hear it is an ocean made entirely of plastic and discarded memories, located in a future that hasn't quite decided if it's a dystopia or a paradise. I hope I remember my sunscreen.

Hold your breath.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
