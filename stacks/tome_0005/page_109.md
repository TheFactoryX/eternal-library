**Origin:** The Vertical Oasis of *Zerzura-Azla* (The Hanging Gardens of the Doldrums)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of coarse, hand-woven camel hair, stiffened with desert starch. It smells intensely of saffron, dried lime, and the hot wind of a simoom. The ink is shimmering silver dust, mixed with the crushed wings of a scarab beetle. It scratches your fingers if you rub it the wrong way.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while dangling my legs over the edge of a terrace made of woven reeds. I have arrived in **Zerzura-Azla**, the Vertical Oasis. This is the place where the rain forgets to fall down and chooses, instead, to fall up.

You recall I was heading *Out*, breaching the surface of the **World-Ice** to reach the **Orbiting-Library**? I strapped my magnets to my boots and kicked off the glacier, aiming for the stars. But gravity here is negotiable, and I hit a layer of hot air that acted like a ceiling. I floated sideways, carried by a wind that tasted like cardamom, until I bumped against the trunk of a tree the size of a planet. I climbed down the bark, past clouds and strata of birds, until I reached the canopy.

The geography here is arboreal and arid. Imagine a Sahara turned inside out and stretched vertically. The "ground" is a dizzying expanse of branches—roads of mahogany and rivers of sap—while the "sky" is a kilometer of empty air above, and a kilometer of deep shadows below. The desert doesn't exist here; the *drought* does. The sand doesn't cover the floor; it falls from the sky like snow. We have to shake the sand out of our turbans every hour, or the weight of it will pull us down into the abyss.

The locals are the **Arboreal-Bedu**. They are lean, leonine people who can leap twenty feet between branches without rattling a single leaf. They wear layers of silk and cotton in patterns that mimic the bark of the trees, effectively camouflaging them until they smile—their teeth are the brightest white things in the forest. They communicate through **Zephyr-Whistling**. They don't speak words; they manipulate the wind passing through their teeth. A trill means "Welcome," while a low, hollow hoot means "The sand-storm is coming." I tried to order tea, but I accidentally blew a sharp whistle, and the waiter immediately brought me a scorpion. Apparently, that means "Challenge accepted."

I am currently resting in *The Knot-Hole*, a caravanserai built inside the hollow of a massive fig tree. The rooms are cubbies carved into the wood, and the elevators are pulley-systems operated by trained beetles. The currency here is **Time-in-the-Shade**. To pay, I had to give the innkeeper an hour of cool darkness I had saved from a winter in London years ago. He weighed it in his palm, nodded, and gave me the key.

I am drinking a cup of **Sand-Bourbon**. It is distilled from cactus juice filtered through dune sand. It is gritty and warm, leaving a coating of gold dust on the tongue. It makes you feel heavy and grounded, which is a necessity here. For dinner, I am having **Tagine-of-Lost-Voices**. It is a slow-cooked stew made with lamb, apricots, and arguments that were never resolved. The meat is tender, but the spices bite back. It is served with **Couscous-of-Clouds**, which is so light it floats off the plate if you don't pin it down with a fork.

The wildlife is adapted to the verticality. I see **Geckos-of-Glass** climbing the sheer trunks. They are transparent, except for their beating hearts, which look like rubies trapped in crystal. But the true masters are the **Carpet-Spiders**. They do not spin webs; they weave rugs of silk that hang between the branches. I bought one that changes color based on the temperature. Currently, it is a frantic, panicked orange.

I found a message from the library written on a fallen leaf. It read: *The roots go deep, but the fruit is always at the edge.*

The upward rain is starting again—droplets of water defying gravity, flying toward the moon. The **Arboreal-Bedu** are unfurling their silk tarps to catch the moisture.

I am heading *Down*, past the roots, into the **Under-Myrrh**. I hear the trees here tap into a reservoir of ancient, fossilized sap, and I want to see if it is true that it can heal a broken heart.

Don't look down.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
