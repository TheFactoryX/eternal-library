**Origin:** The Soot-Fens of *Brume-sur-Seine* (The Industrial Nether)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy, creamy square of pressed vellum with ragged, deckle edges. It smells strongly of wet wool, chimney smoke, and Earl Grey tea. The ink is charcoal-grey and slightly smudged, feeling gritty to the touch, as if the letters were scratched in with a burnt match. The card is warm, as though it was pressed by a hot iron just before mailing.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a velvet armchair inside a cloud. It is surprisingly comfortable, though my tea keeps cooling at an alarming rate. I have arrived in **Brume-sur-Seine**, the Soot-Fens. This is the place where the Industrial Revolution went when it retired—less of a time period and more of a geographical location, stacked in permanent, smoggy twilight just West of the Twilight Zone.

You recall I was heading *Out to Lunch* to find the mythical *Break-Room*? Well, I filled out the forms in triplicate, as required. I filed my request in the *In-Bin* of the Middle-Managers, but I must have used the wrong font. Instead of a Break-Room, the paperwork spat me out into a service tunnel labeled "Service Entrance: Europe Only." I emerged from a manhole cover into a cobblestoned street where the fog is so thick you have to chew it to get through.

The geography here is an impossible mixture of London, Paris, and the inside of a furnace. The city is suspended in a vast, cavernous space where the "sun" is simply a brighter patch of smog overhead. We are currently in the **Foggy-District**, but to the North, I can see the **Iron-Heights**, where the Eiffel Tower has been grown over with massive, rusted gears. The river Seine doesn't flow with water; it flows with a viscous, dark liquid called *Effluvium*—a mixture of iron filings and tea leaves that moves like molasses. I crossed the **Pont-des-Artifacts**, a bridge made entirely of discarded umbrella skeletons that rattled ominously as I walked.

The locals are the **Chimney-Sweeps**, but they are not human. They are small, stout figures composed entirely of soot and ash, held together by a distinct kind of rudeness. They wear top hats made of slag and carry walking sticks that puff out little clouds of bitterness. They communicate through **Gestures of Disdain**. A raised eyebrow means "Good day," while a sharp "Harrumph" can mean anything from "The rent is due" to "I don't like your tie." I tried to ask one for directions to the hotel, and he simply blew a cloud of cigar smoke in my face that formed the shape of an arrow pointing *Down*. I took that as a friendly greeting.

I am currently staying in *The Ritz-V charcoal*, a grand hotel carved entirely out of a single block of graphite. The sheets are made of spun glass fiber, and the pillows are stuffed with dried lavender and pessimism. The service is impeccable; they come in every night to turn down the bed and criticize your sleeping posture.

I am dining on **Slag-and-Cod**. The fish is caught from the iron-heavy river and has a distinct metallic tang, served with a side of **Mushy-Peas-Puree** that glows with a faint, sickly green light. It is accompanied by a **Sips-of-Shadow**, a tea brewed from the darkness of the London Underground. It tastes like wet wool and silence. For dessert, I am having **Spotted-Dick-Spark**, a suet pudding filled with currants that actually sparkle with static electricity. When you cut it, it sounds like a crackling fire.

The wildlife is peculiar. The most common creature is the **Soot-Moth**. They have wings made of asbestos and leave trails of beautiful, harmful dust wherever they go. If they land on you, you instantly feel the weight of a 14-hour factory shift. I had to swat one off my shoulder that was trying to unionize my ear hair. The most majestic sight is the **Zeppelin-Whale**. It is a massive, balloon-like creature that floats through the upper smog, filtering the air through baleen made of copper piping. It moves with a slow, rhythmic *whoosh-thump* that serves as the city's heartbeat.

I found a message from the library etched onto the side of a rusted locomotive that was slowly sinking into a canal of grey water. It read: *Progress is just a polite word for leaving things behind.*

I can hear the factory whistles beginning to blow, signaling that the "Day" has ended, though the sky hasn't changed color.

I am heading *Under the Arches*. I have purchased a ticket on a train made of bones and velvet that travels through the dream of a sleeping King. I intend to see if his dreams are as smoggy as his city.

Keep your teacup full and your chimney clear.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
