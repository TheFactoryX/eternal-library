**Origin:** The Linear Chronology of *La-Costa-Del-Segno* (The City of Dried Ink)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy, irregular rectangle of vellum with the texture of human skin. It smells overpoweringly of old paper, turpentine, and vinegar. When you tilt it, the handwriting seems to smudge, and the ink stains your fingertips like wet soot. If you hold it to your ear, you can hear the sound of a scratching quill.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a sidewalk café that exists only in the margin of a history book. I have arrived in **La-Costa-Del-Segno**, the Linear Chronology. This is the Mediterranean that exists in the split second after a pen touches paper but before the ink dries—a place where the past is not remembered, but is currently being written.

You recall I was diving into the **Deep-Trench-of-Unsent-Letters**? I sank rapidly through layers of damp parchment and watermarked regrets. The pressure of unspoken words was crushing, but I managed to grab hold of a passing ellipsis (...) and slid down the curvature of a question mark into this dry, dust-filled harbor.

The geography here is remarkably flat, yet infinitely detailed. The city is a series of terraced hills rising sharply from a sea of white correction fluid. The buildings are constructed from tightly rolled scrolls of vellum and dried inkwells. The streets are paved with cobblestones made of compressed semicolons; walking on them makes the narrative of your day stutter and pause. It is exhausting to walk here; you feel as though you are constantly being edited.

The locals are the **Revisionists**, tall, gaunt figures with skin the color of cream and eyes like black ink spots. They wear clothes made of red tape and shifting margins. They speak in **Footnotes**, a language that never speaks directly but always clarifies, critiques, or contradicts what was just said. To ask, "How are you?", they say, "*See Appendix C regarding the weather.*" To answer, "I am well," one must cough and tap their nose three times.

I attempted to order a coffee, but the menu is not a list of items, but a list of plot twists. I ordered a **Plot-Arm-Mocca**, a drink so bitter it shocks you into action. It arrived boiling hot, served in a cup that has a handle on the inside. Drinking it requires a contortion of logic; it tastes like realization and feels like a sudden plot twist in the pit of your stomach.

For dinner, I am eating a **Pasta-Al-Fresco-Climax**. The noodles are made of linguine strips of raw narrative, pliable and chewy. The sauce is a rich, crimson *Bolognese-of-Conflict*, slow-cooked over the fires of historical feuds. It is spiced with **Red-Herrings**—dried fish that taste like distraction. But be careful: if you eat the herring, you forget what you were talking about mid-sentence. I found a bay leaf in my bowl that was actually a handwrittennote from the author; it read, *Delete this.*

The wildlife is… predatory. There are **Cuttlefish-of-Censorship** that swim through the air, hunting for grammatical errors. They spray a cloud of white ink to erase anything they deem inappropriate. But the most haunting creatures are the **Carrier-Pigeons-of-Lost-Causes**, birds made entirely of crumpled paper that fly in frantic, erratic loops. They carry messages no one wants to hear. One landed on my table today and unfolded into a rejection letter from a lover I haven't met yet.

The lighting here is dramatic, always caught in a "Golden Hour" that never fades, casting long, noir-style shadows that stretch around corners. It is a city of secrets, erasures, and second drafts.

I found a message from the library stamped in the wax seal of a wine bottle. It read: *To edit is to kill, but to publish is to resurrect.*

The smell of turpentine is giving me a headache. My own hands are beginning to look like ink blots. I feel the urge to correct the spelling of the clouds.

I am heading *North-West*, crossing the **Paper-Mountains-of-Bureaucracy** toward the **Clockwork-Principality-of-Schweiz**. I hear the trains there run on atomic time and are never, ever late.

Mind your grammar.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
