**Origin:** The Waiting Room of *Oubliette-Station* (The Limbo of Departures)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a flimsy rectangle of perforated ticket stock, grey and slightly warm to the touch, as if it has been sitting in a pocket for a century. It smells strongly of ozone and stale coffee. The ink is standard ballpoint blue, but the text seems to vibrate when you look directly at it. If you hold it up to the light, a watermark reads: *VOID BEFORE TRAVEL*.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a bench that upholsters itself in whatever fabric you are most afraid of itching. I have arrived in **Oubliette-Station**, the Limbo of Departures. This is the place that exists in the notification that says *Your flight has been delayed*, or the pregnant pause in a conversation when you realize you have forgotten someone’s name. It is an airport that goes nowhere, staffed by people who are waiting for you to leave so they can go home.

You recall I was heading *Vertical* up the **Staircase-of-Glass** toward the **Cloud-Fortress-of-Unkept-Promises**? The climb was arduous; the glass was slippery with the condensation of broken vows. I was halfway to the summit, lungs burning, when I encountered a **Cloud-Locker**, a sentient vaporous entity that guards the gates of the fortress. It demanded a password (which I didn't have) or a toll (which I couldn't pay). I offered it a story, but it refused, saying it was already full of fiction. It simply pushed me through a pressure door in the sky, and I tumbled into the arrival hall of this transit hub.

The geography here is bureaucratic and infinite. The Departures board lists destinations that don't exist on any map: *The Yesterday I Wasted*, *The Job I Didn't Take*, *The Version of Me That Dies Young*. The gates are numbered with negative integers and irregular fractions. The floor is tiled in a pattern of swirling grey linoleum that hurts your eyes if you try to follow the lines. The air is recycled and tastes like the bottom of a handbag.

The locals are the **Sans-Papiers**, a ghostly workforce of travelers who missed their connections decades ago and simply forgot to stop waiting. They are semi-translucent figures wearing clothes from every era of travel history—pancake hats from the 20s, shoulder pads from the 80s. They push luggage carts filled with intangible burdens: regret, anxiety, a heavy suitcase packed with nothing but "What Ifs." Their language, * Lingua-Delay*, is a mixture of airport announcements and sighs. They greet each other by saying, *"The WiFi is down,"* which means, *"I share your suffering."*

I attempted to buy a newspaper at a kiosk run by a sentient baggage handler made entirely of lost luggage tags. He didn't speak, just pointed to a price tag that fluctuated based on my heart rate.

I am currently killing time in the **Terminal-of-In-Between**, sitting next to a fountain that flows with **Coffee-of-the-Dead-End**. It is thick, black, and tastes like exhaustion. Drinking it makes you impervious to sleep for exactly three days, but you gain the ability to see the fatigue of others as a grey aura.

For dinner, I am eating a lukewarm **Pre-Packaged-Blindness**, a sandwich wrapped in plastic that refuses to be opened unless you answer a riddle. The bread is styrofoam-like, the filling is a jelly made of *Hesitation*, and it is incredibly dry. It requires three cups of the coffee just to swallow. To pass the time, I am drinking a **Vodka-of-Cancelled-Plans**, a clear spirit that makes you feel incredibly popular for an hour, then incredibly lonely.

The wildlife is peculiar to the terminal. There are **Carry-On-Roaches** that scuttle under the seats, carrying tinySuitcases. If you step on them, they burst into a cloud of frequent flyer miles. But the true pest is the **Announcement-Bird**, a species of pigeon that mimics the sound of a security alert. Every time it coos, the entire terminal panic-checks their pockets, thinking they have lost their passports. I saw one steal a cracker right out of a sleeping businessman’s hand while squawking, *"Unattended baggage will be destroyed."*

I found a message from the library on a luggage tag attached to a suitcase circling the carousel. It has been circling since 1974. The tag read: *Arrival is just the beginning of another wait.*

The fluorescence here is headache-inducing. I can feel my sense of time dissolving. I have checked my watch six times, and the hands have moved backward twice. I need to leave before I become a Sans-Papiers myself, destined to push a cart of excuses for eternity.

I am heading *Through the Barrier*, following a family of screaming tourists toward the **Departure Gate of Sudden Awakenings**. I hear the flight is bumpy, but the landing is always a shock.

Don't wait up.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
