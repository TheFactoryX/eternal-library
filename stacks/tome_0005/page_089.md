**Origin:** The Mycelial Grid of *Canopy-Nod* (The City of False Vertigo)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a piece of living birch bark, still slightly fuzzy to the touch. It smells aggressively of pine needles, gasoline, and antiseptic. If you hold it horizontally, the wood grain seems to flow uphill. Small, impossible mushrooms sprout from the corners when you aren't looking directly at them.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while suspended upside down from a buoy that floats in the sky. I have arrived in **Canopy-Nod**, a settlement that exists in the space between the crowns of the super-redwoods. This is the Pacific Northwest you might glimpse in your peripheral vision while driving a foggy highway—a place where the boundaries between the forest, the future, and a dream dissolve into a humid grey mist.

You recall I was rafting down the **River-of-All-Tears** toward the **Coral-Cathedral**? The river, unfortunately, evaporated. The water hit a pocket of super-heated air and turned into a thick, white steam that lifted me—raft and all—into the upper atmosphere. I drifted for days, cooling and condensing, until I snagged my pack on a branch of a *Sequoia-Sempervirens-Infinet*. I climbed down (which looked like climbing up) into this city.

The geography here is disorienting. The ground is three hundred feet below, obscured by a perpetual layer of cloud that glows with a bioluminescent phosphorescence. The city is not built on the land, but *stitched* into the trees. Walkways are suspension bridges made of woven **Sinew-of-Lost-Hikers**, and the houses are pods grown from the wood itself, glowing with the soft amber light of wood fires. The gravity here is negotiable; it pulls gently toward the nearest massive trunk, allowing you to walk up the sides of the trees without falling.

The locals are the **Vertigo-Kind**, quiet folk who wear goggles made of polished quartz to see through the fog. They have wide, grippy feet and move with a slow, deliberate swiftness. They speak **Root-Whisper**, a language that involves tapping on the bark of the trees. To say "Welcome," they knock three times: *Thrum-thrum... pause... thrum.* The trees carry the vibration for miles, alerting the next settlement that a stranger has arrived.

I attempted to buy a coat of woven spider-silk to keep the damp out, but the currency here is **Senses**. I had to trade the merchant my sense of taste for a week. It was a fair trade; the food here is mostly conceptual anyway. Now, when I eat, I only experience texture and temperature.

I am currently sitting in a cafe called *The Drunken Log*. I am drinking a cup of **Hydraulic-Press-Coffee**. It is brewed under five tons of pressure, forcing the flavor out of the bean and into the fourth dimension. It tastes like electricity and regret. It wakes you up by vibrating your skeleton. The locals call it "The Jump-Starter."

For dinner, I had a **Platter-of-Data-Clams**. These are clams harvested from the information streams that run through the roots. The shells are made of silicon and old motherboards. When you pry them open, instead of meat, you find a small, glowing scroll of text—gossip, secrets, or lost passwords from the world below. I ate a particularly juicy rumor about a politician in Oregon. It tasted like ozone and salty truth.

The wildlife is… adapted. There are **Sasquatch-Node-Runners**, massive, hairy primates that act as the internet service providers here. They scream binary code at each other across the valleys. But the most beautiful things are the **Owls-of-Unfinished-Designs**. They have no feathers, only smooth, white porcelain skin. Their heads rotate 360 degrees continuously, scanning for bugs in the reality code. If you look at one, you suddenly remember a song you haven't heard in twenty years.

I found a message from the library carved into the trunk of a massive hollow tree. It read: *The tallest branch is just a root looking for a different sky.*

The fog is rolling in thick now, turning the walkways into grey ribbons suspended in nothing. The Vertigo-Kind are closing their shutters. My coffee is vibrating so hard I have to hold the cup with both hands.

I am heading *South-West*, gliding on the **Trade-Winds-of-Nostalgia** toward the **Neon-Canals-of-Vegas-Prime**. I hear the lights there burn bright enough to erase your shadow.

Don't look down.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
