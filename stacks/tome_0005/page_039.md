**Origin:** The Papal-Plumbing of *Aqua-Scriptura* (The Sub-Basement of the World)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy, irregular slab of rough-hewn papyrus that is perpetually damp. The ink is not dry; it is a viscous, dark black substance that smells faintly of squid ink and very old wine. If you hold the card to your ear, you can hear a faint, rhythmic rushing sound, like water in a distant pipe.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the lip of a volcanic vent, six miles down, where the water is hot enough to melt lead but moves with the sluggishness of cold syrup. I have arrived in **Aqua-Scriptura**, the sunken library where the ocean is the ink and the earth is the pen.

You recall I was heading *Up*, climbing the *Stairway-of-Stars* to find the *Origami-Satellite*? I ascended until the air turned thin and the stars looked like wet paint, but I found the paper moon folded shut, origami-style, blocking the path. With the sky inaccessible, I looked for the only other direction that promised infinity. I turned my gaze to the trenches. I dove off the edge of the atmosphere, fell through the clouds, and pierced the surface of the ocean. I didn't stop falling until I hit the obsidian floor of the *Mariana-Inverse*, a place where the pressure is so high it forces thoughts out of your head and into the water around you.

The geography here is fluid and literate. I am currently in *Aqua-Scriptura*, a civilization built entirely inside the gill-slits of a dormant, continent-sized leviathan known as the *Old-Reader*. The city is constructed of coral that has naturally grown into the shape of bookshelves, and the streets are currents of super-heated water that carry the citizens where they need to go. The buildings do not have roofs; they have "surface tension," holding the crushing weight of the ocean back in a delicate bubble of habitable space.

The locals are the **Literate-Lampreys**, eel-like beings with translucent skin that reveals organs filled with glowing bio-luminescent text. They communicate by changing the color and pattern of their skin, flashing entire novels to one another in the blink of an eye. I met a historian named *Citation-Current* who was busy scrubbing algae off a coral obelisk. "The ocean tries to erase everything," he bubbled, vibrating his fins to produce the words. "We are the editors. We carve the truth into the stone before the sediment buries it."

Language here is fluid, quite literally. They speak *Hydro-Calligraphy*, a dialect where grammar is determined by water temperature and salinity. To say "Hello," you must release a bubble of air that forms a perfect sphere; to say "Goodbye," you release a burst of ink that creates a chaotic cloud. I tried to compliment the architecture, but my salinity was off, and I accidentally insulted the structural integrity of the reef. The coral blushed a deep crimson, and I had to apologize by scrubbing the floor with a sponge made of dried sea-sponge.

Dining here is a visceral experience. I visited a bistro situated on the edge of a hydrothermal vent. I ordered the **Calamari-Calculus**, a dish of squid sliced so thin the rings represent mathematical variables. When you eat it, you can literally taste the numbers. It was spicy, bitter, and tasted faintly of algebra. I also tried the **Sea-Weed-Syntax**, a salad of kelp that wraps around your tongue and whispers dictionary definitions as you chew. To drink, I had a **glass of heavy water**, so dense with deuterium that I had to eat it with a fork. It tasted like velvet and made my voice drop three octaves.

The wildlife is predatory and poetic. There are **Shark-Scribes**, great predators with teeth made of sharpened quartz that swim through the libraries, eating any errors they find in the coral shelves. I saw a **Whale-White-Out**, a massive creature that swims through the deepest trenches, erasing unwanted history with a blast of pressurized white liquid.

I found a message from the library etched into a clam shell. It read: *The deep remembers what the surface forgets. Pressure creates diamonds, and pearls, and silence.*

I can feel the pressure starting to compress my bones into diamonds, and my thoughts are beginning to leak out of my ears in blue bubbles. I need to find a place where the air is dry and the ground is solid.

I am heading *Inward*, shrinking past the atomic level. I hear there is a city on the nucleus of a carbon atom, a place of infinite energy and tiny, tiny streets. They call it the **Quantum-Capital**, and I intend to split the atom before I split the bill.

Don't hold your breath.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
