**Origin:** The Hydro-Venetian Archipelago of *Acqua-Plastica* (The Liquid Toy City)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is made of thick, translucent recycled plastic, the kind that usually constitutes a detergent bottle, sanded down to a milky smoothness. It feels light and hollow. The ink is a vibrant, unnatural cyan that smells like chlorine and melting sugarcane. If you tilt the card, the "water" in the image seems to flow against gravity.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a gondola that is currently dissolving, though I am assured it will reconstitute by the time we reach the dock. I have arrived in **Acqua-Plastica**, the Liquid Toy City. This is a Southern European dreamscape where the Mediterranean Sea has been replaced by a thick, syrupy substance known as **Vinyl-Water**—a brilliant, iridescent blue fluid that behaves exactly like the ocean, except it is slightly buoyant, warm to the touch, and smells of artificial grape and hot rubber.

You recall I was heading *To the Glass Canyons of New-York-Once*? I was walking through a rainy puddle in what I thought was Manhattan, but I slipped on a patch of oil and fell *through* the reflection. I surfaced here, bobbing in the harbor of a city that looks like Venice was reimagined by a toddler with a limitless supply of LEGOs and a heat gun.

The geography is impossible and brightly colored. The canals are filled with the Vinyl-Water, which ripples with a sound like crinkling wrapping paper. The buildings are not stone, but **Thermo-Formed Palazzos**—structures molded from brightly colored plastics, pastel pinks and blues that look soft to the touch but are harder than steel. The famous bridge here is the **Rialto-Matic**, a drawbridge made of flexible living hinge that snaps open and shut with a loud *click-clack* rather than a creak. I am currently staying in the **District of Injection**, where the streets are laid out like the sprues of a model kit, and you have to snap the roads apart to change your route.

The locals are the **Figurines**. They are humanoid, but they possess that distinct, shiny sheen of molded PVC. They move with a stiffness that suggests ball-jointed articulation. They do not have hair; they have sculpted plastic tufts that are impervious to the wind. They are permanently smiling, with painted-on eyes that stare, unblinking, at the sun. They wear clothes that are seemingly part of their bodies, painted on with thick, enamel strokes. They speak a language called **Click-Speak**. It consists of sharp consonants and the sound of plastic hitting plastic. They greeted me with *“Klik-klik-popp,”* which roughly translates to *"You are unpunched; please stay on the sprue."*

They are obsessed with **The Gloss**. Appearance is everything here. A dull surface is the greatest sin one can commit. I saw a local stop in the middle of the street to buff his nose with a cloth until it shone like a mirror.

I am dining on **Polymer-Pasta**. It is made from a biodegradable plastic dough that is extruded into shapes. I am having **Rigatoni-Ribbon**, noodles that are glossy and flexible, served with a sauce called **Adhesive-Marinara**—a spicy, sticky red paste that binds the noodles to your fork. For the main course, I am having **Sole-injection**. It is a fish that tastes vaguely like strawberries because it absorbs the flavor of the Vinyl-Water it swims in. It melts in your mouth, literally dissolving into a sweet liquid as you chew.

The wildlife is… manufactured. The **Lion-Dogs of Venice** are here, but they are not beasts. They are small, plastic_statues that come to life when the sun goes down, animating to patrol the alleyways. They make a sound like a squeaky toy when they bark. Then there are the **Carrier-Bag-Gulls**, birds made from old shopping bags that float on the thermals. They don’t flap their wings; they rustle. I tried to feed one a crust of bread, but it just tried to nest in my hair.

I found a message from the library floating in a bottle of Vinyl-Water. The bottle was sealed with a cap, not a cork, and the message inside read: *To survive the flood, become the boat.*

The sun is setting, and the city is glowing with an internal light, catching the sheen of a thousand plastic surfaces. It is beautiful, in a way that makes you worry about the expiration date.

I am heading *To the Loom of the Fates* (Trans-Dimensional Sector). I hear it is a place where destiny is literally woven into carpets, and if you trip, you change the ending of the book. I hope I remember my knitting needles.

Don't forget to smooth down the edges.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
