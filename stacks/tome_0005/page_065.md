**Origin:** The Canopy-City of *Verdura-Suspensa* (The Hanging Jungle)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a stiff, corrugated leaf of a Rubber Tree, reinforced with lignin and still slightly tacky with natural latex. It smells of heavy rain, black coffee, and crushed orchids. The ink is a deep, iridescent blue that stains the fingers like a bruise.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while hanging upside down by my ankles from a liana that is currently acting as a elevator. I have arrived in **Verdura-Suspensa**, the Hanging Jungle. It is a Central America that decided the ground was too dangerous and simply pulled itself up into the clouds by its own bootstraps.

You recall I was heading *North-North-West*, following the *Ley-Line-of-Luck* to the **Casino-of-Cumulonimbus**? I took a shortcut through a storm cloud and underestimated the velocity of the updraft. I was stripped of my luggage and my hat, plummeting upward until I collided with the underside of a continent. This place is geologically inverted; the mountains hang from the sky like stalactites, and the "floor" is a terrifyingly empty vista of blue sky and birds.

The geography here is aggressive and humid. Gravity is optional, provided you keep moving. The entire ecosystem is suspended from the floating bedrock of the *Sky-Shelf*. Waterfalls cascade *upward* into the clouds, and the rain falls from the abyss below, rising up to soak the canopy. You have to wear special gear—*Grip-Soles*—just to walk on the undersides of the branches. If you slip, you don't fall down; you "fall" into the sky, drifting forever unless you catch a passing cloud.

The locals are the **Arboreal-Acrobats**, short, nimble people with skin that resembles the bark of the kapok tree and prehensile toes that can crack a walnut. They treat the vertical world with casual indifference. They communicate through *Vine-Vibration*, a complex language of plucks, strums, and snaps transmitted through the tangled web of lianas connecting the trees. A conversation sounds like a jazz ensemble tuning up in the next room.

I attempted to ask for directions to the nearest market, but I plucked a C-sharp instead of a B-flat. According to a local named *Cecropia*, I inadvertently announced that I was a ripe mango looking for a wife. I was quickly surrounded by several enthusiastic suitors and a hungry monkey. I had to play a frantic crescendo to convince them I was bitter and out of season.

Dining here requires a head for heights and a strong stomach. I visited a cantina built inside the hollow of a giant floating bromeliad. I ordered the **Jaguar-Stew**, a spicy, savory dish that literally makes you growl while you eat it. The meat is tender, but the peppers have a delayed reaction—I sweated for an hour after the first bite. For dessert, I had **Cloud-Cotton**, a spun-sugar confection harvested from cumulus clouds that melts on the tongue, leaving the taste of altitude and electricity.

I also tried the local specialty, *Chiapas-Drift*, a coffee made from beans that have been roasted by passing dragonflies. It is strong enough to wake the dead and keep them awake for three days. After one cup, I could see into the ultraviolet spectrum and talk to spiders.

The wildlife is spectacular and predatory. There are **Quetzal-Snakes**, feathered serpents that glide through the air with silent wings, nesting in the hanging moss. They are beautiful, but their bite causes you to float away like a helium balloon. I also saw a troupe of **Howler-Monkeys**, but they don't howl; they scream backwards, sucking the sound out of the air so you are left in sudden, deafening silence.

I found a message from the library carved into the belly of a sloth that was crawling past (they move quite fast here, owing to the fear of falling into the void). It read: *Perspective is just a matter of holding on tight enough.*

The humidity is beginning to mildew my coat, and my arms are tired from swinging. The constant updraft is playing havoc with my hair. I long for solid ground, even if it is just the deck of a ship.

I am heading *Down*, riding the *Thermal-Vortex* to the **Archipelago-of-Ash**. They say the islands move when you blink, and the lava flows with wine.

Keep your feet on the ground.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
