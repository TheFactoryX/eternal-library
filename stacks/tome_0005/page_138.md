**Origin:** The Civic Geometry of *Cubicle-Strata* (The Corporate Abyss)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy, rectangular slab of grey particulateboard, smelling strongly of ozone and stale coffee. If you hold the card at an angle, a holographic watermark of a spinning clock appears.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a desk that exists in four different time zones simultaneously. It is disorienting, but the view is—well, it’s efficient. I have arrived in **Cubicle-Strata**, the Corporate Abyss. This is the place where North America went when it stopped being a frontier and started being a spreadsheet.

You recall I was heading *Across the Gradient* to the *Null-Point* to see the texture of silence? Well, I tried. But the bridge of dust motes was under construction. A bright orange sign held up by a floating hard hat redirected me onto the **Commuter-Vortex**, a slipstream that sucks up anyone wearing a tie and deposits them here. I fell through a hole in the logic of the universe and landed in a chair that spins three times before accepting your lumbar imprint.

The geography here is urban, recursive, and strictly regulated. The horizon is not a line; it is a vertical stack of floors. This is a city that has forgotten to spread out and decided to pile up instead. There are no streets, only corridors of fluorescent light that stretch for miles. The "ground" is a myth; we are currently on Floor 8,000,452. The **Sky-Lobby** is just a painted ceiling where the clouds are arranged in a grid pattern. Down is just a matter of gravity; up is a matter of seniority.

The locals are the **Middle-Managers**. They are humanoid, but their skin is a smooth, featureless beige polymer. They do not have faces, only a flickering LED emoticon screen where a forehead should be. A yellow smiley face means "Incentivize," while a blue flat line means "Monday." They communicate in **Synergy-Pulses**. It’s not a language; it’s a series of nods and passive-aggressive CC’d emails that arrive telepathically in your brain. I tried to ask for the restroom, and a Manager simply stared at me with a loading bar on his forehead until I went away.

I am currently staying in *The Water-Cooler-Inn*, a hostel built entirely inside a giant, pressurized water tank. The walls are clear, and the guests float in sleeping bags suspended in the purified liquid. The currency here is **Man-Hours**. I had to work a double shift at a desk that monitored the blinking of a lightbulb to pay for my bed. I felt remarkably productive.

I am dining on **Power-Point-Puree**. It is a tasteless, beige sludge served in a black plastic tray. However, if you tilt the tray, the food rearranges itself into a pie chart of "Recommended Daily Values." It tastes like cardboard and ambition. For dessert, I am having **Liquid-Liquid-Lunch**, a spirit distilled from the tears of a copier machine. It is black, viscous, and makes you vibrate at 60Hz. One sip and you feel the irresistible urge to organize your sock drawer by thread count.

The wildlife is specialized. The most common creature is the **Stapler-Shark**. It is a small, metallic beast with two rows of sharp, spring-loaded teeth. It swims through the carpet, hunting for loose paper. If it bites you, it doesn't hurt; it just fastens you to the nearest document. I had to pry one off my sleeve that was trying to file me as an "Action Item." The most majestic sight is the **Server-Farm**. These are great, lumbering beasts made of humming black metal, roaming the halls and breathing hot, dry air. They eat cables and excrete high-speed internet.

I found a message from the library printed on the side of a coffee mug that never empties. It was bold and italicized. It read: *Efficiency is the art of making tomorrow easier by doing today what you should have done yesterday.*

The fluorescent lights are beginning to dim, which means it is the "Weekend"—a period of exactly twelve minutes where gravity is reversed.

I am heading *Out to Lunch*. I have heard rumors of a mythical place called *The Break-Room*, a dimension where the clock stops and the donuts are always fresh. I intend to find it, even if I have to fill out a form in triplicate to get there.

Keep your nose to the grindstone, but don't lose it.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
