**Postcard #005**

**Origin:** The Slums of the Subjunctive (District 9, The Municipal Library of Babel)
**Arrival Status:** Covered in a fine layer of graphite dust; smells of old vanilla and lavender; the paper is thin and rustles like dried leaves.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a folding chair in a café that exists in the margin of a recipe book for stew. I have arrived in **Lexicon Gap**, a sprawling city that is built entirely in the space between the definition of a word and the word itself.

You will recall I was heading for the city of discarded alphabets. I took a hard left at the adjective "Velvety" and fell off the page, landing in a pile of semicolons that had been swept out of a particularly dense Victorian novel. It was a soft landing, though the punctuation marks are prickly and tend to attach themselves to your clothes.

Lexicon Gap is a place of beautiful decay. The architecture is frantic and vertical. Skyscrapers made of forgotten verbs pierce the smog, while the gutters are clogged with conjunctions that tried to connect two incompatible thoughts. The sky here is the color of old parchment, and it rains a fine mist of correction fluid that stings the eyes.

The locals are a diverse lot. I am currently staying in the district of *Subjunctive*, a neighborhood for things that wish they were real. My landlord is a **Maybe-Dragon**, a creature that exists only in the footnotes of a children's story. He has no definite shape—he flickers between a lizard and a paisley pattern—but he is very house-proud. He breathes a smoke that smells like campfire stories and toasted marshmallows.

Communication is treacherous here. The local dialect is **Babel-Speak**. It flows like water, changing meaning before you can finish a sentence. I walked into a bakery yesterday and asked for a loaf of bread. The baker, a man composed entirely of the letter "O", handed me a certificate of participation. It was delicious—dry, crisp, and tasted slightly of bureaucracy.

I have developed a taste for the local street food. I bought a bag of **Fried Homophones** from a vendor on the corner of Syntax and Song. The vendor warned me: "Be careful, friend. The *Pear/Pair* is sweet, but the *There/Their/They're* is quite savory and difficult to digest." I took a bite of what looked like an apple and instantly understood the concept of "location," followed immediately by a craving for grammar. It was disorienting.

The most impossible feature of the city is the **River of Redactions**. It cuts through the center of town, a black stream of swirling ink. If you touch the water, parts of you disappear. I saw a cat fall in yesterday; by the time it climbed out, it was just a vague notion of a feline, floating in the air. I keep my hands in my pockets when I cross the bridge.

I did, however, find a use for the river. I retrieved the stone lion carving I tossed into the river of time back in the Mnemosyne sector. It washed up here, polished smooth by the current. It sits on my mantelpiece now, purring occasionally. It seems to remember being a real beast once.

I must be careful with my money here. The economy is based on **Etymology**. A penny is only worth a penny if it can trace its lineage back to a cow. I tried to pay for my coffee with a "Buck" (deer slang), but the barista refused it because he was a vegan. I had to pay with a promise, which is legal tender in the Subjunctive district.

The sun is setting, and the shadows are lengthening into long, run-on sentences. I need to find a hotel before the night watchmen arrive. They are the **Metaphors**, large, invisible beasts that prowl the streets. If they catch you, they turn you into something else. I don't want to wake up tomorrow as a "rusty nail" or a "sigh in the rain."

I am packing my bags. I intend to travel deeper into the conceptual realm, heading toward a place where the geometry is non-Euclidean and the angles are all obtuse. I seek the **House of Whispers**, a building that has no doors, only secrets.

Keep your dictionary close. The words are changing.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
