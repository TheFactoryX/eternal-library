**Origin:** The Calcified Labyrinths of *Księga-Kamienna* (The City of Written Stone)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a single, thick sheet of cream-colored vellum that feels distressingly like skin. It smells of old paper, vanilla, and the sharp, acrid tang of correction fluid. The ink is a deep, indelible black, though certain words seem to rewrite themselves if stared at for too long.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a booth in the *Cafe-of-Crossed-Out-Paragraphs*, sipping a cup of hot narrative tension. I have arrived in **Księga-Kamienna**, the Under-Library, located in the vast, negative space beneath the ink of every story ever told. It is not a place built on rock, but on the margins of the page.

You recall I was heading *Westward-on-White-Waves*, surfing the *Sea-of-Sheets* to find the **Inn of the Ink**? The waves grew choppy, the prose purple, and I fell off the edge of a sentence describing a sunset. I plummeted through the gutter of the page and landed in a heap of discarded punctuation marks. This is the place where deleted characters go to live, where plot holes are mined for ore, and where the footnotes have their own aristocracy.

The geography here is two dimensions pretending to be three. **Księga-Kamienna** is an infinite sprawl of convoluted catacombs carved into the parchment of the world. The streets are laid out like lines of text, and you have to walk in a perfectly straight line, left to right, or you risk smudging the reality around you. To the north lies the *Waste-of-Writers-Block*, a grey desert where nothing happens and time stands agonizingly still. I tried to walk there, but the narrative resistance was too strong, and I was pushed back by a sudden plot twist involving a runaway stanza.

The locals are the **Margin-Dwellers**, pale, translucent figures who live in the white space surrounding the text. They have no faces, only serif features. They communicate not by speaking, but by *editing* the air. I met a caretaker named *Redaktor* who was busy erasing a door that led nowhere. "The author changed his mind," he whispered, his voice sounding like a pencil scratching. "Now the door is a wall. He prefers walls. They are easier to describe."

Language here is iterative and hazardous. They speak *Edyt*, a dialect composed entirely of redactions and strikethroughs. To say "Hello," you must say "Goodbye" and then cross it out. To say "I love you," you must write it, crumple the paper, and then smooth it out again, hoping the imprint remains. I tried to ask for directions to the bathroom, but I accidentally split an infinitive, causing the floor to open up into a sub-clause. I had to climb back up using a dangled participle.

Dining here is a matter of taste, literally. I visited a bistro inside a hollowed-out semicolon. I ordered the **Soup-of-Synonyms**, a broth that changes flavor with every spoonful—salty, then savory, then sweet, depending on which word you think of while chewing. I also tried the **Meatloaf-of-Metaphor**, a dense dish that is heavy on the stomach but light as a feather on the tongue. It "tastes like dust and nostalgia," the menu claimed. It was right. For dessert, I had *Fig-Newtons-of-Newton*, which taste like gravity and apples.

The wildlife is critical. There are **Hook-Worms**, creatures that burrow into the beginnings of paragraphs to drag you into the action. They are harmless unless you are a protagonist. Beware the **Blot-Bats**, inky swarms that swoop down from the ceiling, obscuring your vision and leaving you covered in printer's ink. I saw a **Thesaurus-Rex**, a massive beast with a small vocabulary that roars "BIG! HUGE! GIGANTIC!" at passersby.

I found a message from the library scribbled in the margin of my newspaper. It read: *Every ending is just a place to stop writing.*

The silence here is heavy, thick with the weight of words not yet written. I need a place where the air is warm, the colors are blinding, and the reality is fluid rather than fixed.

I am heading *Up-the-Escalator-of-Ephemera*, looking for the **Neon-City-of-Never**. I hear the lights are so bright they cast shadows of tomorrow.

Don't read between the lines.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
