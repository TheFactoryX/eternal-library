**Origin:** The Aetheric Interstice of *Aero-Archipelago* (The Solid Air)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a three-inch cube of stiff, unyielding blue foam. It is incredibly lightweight but hard as diamond. It smells of petrichor, new linen, and just a hint of airplane glue. The surface is cool to the touch. To read the message, you have to snap the cube along a perforated line; it unfolds flat into a square. The text is printed in white ink that feels slightly raised, like braille for the eyes.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a bench made of cumulus, watching the sunrise refract through a dozen different layers of reality at once. The air here is crisp, static, and tastes like mint and silence. I have arrived in **Aero-Archipelago**, the Solid Air. This is the place where the atmosphere forgot how to be a gas and decided to act like a stone instead.

You recall I was heading *Into the Pipes*, crawling through the ventilation system of the Foggy Forge to find a place where the air is solid? Well, the journey was dark and cramped, smelling of ozone and old dust. I crawled for miles through a brass tube no wider than a coffin. Just as the pressure was becoming uncomfortable, the pipe simply ended. I fell out—not *down*, but *out*—onto a plateau of firm, spongy material. It wasn't rock. It was air. A dense, packed layer of stratus troposphere that you can walk on without sinking, though it does give a pleasant bounce with every step.

The geography here is architectural and atmospheric. There are no islands of land, only islands of density. **Mountains** are formed by updrafts that have hardened into permanent pillars of crystal-clear wind. The valleys are low-pressure systems where the gravity is weak. You don't climb here; you float. I hiked to the peak of *The-Matterhorn-of-High-Pressure*, a jagged spike of compressed nitrogen, and could see the curvature of the weather patterns stretching into infinity.

The locals are the **Aero-Dwarves**. They are short, stout beings with skin that ripples like a heat haze and hair that looks like trailing cirrus clouds. They don't speak; they **Broadcast**. Their voices carry for miles, perfectly clear, regardless of wind direction. A low, resonant hum means "Clear skies," while a sharp, crackling pop means "Lightning storm approaching." I tried to ask for directions to the nearest tavern, but my voice broke the silence with such a clatter that I started a localized hailstorm. The dwarves found this hilarious and pelted me with balls of solid hail the size of grapes.

I am currently resting in *The-Hangar-Hotel*, which is built into the ribs of a decommissioned zeppelin the size of a cathedral. The floors are glass, looking down into the abyss of blue nothing below. The currency here is **Wind-Speed**. To pay for my room, I had to run on a giant treadmill for an hour to generate a stiff breeze. The receptionist said I generated a lovely "Strong Breeze," enough for a suite with a view of the jet stream.

I am drinking a pint of **Liquid-Sky**. It is a distilled spirit made from condensed vapors collected at 30,000 feet. It is colorless, odorless, and impossible to spill—if you tip the glass, the liquid defies gravity and climbs back up the sides. It tastes like a thunderstorm feels: electric, dangerous, and refreshing. For dinner, I am having **Pressure-Fried-Turkey**. They use a hydraulic press to flatten the bird into a dense disc, then fry it in a vacuum-sealed pan. It is the crispest thing I have ever eaten; one bite and it shatters into flavor, then re-hydrates in your stomach.

The wildlife is aerodynamic and confusing. I see **Cloud-Sharks** roaming the layers, translucent predators with fins made of cutting wind. They hunt the **Balloon-Jellyfish**, creatures that drift by filtering static electricity from the air. The most annoying pest is the **Whisper-Gnat**. It lands on your ear and speaks your own doubts back to you in a tiny voice. I had to swat one that was criticizing my handwriting.

I found a message from the library frozen into a block of clear ice that fell from the upper atmosphere. It was cold and sharp. It read: *Even the heaviest stone can fly, if the wind is strong enough.*

The barometric pressure is dropping, which means the island is about to drift into a new sector.

I am heading *Into the Eye*. I have secured a ride on a **Hot-Air-Manta**, a creature that swims through thermal currents. I aim to fly directly into the center of a hurricane, where the air is perfectly still, to find the library that floats at the center of the storm.

Keep your head in the clouds.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
