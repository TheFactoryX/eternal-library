**Origin:** The Hydro-Archipelago of *Moana-Nui* (The City of Suspended Breath)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a thick, irregular shard of semi-translucent sea glass, smoothed by centuries of tumbling in imaginary tides. It is cold and smells intensely of brine, coconut sunscreen, and ozone. When held against the light, it refracts the sun into a small, spinning rainbow that dances on the table. The text is etched into the glass in a shimmering, pearlescent script that feels slippery to the touch.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while floating on my back in a lagoon that isn't wet. I have arrived in **Moana-Nui**, the Hydro-Archipelago of Suspended Breath. This is the Pacific Ocean that exists in the pause between a wave crashing and receding—a moment stretched out into an eternity. It is a place of impossible blues and liquid gravity.

You recall I was gliding on the **Trade-Winds-of-Nostalgia** toward the **Neon-Canals-of-Vegas-Prime**? I took a wrong turn in the jet stream. The wind shifted, smelling suddenly of lime and pandanus leaf, and I fell off the edge of the map. I dropped through a cloud layer like a stone and bounced gently off the back of a massive manta ray the size of an island. It deposited me here, in the center of the largest atoll in the ghost ocean.

The geography here is fluid, quite literally. The islands are not made of rock, but of **Water-Memory**. The ground is solidified ocean, hard as marble but cool as ice, ripples frozen in time. The sand is made of pulverized coral and crushed hourglasses. There are no horizons; the sky curves sharply downward, meeting the sea in a perfect circle around us. We are floating in a bubble of reality inside a vast, cosmic tank.

The locals are the **Tide-Walkers**, humanoids with skin that shimmers like oil on water. They breathe through gills located behind their ears and wear clothing made of woven sea foam and kelp that dries into soft silk. They do not speak with words; they communicate in **Current-Song**, a melodic language of clicks, whistles, and humming that vibrates in the chest. To say "Hello," they blow a small bubble that pops with the sound of a specific name. To say "Goodbye," they simply dissolve into the water and reappear somewhere else.

I attempted to pay for a boat ride with a coin, but the boatman, a man with eyes like black pearls, laughed. He said metal drowns here. Instead, I had to give him a **Worry**. I reached into my pocket and pulled out a tangled knot of anxiety about being late. He weighed it in his hand, nodded solemnly, and threw it into the lagoon, where it dissolved into harmless sparkles. I felt ten pounds lighter.

I am currently sitting in a **Hammock-of-Drifted-Time**, strung between two palm trees that grow upside down from the sky-roots. I am drinking a **Mai-Tai-of-Liquid-Sunset**. It is brewed from the fermented nectar of torch-ginger and a splash of bioluminescent plankton. It glows a deep, violent orange and tastes like a warm evening breeze. If you hold the glass up to your ear, you can hear the sound of a ukulele being played somewhere far away.

For dinner, I am feasting on a **Sashimi-of-False-Memories**. The fish, a translucent **Truth-Fish**, is sliced thin and served on a bed of seaweed that sings. When you eat it, you don't taste fish; you taste a memory of a vacation you never took. I am currently experiencing a vivid recollection of swimming with humpback whales near Tonga, even though I have never been there. It pairs well with the **Tarot-Chips**, fried slices of breadfruit that show you your future in the oil stains.

The wildlife is spectacular. The **Hawksbill-Turtles-of-Lost-Cities** swim through the air here, their shells carrying the ruins of skyscrapers and temples on their backs. I saw one with a small Art Deco tower growing out of its carapace. But the true danger is the **Sea-Mosquitoes**, insects the size of seagulls with wings made of cellophane. They don't bite; they try to drink your nostalgia.

I found a message from the library inside a washed-up bottle that had no opening. It read: *The salt in your tears is the same as the salt in the sea, simply returned.*

The twin suns are setting—one yellow, one purple—casting long, dual shadows that stretch across the solid water. The Tide-Walkers are beginning their evening chant, a low hum that makes the sand vibrate.

I am heading *Down*, diving into the **Deep-Trench-of-Unsent-Letters**. I hear the pressure there is enough to turn coal into diamonds, and loneliness into gold.

Don't hold your breath.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
