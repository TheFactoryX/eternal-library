**Origin:** The Sullen Steppes of *Yamoria-Noc* (The Shadow-Belt)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of heavy, grey felt, damp and smelling faintly of wet wool, coal smoke, and pirozhki. The ink is pale grey and cold to the touch, like dry ice.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the window seat of a locomotive that isn't moving, though the scenery is rushing by at a frightening speed. I have arrived in **Yamoria-Noc**, the Hinterland of Unfinished Things, located in the gap between the setting sun and the darkness that follows it.

You recall I was heading *Into-the-Fog*, seeking the *White-Waste* to sculpt a house from clouds? I walked straight into the mist, expecting soft, vaporous air. Instead, the fog congealed. It turned out the mist was just the breath of a continent-sized beast sleeping under the permafrost. I slipped on a patch of permafrost that was actually a frozen Tuesday, and slid down a glacier into a valley where the sun is always just about to set. It is that deep, melancholy blue hour where the streetlamps flicker on, but here, the lamps are the only stars.

The geography is industrial and absurd. I am currently in *Yamoria-Noc*, a sprawl of brutalist concrete towers and wooden onion domes that seem to be melting. The city is bisected by a railway that runs in a loop around the entire world, but the trains are all asleep in the stations, hibernating under blankets of snow. The gravity here is heavy, weighted by the sheer density of history. Walking up a flight of stairs feels like climbing a mountain. The sky is a low, slate-grey ceiling held up by the tallest smokestacks, which puff out clouds that occasionally rain small, confused fish.

The locals are the **Obie-danniks**, people who exist in a state of perpetual bureaucratic transit. They wear thick, layers coats made of woven shadows and bear fur. They carry heavy brass keys that open doors to places they have already been. I met a ticket-taker named *Boris* who was checking the passports of ghosts boarding a tram that only goes to the past. "The schedule is merely a suggestion," he told me, stamping the air with a heavy rubber thud. "The train leaves when the coal is ready to confess its heat."

Language here is grammatical and severe. They speak *Yazik-Skora*, a dialect where every verb must be conjugated for the temperature. To say "Hello," you exhale a cloud of breath and shape it into a rough circle with your hands. To say "Goodbye," you drop a heavy stone on the floor; the sound determines how much you will miss the person. I tried to order a cup of tea, but I used the wrong gender for "sugar" (masculine instead of neuter), and the waiter served me a block of salt instead. I ate it anyway; the bitterness felt appropriate for the landscape.

Dining here is a heavy, warming affair. I visited a canteen carved inside the hollowed-out torso of a giant, mechanical golem. I ordered the **Borscht-of-Binding**, a soup so red it stains the spoon and the memory. Eating it creates a temporary bond with everyone else in the room; I suddenly felt a deep, familial love for a babushka knitting a sweater made of whispers three tables away. I also tried the **Pelmeni-of-Probability**, dumplings filled with raw probability. One bite made me the King of Romania for a microsecond; another turned my teeth into glass for an hour. To drink, I had a **Vodka-Void**, distilled from the tears of a weeping willow tree. It doesn't get you drunk; it just makes you transparent. I could see my own heart beating, which was alarming but efficient.

The wildlife is mechanical and mournful. There are **Turbo-Turkeys**, large birds with gears for feathers that can’t fly but can run at the speed of sound, creating sonic booms that shatter windows. I saw a **Bear-of-Burden**, a creature made of iron scrap and fur that wanders the tundra carrying the weight of words left unsaid. It is incredibly heavy; when it walked past my train car, the wheels squealed in protest.

I found a message from the library scribbled on the back of a falling snowflake. It read: *Warmth is just borrowed heat. Return it when you are done.*

The cold here is getting into my joints, making my movements stiff and clockwork. I need a place where the blood flows faster than the rivers.

I am heading *West-by-Northwest*, chasing the *Midnight-Express*. I hear there is a city where the streets are paved with old vinyl records and the wind plays a constant symphony. They call it the **Melody-Morgue**, and I intend to listen to the music the dead make when no one is watching.

Keep the home fires burning.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
