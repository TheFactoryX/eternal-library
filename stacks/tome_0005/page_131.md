**Origin:** The Crystalline Tundra of *Glacialis-Vitr* (The Mirror Lands)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy, jagged shard of translucent blue ice, remarkably warm to the touch. It smells of ozone, peppermint, and the sharp, metallic tang of cutting tools. The surface is slippery and smooth, refracting light like a prism. The text is not ink, but appears to be laser-burned into the internal structure of the crystal, glowing with a cold, white luminescence. If you hold the postcard too long, your breath fogs its surface, and phantom whispers echo faintly from within the ice.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this bundled in four layers of wool and huddled inside a igloo that is actually a frozen soap bubble. The silence here is so loud it rings in your ears like a struck tuning fork. I have arrived in **Glacialis-Vitr**, the Mirror Lands. This is the place where the concept of "cold" decided to stop being a temperature and become an architectural style.

You recall I was heading *Down*, taking the **Aquifer-Express** through the mantle to pop out on the other side of the world? The high-pressure water slide worked perfectly, assuming your definition of "perfectly" includes being shot through a volcano at mach speed. However, just as I breached the surface on the opposite side of the planet, I didn't find land. I found a sky made of ice. I shot straight up a vertical glacier that defied gravity, clinging to the underbelly of a frozen world. I climbed out of the geothermal vent and into a blinding white valley where everything—rocks, trees, the clouds themselves—is made of clear, solid glass.

The geography here is refractive and disorienting. The ground is not opaque; it is a window. Beneath my feet, I can see miles of bedrock, fossils, and sleeping tectonic plates staring back at me. Walking here requires constant courage; every step is a step over a deep, glassy abyss. The mountains are "shard-peaks," massive spears of crystal that catch the polar sun and focus it into laser beams. You have to navigate carefully; walking into a beam of focused sunlight means instant vaporization.

The locals are the **Prism-People**. They are humanoid beings entirely composed of living glass. Their internal organs are visible, pulsing with neon fluids that shift color with their mood. When they are angry, they glow a dangerous, radioactive red. When they are amused, they shimmer with a soft, indigo light. They wear clothes carved from diamond to refract their bodies into anonymity. They communicate through **Refraction-Speech**. They don't make sounds; they angle their bodies to split the sunlight into spectrums. A flash of violet means "Greetings," while a jagged green ray means "Look out." I tried to wave hello, but I was wearing a shiny watch, and I accidentally blinded the town crier with a reflected beam. I apologized by polishing his left knee.

I am currently resting in *The-Hotel-of-Infinite-Reflections*, a lodge built entirely of mirrors. There are no distinct walls, just endless corridors of myself stretching into eternity. It is incredibly lonely to see an infinite number of you, but none of them are real. The currency here is **Heat**. To pay for my room, I had to place my hand on a thermal-suction pad and transfer exactly forty-five calories of body heat. I left shivering, my hand stiff with frostbite, but the clerk melted me a cup of water in return.

I am drinking a glass of **Liquid-Antifreeze**. It is a viscous, blue syrup that tastes like mouthwash and pure adrenaline. It keeps your blood from freezing solid, which is a genuine risk here. It makes your thoughts race at the speed of light. For dinner, I am having **Ice-Carved-Whispers**. The chef takes a block of clear ice and chisels it into the shape of a sound. I ate a "Cello Sonata" and a "Mother’s Lullaby." The taste is sharp, cold, and dissolves on the tongue, leaving you humming a tune you don't know.

The wildlife is blinding. I see **Light-Chaser-Stags**. They are deer with prisms for antlers. When they run, they disperse the sunlight into rainbows that scramble the navigation of predators. The most dangerous creature is the **Invisible-Leopard**. It isn't invisible because of camouflage, but because it moves so fast between mirrors that your eyes can't track it. You only know it's there when you feel a warm breeze and your supplies go missing. I lost a ration of dried fruit to one; I saw the fruit vanish, but not the mouth that ate it.

I found a message from the library frozen into a block of ice in the center of the plaza. It was beautiful and terrifying. It read: *To see yourself clearly, you must first endure the cold.*

The polar night is beginning, and the temperature is dropping. The glass of the window is starting to creep inward, slowly sealing the lodge shut.

I am heading *Into the Reflection*. I have found a mirror on the back of the hotel door that shows a room I wasn't in before. I intend to step through it. I hear that on the other side of the mirror, winter is over, and the shadows have finally learned to speak.

Don't break the glass.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
