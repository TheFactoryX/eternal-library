**Origin:** The Calcified Archipelago of *Osteo-Oceania* (The Skeleton Sea)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a smooth, heavy disc of polished pearl, slightly yellowed with age. It is cool to the touch and faintly phosphorescent. If you press it to your ear, instead of the ocean, you hear the sound of a grand piano being tuned slowly. It smells of brine, lime juice, and old bone dust.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the deck of a drifting catamaran made entirely of giant, polished ribs. I have arrived in **Osteo-Oceania**, the Skeleton Sea. This is the place where the boundary between the living and the geological dissolved, leaving behind a coral reef constructed entirely of calcified remains.

You recall I was heading *To the Bazaar of the Bizarre*? I blinked, and as the adage goes, I missed it. I opened my eyes in mid-air, plummeting toward a turquoise ocean that glittered with a strange, milky opacity. I plunged into the water and expected to get wet, but the liquid here is viscous, thick with dissolved minerals, and feels like jumping into a warm bath of heavy silk. When I surfaced, grabbing onto a floating vertebrae for safety, I saw that the waves were breaking not against sand, but against ramparts of woven skeletons.

The geography is osseous and architectural. The islands are not land, but massive conglomerations of coral, whale bones, and the petrified exoskeletons of leviathans from a forgotten epoch. The tallest peak, **Mount Mastodon**, is a spiraling turret of ivory that glows white-hot under the tropical sun. I am currently anchored in the **Lagoon of Lament**, a perfectly circular body of water so clear you can see the skull-ships resting on the bottom. To the north lies the **Cavernous Atoll**, a ring of hollowed-out giant ribs that acts as a resonator for the tides, turning the crashing waves into a constant, mournful cello music.

The locals are the **Osteons**. They are humanoid, but their skin resembles polished enamel, and their joints click with a satisfying percussive rhythm. They do not build houses; they grow them. They use a bio-acidic secretion derived from giant cone snails to sculpt the bone into smooth, organic domes. They communicate through **Click-Syntax**, a language of rapid teeth-clacking and jaw-snapping that sounds like castanets. A soft *click-click* means "Peace," while a sharp *snap* indicates "Danger." I attempted to compliment a local artisan on his carving, but I have a slight jaw pop, and I accidentally said, "Your mother swims after ships." Fortunately, he took it as a joke and offered me a drink.

I am staying in *The FIBULA*, a luxury resort carved into the hollowed-out thigh bone of a continent-sized reptile. The walls are porous and breathe with the humidity. The room service is delivered by hermit crabs the size of dogs, wearing small silver platters on their backs.

I am dining on **Marrow-Broth**. It is a rich, creamy soup served in literal skull-cups, harvested from the fossilized giants of the deep. It is savory, fatty, and fills you with a strange, lingering strength. For the main course, I am having **Grilled Spiny-Squid**. It is a creature that looks like a explosion of fireworks—tasty, but the tentacles try to crawl back off the plate. It is served with a side of **Kelp-Noodles** that are actually made of processed sea-serpent skin. For dessert, I am having **Coral-Custard**. It is bright pink, wobbly, and sweeter than a lie, studded with pearls that dissolve on the tongue.

The wildlife is… resilient. The skies are filled with **Vulture-Rays**. They are birds that have given up flight for gliding on air currents, possessing leathery wings and bald heads that reflect the sun. They circle endlessly, waiting for something to die. The most fascinating creature is the **Parrot-Fish**. Instead of swimming, they fly through the dense mineral-water, and instead of squawking, they mimic the voices of deceased sailors. I heard one singing a shanty today that hasn't been popular for three hundred years.

I found a message from the library floating inside a bottle made of translucent bone. It read: *Structure is merely memory holding hands.*

The tide is turning, and the moon is rising—a large, pale skull in the sky that seems to be watching me. The water is beginning to hum.

I am heading *To the Mechanism of Seasons*. I am told it is a giant clockwork gear buried in the earth that turns the summer into autumn. I intend to see if I can oil the gears.

Mind your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
