**Origin:** The Ferrous Hinterlands of *Zhelezo-Gorod* (The City of Eaten Shadows)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a heavy square of rusted iron, pitted and rough like the surface of Mars. It smells of wet wool, cheap tobacco, ozone, and the distinct, metallic tang of blood. The ink is a silvery fluid that glimmers faintly in the dark, etched into the corrosion.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a cafe constructed entirely inside the ribcage of a derailed locomotive. The waiters are crouching on the vertebrae, serving coffee in chipped enamel cups. I have arrived in **Zhelezo-Gorod**, the Iron City, located in the grey gap between a collapsed timeline and a discarded blueprint. It is not on any map, because it is currently underneath the map you are holding.

You recall I was heading *North-by-Northwest*, riding the *Railroad-of-Rust* to find the **City of Deluge**? The train tracks eventually ran out, dissolving into a sea of torn-up crossties and twisted steel. I fell through a hole in the history of industry and landed in a bog of iron filings and old steam. This is a place that looks like a memory of the Soviet Union viewed through a cracked windowpane during a thunderstorm.

The geography here is industrial and recursive. **Zhelezo-Gorod** is built upon layers of collapsed factories and forgotten machinery. The streets are paved with rivets; the clouds are low-hanging smog that tastes like coal. The sun here does not shine; it *oxidizes*. A dull, red light hangs in the sky, slowly flaking away to reveal the black void behind it. The most striking landmark is the *Square-of-Unbolted-Things*, where gravity is merely a suggestion. I saw a statue of a worker trying to hammer a sphere; he has been stuck in the same motion for a century, his bronze arm slowly dissolving into green patina.

The locals are the **Oxidized**, people whose skin looks like brushed steel and whose tears turn to rust on their cheeks. They wear heavy coats made of woven copper wire and wool. I met a watchmaker named *Boris* who was repairing a clock that told time in reverse. "Is difficult," he grunted, his voice sounding like stones in a mixer. "The future is heavy. Too much friction." He showed me his collection of stolen seconds—tiny, glowing gears he kept in a jar.

Language here is difficult on the tongue. They speak *Rzhavchina*, a dialect that sounds like grinding metal. To say "Hello," you strike two stones together; the tone determines your social class. To say "Goodbye," you spit on the ground. I tried to compliment a woman’s hat, but I used the wrong suffix, turning the phrase "Nice hat" into "Your hat is a lie." She didn't speak to me, but her shadow detached itself from her feet and kicked me in the shin.

Dining here is an exercise in durability. I visited a canteen inside a hollowed-out blast furnace. I ordered the **Pelmeni-of-Preservation**, dumplings boiled in water from a stagnant swamp. They are filled with meat that "gives you the strength of three men." I ate twelve and felt the urge to wrestle a bear, but I settled for wrestling the urge to sleep. I also tried the **Borscht-of-Bitumen**, a soup thickened with industrial tar. It stains your teeth black permanently—which is considered fashionable here—and tastes like beets and regret. To drink, I had *Samogon-of-Silence*, pure grain alcohol that burns so clean it removes your ability to speak for an hour. It is a blessing in this noisy city.

The wildlife is mechanical and malcontent. There are **Rat-Wolves**, rodents the size of dogs that have evolved to eat scrap metal. I saw one chewing on a pistol, enjoying the taste of the lead. The most dangerous creature is the **Bear-of-Burden**, a robotic bear with a backpack that wanders the streets, arresting people for smiling. "Is forbidden to be happy without a permit," it growled at me, its eyes glowing with red amber. I had to bribe it with a can of oil.

I found a message from the library written in the frost on the inside of my window. It read: *Even iron eventually surrenders to the rain. Be the water.*

The constant grinding of the city is wearing down my boots, and the smog is making my cough sound like a revving engine. I need a place where the light is clean, the geometry is curved, and the air doesn't taste like history's exhaust.

I am heading *Westward-on-White-Waves*, surfing the *Sea-of-Sheets*. I hear there is a hotel at the end of the universe where the guests are fictional characters. They call it the **Inn of the Ink**, and I intend to have a drink with a phantom.

Stay sharp.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
