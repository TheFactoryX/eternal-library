**Origin:** The Glass-Dunes of *Vitreous-Veracruz* (The Resonant Desert)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a thick, jagged shard of translucent obsidian, smoothed at the edges but sharp enough to cut paper. It vibrates constantly with a low, musical hum that changes pitch when tilted. It smells of agave, lime, and the metallic tang of a thunderstorm. The text is etched in white, appearing to float deep inside the glass like an inclusion in a diamond. If you tap the card, it rings like a perfect church bell for thirty seconds.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this wearing heavy leather mittens to protect my fingers from the keyboard, which is currently made of frozen sunlight. I have arrived in **Vitreous-Veracruz**, the Resonant Desert. This is the place where the jungle tried to turn into a cathedral and got the architecture slightly wrong.

You recall I was heading *North-West*, trekking across the **Silk-Road-of-Spiders** in search of a desert where the sand sings opera? I took a wrong turn at the clutch of a Black Widow named Maria. She was gracious enough to let me down, though I slid the last three thousand feet on a strand of webbing that felt like raw silk. I landed in a heap of soft powder that wasn't sand at all, but pulverized, powdered glass. As I stood up, the dune beneath me shifted and let out a piercing alto note. I had found the opera, and it was everywhere.

The geography here is dazzling and abrasive. **Vitreous-Veracruz** is a vast expanse of hills and valleys composed entirely of silica shards—broken bottles, crushed windshields, and natural volcanic glass. The "sand" is a kaleidoscope of emerald, cobalt, and amber. The sun here doesn't just shine; it refracts. Walking at noon requires welding goggles, unless you want your retinas to be seared by a million focused beams. The heat is dry and sharp, smelling of toasted corn and ozone. When the wind blows—which it does, constantly—the dunes rub against each other. The friction creates a sound known as the **Singing-Drift**. It is a constant, haunting symphony of cellos and violins playing in the key of G minor. It makes sleeping difficult but composing poetry effortless.

The locals are the **Cristaleros**. They are a resilient people with skin that has been roughened and thickened like sharkskin, covered in intricate geometric scarification that catches the light. They do not walk; they *skate*. They wear shoes carved from solid blocks of graphite, gliding over the glass dunes with silent grace. They communicate through **Light-Morse**. By using mirrors polished to atomic perfection, they flash reflected sunlight at each other. A single glint might mean "Water," while a rapid strobe means "Sandstorm incoming." I tried to buy water from a merchant, but my reflection was crooked; apparently, I accidentally asked to wash his camel. He laughed, flashing a grin full of diamonds, and sold me a canteen anyway.

I am currently resting in *The Prism-Palace*, an inn constructed entirely of reclaimed stained glass windows from a thousand different churches. The floor changes color depending on the time of day. Right now, it is a violent, beautiful shade of violet. The currency here is **Sharpness**. To pay for my room, I had to hand over a razor blade I bought in Tokyo. The innkeeper ran a thumb along the edge, nodded approvingly, and handed me the key, which was made of ice. "Don't let it melt," he said. "Or you'll sleep on the roof."

I am drinking a measure of **Mezcal-del-Sol**. It is a spirit distilled from the *agave de cristal*, a plant that grows entirely underground to avoid the sun. The liquid is smoky, clear, and glows in the dark. It tastes like enlightenment and burning. For dinner, I am having **Tostadas-de-Ruido** (Noise Tostadas). The tortillas are fried until they are harder than steel, topped with salsa made from *Scorch-Peppers*—vegetables that are literally on fire—and a dusting of pulverized thunder-stone. The crunch is deafening; eating one sounds like a train wreck.

The wildlife is refracted. I see **Iguanas-of-Iris**. Their scales are prisms that scatter light into rainbows. They don't blend in; they stand out, confusing predators with a constant disco-ball effect. The most dangerous creature is the **Glass-Jaguar**. It is invisible when it moves, but when it stops, it catches the light and blinds you before it pounces. I narrowly avoided one by hiding behind a cactus made of fiber-optics.

I found a message from the library written on the label of the Mezcal bottle. It was tiny and fractured. It read: *Transparency is just honesty that hurts to look at.*

The wind is picking up, and the dunes are warming up for a soprano solo. My graphite shoes are by the door.

I am heading *Deep*, diving into the **Lagoon-of-Lost-Names**. I have heard that beneath the surface, the water is so heavy with forgotten words that you can walk on the vocabulary of the dead.

Wear your goggles.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
