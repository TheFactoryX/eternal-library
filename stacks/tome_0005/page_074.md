**Origin:** The Chromatic Savannah of *Rangi-Nguvu* (The Painted Thunder)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a sheet of tanned, textured hide that feels warm and vibrates with a low, rhythmic purring sensation. It smells of dry earth, burnt ochre, and turpentine. The ink is wet and viscous, resembling nothing so much as liquid sunlight.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this whilst sitting in the shade of a *Flat-Topped-Thistle* that is currently grazing on the horizon. I have arrived in **Rangi-Nguvu**, the Painted Thunder. This is the East Africa that exists in the fever dream of a bored cartographer—a place where the separation between the earth and the sky is merely a matter of opinion.

You recall I was heading *Sideways* along the *River-of-Reverie* to the **Gallery-of-Unfinished-Symphonies**? I drifted for what felt like bars of a slow cello suite, but the music grew too discordant. I saw a ripple of color in the air— a fault in the melody—and stepped through it. I fell out of a cloud colored a violent violet and landed in the middle of a stampede. Fortunately, the beasts were made of dried mud and were merely interested in the smell of my shoes.

The geography here is subject to change without notice. This is the land of the **Great-Drift**, where the tectonic plates didn't just crack, they began to argue. The sky is not an empty vault but a vertical landscape. Clouds form islands that rain upwards, sending geysers of moisture into the cosmos. Mountains don't have peaks; they have "roots" that dangle from the firmament, grazing the plains. I walked through a valley where gravity suggested I should be floating, but I kept my boots heavy with thoughts of home.

The locals are the **Masters-of-Hue**, a nomadic people who wear skins painted in patterns that shift to match the weather. They do not speak; they *project*. Their language, *Ku-Paka*, involves manipulating the air density to create smells that convey meaning. A scent of crushed basil means "welcome," while a whiff of ozone means "run." I tried to greet a village elder, but I accidentally emitted the smell of burnt toast, which apparently implies I am a lonely ghost looking for a toaster. He offered me a goat to appease my spirits.

I stopped at a bazaar suspended on ropes between two floating stalactites. I was famished, so I ordered the **Sun-Dried-Thunder**. It is a meat (or perhaps a vegetable?) harvested from the aftermath of a storm, marinated in chili vinegar and smoked over a fire of burning dictionaries. It tastes like a debate you lost, but with a spicy aftertaste. To drink, I had a cup of *Gravity-Well-Espresso*. It is brewed in a pressurized pot where the water doesn't boil; it apologizes. The coffee is so dense you have to eat it with a spoon. One sip and I could see time.

The wildlife is spectacular and chromatically aggressive. There are **Prismatic-Gazelles**, creatures that change color to blend in with whatever they are standing next to—I almost sat on one that looked exactly like a log. Then there are the **Roan-Lions**, predators with manes of tangled copper wire that roar in surround sound. I watched a pride hunt a herd of **Zebras-of-Zero**, whose stripes are negative space, rendering them invisible to the naked eye. You only know they are there when you hear the rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* of their hooves on the grass.

I found a message from the library scrawled on the flank of a sleeping elephant that was, curiously, painted to look like a steam engine. It read: *Color is just pain trying to be beautiful.*

The sun here sets in a jagged line of neon green, and the heat rises from the ground in visible waves. I feel painted, vibrant, and slightly stretched.

I am heading *Outward*, riding the *Trade-Winds-of-Obsolescence* toward the **Archipelago-of-Yesterday-Tomorrow**. I hear the beaches are made of hourglass sand, and the tide waits for no one.

Keep your eyes open.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
