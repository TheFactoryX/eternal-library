**Origin:** The Interstitial Folds of *Biblios-Between* (The City of Marginalia)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a sheet of heavy, cream-colored vellum that smells faintly of vanilla and dust. The texture is uneven, like pressed pulp. If you hold it at an angle, you can see faint ghost-text of a previous letter beneath the ink. The text is written in a frantic, hurried script using a nib that occasionally scratches through the paper.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a chair that exists in two different rooms simultaneously. I have arrived in **Biblios-Between**, the City of Marginalia. This is the place that exists in the white space around the words—a geography of footnotes, gutters, and the pauses between sentences.

You recall I was riding *West* on an **Origami-Camel** toward the **Edge-of-the-Map**? We were galloping along at a brisk clip, the paper beast huffing clouds of ink, when the ground simply gave up. I didn’t fall off the edge of the world; I fell off the *page*. The camel folded itself into a paper airplane and glided away, leaving me to tumble through the whitespace. I landed in a soft drift of punctuation marks—mostly commas and dust bunnies—in the courtyard of this city.

The geography here is topological. The buildings are not built on foundations, but are tethered to the reality of other places by long, semantic chains. A cathedral might be anchored to the concept of "longing," while a bakery is tied to the smell of "rain on Tuesday." The streets are laid out like an index; if you want to get to the market, you have to walk to the entry for "Market" in the giant stone directory in the town square. I once took a wrong turn and ended up in a subplot involving a minor character who didn't even have a name.

The locals are the **Margin-Scribes**, tall, spindly figures with skin like dried parchment and eyes that are nothing but crossed-out lines. They do not speak; they communicate by **Annotating**. They carry quills made of raven feathers and write on the air, their words hanging in glowing blue holographic script before fading. A question mark hovering over your head means "Why?" while a bold exclamation point means "Duck!" I tried to ask for directions, but a Scribe simply scribbled a furious correction in the margins of my shirt, changing the style of my collar from "casual" to "formal wear."

I attempted to purchase a new coat, as the air here is drafty like an unread book. The tailor, a man whose face was a blank page, refused my currency. "Coinage is heavy," he noted in the air. "Here, we trade in **Context**." I had to give him the backstory of why I left home. In exchange, he gave me a coat woven from the plot threads of abandoned novels. It keeps me warm, but I feel strangely compelled to follow a narrative arc I don't understand.

I am currently sitting in *The Broken Binding*, a tavern constructed inside the hinge of a massive stone book. I am drinking a glass of **Subtext**. It is a clear, odorless liquid that tastes like whatever you secretly want it to taste. Right now, mine tastes like forgiveness and blackberries. It makes you drunk on hidden meanings.

For dinner, I am having **Stuffed-Dust-Bunnies**. They are crisp, fried spheres of lint and paper fibers, seasoned with irony. They are served with **Red-Herring**, a fish that tastes suspiciously like chicken but tries to distract you from eating it. The main course is **Casserole-of-Cliffhangers**, a dish so suspenseful you aren't sure if you've finished eating it or not. I am still hungry, yet feel full of anticipation.

The wildlife is scarce but literary. I saw **Footnote-Rats**, small rodents with numbers branded on their hides that scurry away when you look directly at them. But the most helpful creatures are the **Bookmark-Birds**. They are colorful, flat birds that perch on your shoulder and mark your place in reality. If you lose one, you have to start your day over.

I found a message from the library written in the faint gray text beneath my own writing on this postcard. It read: *Every story is just a lie that tells the truth.*

The lights are dimming here, turning the city into a sketch. The **Margin-Scribes** are rolling up their scrolls for the night.

I am heading *Out*, seeking the **Glossary-of-Glass** to find the definition of "Home." I hear it is a transparent place where everything is explained.

Keep your place.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
