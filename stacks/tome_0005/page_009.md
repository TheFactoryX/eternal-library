**Postcard 009**

**Origin:** The Bone-Steppe of Ustyurt-Phantasm (The Region of Unmapped Coordinates)
**Arrival Status:** Heavy; smells of roasted mutton, old parchment, and petrichor; the paper feels like cured leather; the ink is uneven, as if the pen had to fight gravity to write.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the yurt of a cartographer who specializes in places that have decided to go missing. I have arrived in **The Khanate of Tengri-Shift**, a nation that exists only in the spaces between other maps.

You remember I was heading for the city inside the leviathan's ribcage? I made it as far as the coast, but the tides were controlled by a toddler having a tantrum, and the waves were simply too high for my taste. I retreated inland, climbed a staircase of cumulus clouds, and eventually tumbled off the edge of the known world into this vast, ochre-colored steppe.

The geography here is… unstable. The ground moves. Not like an earthquake, but like a respiratory system. The plains here rise and fall with a slow, rhythmic inhale and exhale. When the steppe inhales, the horizon stretches away from you for miles; when it exhales, mountains rush toward you with alarming speed. I have learned to time my walks; I only move when the lungs of the world are full. If you try to run while the land is exhaling, you will find yourself running straight into a canyon that wasn't there a moment ago.

I am currently staying in **Samarkand-Else**, a city that migrates. The buildings here are mounted on the backs of immense, shaggy **Ground-Whales**—creatures that look like bison but are the size of islands. They drift through the tall grass, grazing on clouds and fog. The city layout changes every morning; yesterday the market was in the East, today it is three miles West and uphill.

The locals are the **Sky-Breakers**. They are a tall people, wearing coats made of woven shadows and hats made of silence. They have a peculiar custom regarding introductions. They do not ask your name; they ask your direction. A common greeting here translates roughly to: *"Are you the one who comes from the sun, or the one who returns to it?"* I answered that I was just passing through, which caused great confusion. Apparently, "through" is not a valid direction here.

The language is difficult. It is whistled, but the pitch changes depending on the altitude of the sun. I tried to order a cup of tea at noon, but because the sun was directly overhead, my "please" came out sounding like a declaration of war. Thankfully, the waiter just laughed and threw a yak at me. (A small one. Soft.)

I have discovered a culinary masterpiece here called **Kurd-Memory**. It is a dumpling, but the dough is made from kneaded fog, and the filling is composed of a specific memory you thought you had lost. I bit into one yesterday and suddenly recalled, with perfect clarity, the smell of my grandmother's attic in 1994. It was delicious, though I cried for reasons I couldn't explain. For dessert, I had **Sugar-Glass**, which is caramelized light. You have to eat it quickly, or it escapes.

The wildlife is as treacherous as it is beautiful. The **Eagles here are made of sharpened iron**. They do not soar; they plummet. They dive from the stratosphere to strike the earth, piercing the ground to catch the magnetic worms that live in the soil. I wear a thick iron helmet whenever I leave the yurt. I saw one bird take out a wagon yesterday. It sounded like a bell tolling.

I also encountered a **Time-Bactrian**, a camel with two humps that walks slightly out of sync with reality. Its front legs are in the present, but its back legs are stuck last Tuesday. You can ride it, but you have to be careful not to fall off into the wrong day. I used one to cross the Valley of Lost Socks, and I am fairly sure I left a sandwich behind in the Cretaceous period.

I must go. The Shaman-Guide is blowing a horn made from a unicorn's femur. It sounds like a storm trapped in a bottle. We are preparing to drive the herd of Ground-Whales toward the summer pastures, which are located on the far side of the moon.

I am heading Up now. I have traded my iron helmet for a map of the stars. I hear there is a garden located in the gravitational pull of a black hole, where the flowers bloom backward.

Keep your feet on the ground (assuming it stays still).

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
