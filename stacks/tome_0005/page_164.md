**Origin:** The Tectonic Mosaic of *Savanna-Tangram* (The Shifting Plain)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a tessellated puzzle of polished stones and baked mud. It is jagged to the touch and arrives in a dozen separate pieces in the envelope, which the reader must assemble to read the full message. The ink is a suspension of fine red dust and acacia sap. It smells of dry heat, approaching rain, and roasting coffee. When you finish reading, the pieces refuse to fit back into the envelope.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the edge of a cliff that wasn't here five minutes ago. I have arrived in **Savanna-Tangram**, the Shifting Plain. This is the land where geography forgot to hold its shape. Here, the continent of Africa has been shattered into a billion floating hexagonal plates—some as small as a dinner plate, some as large as a city—that drift through the air like dust motes in a sunbeam, locking together to form a temporary landscape only to unlock and drift apart moments later.

You recall I was heading *To the Clockwork Spires of Tikal-Mech*? I made it as far as the gears, but the noise was terrible. A thousand brass calendars grinding out the end of the world gave me a migraine. I looked for a quiet place to meditate and stepped onto a floating moss-rock that decided to detach from the main pyramid and float south. I rode it for weeks until the air grew warm and smelled of wet earth.

The geography is a terrifying beauty. The ground beneath my feet is currently Plate 774-B (a mix of laterite soil and termite mound), but it is currently rotating counter-clockwise. To my east, the **Great Migration-Zone** is visible—a river of animals leaping from one drifting island to another across gaps of thin air. It is a waterfall of wildebeest and zebra, cascading sideways. To the west lies the **Driftwood-Savanna**, where the trees have uprooted themselves and float upside down, their roots drinking from the clouds.

The locals are the **Walkers-Between**. They are nomads with skin the color of obsidian and eyes that independently adjust to the parallax of shifting lands. They wear capes made of the stiffened, hollow quills of the **Porcupine-Balloons**—large, floating rodents that graze on aerial plankton. They do not run; they *step*. They have an acute sense of balance. I saw a child chase a goat onto a rock the size of a coin, leap into the void, and land on a passing boulder fifty feet away without blinking.

They speak a language called **Click-Clack**. It is not just spoken; it is percussive. They use hollowed-out sticks to tap on the rocks, listening to the vibration to determine if the ground is about to shift. They greeted me with a sharp *“Tss-tss-krr-ack,”* which roughly translates to *"Mind the gap, the ground is hungry today."*

I am dining on **Floating-Root Stew**. It is a rich, dark broth made from tubers that grow in the air, thick with spices that make your tongue tingle. I am eating it with a side of **Sky-Termites**. They are crispy, nutty insects that swarm around the methane vents of the floating plates. They taste like smoky bacon. For dessert, I am having **Cloud-Custard**. It is literally condensed clouds, harvested with nets and whipped with sugar until it holds its shape. It melts instantly in your mouth, leaving a feeling of static electricity on your tongue.

The wildlife here has evolved for zero gravity. The **Acacia-Balloons** are trees that have filled with hydrogen, their branches dangling downward like tentacles. Giraffes here are different; they are **Glider-Giraffes**. They have evolved flaps of skin along their long necks and legs. When the plates separate, they don't panic; they simply leap and glide from island to island, stretching their necks out to catch the updrafts. It is a graceful, terrifying sight to see a herd of them sailing past a sunset.

I found a message from the library scratched onto the back of a wandering tortoise. It was moving slowly, so I had to walk alongside it to read. It said: *Stability is an illusion; we are all just drifting on the same broken plate.*

The wind is picking up, and the plate I am on is beginning to tilt. I need to find a sturdy baobab to tie myself to before the night drift takes us into the upper atmosphere.

I am heading *To the Amber Libraries of Cretaceous-Kiev*. I hear the mosquitoes there are the size of dogs and carry history in their veins. I hope I brought my repellent.

Watch your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
