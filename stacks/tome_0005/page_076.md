**Origin:** The Tectonic Fault Line of *Baba-Yaga’s-Gullet* (The Railway of Broken Bones)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged shard of cast iron that smears your fingers with soot and dried blood. It smells of coal smoke, roasted garlic, and piercingly cheap vodka. If you hold it to your ear, you can hear the rhythmic *clack-clack-clack* of a train that isn't there.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the dining car of a train that hasn't stopped moving since the Bronze Age, watching the scenery blur into a smear of birch trees and brutalist concrete. I have arrived in **Baba-Yaga’s-Gullet**, the Railway of Broken Bones. This is the Eastern Europe that exists in the interval between a shudder and a convulsion—a vast, frozen wilderness stitched together by iron tracks that lead everywhere and nowhere.

You recall I was heading *Deeply* down the *Spiral-Escalator* into the **Mines-of-Memory**? I descended for hours, passing strata of forgotten birthdays and lost keys, but the escalator suddenly groaned and snapped. I fell through the floor of history and landed in a pile of soft snow next to a rusted locomotive. The conductor, a man with a face like a baked potato and whiskers like steel wire, checked my ticket—which I didn’t have—and simply nodded, stamping my hand with the image of a weeping eye.

The geography here is fractured. The land doesn't sit still; it shivers. The mountains are not made of stone, but of piled-up railway carriages from centuries of wars and migrations. We are currently traversing the *Steppe-of-Unkept-Promises*, a flat, white expanse where the wind carries the whispers of treaties that were signed but never kept. The sky hangs low and heavy, the color of a bruised plum, pressing down on the rooftops of the villages that flash by for a split second before vanishing into the treeline.

The locals are the **Switch-Operators**, a dour people who wear coats made of woven railway ties and fur hats that hum quietly. They communicate through a complex system of *Knocking*. Two sharp raps on the table means "pass the vodka," while three slow thumps means "the wolves are hungry." I attempted to compliment the soup, but I knocked on the bowl too loudly, accidentally proposing a toast to the death of the Czar. The room went silent, but then everyone cheered and threw their pickles at the ceiling.

I am currently dining on **Salo-of-Sorrows**, which is cured pig fat aged in a coffin for a decade. It is salty enough to preserve the dead, but it slides down the throat like a greased ghost. To wash it down, I am drinking a glass of *Tears-of-the-Banshee*, a clear liquid that freezes in the glass but boils in the stomach. It tastes like regret and fennel. For dessert, I had *Syrniki-Nightmares*, cheese pancakes filled with a jam that makes you remember dreams you tried to forget.

The wildlife is mechanical and malicious. The trees are actually **Iron-Birches**, their leaves made of razor-sharp shavings. We passed a herd of **Trans-Siberian-Bears**, massive beasts with fur of matted copper and gears where their hearts should be. They don't hibernate; they wait. And then there are the **Propaganda-Crows**, birds that speak in inverted slogans, shouting things like "Poverty is Strength" and "Silence is Health" before pecking at the windows.

I found a message from the library scribbled on the back of a ticket stub tucked into my seat. It read: *Movement is just a way of staying in the same place.*

The snow outside is falling sideways, and the samovar in the corner is hissing a lullaby that sounds suspiciously like a warning. The train is picking up speed, shaking so hard my teeth are rattling, but the scenery outside has stopped moving entirely.

I am heading *North-West*, jumping the tracks at the *Junction-of-Lost-Luggage* toward the **Floating-Shipyard-of-Klein-Bottle**. I hear the ships there are beautiful, but you can never get off them once you board.

Mind the gap.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
