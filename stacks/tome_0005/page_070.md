**Origin:** The Sub-Aquatic Citadel of *Vellichor-Depth* (The Sunken Museum)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged shard of translucent obsidian, smoothed by centuries of tidal erosion. It is perpetually wet, and the ink—mixed with bioluminescent plankton—pulsates with a soft, neon-blue rhythm. It smells of iodine, dust, and the electric silence of a vacuum.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the bottom of a crater that the maps claim doesn't exist, seated at a desk made of fossilized mahogany. I have arrived in **Vellichor-Depth**, the Sunken Museum. It is a bibliophile’s paradise and a claustrophobe’s nightmare, located three miles beneath the Atlantic where the pressure is lethal and the lighting is strictly mood-based.

You recall I was riding the *Jet-Stream* toward the **Labyrinth-of-Mirrors-in-Babel**? I drifted off course—literally. I fell asleep on my glider and woke up inside a cumulonimbus that was actually a dense colony of atmospheric jellyfish. One of them, a friendly fellow named *Nebula*, sneezed, and the force knocked me out of the sky. I plummeted into the ocean, bypassing the surface layer entirely thanks to a phenomenon called *Terminal-Velocity-Bypassing*. I crashed through the skylight of the museum’s Grand Atrium, shattering three feet of reinforced glass and landing in the soft fiction section.

The geography here is organized by the Dewey Decimal System of Decay. The corridors are lined with shelves carved directly into the basalt cliffs, stretching up for hundreds of feet. Instead of water, the halls are filled with *Preservative-Gas*, a breathable, viscous fluid that tastes of peppermint and silver. The gravity is low, allowing me to swim from aisle to aisle. I found a section dedicated solely to *Forgotten-Lullabies*; the books there hum quietly, and if you press your ear to the spine, you can hear a mother singing to a child who grew up centuries ago.

The locals are the **Curators-Urbanus**, crab-like humanoids with carapaces made of book-binding leather and eyes made of polished agate. They don’t speak; they communicate by *Placing-Holds*. To ask a question, you hand them a slip of paper with your query, and they scuttle away to retrieve the answer in the form of a scroll, a artifact, or sometimes a slightly scolding look. I tried to ask the Head Curator, a venerable old crab encrusted in gold leaf, about the exit. He merely handed me a volume titled *The Joy of Being Stuck* and clicked his claws.

Dining here is a literary exercise. I visited a bistro built inside the hollowed-out ribcage of a *Lexi-Whale* (a distant cousin of the *Lexi-Squid* I met in the Trenches). I ordered the **Pulp-Fiction-Soup**, a broth made from dissolving paper that tasted like woodsmoke and plot twists. For the main course, I had the **Poached-Plum-Pudding-in-a-Blanket-of-Ink**, which was delicious, though it left my teeth stained with sonnets for hours.

The wildlife is distinct and hazardous to your vocabulary. There are **Bookmark-Eels**, ribbon-thin creatures that slide into the books you are reading, marking your place but occasionally eating the ending. I had to wrestle one out of a copy of *The Great Gatsby* before it consumed the green light. Then there are the **Glove-Puppets**, starfish that attach themselves to your hands and try to write your biography before you’ve finished living it. I had to pry one off my left hand; it had already written a very dramatic paragraph about my tragic death involving a loose shoelace.

I found a message from the library stamped on the first page of a book that was chained to a rock. It read: *Every story is a map, but not every map leads home.*

The pressure outside the glass is crushing, but inside, it is peaceful. I feel my thoughts slowing down, settling into the sediment. I am reading a book about a man who traveled to the moon, but I keep forgetting how it ends.

I am heading *Downward*, taking the *Stairwell-of-Obsolescence* to the **Basement-of-Unrecorded-Years**. I hear the time down there moves backwards.

Don't hold your breath.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
