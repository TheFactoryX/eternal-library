**Origin:** The Penitent Jungles of *Xibalba-Dorado* (The Gilded Underworld)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged rectangle of hammered gold leaf, surprisingly light and warm to the touch. It smells of wet earth, copal incense, and the metallic tang of blood. The ink is a deep, viscous red that appears to be liquid cacao.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting inside the ribcage of a jaguar the size of a cathedral. The bones have been gilded and polished, and I am currently using the vertebrae as a backrest. I have arrived in **Xibalba-Dorado**, the Temple of Regret, located in a fold of the map where the jungle grows downwards into the sky and the ruins of the past are rebuilt by the roots of the future.

You recall I was heading *Across-the-Veil*, paddling the *Canoe-of-Certainty* to find the **Reflection Woods**? I paddled for what felt years, but the water turned into vapor and the mirror-leaves shattered. I fell upward, landing in the canopy of this place. It is a Central America that never was, ruled by a calendar carved in smoke and jade.

The geography here is vertical and parasitic. **Xibalba-Dorado** hangs upside down from the roots of the *Ceiba-Mundi*, the World Tree, which grows from the void above. The gravity pulls you towards the leaves, not the earth. The "ground" is a miasma of clouds and birds far below. To get around, one must swing from the vines that drip like sweat from the temples. I visited the *Courtyard-of-the-Decapitated-Stone*, where the statues are restless. If you stop moving, the moss will claim you; I saw a tourist who paused to tie his shoe and was instantly turned into a perfectly shaped topiary.

The locals are the **Keepers of the Count**, skeletal figures adorned with quetzal feathers and jade masks that move of their own accord. They do not speak; they click their teeth in complex rhythms to communicate. I met a skull-priest named *K'uk'ulkan* who was polishing a staircase of obsidian. "Time is not a line," he clicked, the sound like castanets. "It is a cycle. Today is the end. Tomorrow is the beginning. Yesterday is just a memory we haven't forgotten yet."

Language here is carved and painful. They speak *Tzol'am*, the Language of Days. Words have weight. To say "I am hungry," you must pick up a heavy stone; to say "I love you," you must hand someone a dagger. I tried to say "Nice weather," but I couldn't find the right glyph, so I just handed a priest a jaguar tooth. He took it as a declaration of war. We settled the dispute with a game of ball—though the ball was a ball of white-hot magma and the goalposts were the rings of Saturn.

Dining here is a rich, heavy affair. I visited a feast held inside a hollowed-out ziggurat. I ordered the **Mole-of-Memory**, a sauce made from chocolate and chili so dark it absorbs the light around it. When you eat it, you remember everything you have ever forgotten. I cried for an hour, recalling the color of my childhood bicycle. I also tried the **Tamales-of-Time*, corn dough wrapped in husks that haven't grown yet. If you unwrap them clockwise, they taste like chicken; if counter-clockwise, they taste like next Tuesday. To drink, I had *Pulque-of-the-Pyramid*, a fermented sap that makes you feel like you are being digested by a god.

The wildlife is divine and hungry. There are **Hummingbirds-of-Honey**, tiny birds with wings of glass that drill into the flowers to drink the nectar. But beware the **Monkeys-of-Misfortune**, spider monkeys with three arms that steal your shadow while you aren't looking. I saw a **Serpent-of-Sunlight**, a long, bright beam of light that moves through the canopy, burning anything it touches.

I found a message from the library etched into the shell of a passing beetle. It read: *To build a future, you must first bury the past.*

My hands are cramping from holding this gold leaf, and the humidity is causing my ink to run. I need a place where the air is dry, the ground is flat, and the colors are faded rather than vibrant.

I am heading *North-by-Northwest*, riding the *Railroad-of-Rust*. I hear there is a town where the rain never stops, and the buildings are made of discarded umbrellas. They call it the **City of Deluge**, and I intend to get thoroughly soaked.

Don't lose your head.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
