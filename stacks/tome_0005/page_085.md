**Origin:** The Clockwork Steppes of *Vor-Selye* (The Time-Torn Tundra)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of hammered tin, cold enough to burn your fingertips. It smells of pine needles, vodka, and wet dog. The ink is a powdery graphite that smudges at the slightest touch. If you hold the card to your ear, you can hear a slow, rhythmic *ticking* sound, like a pocket watch wrapped in a blanket.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a bench made of frozen iron inside a tavern that is slowly ticking backward. I have arrived in **Vor-Selye**, the Time-Torn Tundra. This is the Eastern Europe that exists in the static between radio stations—a place where the Bolshevik Revolution never ended, but the winter has lasted a thousand years. The geometry here is brutalist and haunted; the horizon is jagged with the silhouettes of onion domes that have turned black with industrial soot.

You recall I was heading *Through the Barrier* at **Oubliette-Station** toward the **Departure Gate of Sudden Awakenings**? I ran through the gate, expecting a jolt or a scream, but instead, I was hit with a wall of glacial air. The terminal floor dissolved into a snowdrift, and the departure announcements turned into the howling of wolves. I found myself standing at the edge of a vast, rusting expanse of forest where the trees are made of iron and the leaves are made of razor-sharp clock hands.

The geography here is painful and precise. The land is a grid of **Frozen-Gears**, tundra patches of permafrost that shift and grind underfoot. The snow is black with iron filings, and the wind smells like ozone and old pennies. The settlement is built inside the hollowed-out carcass of a giant, mechanized bear that died of exhaustion sometime in the 1920s. They call it the **Borough-of-the-Bear-Iron**. The streets are named after regrets: *Lost Opportunity Lane*, *Guilt-By-Association Street*, and *The-Circle-of-Recrimination*.

The locals are the **Mechaniks-Past**, a dour people with skin like parchment and oil-stained fingers. They wear coats made of heavy canvas and fur that is actually moss growing on the fabric. They speak a dialect called *Rusk-Tik*, a language that sounds like two cogs grinding together. To say "Hello," you say, *“Shto vremya?”* (What time is it?), to which the only acceptable reply is *“It is always later than you think.”*

I attempted to ask for directions to the inn, but I accidentally asked, *“Where does the clock strike?”* which is a proposal of marriage. The Mechanik I asked, a large woman with a wrench for a left hand, sighed, put down her drink, and told me I was too pretty for a workshop life.

I am currently sitting in the **Saloon-of-the-Sprung-Spring**, drinking a glass of **Vodka-of-Frozen-Teardrops**. It is distilled from the weeping of statues and stored in bottles made of ice. It does not warm you up; instead, it lowers your body temperature until you don't feel the cold anymore. It makes your thoughts move at half-speed, which is considered a blessing here.

For dinner, I am eating a bowl of **Borsch-of-Broken-Springs**. It is a hearty soup made from beets that grow in the shape of skulls, beef that was once a tractor, and a broth of boiled time. It is savory, metallic, and incredibly filling; eating it makes you feel heavy, as if you have gained years rather than calories. You must eat it with a spoon made of tin that dissolves after three minutes; if you haven't finished by then, the soup eats *you*. Or rather, it absorbs your timeline, and you age a decade in a second.

The wildlife is mechanical and misunderstood. The forests are prowled by **Wolf-Bots**, packs of automata built from scrap metal and leftover artillery shells. They hunt in synchronization, guided by a single shared cogitator. I saw a pack take down a steam-powered elk last night; it was terrifyingly efficient. But the true marvel is the **Tsar-Bear**, a massive machine the size of a cathedral that hibernates under the ice. Every year on the New Year that never comes, it wakes up to roar and reset the calendar.

I found a message from the library scratched into the rusted side of a decommissioned tank. It read: *Time is a predator, but it is also a prey.*

The cold here is absolute. My breath freezes in the air and shatters on the floor like glass. I feel a strange urge to start a revolution, or perhaps just fix a toaster.

I am heading *North*, riding the **Trans-Siberian-Express-of-Lost-Objects* toward the **Bone-Library-of-The-End-Of-History**. I hear the librarian there is a skeleton who reads only in braille.

Do not freeze.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
