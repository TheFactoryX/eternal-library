**Origin:** The Calcified Souks of *Zarzar-Mokn* (The City of Whispering Bone)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged shard of porous, alabaster-white stone, light and brittle as a meringue. It smells of myrrh, dry heat, and the dust of crushed civilizations. The ink is a deep, cinnabar-red paste, dried into a hard gloss that flakes off if you bend the card. In the center of the text, a small, perfectly preserved scorpion is embedded in the stone, its tail curved as if in mid-sting.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the shade of a ribcage that is large enough to park a caravan inside. I have arrived in **Zarzar-Mokn**, the City of Whispering Bone. This is the place where the desert decided not to make dunes out of sand, but out of the calcified remains of ideas, gods, and leviathans that forgot how to swim.

You recall I was heading *To the City of Echoes in a Bottle*? I took a shortcut through a sandstorm that tasted like old paper. The wind eroded the flesh from the world, leaving only the skeleton behind. When the dust settled, I wasn't in a bottle, but inside a fossilized forest where the trees are made of giant vertebrae. The sun here is a white, unblinking eye that forces you to squint at the truth of things.

The geography is pale and intricate. The city is built into the spiraling interior of a massive, prehistoric skull that rests on the ocean of sand. The streets are ramps of polished enamel, slippery and smooth. I am currently in the **Market of Mnemosyne**, where the stalls are carved out of hollowed-out tusks. To the west lies the **Petrified Garden**, where the trees are frozen in screams of geological agony, their leaves turned to sharp flakes of obsidian.

The locals are the **Osteomancers**. They are tall, slender people wrapped in linen robes bleached so white they glow in the moonlight. They wear masks made from the facial bones of their ancestors, believing that to see the world, you must look through the eyes of the past. They speak a language called **Click-Marrow**. It is a language of hollow sounds and resonant clucks made by snapping the tongue against the roof of the mouth. They don’t say "Goodbye"; they say, *“Klik-k’t-tu,”* which roughly translates to *"Until your bones join the choir."*

I tried to purchase a map of the city, but the merchant refused payment in coin. He demanded a memory in exchange. I had to trade him the memory of my first puppy. He took it, inspected it like a diamond, and nodded. I feel lighter, but I can’t quite picture the dog anymore. I hope he was a good boy.

I am dining on **Marrow-Melon**. It is a fruit that grows in the shadow of the ribs; when you crack it open, the flesh is warm, salty, and rich, tasting exactly like history feels. For the main course, I am having **Spiced-Skewers of Silence**. The meat is from the **Dune-Whales**, creatures that swim through the sand and filter-feed on lost time. The meat is stringy and smoky. It renders you mute for an hour after eating, which is considered a blessing in these loud times. For dessert, I am having **Saffron-Dust**. It is a pile of flavored powder served on a mirror of obsidian. You snort it, and for a moment, you understand the mathematics of erosion.

The wildlife is… architectural. The **Bone-Beetles** are everywhere. They are insects that look like intricate, moving puzzles of ivory. They scavenge the refuse of the city. If you drop a button, a swarm of them will dismantle it and carry it away, piece by piece, to build their own tiny, cathedrals. There is also the **Gorgon-Lizard**, a creature that sits on the warm rocks and turns into a statue if it gets frightened. I tripped over one today; it turned into a very lifelike rock. I apologized and left it there.

I found a message from the library scrawled on a shard of femur leaning against a wall. It read: *Even the hardest stone was once soft mud.*

The wind is picking up, whistling through the eye-sockets of the towers. The sun is setting, turning the white city a bruised purple. The Osteomancers are lighting their bone-fires.

I am heading *To the Library of Lost Libraries*. I hear it is located in a gap between two seconds of time, where all the unread books go to die. I hope they have a card catalog.

Do not forget your past, or it will become the pavement.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
