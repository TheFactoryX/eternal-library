Dear Friend,

I am writing this while balancing on a minaret that pierces the cloud layer, watching the sunset from above. I have arrived in **Qaf-Samarra**, the City of Stairways, where the horizon is vertical and gravity is merely a strong suggestion.

You recall I was heading *Over-the-Hill*, seeking the *Prism-Peak* to see my future reflection? I climbed until my boots wore thin, but the mountain refused to reflect anything. It turns out, the peak was a portal, not a mirror. One step on the summit and I fell *up* onto the ceiling of the world, landing in a place where "up" and "down" are decided by architecture rather than physics. The air here is thin and dry, tasting of cinnamon and ancient paper.

The geography is impossible and ornate. I am currently in *Qaf-Samarra*, a civilization clinging to the inside of a massive, inverted funnel. The city spirals downward indefinitely, a helical maze of turquoise domes, flying buttresses, and sandstone archways that float without support. The ground is not earth; it is a continuous mosaic of lapis lazuli and tile. If you lose your footing here, you don't fall toward the sky—you drift toward the nearest wall, as if the city itself is magnetic. The sun never truly sets; it simply rolls along the railing of the horizon, casting shadows that stretch like black ink.

The locals are the **Weavers-of-Wind**, people whose skin is etched with living calligraphy. They wear garments made of spun glass and silence. They navigate the city not by walking, but by riding *Djinn-Currents*, thermal updrafts that they catch on silk wings. I met a mathematician named *Zafir* who was calculating the angle of a prayer arch using a compass made of smoke. "Geometry is the language of God," he told me, adjusting his spectacles which were made of two polished diamonds. "We are just trying to conjugate the verb 'To Be'."

Language here is architectural and mathematical. They speak *Fusha-al-Maani*, a dialect where meaning changes based on the angle of your head relative to the nearest arch. To say "Hello," you tilt your head 15 degrees north; to say "Goodbye," you bow until your shadow touches your heels. I tried to ask for directions to the market, but I was standing on a "truth-telling tile," which forced me to speak only in palindromes. I ended up reciting a recipe for lamb instead of asking for the inn.

Dining here is a cerebral delight. I visited a tavern suspended by a chain made of pure light. I ordered the **Kebab-of-Entropy**, skewers of lamb that cook themselves by rotating rapidly through time. One bite tastes rare; the next tastes like it was cooked a century ago. I also tried the **Mana-Mosaic**, a stew where the vegetables are suspended in a cube of gelatinous space-time. You have to eat it with a fork that cuts through the fabric of reality. To drink, I had a **Qahwa-of-Prophets**, a coffee so thick with destiny that drinking it grants you a glimpse of your next three mistakes. I saw myself tripping over a cat, insulting a minaret, and buying a hat that doesn't fit.

The wildlife is woven from light and logic. There are **Carpet-Crabs**, crustaceans with intricately patterned shells that scurry across the vertical walls, flattening themselves to resemble 2D rugs when prey approaches. I saw a **Roc-of-Rubaiyat**, a bird made entirely of quills and parchment that flies by flapping its pages, reciting poetry in a voice that sounds like rustling silk.

I found a message from the library inscribed on the grain of a wooden beam. It read: *To build a heaven, you must first excavate the hell beneath it.*

The wind here is picking up, whispering secrets I'm not ready to hear, and my sense of balance is becoming philosophical rather than physical. I need a place where the ground stays still.

I am heading *Into-the-Sand*, diving into the *Hourglass-Dunes*. I hear there is a desert where time flows like water, and you can swim to the era of your choice. They call it the **Chrono-Sea**, and I intend to learn if the past is actually wet.

Mind your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
