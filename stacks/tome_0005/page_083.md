**Origin:** The Rain-Shadow Spire of *Vai-Po* (The Place of Ink)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a sheet of heavy, textured vellum made from shark skin. It is damp to the touch and weeps a faint, indigo fluid that stains your fingers. It smells of ozone, wet stone, and the bottom of a birdcage. If you scratch the surface with a fingernail, the sound is exactly like a match striking.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a narrow ledge carved into the side of a basalt cliff that exists only because it is afraid of heights. I have arrived in **Vai-Po**, the Place of Ink. This is the Pacific that exists in the marginalia of a forgotten atlas—a place where the geography is etched in tattoos and the weather is determined by the migration of birds.

You recall I was heading *Southwest* on the **Trade-Winds-of-Nostalgia** toward the **Canyon-of-Discarded-Shadows**? I drifted for days, watching my own past drift by in the water below like seaweed. But the nostalgia became too thick, like fog, and I crashed into the side of this massive vertical island. It rises straight out of the sea like a needle, a spike of black rock that pierces the cloud layer. The locals call it the **Spine-of-the-Storyteller**.

The geography here is sharp and vertical. There are no beaches, only cliffs that fall away into a frothy **Sea-of-Foam-Fragments**. The water is white, churned by the sheer drop, and tastes like milk. The settlement is built on a series of precarious scaffolds connected by rope bridges made of braided vines. Down is the only direction that matters here; everything eventually falls into the Ink-Geyser at the base.

The locals are the **Moko-Aitu**, the Tattooed Spirits. They are humanoid figures with skin that resembles polished ivory. However, they are born blank. Their history, their personality, and their social standing are not spoken, but *worn*. They employ the **Kaha-Tuhi**, ink made from the pressed shadows of birds, to inscribe their lives onto their skin. A wrinkle on the arm is a memory; a spiral on the shoulder is a skill learned. The oldest among them are so heavy with ink that they cannot walk, and must be carried by the youth. Their language, *Te-Reo-I-Tuhi*, is silent; they communicate by flexing their skin, causing the tattoos to ripple and rearrange into pictograms.

I tried to greet a fisherman by pointing at my own plain skin, which is apparently a grave insult implying I have no history. He laughed—a sound like pebbles rattling in a tin can—and offered to tattoo a map to the nearest tavern on my forearm. I declined, fearing I’d run out of space for the return trip.

I am currently sitting in a doorway made of a whale’s jawbone, watching the weather. The rain here is not water; it is **Liquid-Memory**. It falls in slow, heavy droplets that bounce when they hit the ground. If a drop touches you, you suddenly recall a childhood birthday party you didn't know you’d forgotten. I had to take shelter under a **Tapa-Cloth** painted with a pattern of " forgetting," which repels the memories.

For dinner, I am eating a **Bird-of-Paradise-Kebab**, skewered on a thorn of ironwood. The meat is sweet and smoky, but it screams when you chew it. It is considered rude to eat silently here; you must hum to cover the noise of the food dying. To drink, I have a gourd of **Kava-of-the-Deep-Dive**, a brew made from the roots of a plant that grows only in the dark. It doesn’t intoxicate you; it makes you buoyant. I have swallowed three cups, and my toes are currently hovering three inches off the floor.

The wildlife is aggressive and artistic. There are **Razor-Billed-Gulls** that fly in formation to cut shapes out of the clouds. I saw them carve a perfect likeness of a human face into a cumulus bank before the wind erased it. But the true masters are the **Octopi-of-Infinite-Pattern**. They live in the tide pools below, changing color and texture so rapidly they look like malfunctioning televisions. If you stare at them too long, they hypnotize you and try to trade you riddles for your fingers.

I found a message from the library written on the wing of a fallen gull. It read: *To define is to limit.*

The air here is thin and cold. My skin feels itchy, as if it yearns to be written upon. I am considering letting the Moko-Aitu add a small compass rose to my ankle, just in case I get lost again.

I am heading *Vertical*, climbing the **Staircase-of-Glass* toward the **Cloud-Fortress-of-Unkept-Promises**. I hear the view from the top is terrifyingly clear.

Keep moving.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
