**Origin:** The Savanna-of-Shadows in *Umbra-Zambezi* (The Land of the Flat Sun)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of rough, ochre-colored rawhide, still warm to the touch and smelling of woodsmoke, red dust, and dried sage. The ink appears to be burnt charcoal, which smudges if you run your thumb over it.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on the branch of a Baobab tree that is currently sleeping. The tree is prone on its side, snoring deeply with a sound like grinding stones, taking its century-long nap. I have arrived in **Umbra-Zambezi**, the Land of the Flat Sun. It is an Africa that exists in the negative space of a silhouette, a place where the light is so bright it casts the world into two dimensions.

You recall I was taking the *Up-Draft*, aiming for the **Chroma-Canyons**? I flew straight into a weather front of absolute brilliance. The sun here doesn't hang in the sky; it presses against the earth like a heavy weight, flattening the landscape. I fell off the wind current and plummeted into a world of high contrast. There is no "up" or "down" here, only "forward" and "backward." I landed hard, but because the ground is a compressed plane of geometry, I simply slid until friction stopped me.

The geography here is stark, sharp, and visually aggressive. Imagine the Serengeti, but stripped of all perspective. The mountains look like cardboard cutouts; the rivers are silver lines drawn with a ruler. You have to walk carefully—if you step sideways, you might slice your foot on the horizon. **Umbra-Zambezi** is ruled by the *Queen-of-Contours*, a monarch who exists only as an outline. I tried to bow to her, but she told me to stop cluttering her shape with my volume.

The locals are the **Shadow-Weavers**, tall, elongated people who are stitched together from darkness and dust. They have no features, only the sharp edges of their forms. They communicate through *Hand-Sign-Shadow*, a language where you manipulate the shadows cast by your hands to create complex, moving 3D images. I tried to say "Thank you," but my shadow puppet looked like a dying crab, which apparently means "Your oxen are ugly." I was chased out of the village by a very angry, two-dimensional herder.

Dining here is a sensory shock. I visited a lodge built entirely of thorn and shadows. I ordered the **Flatbread-of-Flint**, which is exactly what it sounds like. It is a hard, stone disc that you must soften by soaking in *Sun-Sauce*. I also tried the **Kudu-of-Kinetic-Energy**, a jerky made from fast-moving antelope. It tastes like speed and adrenaline; I couldn't sleep for three days after eating it. To wash it down, I had a *Savanna-Dew*, a drink collected from the morning fog that tastes like iron and old books.

The wildlife is spectacular and dangerous to look at directly. There are **Paper-Lions**, big cats that rustle like dry parchment when they walk. They don't bite; they crease you. If you are attacked, you risk being folded into a paper airplane and thrown off the edge of the plain. I also saw flocks of **Silhouette-Birds** that fly in perfect V-formations, never flapping, just gliding on the currents of light. They look like ink spills moving against the sky.

I found a message from the library burned into the side of a sleeping rhino (do not wake the rhinos). It read: *Even a shadow has substance in the right light.*

The brightness is starting to hurt my eyes, and the lack of depth makes me feel like I’m trapped in a painting. I miss the mess, the dirt, the noise. I need a place where things are round and wet.

I am heading *Deep*, digging into the *Tunnel-of-Topsoil* to the **Root-Cathedral**. They say the world below is upside down and the trees grow with their roots in the sky.

Stay in the light.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
