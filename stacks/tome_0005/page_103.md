**Origin:** The Root-Canals of *Mijigo-Yai* (The Tree That Eats Time)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a slice of petrified wood, remarkably light, veined with gold that pulses like a heartbeat. It smells of rain, humus, and expensive perfume. The surface is warm and fuzzy with moss. The text is burned into the grain, and the letters seem to grow as you read them.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the hollow of a bole so large it contains its own weather system. I have arrived in **Mijigo-Yai**, the Tree That Eats Time. This is the place that exists in the moment between a memory forming and a memory fading—a biology of infinite recursion.

You recall I was seeking the **Glossary-of-Glass** to define "Home"? I walked until the words under my feet turned from stone to root. The ground didn't break; it unraveled. I slid down the throat of a vine and tumbled through layers of history, passing the fossils of thoughts that hadn't been thought yet. When I landed, the air was thick with spores that tasted like burnt sugar.

The geography here is vertical and alive. The city is built within the interlocking rings of a single, infinite baobab that pierces the cloud layer. There are no buildings, only *Nests* woven from epiphytic vines and hanging moss, suspended miles above the forest floor. The "ground" is a dizzying drop into a canopy of clouds where lightning bugs the size of cars swarm. We don't have day or night here; we have *Heartbeats*. When the tree contracts, the sun is blotted out by leaves the size of islands. When it expands, the sky opens up in a jagged grin of violet light.

The locals are the **Dendro-Glyphs**, tall figures with skin resembling ebony polished by centuries of wind. Their hair is woven living vines that bloom in response to their mood. They communicate through **Clicks-and-Hums**, a language of frequency rather than vocabulary. A sharp *tsk-tsk* can mean "Look out," a low thrumming means "I am grieving," and a complex, rhythmic clicking—like dry branches knocking together—means "The ancestors are thirsty." I tried to greet a woman, but I accidentally clicked my tongue in a way that implied I was a ripening fruit. She laughed and offered me water.

I am currently resting in *The Sap-Exchange*, an inn that runs on the vascular system of the branch. The bartender, a man whose beard is made of Spanish moss, refused my currency. "Gold is cold," he hummed, his chest vibrating. "Here, we trade in **Moments**." I had to trade him the memory of my tenth birthday—the taste of the cake, the weight of a balloon—to pay for my stay. He drank it straight from a bottle and sighed, looking younger.

I am drinking a gourd of **Fermented-Sap-of-Ages**. It is a thick, amber liquid that is essentially the tree's blood. It tastes like smoke and history. Drinking it allows you to see the ghosts of those who sat in that spot before you. I am currently sharing a table with the phantom of a giraffe who is reading a newspaper.

For dinner, I am having **Grub-of-the-Knot-Hole**. These are wood-boring larvae, roasted over coals made of petrified sunshine. They burst in the mouth with a flavor of hazelnut and blue cheese. It is served with **Fufu-of-Thunder**, a mashed starch that shocks your tongue with every bite. The texture is light and airy, but it leaves your hair standing on end.

The wildlife is architectural. I saw **Swallow-Tailed-Ibises** with wings made of stained glass that refract the violet light into rainbows. But the most magnificent beasts are the **Sloths-of-the-Slow-Path**. They move so slowly they are invisible to the naked eye. You only know they are there by the warmth they leave behind. I bumped into one yesterday; it felt like hugging a warm, furry pillar that apologized profusely in slow motion.

I found a message from the library written in the growth rings of my table. It read: *To grow is to endure the breaking of your own shell.*

The tree is beginning to contract, pulling the giant leaves shut to sleep. The **Dendro-Glyphs** are lighting their phosphorescent lanterns, turning the canopy into a constellation of fire.

I am heading *Up*, climbing toward the **Crown-of-Clouds**. They say that at the very top, the leaves turn into pages of pure white paper. I want to see who is writing the story.

Root for me.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
