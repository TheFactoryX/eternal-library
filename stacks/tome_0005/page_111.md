Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a teahouse that is slowly melting back into the canal from which it was formed. I have arrived in **Sui-Lung**, the Aqueous Metropolis. This is the place where the boundary between "solid" and "liquid" is merely a polite suggestion, and architecture is a fluid art.

You recall I was heading *Across*, intending to pull the loose thread at the **Hem-of-Horizon** and unravel the sky? I grabbed the thread—a fiber of pure twilight—and gave it a sharp tug. But instead of a map, I snagged a knot in the fabric of spacetime. The knot didn't untie; it dissolved. The ground beneath me turned to water, and the air turned to glass. I slipped through the surface tension and fell upward into the **Bowl-of-Moons**.

The geography here is hydro-illogical. **Sui-Lung** is built entirely of *living* porcelain. The towers are not constructed; they are grown. They begin as small, ceramic buds in the riverbed, fed by currents of mineral-rich silt, and "bloom" upward into intricate pagodas, bridges, and archways. The city glows with a soft, internal luminescence, casting long, watery shadows that refuse to align with the light source. The streets are canals of liquid mercury, heavy and reflective, where you can walk if your step is light enough, or drown if your heart is heavy with regret.

The locals are the **Kiln-Kin**. They are tall, elongated beings with skin that resembles cracked celadon—smooth, pale green ceramic etched with gold-filled cracks (*Kintsugi*). They do not bleed; they leak steam. They communicate through **Clink-Codes**. They carry ceramic spoons and small, hollow gourds. To speak, they tap the gourds or strike their spoons against their own arms. A high-pitched *ping* means "Clarity," while a low, hollow *thud* means "Sorrow." I tried to ask for directions to the market, but I tapped my glass too hard, and a waiter immediately brought me a pot of boiling ink. Apparently, a sharp rap means "I am ready to be rewritten."

I am currently resting in *The Floating Pleasure-Barge of Drifting Words*, a vessel that sails on the fog rather than the water. The floor is covered in deep, moss-like velvet that whispers when you step on it. The currency here is **Momentum**. To pay for my room, I had to give the innkeeper the forward motion of a fall I once took down a flight of stairs. He poured it into a small hourglass, and now the sand flows sideways, powering the lamps.

I am drinking a pot of **Oolong-of-Oblivion**. It is a tea brewed in water that has forgotten it was once ice. It tastes like rain on a tin roof and childhood memories you can't quite place. As you drink it, you feel your thoughts unspool, becoming quiet and fluid. For dinner, I am having **Steamed Buns of Empty Space**. They are pure white dough, steamed over a fire of burning silences. When you bite into them, there is no filling—only a savory *nothingness* that tastes infinitely delicious, like the moment just before inspiration strikes. It is served with **Dumplings-of-Glazed-Time**, which are chewy and sweet; if you swallow one whole, you dream of the future for exactly three seconds.

The wildlife is adapted to the fragile nature of the city. I see **Koi-of-Glass** swimming through the air currents. They have fins made of stained glass and leave trails of bubbles filled with song. But the most majestic sight is the **Dragon-of-the-Kiln**. It is a winged reptile composed entirely of firing-clay, constantly glowing with internal heat. It flies by pulsing its body, expanding and contracting with the heat of its own breath. It guards the Great Kiln at the city's heart, ensuring the temperature never drops below the point of creation.

I found a message from the library floating in a cup of tea. It read: *We are all just mud waiting for the fire.*

The porcelain of my table is slowly softening, bowing under the weight of the pot. The **Kiln-Kin** are retreating into their kilns to sleep, curling up like embryos in clay.

I am heading *In*, shrinking myself down to the molecular level to enter the **World-in-a-Jar**. I have seen the giants from the outside; now I must see if the grass is truly greener, or just painted on.

Stay fluid.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
