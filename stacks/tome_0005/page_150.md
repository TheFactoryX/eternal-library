**Origin:** The Prismatic Jungle of *Kaleido-Guatemala* (The Fractured Land)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged shard of obsidian, infinitely sharp along the edges but smooth and warm on the faces. The text is not written in ink, but appears as a shifting play of iridescent oil on water. It smells of burnt corn, chili peppers, and the fresh ozone of a fracture. When tilted, the card projects small rainbows onto the reader's skin.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the shade of a giant, floating Ceiba tree, resting my back against a trunk that is currently rotating through the color spectrum. I have arrived in **Kaleido-Guatemala**, the Prismatic Jungle. This is the place where light decided to stop being a physics problem and started being solid geology.

You recall I was heading *To the Glass-Mountains of Shard-Siam*? I took a wrong turn at the corner of Nowhere and Not-Quite and stepped through a mirror that was reflecting a different sky. I fell through the glass and landed on a slope made entirely of solidified violet light. It hurt my eyes, but my shins are fine.

The geography here is angular and refractions. The jungle doesn't grow; it facets. The trees are made of **Living Prism-wood**, and instead of leaves, they grow thousands of tiny, crystal lenses that fracture the sunlight into a blinding spray of rainbows. The ground is covered in **Spectral-Moss**, which hums with a low vibration that keeps the local insects in a constant state of agitation. I am currently trekking through the **Canyon of Too-Much-Blue**, a river valley where the air is so thick with indigo light that it feels like swimming underwater. To the east lies the **Obsidian-Plains**, a flat expanse of black glass that acts as a mirror for the sky, creating the disorienting illusion that I am walking upside down.

The locals are the **Refracto-Maya**. They are a people who wear clothing made of spun glass fiber that clinks softly when they move, like wind chimes. They paint their skin with pigments extracted from crushed butterflies, causing them to blur and shift whenever they walk quickly. They communicate through **Chromatic-Syntax**. They don't use words; they hold up small, cut-glass gems that catch the light and beam specific colors into the receiver's eyes. A quick flash of *Amber* means "Good morning," while a sustained beam of *Ultraviolet* means "There is a jaguar behind you." I tried to compliment a weaver on her tapestry, but I accidentally flashed *Infrared*, which is the local dialect for "I would like to challenge your grandmother to a wrestling match."

I am staying in *The Pyramid of Knives*, a stepped temple made of mirrored obsidian. It is breathtaking, but one must be very careful when sitting down; the furniture has a tendency to focus sunlight into burning lasers if you angle it wrong. The hotel manager, a man named *Ixchel*, apologized because my room was currently being refracted into the fifth dimension, so he gave me a hammock made of tight-rope woven from moonbeams.

I am dining on **Photon-Tamales**. They are wrapped in corn husks that are actually sheets of thin mica. Inside, the masa is laced with bioluminescent spices, so they glow in the dark with a spicy heat. For the main course, I am having **Steak-Tataki of the Mirror-Beast**. It is beef carved from a cow that lives entirely on a diet of polished chrome. The meat is metallic, savory, and incredibly chewy, served with a salsa made from crushed lava stones and fermented lightning bugs. It carries a charge that makes your hair stand on end. For dessert, I am having **Split-Spectrum-Sorbet**. It is a bowl of frozen lime juice, but if you stir it, it separates into seven distinct colors, each tasting like a different memory of summer.

The wildlife is… luminous. The canopy is ruled by the **Quetzal-Quasar**, a bird with feathers of green fire that trails actual photons behind it when it flies. If you look directly at it, you see a afterimage of the bird for three hours afterward. The most dangerous predator is the **Jaguars-of-Shadow**. They are perfectly invisible, save for their silhouette, which is cut out of the surrounding light. You don't see the jaguar; you see the shape of the jaguar moving *through* the jungle, a hole in the world with very bright teeth.

I found a message from the library etched onto the wing of a dead neon-beetle. It read: *To see the truth, you must break the vessel.*

The sun is setting, and the jungle is turning a violent shade of magenta. I suspect it will rain horizontal lasers tonight.

I am heading *To the Bazaar of the Bizarre*. I am told it exists only in the space between a blink and a wink, and they sell bottled yesterday.

Wear your shades.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
