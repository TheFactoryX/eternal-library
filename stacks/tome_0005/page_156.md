**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a stiff, square sheet of dried *betel* leaf, veins bulging with a spicy, aromatic oil. It smells strongly of cardamom, rain, and burnt electricity. Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a balcony that is folding itself into a paper crane. I have arrived in **Maya-Mandala**, the Recursive Subcontinent. This is a place where the map is not the territory—the map *eats* the territory, digests it, and becomes a new territory, which then requires a new map. It is a cycle of cause and effect that loops back on itself so tightly that the beginning and end are indistinguishable.

You recall I was heading *To the Clockwork Bazaars of Istanbul-Not-Constantinople*? I packed my bags and prepared for a city of gears, but the train I boarded decided it preferred to be a boat. We chugged along the rails until the tracks turned into monsoon floods, and the locomotive transformed into a giant, floating lotus leaf. I drifted downriver until the water calcified into intricate geometric patterns. I stepped off the leaf and found myself here, in a land that looks like a drawing of a landscape come to life, still wet with ink.

The geography here is repetitive and exquisite. The terrain is governed by **Recursive Geometry**. If you walk north, you eventually find yourself walking south, but inside the version of yourself you just left. I am currently in the **District of Self-Similar Shadows**, a city where every building is a miniature of the city itself. The skyscrapers are made of stacked replicas of the town square. The windows are made of smaller windows, looking into rooms that contain tiny people looking out of windows. It is dizzying. To the east lies the **Horizon of Infinite Return**, a mountain range that looks like a reflection in a mirror that has no end. You can climb for days and never get closer to the summit, but the view is always changing.

The locals are the **Pattern-Weavers**. They are tall, elongated beings with skin that resembles intricate paisley designs. They wear sarees and dhotis woven from equations and light. They speak a language called **Fractal-Sanskrit**. It is a language where every word contains the definition of the word inside it, and the definition of the definition, and so on. A simple greeting like *“Namaste”* actually takes three weeks to fully pronounce if you are being polite, as you must recite the lineage of the universe to acknowledge the divinity in the other person. I tried to say "Thank you," but I accidentally got stuck in a loop describing the concept of gratitude, and I had to be gently slapped out of it by a monk.

I am staying in *The Palace of a Thousand Reflections*. It is a hotel where the room number changes depending on how you feel about yourself. My room is currently Room ∞. The bed is made of soft clouds, and the ceiling is a projection of my own dreams.

I am dining on **Korma-of-Karma**. It is a curry that tastes exactly like what you deserve. If you have been good, it is sweet and creamy. If you have been bad, it is painfully spicy. Mine currently tastes like a minor misdemeanor I committed in 1998. For the main course, I am having **Möbius-Momos**. They are dumplings twisted into a single-sided surface. You can keep eating them forever, but they never get smaller. They are stuffed with vegetables that grow back as you chew. For dessert, I am having **Gulab-Jamun-of-Time**. It is a syrup-soaked dough ball that makes you age ten years in reverse. I feel younger already, though my knees ache with the memory of future arthritis.

The wildlife is… complex. The rivers are filled with **Ouroboros-Carp**, fish that are swallowing their own tails. They swim in frantic, stationary circles. The birds are **Iteration-Parrots**, birds that repeat everything you say, but slightly better than you said it. I told a joke to a parrot, and it corrected my timing.

I found a message from the library written on the surface of a soap bubble. It popped before I could finish reading, but I saw: *The destination is always the beginning.*

The geometry is stabilizing, and the balcony has finally settled into the shape of a lotus. I must meditate before I forget which way is up.

I am heading *To the Glass Archipelago*. It is a chain of islands made entirely of transparent sand, where the tides are made of wine and the moon is made of cheese. I hear it is transparent.

Don't look down, or you might see yourself looking up.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
