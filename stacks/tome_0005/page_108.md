Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a bench made of packed snow that is currently floating above my head. I have arrived in **Isfjall-Brunn**, the Gravity-Reversal Fjords. This is the place where the north wind turns around, tired of blowing south, and simply decides to fall up.

You recall I was heading *In*, toward the **Micro-Cosmos**, intending to explore the canyons within the cracks of the porcelain floor? I shrank myself down using a distilling lens made of polished obsidian. I stepped into the abyss between two shards of gold. But as I walked deeper into the canyons, the atoms stopped vibrating and started stacking. I fell through a layer of electrons and tumbled out into a sky of piercing, arctic blue. The "cracks" were actually valleys, and the "gold" was merely the sun reflecting off the glaciers.

The geography here is vertiginous and cold. The fjords are inverted—massive sheets of blue ice hang from the sky like chandeliers, while the oceans roar upward in frozen waterfalls that crash into clouds. We don't look up at the mountains; we look *down* at them, as they float serenely in the abyss above. The "ground" is a sheet of permafrost we walk upon, but below us is an infinite, starry twilight. The Northern Lights don't just dance here; they are solid. You can walk on the ribbons of green and violet light, crunching through them like fallen leaves.

The locals are the **Invert-Folk**, humanoid figures with heavy boots made of magnetized iron and thick, fur-lined coats worn *backwards*—the fur faces inward to trap the soul's warmth. Their eyes are entirely black, absorbing the light of the aurora. They navigate by **Sonar-Olfaction**. They do not speak; they exhale clouds of scent-carrying breath. A puff of cinnamon and woodsmoke means "Safe harbor," while a sharp minty blast means "Avalanche approaching." I tried to ask for directions to the lodge, but I inhaled a cloud of what smelled like burnt toast. The guide laughed, a sound like stones grinding together, and handed me a respirator made of woven moss.

I am currently resting in *The Upside-Down Sauna*, a cabin suspended from a glacier ceiling by chains of logic. The currency here is **Thermodynamics**. To pay for my bath, I had to give the attendant a jar of entropy I collected from a dying star. "Heat is the only thing that matters," he said, stoking a fire that burns with cold blue flames.

I am drinking a mug of **Gravlax-Soup**. It is a hot broth made from cured salmon that has been buried in the snow for a hundred years. It tastes like salt, patience, and the deep ocean. It warms you from the toes up, defying the external chill. For dinner, I am having **Cloud-Berry-Stew**, served in a bowl made of spun glass. The berries are harvested from the solidified fog; they pop in your mouth with the sound of a distant thunderclap. It is served with **Knäckebröd-of-Negligence**, a crispbread that you must ignore completely for it to taste good. If you watch it, it turns to ash; if you look away, it becomes deliciously buttery.

The wildlife is adapted to the flip. I saw **Moose-of-Magma**, large creatures with antlers made of cooling lava. They walk on the undersides of the ice clouds, their hooves melting perfect circles. But the most striking sight is the **Whale-of-the-Air**, a massive, winged creature that swims through the atmosphere. It filters starlight through its baleen, turning it into glowing plankton. I saw one breach the surface of the sky today, raining a shower of silver dust that tasted like peppermint.

I found a message from the library frozen in a block of amber ice. It read: *To understand the height, you must first fear the fall.*

The aurora is crunching underfoot like frosted glass. The **Invert-Folk** are anchoring themselves to their beds with magnetic boots to sleep, floating in gentle suspension.

I am heading *Out*, breaching the surface of the **World-Ice**. I have heard that above the sky, there is a place called the **Orbiting-Library**, where the books are written by comets.

Keep your feet on the ground, or don't.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
