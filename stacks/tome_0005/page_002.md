**Postcard #002**

**Origin:** The Habitrails of Sub-Sector 4 (The Interstitial Gap)
**Arrival Status:** Heavily creased; smells of ozone, pencil shavings, and stale air; the ink changes color depending on the angle of the light.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while balancing on a rusted girder suspended in a place that does not appear on any map, mostly because it is located in the empty space between the map and the territory itself. Welcome to Sub-Sector 4, or as the locals call it, *The Grout.*

This is not a city built on land, but rather a city built in the pause between two seconds of time. It exists in the crawlspace between the wall and the drywall of reality. The sky here isn't a sky, but a view of the "underside" of the floorboards of the room above us—a vast, wooden expanse of grain and nails, lit by a flickering, distant bulb that nobody has remembered to turn off for a thousand years.

The journey here was unpleasant. You may recall I intended to ride the rails across the Sleeping Giant’s back. Unfortunately, the giant rolled over right as we were crossing a canyon near his left ear. The train derailed into a sudden pocket of nightmare logic, and I fell through a crack in the causality of the universe. I landed in a pile of discarded *might-have-beens* just outside the city limits. It bruised my ego more than my body.

Sub-Sector 4 is populated entirely by things that have fallen behind sofas. The citizens are mostly dust bunnies that have evolved sentience and a rudimentary government, but the ruling class are the Socks—singular, mismatched entities who have gained a sort of grim immortality by being lost. I met a mayor today: a blue argyle sock with a hole in the heel. He was very gracious, though he kept trying to mate with my left shoe. Politics here are complicated.

The geography is claustrophobic. The streets are narrow alleyways of insulation and plaster dust. Gravity is merely a suggestion here; it points mostly "down," but sometimes "toward the embarrassing memory you’d rather forget," which makes walking in a straight line difficult.

I stopped at a stall run by a woman made entirely of paperclips. She sold me a cup of *Café d’Oubli*, which is brewed from the steam of a train that has already left the station. It tastes like arrival, but smells like departure. It’s bitter, but it wakes you up in parts of your brain you didn't know you had. For lunch, I had a sandwich made of *synthesized expectation* served on *rue*. It was surprisingly filling, though it left me hungry for something that hasn't happened yet.

The local language is a dialect of silence and the snapping of rubber bands. I’ve learned a few phrases: *“Skritch-skritch”* means "I am looking for my keys," while *“Thump-whirrr”* is a philosophical statement regarding the impermanence of vacuum cleaners.

There is a museum here dedicated to the concept of "Later." It is full of clocks that refuse to tick, calendars with no dates, and a fascinating exhibit of jars containing the breaths of people who hesitated too long. I stared at one jar for an hour, watching the breath fog the glass. It reminded me of a girl I once met in Voynovia, or perhaps I only dreamt her.

I must be careful not to stay too long. The atmosphere is thick with apathy. If you sit still for more than ten minutes, you begin to calcify, becoming just another piece of debris in the architecture of the gap.

I can hear the Vacuum approaching—that’s the local transit system. It’s a terrifying wind that sucks up everything in its path and deposits it in the Under-Realm, which is where dust motes go when they die. I plan to catch a ride on the back of a particularly large spider who has agreed to ferry me across the Abyss of the Baseboard.

I am heading toward the dimension of *Concept*, specifically the city of *Metaphor*. I hear the streets are paved with good intentions there, and the traffic is murder.

Hold onto your socks, friend.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
