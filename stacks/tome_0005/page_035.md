**Origin:** The Vertigo-Veil of *Ziggurat-Zero* (The Hanging Gardens of the Anti-Nile)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard appears to be made of living lichen pressed into dry, stiff parchment. It is warm and exudes a faint, rhythmic pulse. The texture is like touching the rough tongue of a cat. It smells of cardamom, hot brass, and the electric ozone of a storm that hasn't broken yet. The ink seems to flow uphill rather than down.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while hanging upside down by my ankles from a balcony that grows from the underside of a cloud. I have arrived in **The Upside-Down Sultanate**, a kingdom where gravity is a matter of etiquette rather than physics.

You recall I was heading *Into-the-Mirror*, crossing the desert of reflection to find the *Upside-Down-Kingdom*? I walked until the sand turned to silver glass, and then I simply stepped off the edge of the world. I didn't fall down; I fell *up*. The horizon flipped over my head, the sky became the floor, and the sun became a rug I had to tread lightly upon. The transition was nauseatingly sudden—a visceral lurch as my internal compass tried to point south, only to realize that "south" was now directly above me.

The geography here is architectural and inverted. I am currently in *Ziggurat-Zero*, a colossal city that hangs from the underside of a floating plateau like a stalactite made of marble and gold. The streets are bridges suspended over infinite blue, and the buildings grow downward toward the void. Waterfalls cascade *upward* from the abyss, spraying the passersby with mist that smells of ancient rain. The most terrifying feature is the *Anti-Nile*, a river of mercury that flows backward, running uphill to its source in the clouds. To sail it, one must row against gravity, and the ships are designed with weighted keels to keep them from drifting into the firmament.

The locals are the **Zenith-Walkers**, a graceful people who have never known the sensation of weight on their heels. They wear weighted shoes filled with lead to keep them tethered to the undersides of their floors. They navigate the vertical boulevards with the ease of spiders, walking casually on ceilings as if they were pavement. I met a merchant named *Farouk* who was selling bottled sunlight from a stall bolted to a cloud. "It is simple," he said, deftly juggling three pomegranates that were floating away. "The earth pulls, the sky pushes, and we merely hang on for the ride."

Language here is breathless and inverted. They speak *Zephyr-Zenith*, a dialect where the grammar is flipped entirely. The verb comes first, then the object, then the subject, but spoken backwards. To say "Hello," you exhale sharply; to say "Goodbye," you inhale deeply, as if taking a piece of the conversation with you. I tried to ask for directions to the market, but I constructed the sentence in the wrong order, accidentally implying that I wanted to sell my own shadow. Farouk chuckled—a sound like wind chimes in a draft—and told me my shadow was too heavy to fetch a good price anyway.

Dining here is a balancing act. I visited a cafe suspended on a chain over the abyss and ordered the **Falafel-of-Float**. These are chickpea balls so light and porous they try to drift off the plate; you have to hold them down with a heavy fork made of iron. They taste of lemon and dill, and eating them makes you feel buoyant. I also tried the **Anti-Gravity-Baklava**, a pastry made of spun sugar and air-honey that dissolves before you can chew it, leaving a sweetness that lingers in your sinuses. To drink, I had a **Cup-of-Dust**, a thick coffee made from ground roasted date pits and sand, so heavy it sits in your stomach like a stone, keeping you grounded when the wind gets strong.

The wildlife has adapted to the inversion. There are **Carpet-Carp**, fish that swim through the air as if it were water, hovering near the lamps. I saw a flock of **Inverse-Vultures**, birds that cannot fly but have massive, soft talons for gripping the ceilings of the canyons, walking upside down with a dignified waddle.

I found a message from the library etched onto the rim of a well that leads down into the sky. It read: *To find solid ground, you must first learn to trust the air.*

My internal gravity is starting to adjust, but my blood still rushes to my head. The lead shoes Farouk sold me are chafing my ankles, and I feel the need to walk on something flat, even if it's moving.

I am heading *Sideways*, slipping into the *Shadow-City*. I hear there is a metropolis that exists only in the peripheral vision of those who hurry. They call it the **Blink-and-You-Miss-It-Metropolis**, and I intend to slow down enough to live there.

Watch your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
