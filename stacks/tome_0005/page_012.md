**Origin:** The Obsidian Souks of Ubar-Inverted (The Quarter of Glass Whispers)
**Arrival Status:** Hot to the touch; covered in a fine, shimmering grit that sings when brushed off; smells of cardamom, sulfur, and ozone; the paper feels like cooled lava.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a carpet shop that hangs suspended from the ceiling of a cavern deep beneath the Rub' al Khali—or perhaps, above it. Gravity is merely a suggestion here. I have arrived in **The Mirrored Caliphate of Samawat**, a kingdom buried in the eye of a storm that has been spinning since the dawn of time.

You remember I was heading for the Kelp-Primeval, seeking the swimming trees? I took a wrong turn at a reflective puddle and fell straight through the surface of the world. I landed on a dune of crushed sapphires, softer than silk and sharper than glass.

The geography here is blinding. The desert is not made of sand, but of time. The "dunes" are actually frozen waves of the past. To walk here is to walk through history. I took a step to the left and sunk ankle-deep into the year 1206. I had to be careful not to drag my heels, or I would have tripped over the Crusades. The wind does not blow air; it blows whispers. If you stand still too long, the wind will tell you secrets you aren't ready to hear. I learned that my great-aunt once stole a spoon, which was disappointing.

I am currently in the **City of Iram-of-the-Pillars**, but it is not built on earth. It is carved entirely into the interior of a giant, floating diamond. The sun does not shine here; instead, the city is illuminated by the bioluminescence of giant **Sand-Whales** that swim through the air like dirigibles. Their skin is translucent, revealing gears and clocks working inside them. They hum a low, vibrating note that vibrates your fillings loose.

The locals are the **Saffron-Ghouls**, though they prefer to be called *Keepers of the Glare*. They wear robes woven from fiber-optics that shift color depending on the truthfulness of their words. A liar is instantly draped in neon pinks and greens, while a honest man blends into the beige sand. It makes politics very quiet and very bright. They do not shake hands; they press their foreheads together to exchange bandwidth. I tried to greet a rug merchant, and we accidentally downloaded a folk song into each other's heads. I've been humming it for three days.

The language is intricate, a mix of Arabic and geometric algebra. To say "Hello," you must describe a perfect circle. To say "Goodbye," you calculate the trajectory of a star. I tried to ask for the restroom, but unfortunately, I used the wrong variable and accidentally proposed marriage to a lamppost. The lamppost said yes, but I think it was just being polite.

Dining is a high-stakes gamble. I visited a stall in the bazaar selling **Baklava-of-Dreams**. It is layered not with honey and nuts, but with folded-up episodes of *Sleep*. You eat a piece, and you dream while you are awake. I ate three squares and spent an afternoon living the life of a cloud. It was exhausting work, raining on people. For the main course, I had **Lamb-Sub-Text**, a stew where the meat is so tender it speaks to you. My mutton confessed its fears about the economy while I chewed. It tasted like anxiety and mint.

The wildlife is beautiful and lethal. The **Scorpions here have tails made of glass fiber**. They don't sting; they impale you with a high-definition video of your own death. It’s very distracting. I stepped on one, and had to watch myself trip over a root six years from now. I was relieved to see I survived, though I ruin a nice pair of pants.

I did find a connection to the past, or perhaps a future that hasn't happened. In the corner of the souk, a merchant was selling jars containing the silence of specific libraries. One jar was labeled "The Library of Alexandria, 48 BC." I bought it. It’s heavy. I intend to return it, assuming I can find a timestamp that accepts returns.

The sun is setting, or rather, the giant Sand-Whales are migrating to the darker side of the diamond, casting long, indigo shadows across the plaza. The carpet seller is rolling up his wares, rolling them so tight they disappear into the second dimension.

I am heading *Inward* now. I hear there is a place located in the space between two heartbeats, where the architecture is built entirely of sound. They call it **The Echo-Spiral**, and I am told the silence there is deafening.

Mind your step, and watch out for scorpions showing home movies.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
