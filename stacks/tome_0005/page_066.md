**Origin:** The Subglacial Tropics of *Polus-Rosetta* (The Mirror Basement)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a pane of heavy, clear glass, three millimeters thick, encasing a preserved deep-sea shrimp that watches you. It smells of brine, disinfectant, and peppermint. The text is etched in reverse on the back; you must hold it up to a mirror to read, or simply squint until your brain adjusts.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this wrapped in three layers of wool, sitting on a beach of black sand that is comfortably warm, though the "sun" here is a bioluminescent jellyfish the size of a city block. I have arrived in **Polus-Rosetta**, the Mirror Basement. It is an Antarctica located underneath the Antarctica we know, separated by four miles of ice and a complete inversion of thermodynamics.

You recall I was riding the *Thermal-Vortex* *Down*, aiming for the **Archipelago-of-Ash**? I must have passed through the crust and kept going. I fell through the bottom of the world and landed in a ocean that doesn't freeze because it remembers the warmth of the earth’s core. This place is a paradox wrapped in a riddle: it is a frozen wasteland where you can go swimming in a bikini. The ice above us acts as a colossal lens, refracting the cold blues of the sky into a saturated, tropical magenta that paints the waves.

The geography is disorienting. Here, gravity pulls towards the ice ceiling. We walk on the "seafloor," but the rain—tears of meltwater from the glacier above—falls *up*, splashing against the bottom of the ice and running upward into the sky. The mountains are inverted stalactites of glacial ice, piercing down into the warm water like the teeth of a frozen whale. The horizon is curved; you can see the back of your own head if you look far enough through the atmospheric distortion.

The locals are the **Shale-Shapers**, beings that look like they were chiseled from smooth grey slate. They have no eyes, but their skin is covered in microscopic suction cups that taste the light and texture of everything they touch. They move with a grinding, geological grace. They communicate through *Glyph-Gestures*, rubbing their limbs together to create vibrations in the water. To say "Welcome," they sound like two tectonic plates kissing. To say "Goodbye," they mimic the sound of a calving glacier—a thunderous crack that echoes in your chest.

I met a Shale-Shaper named *Obsidian* who offered me shelter from the upward-rain. He lives in a geode half-buried in the black sand. Inside, it is filled with air harvested from bubbles in the ice. It tastes prehistoric.

Dining here is a study in contrast. I visited a tavern called *The Updraft*. The main course was **Magma-Mussel** soup, served boiling hot in a bowl made of salt. The mussels glow red in the dark, and eating them feels like swallowing a tiny, happy sun.

To drink, I had *Glacier-Milk*, a thick, white fluid harvested from the very bottom of the ice sheet. It tastes like vanilla, diamonds, and the memory of snow. It makes you feel heavy and grounded, which is necessary here, as the buoyancy of the water makes it hard to stay "down."

The wildlife is translucent and indignant. There are **Glass-Squid**, which float around the ice-ceiling, changing color to match the refractions. They are intelligent and can reflect your own face back at you to distract you while they steal your soup. I also saw a **Plesiosaur-Penguin**, a majestic creature with the flippers of a bird and the neck of a dinosaur, which sang to the ice in a baritone voice to keep the ceiling from collapsing.

I found a message from the library frozen in a block of clear ice that washed up on the black sand shore. It read: *Looking glass, looking glass, on the wall, who is the warmest of them all?*

The pink twilight is fading, and the bioluminescent jellyfish-sun is dimming, preparing to "sleep." The air is getting colder, or perhaps I am just losing the argument with the wind. I have grown used to the upside-down rain, but I miss the moon. This place has a moon, but it is a reflection of the earth, and it hurts to look at it for too long.

I am heading *Up*, climbing the *Staircase-of-Frost* to the **Cloud-City of Stratus**. I hear they harvest the silence from between the thunderclaps.

Stay warm.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
