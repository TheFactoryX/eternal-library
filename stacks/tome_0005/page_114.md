**Origin:** The Terraced Peaks of *Nong-Zhen* (The Vertical Rice Paddies)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a single, broad blade of emerald-green rice paper, dried stiff. It is striated with texture, smelling faintly of wet soil, ozone, and burning incense. The ink is a rich, ochre mud, glossy and still damp. If you hold the card at an angle, the text seems to flow upward like water draining in a sink.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while suspended in a bamboo chair bolted to the side of a cliff, watching the farmers harvest clouds from the mud. I have arrived in **Nong-Zhen**, the Vertical Rice Paddies. This is the place where agriculture defies gravity, and the staple crop is not grain, but condensed weather.

You recall I was heading *Up*, ascending the **Staircase-of-Stratosphere** to walk on the ceiling of the atmosphere? I climbed for days, my lungs burning in the thinning air. Just as I reached the "gold" layer, I encountered a **Storm-Blocker**. He was a giant of a man, skin like polished mahogany, wearing a cloak made of tornado funnels. He told me the atmosphere was closed for renovations—too many holes in the ozone layer being patched with solid lightning. He suggested I take the detour *Down-and-Sideways*, and tossed me a parachute made of woven spider silk. I drifted for hours, eventually snagging my foot on a terrace jutting out from the side of a floating mountain.

The geography here is a terrifying miracle. **Nong-Zhen** consists of thousands of shelves cut into the vertical faces of mountains that hang suspended in the air, tethered to the earth by massive, rusted iron chains thicker than redwoods. There are no horizontal fields here; every inch of arable land is a step on a ladder to the stars. Water does not flow down; it is pumped up from the cloud layer below, irrigating the crops that grow sideways out of the rock.

The locals are the **Cloud-Shearers**. They are small, nimble people with hands stained permanently green from chlorophyll and feet that can grip the slickest wet granite. They wear wide-brimmed hats made of condensed fog, which drips continuously, watering their footsteps. They communicate through **Bamboo-Tones**. They carry hollow reeds of different lengths, tapping them against their thighs or the rocks. A sharp *tock-tock* means "Danger," while a soft, breathy *whoosh* means "The rain is ripe." I tried to compliment a farmer’s hat, but I tapped a rhythm that apparently meant "Locusts are coming," causing a minor panic.

I am currently resting in *The Inn of the Drunken Stone*, a structure built entirely inside a hollowed-out boulder hanging by a giant chain. The floor gently sways. The currency here is **Potential Energy**. To pay for my room, I had to carry a heavy iron weight up a flight of stairs and deposit it at the top. The innkeeper measured the "lift" I had generated, weighed it against my debt, and nodded. "Good gravity," he said.

I am drinking a bowl of **Thunder-Broth Tea**. It is brewed from rainwater harvested during a typhoon and infused with lightning-roasted rice. It fizzes in the cup, and when you drink it, you feel a distinct jolt behind your eyes, followed by a profound sense of clarity. It tastes like ozone and cinnamon. For dinner, I am having **Stir-Fried Horizon**. It is a dish made of thinly sliced clouds, wok-fried with spicy peppers and starlight. The texture is like eating a sponge that melts into warm mist. It is served with **Steamed Moss-Dumplings**, which are earthy, gritty, and taste of iron and patience.

The wildlife here has adapted to the vertical life. I see **Geckos-of-Glass**, transparent lizards that cling to the undersides of the rock ledges, changing color to match the sky. The most magnificent creature, however, is the **Koi-of-the-Air-Jet**. These are great, carp-shaped fish that "swim" through the air using large, fan-like fins that displace air with incredible force. They hover around the terraces, snatching insects out of the sky with a loud *snap*.

I found a message from the library folded into an origami crane and left on my windowsill. It read: *To grow, you must sometimes learn to hang upside down.*

The wind is picking up, and the chains are groaning. The **Cloud-Shearers** are rushing to harvest the mature hailstones before they become too heavy.

I am heading *Deep*, diving into the **Mouth-of-the-Mountain**. I saw a cave entrance swallowing the clouds, and I am curious to see where the weather goes when it dies.

Watch your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
