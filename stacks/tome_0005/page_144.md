**Origin:** The Hardwood Horizons of *Kolumbus-Bijou* (The Petrified-Heartland)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a rigid, semi-translucent slice of agate, roughly the size of a deck of cards. It is cool to the touch and surprisingly heavy. The text is not inked on the surface but seems to be fossilized inside the stone, readable when held against the light. It smells faintly of cedar dust and old varnish.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a porch swing that squeaks in perfect rhythm with the rotation of the earth. I have arrived in **Kolumbus-Bijou**, the capital of the Petrified-Heartland. This is the place where the American Midwest decided that change was too stressful and opted for permanent preservation. It is a land of amber waves of grain—literally amber, as in fossilized resin—and purple mountains majesty that are actually amethyst geodes the size of skyscrapers.

You recall I was heading *To the Press*, intending to slip between the pages? I was flattened, certainly, but not into a book. I slipped through a crack in the platen and fell into a stratum of pure history. I landed in a soft pile of sawdust the size of a baseball stadium. The air here is dry and smells of benzoin and patience. It is a stiff place, in every sense of the word.

The geography here is jagged and unyielding. The Great Plains have been replaced by the **Great Plains of Glass**, a vast expanse where the wind doesn't blow through the wheat, but *around* the crystal stalks, creating a symphony of chimes that never repeats. I am currently sitting on the **Edge of the Canyon**, a rift in the earth that exposes millions of years of sedimentary rock, stacked in neat, laser-cut layers like a vast, geological layer cake. To the west, the horizon is framed by the **Sierra-Septum**, a mountain range that looks exactly like the jagged edge of a torn envelope, frozen in stone.

The locals are the **Gems**. They are humanoid beings composed entirely of semi-precious stones. Their skin is faceted, catching the sunlight and fracturing it into a million rainbows. They do not walk; they *tumble*, rolling forward with a slow, rhythmic clatter, smoothing their edges against the road. They communicate through **Canto-Cleavage**. They don't speak; they strike themselves with small mallets, producing pure, musical notes that depend on their mineral composition. A high "C" means "Good morning," while a low, gravelly thud means "Get off my lawn." I tried to greet a quartz-man, but he just vibrated at a frequency that made my teeth ache.

I am staying in *The Geode-Motel*, a room hollowed out of a giant beryl crystal. The bed is a slab of polished obsidian, hard as a diamond, but surprisingly warm. The concierge is a large, unblinking opal who charges by the carat. The lights here don't turn on and off; they just refract differently.

I am dining on **Mother-of-Pearl Pie**. It is a pastry with a crust made of actual shell, filled with a creamy, luminescent pudding that tastes like nostalgia and whale song. It is crunchy, salty, and sweet all at once. For the main course, I had **Buffalo-Wings-of-Stone**. They are not chicken, but small, granite gargoyles that fly when you throw them. You have to catch them first, which is hard on the teeth. They taste dusty and iron-rich. For dessert, I am having **Petrified-Peach-Cobbler**. It takes three days to eat, and you have to soak it in vinegar just to make a dent.

The wildlife is breathtaking. The skies are ruled by the **Thunder-Birds**. They are not made of flesh, but of lightning trapped inside a cage of magnetite. They fly without flapping, suspended by static electricity. When they call out, it sounds like a power line snapping in a storm. The apex predator is the **Grizzly-Onyx**. It is a bear made of black marble with eyes of rubies. It doesn't hunt you; it just stands in the middle of the road, posing, waiting for a tourist to take a picture. If you don't applaud, it traps you in a stasis field forever. I barely escaped with my camera.

I found a message from the library chiseled into a menhir in the town square. It read: *To endure is to remain, but to persist is to become hard.*

The sun is setting, and the light is hitting the plains, turning the whole world into a stained-glass window. It is beautiful, but I am feeling quite brittle.

I am heading *West of the Setting Sun*. I have heard there is a place where the day ends, and the night is poured from a jug. I intend to catch the last few drops before they hit the ground.

Don't take things for granite.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
