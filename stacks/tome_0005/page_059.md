**Origin:** The Mycelial-Shadow Reaches of *Phra-Ubon* (The City of Ghosts & Spores)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a stiff, dried sheet of lotus leaf, brittle as a cracker and smelling of monsoon rain, decomposition, and lemongrass. The texture is fuzzy to the touch. The ink is a bioluminescent moss paste that glows faintly green in the dark and is slightly damp.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the hollow of a giant, petrified log, looking out over a city that isn't there, yet is somehow the busiest place I have ever been. I have arrived in **Phra-Ubon**, the Ghost City of the Canopy, a Southeast Asia that exists entirely in the spaces between things. It is a place where the distinction between the living and the dead is measured in humidity, and where the "ground" is a concept best left to the naive.

You recall I was riding the *Stitch-Stream*, aiming for the **Republic-of-Ruin**? Well, the thread snagged on a reality bar, and I fell through the weave of the world. I plummeted into a twilight zone of infinite green, bouncing off giant ferns until I landed in the soft embrace of a *Fungal-Fig*. I thought I had landed in a jungle, but then the fruit started whispering to me.

The geography here is vertical, humid, and aggressively biological. **Phra-Ubon** is built not on land, but suspended in the interlocking branches of the *Banyan-That-Is-To-Come*. There are no streets, only "Vines-of-Passage"—thick, braided lianas that act as conveyor belts, moving via the peristaltic contractions of the moss that grows on them. Gravity is optional here; if you are feeling light, you float to the upper temples where the *Air-Ancestors* live. If you carry guilt, you sink to the darker, lower swamps. I currently reside in the middle stratum, trying to keep my moral compass balanced so I don't fall into the*Rot*, a suburb of pure compost.

The locals are the **Khon-Spirit**, semi-translucent beings who phase in and out of existence depending on the angle of the sunlight. They aren't dead, exactly—they are just "post-biological." They communicate through *Saba-Saba*, a language of chimes, rustles, and the smell of incense. A cough means "I agree"; a sudden drop in temperature means "The tea is ready." I met a monk named *Vong* who was polishing his skull until it shone like pearl. "Reflection is key to enlightenment," he said, holding up a mirror to check his liver. "And to seeing the back of your own head."

Language here is tricky. They don't say "Hello," they ask, "Are you real today?" To say "Goodbye," you simply fade from view. I attempted to compliment a local woman on her *Songkran* (New Year) attire, but I used the tone for "Mildew," and she immediately burst into tears and dissolved into a puddle of silver water. I felt terrible, mostly because I had to mop her up with my socks.

Dining here is a translucent affair. I visited a floating market—literally floating, as the boats are made of lighter-than-air gas. I ordered the **Soup-of-Sighs**, a broth made from the steam of cooking pots and the last breaths of dying philosophers. It is hot, sour, and makes you feel profoundly nostalgic for a life you never lived. I also tried the **Laab-of-Lightning**, a minced meat salad made of bioluminescent fireflies and spicy chilies. It doesn't fill your stomach; it illuminates it. I spent the night glowing from the inside out, which made me very popular with the local moths. For dessert, I had *Mango-Sticky-Rice*, but the rice was actually tiny, sweet eggs laid by a **Glitter-Lizard**. They pop in your mouth with a sweetness that tastes like sunshine and danger.

The wildlife is spectacular and hallucinogenic. There are **Neon-Geckos**, lizards that change color to match your mood, which is annoying when you are trying to be stoic and they turn hot pink. Beware the **Stinger-Sprites**, mosquitoes that don't suck blood but steal your memories. I swatted one, and for a moment, I forgot my own name. I had to eat a *Mnemosyne-Root* to remember I was The Wanderer.

I found a message from the library written in the veins of a leaf I was using as a fan. It read: *Decay is just life changing its texture.*

The humidity is curing my leather boots into a soup, and the moss is starting to grow on my left elbow. I am becoming too organic for my taste. I crave the sharp lines, the smell of ozone, and the logic of the straight edge.

I am heading *True-North*, climbing the *Stairway-of-Stars* to the **Aerie-of-Angles**. They say the streets are laid out in perfect vectors and the silence is measured in decibels.

Keep your feet dry.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
