**Origin:** The Soaring Steppes of *Aero-Argentina* (The Vertical Pampas)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a single, thick, leather-like leaf from a Cloud-Gum tree. It is dry and brittle, covered in fine, white fuzz. When held, it feels lighter than it should, threatening to float out of the hand if not anchored. The ink is a deep, charcoal grey, stamped into the fibers with a searing iron. It smells of ozone, heavy rain, and roasting coffee.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this while tethered to the railing of a café that is drifting approximately three thousand feet above the ground. I have arrived in **Aero-Argentina**, the Vertical Pampas. This is the place where the ground decided it was overrated and the landscape took to the sky, leaving the dusty earth below as a mere suggestion.

You recall I was heading *To the Stratospheric Gardens*? I hitched a ride on a thermal updraft, intending to prune a few cumulus clouds, but I overshot the troposphere and fell through a weather front that smells faintly of cinnamon. I landed in a soft bank of moss that turned out to be the back of a grazing **Cloud-Wombat**. I slid down its woolly flank and found myself standing on a floating island of ferrous red dirt, suspended in the blue void by nothing but the sheer stubbornness of its geology.

The geography here is altitude and attitude. The pampas haven't disappeared; they have simply stacked themselves into a vertical spiral. The grasslands roll upward into the sky, defying gravity through a collective root system that grips the air itself. I am currently trekking across the **Floating Plateau of la-Plata-Alta**, a swath of flat grasslands that hovers upside down, raining upwards onto the lands below. To the west, I can see the **Andes-of-Air**, a mountain range that is not solid rock, but pressurized currents of wind shaped into jagged, snowy peaks. You can climb them, but you have to be very quiet, or a sudden gust will erode the handholds.

The locals are the **Gauchos-of-the-Gale**. They are master horsemen who ride **Cyclone-Stallions**, horses made of storm clouds and thunder that can gallop on lightning. They wear *bombachas* (baggy trousers) stitched from thunder-skins and wide-brimmed hats that act as lightning rods. They communicate through **Zephyr-Cant**, a language of whistled melodies that carry for miles. A trilling ascent means "The cattle are restless," while a low, mournful whistle indicates "A storm is coming in." I tried to order a coffee, but I whistled a flat note, which apparently translates to "I enjoy dancing with scorpions." The barista, a woman named *Evita*, laughed and served me a double-espresso anyway.

I am staying in *The Estancia of the High-Altitude*, a ranch house built on a floating boulder. It is anchored by chains made of braided gravity. The floors are carpeted with living grass, and the windows are always open to the rush of the wind.

I am dining on **Asado-de-Ozone**. It is beef steak grilled over a fire of burning violet flames, which sears the meat with a smoky, electric flavor. For the main course, I am having **Empanadas-of-the-Void**. The pastry is puffed with helium, causing the pies to float on the plate. Inside, they are filled with spiced air and starlight, savory yet weightless. You have to pop them in your mouth before they drift away. For dessert, I am having **Dulce-de-Leche-Dreams**. It is a caramel so thick and sweet it causes vivid hallucinations of better childhoods. It is served on wafers of edible mica.

The wildlife is… buoyant. The skies are filled with **Pampas-Foxes**, which have evolved small gliders made of skin between their legs, allowing them to swoop from island to island. The most majestic creature is the **Rhea-of-the-Rising-Sun**. It is a large, flightless bird that runs on the air currents, its massive toes splayed out to catch the updrafts, leaving footprints of mist in the sky.

I found a message from the library written in the vapor trail of a passing jet. It read: *Gravity is just a bad habit.*

The wind is picking up, and the sun is setting—a riot of purple and gold that tastes like grape soda. I must anchor my tent before the night winds blow us into the stratosphere.

I am heading *To the Library of Lost Echoes*. I believe it is located in a canyon where every word ever spoken is still bouncing off the walls.

Watch your step, or you might fall up.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
