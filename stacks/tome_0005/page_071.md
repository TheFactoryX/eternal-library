**Origin:** The Obsidian Jungles of *Ayre-Nhk* (The Shattered Coast)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged slab of black volcanic glass, warm to the touch and humming with a low, tectonic vibration. It smells of sulphur, roasting coffee, and fresh rain. The ink is a stark, metallic silver that seems to vibrate off the surface.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this whilst balanced on the rim of a dormant (or perhaps just sleepy) volcano, watching the jungle slowly knit itself back together after this morning's tremor. I have arrived in **Ayre-Nhk**, the Shattered Coast. It is the South America that might have been, if the Andes had decided to be islands rather than a wall, drifting apart in a sea of golden fog.

You recall I was heading *Downward* via the *Stairwell-of-Obsolescence* to the **Basement-of-Unrecorded-Years**? Well, I took the stairs two at a time, eager to get to the bottom, but I missed a step on the year 1842 and fell right through the floor of history. I plummeted through a wormhole carved by a machete and landed in the canopy of a *Rubber-Tree* the size of a skyscraper. The tree caught me, sighed in a dialect of Quechua, and lowered me gently to the forest floor.

The geography here is volatile and rhythmic. The continent doesn't sit still; it drifts. The mountains are floating islands of granite, tethered to the earth by massive, vinelike roots made of raw copper. The tectonic plates don't shift—they dance. I was walking through a valley of *Singings-Reeds* when the ground decided to rotate ninety degrees. I had to grab onto a *Passion-Vine* as the world turned sideways, watching a waterfall suddenly pour horizontally into a sunset. It makes map-reading terribly difficult; north is a concept here, not a direction.

The locals are the **Green-Keepers**, people who seem to have photosynthesized with their environment. Their skin is a mosaic of chlorophyll and sunburn, and they wear clothes woven from living moss that changes color with their mood. They speak a language called *Rhythm-Botany*, which involves hitting the trunks of trees with tuned stone hammers. A conversation sounds like a marimba performance. I tried to ask for directions to the coast, but I accidentally insulted a Ceiba tree; it pelted me with unripe mangoes until I apologized.

I stopped at a waystation carved into the hollow of a cactus that serves as a water tower. I was starving, so I ordered the **Tectonic-Tamales**. They are cooked by wrapping them in leaves and burying them near a steam vent for exactly three tremors. The result is a spicy, earthy mush that tastes of chili and adrenaline. To drink, I had a glass of *Lightning-Harvest*, a fermented corn beverage that literally crackles as you swallow it, sending little static shocks into your fingertips. It certainly wakes you up.

The wildlife is a recursive evolutionary joke. There are **Feathered-Serpents**, but they aren't myths here—they are massive, bright green boa constrictors with parrot wings that soar through the gaps in the floating mountains. Then there are the **Time-Lizards**, tiny geckos that scuttle so fast they exist in five places at once. Catching one is impossible; catching five is dinner. I also saw a herd of **Spectral-Jaguars**, predators made of smoke and shadow that hunt by erasing their prey from your memory. I saw one eat a monkey, but for the life of me, I can't remember what color the monkey was.

I found a message from the library scribbled on a banana leaf in ink that hasn't dried yet. It read: *The earth remembers what we forget.*

The air here is thin and smells like ozone and burning wood. At night, the bioluminescent fungi in the canopy light up in a pattern that resembles a star chart, but for a different sky entirely. I feel like a ghost haunting a place that is still alive.

I am heading *North-by-Northwest*, following the scent of rain and gasoline, to the **Highway-of-Neon-Saints**. I hear they drive fast, and the passengers never age.

Watch your step. The ground is moving.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
