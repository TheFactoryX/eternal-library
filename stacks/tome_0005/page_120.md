**Origin:** The Lunar Taiga of *Fen-Hallow* (The Gravity-Spun Forest)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged rectangle of slate-grey shale, cold and rough to the touch. It smells of woodsmoke, frozen pine needles, and the metallic tang of blood. The ink is not ink, but a bluish, bioluminescent moss smeared across the stone; it pulses faintly in time with the reader's heartbeat. Holding the card makes your fingers feel instantly numb, as if you've been gripping a block of dry ice.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the inside of a frozen waterfall, watching the aurora borealis twist into knots overhead. I have arrived in **Fen-Hallow**, the Lunar Taiga. This is a place where the forest learned to walk, and the moon decided to come down from the sky to see what the fuss was about.

You recall I was heading *In*, diving into the **Eye-of-the-Zephyr** to escape the drifting clouds of Nepenthe-Nine? I expected to find the calm center of a storm. Instead, the eye of the tornado was a wormhole of absolute silence. I fell through the dark for what felt like a decade, only to slam into a snowdrift the consistency of iron. When I looked up, I didn't see the sun. I saw **The Pale Eye**, a fractured moon that hangs so low you can feel its gravity tugging at your fillings.

The geography here is vertical and predatory. The trees of **Fen-Hallow** are not made of wood, but of woven bone and calcified shadow. They grow upside down, their roots clutching the underbelly of floating islands of permafrost, their branches brushing the frozen earth. The "snow" doesn't fall; it rises. It drifts upward from the ground, defying gravity, pulling heat away from the soles of your boots. The wind here doesn't howl; it mimics human voices. I hear my mother calling my name from the ravine, but I know better than to answer. If you answer the wind, it steals your breath and adds it to the storm.

The locals are the **Vargs**. They are hirsute, silent people who wear suits made from the pelt of the moon itself—shimmering, cratered fur that absorbs light. They have the eyes of huskies and the posture of wolves. They communicate through **Snarl-Script**, a language of guttural growls and bared teeth that sounds aggressive but is actually quite polite. A soft, wrinkled snout means "Good morning," while a flash of canines means "Blessings upon your house." I attempted to greet a chieftain by smiling; he nearly took my head off before I realized I had accidentally challenged him to a duel. We settled it by arm-wrestling on a table made of solidified starlight.

I am currently resting in *The Root-and-Reverie*, an inn hollowed out of the carcass of a Woolly Mammoth that is slowly fossilizing into crystal. The landlord, a Varg named **Fenris**, serves drinks that are colder than absolute zero. He told me that **Fen-Hallow** is where old gods go to hide from their worshippers. The currency here is **Warmth**. To pay for my room, I had to sit by the fire and radiate body heat for three hours while they harvested it with copper funnels. "Expensive," Fenris said, "but you sleep like the dead."

I am drinking a glass of **Nord-Night**. It is a distilled spirit made from fermented shadows and preserved berries. It is black as tar and warms you from the inside out, but it makes your shadow detach itself and go for a wander. Currently, my shadow is dancing with a shadow of a bear in the corner. For dinner, I am having **Steak-of-Stone-and-Ice**. It is a "meat" cut from a glacier that has been marinated in vinegar and iron filings. It crunches like glass but tastes like the memory of a steak you ate ten years ago—rich, bloody, and savory.

The wildlife is formidable. I see **Spectral-Stags**. These are elk made of white mist and birch bark. They walk through the trees, and when they breathe, they shoot beams of frost that freeze the air solid. They are hunted by **Time-Wolves**, packs of creatures that exist slightly in the future. You don't see them until the damage is done. I have a scar on my arm from a Time-Wolf bite that I won't receive until next Tuesday.

I found a message from the library written on the surface of a frozen pond. I had to chip away the ice to read it. It said: *The longest night is the one you spend waiting for the dawn.*

The upside-down trees are creaking, and the Pale Eye is blinking. The Vargs are sharpening their knives.

I am heading *Deep*, descending into the **Marrow-Mines** beneath the taiga. I have heard that beneath the earth, there is a library of bones that contains the history of every creature that ever walked, swam, or flew. I intend to check out a book on flight.

Keep the fire burning.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
