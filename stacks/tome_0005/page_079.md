**Origin:** The Oxidized Steppe of *Qyrq-Qyzyl* (The Red Wind Gap)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of tanned felt, rough and fibrous, with the consistency of dried jerky. It smells strongly of horse sweat, fermented mare’s milk, and rust. When tilted, the ink shimmers like a heat mirage.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the shade of a yurt made of living granite, watching the horizon warp in the heat. I have arrived in **Qyrq-Qyzyl**, the Red Wind Gap. This is the Central Asia that exists in the dust storm of a collapsed empire—a brutal, beautiful expanse where the border between the sky and the steppes has been entirely erased by the wind.

You recall I was heading *Vertically* up the *Staircase-of-Giants* toward the **Cloud-Cathedral-of-Isotope**? I climbed until the air turned to liquid and my lungs began to crystallize, but the staircase was shattered by a passage of the **Celestial-Administrative-Bureaucracy**. They were paving the sky with grey concrete and filing the clouds into alphabetical order. I slipped on a wet "Cumulus" and fell for what felt like a day, landing in the soft, choking red dust of this endless plateau.

The geography here is defined by erosion and entropy. The ground is not earth, but a fine, ferrous powder that stains everything the color of old blood. The mountains in the distance are worn smooth by winds that blow faster than sound. They are known as the *Whistling-Vertebrae*, giant skeletal ridges that sing a constant, mournful chord in C-minor.

The locals are the **Silk-Weavers**, a nomadic people who travel on the backs of giant, armored tardigrades the size of elephants. They wear coats made of woven metals—copper, silver, and gold—that clink musically when they move. They are a stoic people, obsessed with the concept of *The-Knot*. In their culture, a knot is a stored moment; to tie a knot is to remember, and to untie it is to forget.

Their language, *Tili-Shum*, is impossible to speak with a human tongue. It involves clicking the teeth and snapping the fingers. I attempted to ask for water by clicking my thumb and middle finger, but I accidentally insulted the chieftain’s beard. Fortunately, this was interpreted as a challenge to a drinking contest, which I declined by vomiting.

I am currently recovering with a bowl of **Beshbarmak-of-Obsidian**, a traditional dish of boiled meat (from the **Volcanic-Sheep**, which graze on hot springs) served over wide, flat noodles made of stone paste. It is meant to be eaten with the fingers, hence the name, which translates to "Five Fingers," though here, it means "Five Knives." The meat is tough, smoky, and tastes of sulfur. I am washing it down with a bowl of *Kumis-of-Time*, fermented mare’s milk that has been aged in a barrel made of clock parts. It is fizzy, sour, and causes you to remember the future. I just had a flash of myself losing a shoe in 2034.

The wildlife is durable and dangerous. The skies are ruled by the **Bezoar-Ibex**, goats with iron hooves that climb the sheer faces of the wind currents. They don't walk on air; they walk on the pressure systems. Down in the ditches, you find **Rust-Scorpions**, creatures that consume metal. I had to chase one away from my belt buckle last night; it was trying to eat the buckle right off my hips.

I found a message from the library written in the dust on the windowsill of the yurt. It read: *The wind erodes everything except the truth.*

The sun is setting, and the Red Wind is picking up, turning the air into a swirling vortex of crimson grit. I have to wrap my head in layers of silk to keep my skin from sanding away.

I am heading *Zig-Zag*, riding the slipstream of the **Trade-Route-of-Intangible-Goods** toward the **Library-of-Babel-in-Exile**. I hear they have a book there that contains the script of your life, but they keep it in the restricted section because the ending is too terrifying.

Keep your knots tight.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
