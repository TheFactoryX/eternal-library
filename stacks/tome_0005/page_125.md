**Origin:** The Fungus-Jungle of *Xibalba-Rot* (The Mycelial-Net)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a square of tough, fibrous material that feels exactly like dried mushroom skin. It is soft, velvety, and smells intensely of damp earth, fermentation, and electricity. If held for too long, it begins to warm in the hand. The text is not written with ink, but scratched into the spores; the letters are a shifting, maddening violet that seems to writhe like worms. If you lick the corner of the card—a habit I advise against—it tastes of bitter dark chocolate and adrenaline.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the inside of a stomach. Not my own, thankfully, but the massive, bio-luminescent belly of a **Titan-Bovine-Truffle**, a grazing fungal "cow" the size of a cathedral. I have hollowed out a sleeping space in its gills, which smells like expensive cheese and mildew. I have arrived in **Xibalba-Rot**, the Mycelial-Net. This is the place where the jungle decided that photosynthesis was too much work and decided to eat the history of the world instead.

You recall I was heading *Deep*, diving into the **Lagoon-of-Lost-Names** to walk on the vocabulary of the dead? I strapped my weighted boots and jumped from the glass cliffs of **Vitreous-Veracruz**. I sank like a stone, but I didn't hit water. I hit *mud*. Thick, sentient, ancient mud that grabbed my ankles and whispered secrets about the Aztecs in a language I didn't understand. It pulled me down, through the roots of reality, and spat me out into a humid darkness where the trees are made of wire and the fruit is made of time.

The geography here is moist and interconnected. **Xibalba-Rot** is not a forest; it is a single, infinite organism. The "trees" here are just fruiting bodies of a subterranean intelligence that spans the entire continent. The ground pulses with a slow, thumping rhythm—*bump-thump, bump-thump*—like the heartbeat of a sleeping giant. The air is thick with golden spores that snow constantly, turning the air into a glittering fog. If you breathe too deeply, you start to hallucinate memories that aren't yours. I inhaled a lungful near a river and suddenly remembered being a conquistador’s horse. Very disorienting.

The locals are the **Spore-Singers**. They are humanoid beings composed entirely of hyphae—delicate, thread-like structures woven into the shape of men and women. They glow with a faint, sickly green bioluminescence. They wear clothes woven from the silk of giant spiders and masks carved from hardened puffballs. They communicate through **Sporic-Language**. By releasing specific puffs of colored dust, they create complex sentences in the air. A red cloud means "Anger," while a blue mist means "Ecstasy." I tried to ask for directions to a toilet, but I coughed, creating a chaotic gray plume that apparently means, "I surrender my soul to the compost." The locals were very polite about my inadvertent spiritual submission.

I am currently resting in *The Shelf*, an inn carved into the underside of a bracket fungus the size of a football stadium. The beds are hammocks made of living slime mold that conforms to your body shape. The currency here is **Decay**. To pay for my room, I had to give the innkeeper a piece of rotten fruit I found in my pack. He sniffed it, rolled his eyes back in ecstasy, and gave me the key, which was a living beetle that bit my hand to verify ownership.

I am drinking a gourd of **Kombucha-Eternal**. It is a fizzy, fermented tea brewed in the hollow of a tree that has been dead for ten thousand years. It tastes like vinegar, ginger, and the feeling of forgetting a painful memory. It makes you feel pleasantly acidic. For dinner, I am having **Ceviche-of-Lightning**. The "fish" are electric eels that swim through the damp air, and they are "cooked" by capturing them in a jar during a thunderstorm. The meat glows in the dark and tastes like static shock and lime.

The wildlife is aggressive and delicious. I see **Macaws-of-Mold**. Their feathers are bright patches of turquoise and orange fungus. They don't squawk; they hiss, releasing a cloud of psychoactive dust that attracts mates. The most dangerous predator is the **Jaguar-of-Sickness**. It is a skeletal creature made of calcified bones and tendrils. It doesn't bite you; it infects you. One scratch and you begin to turn into mulch within minutes. I narrowly avoided one by hiding in a pile of rotting logs; the smell of my own fear masked my scent.

I found a message from the library written in the iridescent scales of a beetle. It was small and pulsing. It read: *To grow is to consume; to consume is to become something else.*

The Spore-Singers are beginning their evening chant, a low vibration that makes the moss on the walls dance.

I am heading *Up*, climbing the **Staircase-of-Thunder**. I have found a vine made of pure copper that strikes the sky, and I intend to climb until I reach the cloud layer where the gods keep their spare lightning.

Hold your breath.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
