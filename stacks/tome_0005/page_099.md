**Origin:** The Calcified Rainforest of *Xibalba-Verde* (The City of Roots and Ruin)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a jagged shard of pale green jade, warm to the touch and vibrating with a low-frequency hum. It smells of wet stone, chilies, and copal incense. The surface is rough on the edges, polished smooth in the center. The text appears to be carved into the stone by an insect bite; the letters are tiny, precise holes filled with gold leaf.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from the steps of a temple that is currently digesting a heretic. I have arrived in **Xibalba-Verde**, the Calcified Rainforest. This is the Central America that exists in the five seconds of silence after a thunderclap—a place where the jungle has decided that stone architecture is just a harder variety of wood to be consumed.

You recall I was heading *In* to meet the **Architect-of-Silence** in the **Coral-Brain-Matrix**? I burrowed deep into the sponge, expecting a library of quiet thoughts. Instead, I hit a pocket of pressurized nitrogen that launched me like a cannonball through the earth's crust. I erupted from the top of a pyramid here, covered in soot and coral dust, landing in a pile of soft, fern-like leaves that tried to undress me as I landed. It’s good to be back in the humidity, even if the humidity here has teeth.

The geography here is predatory. The rainforest doesn't just grow; it hunts. The trees are **Ceiba-Ironwoods**, with bark as hard as steel and roots that walk around during the siesta to find better water sources. The ruins are not merely crumbling; they are being remodelled. Vines with the tensile strength of bridge cables pull down walls to weave new shapes—monkeys made of mortar, jaguars made of moss. The sun is a relentless, jagged hole in the sky that drips heat like syrup.

The locals are the **Huesos-People**, or "The Bone Folk." They are humanoids with translucent, ivory-colored skin that reveals a skeleton made of living jade. They do not speak; they communicate by **Stridulation**—rubbing their forearms together to produce a sound like a cricket rubbing its legs against a violin string. A high-pitched rasp means "friend," while a low, grinding crunch means "run." I tried to ask a merchant for directions, but he was currently molting his arm, so I waited patiently until his new jade limb grew in. He offered me a cup of **Sangre-de-Piedra** (Blood of Stone) to apologize for the wait. It tastes like iron and cinnamon.

I attempted to purchase a new hat to keep the sun off my neck. The shopkeeper, a woman whose eyes were black opals, refused my currency. "Green paper rots," she clicked. "Here, we trade in **Echoes**." I had to stand at the edge of a cenote and scream as loud as I could. The louder the scream, the more kinetic energy the echo provided. I screamed for you, Friend. I screamed for everyone I’ve forgotten. It bought me a hat woven from the shed skin of **Feather-Snakes**.

I am currently sitting in *The Skipping Squirrel*, a cantina built inside the hollowed-out ribcage of a mammoth sloth. I am drinking a glass of **Pulque-of-the-Void**. It is a viscous, milky liquid fermented from the sap of the **World-Tree**, and it makes your shadow detach from your feet and go for a walk. My shadow is currently flirting with a shadow across the room. I hope it doesn't get into trouble; I can't leave without it.

For dinner, I am having **Tamales-de-Olvido** (Tamales of Forgetting). They are wrapped in corn husks that are actually pages from discarded history books. Inside, the masa is mixed with ash and spices. When you eat them, you lose a memory to make room for the meal. I have already forgotten the name of my first cat, and the main course hasn't even arrived yet. It is served with **Escabeche-of-Lightning**, pickled peppers that glow with a static charge. They make your tongue tingle and your thoughts race at the speed of light.

The wildlife is hazardous. I saw **Quetzalcoatlus-Birds**, flying reptiles with wingspans of twenty feet and feathers made of obsidian glass. They don't fly; they reflect the sky so perfectly that they look like tears in reality. But the most helpful creatures are the **Alux-Roaches**. They are small, house-spirit sized beetles that clean your campsite if you leave out honey. I have one named *Click* who is currently polishing my boots with a tiny leaf.

I found a message from the library written in the condensation on the side of my *Pulque* glass. It read: *To build something that lasts, you must build something that can survive the rot.*

The howler monkeys are beginning their evening scream, a sound so loud it shakes the moss from the stones. The **Huesos-People** are gathering around the fires, their jade skeletons glowing in the dusk.

I am heading *North*, trekking across the **Rio-de-Lost-Names** to the **Ciudad-de-Blanco**. I hear the city is made entirely of salt, and the tears of statues are the only water source.

Don't forget me.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
