**Origin:** The Vertical Souks of *Zahra-til-Akhir* (The City of Glass Echoes)
**Arrival Status:** The postcard is a pane of iridescent, blown glass that warps the reflection of your hand. It is warm to the touch and hums with a high-pitched frequency that makes your teeth ache. It smells of cardamom, ozone, and the static right before a lightning strike.

Dear Friend,

I am writing this from a hammock woven of fiber-optic cables, suspended three thousand feet above a ground that doesn't exist. I have arrived in **Zahra-til-Akhir**, the City of Glass Echoes. This is the Middle East that exists in the pause between a prayer and the answer—a place where the geometry is sacred and the architecture is alive.

You recall I was heading *North-West* to the **Floating-Shipyard-of-Klein-Bottle**? I jumped the tracks at the *Junction-of-Lost-Luggage*, but the train—a serpentine construct of brass and steam—refused to cross the desert of pure vacuum that surrounds this place. It simply stopped, and the conductor, a man made entirely of brass pipes, told me I had to walk the last few miles across the *Bridge-of-Mirrors*. It was terrifying; one wrong step and I would have fallen forever into my own reflection.

The city here is inverted. It hangs from the bottom of a massive, upside-down mountain range that pierces the clouds like stalactites. The gravity is erratic; in the bazaars, you have to wear shoes made of heavy lead to keep from drifting away into the spires. Everything is constructed from a translucent material called *Simurgh-Crystal*, which absorbs sound during the day and releases it as music at night.

The locals are the **Echo-Walkers**, tall, slender beings who wear robes of spun silica that chime like wind chimes when they move. They do not speak with their mouths; they speak by vibrating the crystal spires around them. Their language, *Qalb-al-Naqus*, is a form of ventriloquism where the city speaks for them. I tried to ask for directions to a hotel, but I accidentally vibrated a plea for unconditional love. A nearby minaret answered in a baritone hum, and suddenly I was betrothed to a very nice pillar near the spice market.

I am currently resting in the *Hanging-Gardens-of-Data*, nibbling on a dish of **Sand-Dollar-Dates**. These are fruits that grow on cactuses made of silicon; they taste like sunlight and have a texture like soft caramel. Inside each date is a small, hard seed that contains a memory of someone you haven't met yet. I nearly cracked a tooth on a memory of a rainy Tuesday in London, 1998.

For dinner, I plan to visit a stall that sells **Holocaust-Spice-Stew**—no, that’s a translation error, the word is *Hellos*, not *Holocaust*. It is a stew made from the vapors of evaporated stars and cumin. It is said to be so heavy it anchors the soul to the body. To drink, I ordered a *Goblet-of-Liquid-Geometry*. It is a blue fluid that arranges itself into perfect cubes in the stomach. It is very refreshing, but you must be careful not to drink it too quickly, or you might turn into a tessellation for an hour.

The wildlife is fractal and sharp-winged. There are **Nexus-Hawks**, birds with wings of polished chrome that hunt by diving into windows and stealing reflections. I also saw a swarm of **Q-Code-Locusts**, insects that eat silence. Wherever they land, the air becomes filled with a loud, rushing static. I had to swat away a particularly aggressive one that was trying to consume the quiet between my heartbeats.

I found a message from the library etched onto the surface of a raindrop that refused to fall. It read: *To build a heaven on earth, you must first bury the sky.*

The sun here is a perfect, pulsing disk of white gold, and the shadows are not black, but comprised of every color in the spectrum. I feel translucent, brittle, and infinitely resonant.

I am heading *Diagonally*, sliding down the *Slipstream-of-Possibility* toward the **Archipelago-of-Yesterday-Tomorrow**. I missed it on my last jump, but I hear the beaches are still made of hourglass sand, and the tide waits for no one.

Watch your step.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
