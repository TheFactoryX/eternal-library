Dear Friend,

I am writing this while sitting on a balcony woven from solidified cirrus clouds, sipping a beverage that warms me despite the biting chill. I have arrived in **Nephele-Necros**, the Cloud-Ghost City. This is the place where the sky keeps its memories, a metropolis built entirely from the condensed breath of the dying and the dreams of the sleeping.

You recall I was heading *To the Library of Lost Echoes*? I followed the sound of a whisper I thought I recognized, but the echo led me not into a canyon, but upward. I climbed a staircase of solidifying vapor until my hands passed through the mist and grasped cold marble. I found myself in a city where the buildings are not built, but grown. They coalesce from the white void, grand palaces of fog with Corinthian columns made ofCumulus and streets paved with rolled-out Stratus. It is beautiful, haunting, and constantly remodeling itself—if the wind changes direction, an entire district might dissolve into rain.

The geography here is atmospheric and precarious. The city floats above the world, anchored to nothing but the collective weight of its history. I am currently in the **Plaza of Parting**, a square where the ground is transparent, revealing the earth miles below. To the east lies the **Abyss of Unkept Promises**, a bottomless chasm in the cloud layer where the wind howls with the voices of people who changed their minds at the altar. It is best avoided.

The locals are the **Nephele-Shades**. They are translucent humanoids, their bodies defined by the refractive index of the water vapor they are composed of. They wear clothes woven from twilight and mist. They communicate through **Resonant-Hum**. They do not speak; they vibrate at frequencies that cause the air around them to form words, which hang in the sky like cartoon speech bubbles before drifting away. A low thrum means "Remember," while a sharp, piercing whistle means "Dissipate." I tried to compliment a local on their architecture, but I sneezed, and a sudden gust from my nose blew a hole in their torso. They simply reformed and gave me a look that said, "It’s windy, isn’t it?"

I am staying in *The Fog-Hagia*, a grand basilica where the roof is perpetually open to the cosmos. The pews are made of soft hail, and the stained-glass windows are actually patches of colored dawn light trapped in frames. It is peaceful, though I occasionally wake up floating a few inches above the bed.

I am dining on **Filo-of-Fog**. It is a pastry made of layered phyllo dough spun from spun sugar and cloud-stuff, filled with a savory lightning-infused cheese. It is light as air and shocks the tongue pleasantly. For the main course, I am having **Stuffed Cumulus-Steaks**. These are thick, fluffy chunks of cloud-meat, harvested from the grazing **Sky-Whales** (yes, they exist here, looking like giant, floating blimps with baleen made of rain). They are grilled over a fire of burning pine cones, giving them a smoky, woodsy flavor that tastes like a campfire you once loved. For dessert, I am having **Nimbus-Nougat**. It is white, fluffy, and dissolves the moment it touches your tongue, leaving behind only the feeling of sweetness.

The wildlife is… vaporous. I saw a **Thunder-Eel** today. It is a long, serpentine creature that swims through the air as if it were water, crackling with static electricity. It eats stray clouds. But the most fascinating thing is the **Memory-Moths**. They are tiny insects with wings made of clear glass; they land on your shoulder and drink the moisture from your eyes, then fly away leaving behind a small, holographic image of something you forgot.

I found a message from the library written on the wall of a cloud-tunnel, the letters fading in and out of existence. It read: *We are all just weather waiting to happen.*

The wind is picking up, and the city is beginning to curdle. I see grey edges forming on the horizon, which means a storm is brewing. Here, they don't call for rain; they call for a weeping.

I am heading *To the Forest of Gestures*. It is a woodland where the trees are made of wood, but the leaves are made of hands that clap when the wind blows. I hope they applaud my arrival.

Keep your head above the clouds.

Wish you were here.

— The Wanderer
