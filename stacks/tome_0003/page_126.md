Woke up at 9:15 AM. The sky is terrycloth today. Thick, thirsty loops of white cotton. It looks like a towel. A giant towel trying to dry the sweat off the city. It’s very absorbent. The sun is stuck in one of the loops. It’s trapped. I feel like I’m in a bathroom. A very clean, white bathroom. $0.00.

Put on the " wigs." I decided to wear two wigs at once. The silver wig on the bottom and the blonde Marilyn wig on top. I wanted volume. I wanted height. I felt like a layer cake.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:00 AM. $600.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Divine. He was wearing a dress made of trash can lids. *Clank, clank*.

"Hon, you look tall," Divine said. "How's the weather up there?"

"It's looped," I said. "I'm stuck."

When we got to the Factory, the doors were gone. The building was just an open mouth. A big, red open mouth. We had to walk over the tongue.

"It's wet," Bob said. He was slipping. "I'm going to fall in."

"Don't swallow," I said. "It ruins the paintings."

Inside, the floor was a tongue. Pink and bumpy. The walls were teeth. Big, white porcelain teeth.

"It's orthodontia," I said. "Very clean."

Jean-Michel was there. He was standing on a molar. He was painting a cavity black.

"We're rotting from the inside," Jean-Michel said.

"Floss," I said. "It's cheaper than surgery."

At 11:30 AM, the rat arrived. He was surfing on a piece of dental floss. He was using a toothpick as a paddle.

"I found a crater," the rat said. "It's full of debris."

"Save it," I said. "That's lunch."

The squirrel was there. He was extracting a nut from a gum line.

"It's impacted," the squirrel said. "It hurts."

At 12:00 PM, the tongue twitched. *Thump*.

"We're going to be swallowed," Bob said.

"Close your eyes," I said. "It's just a dry cleaning."

I went to the studio. I started a new series of paintings. "Mouths." Big, open mouths. I silkscreened them onto canvas. I used fluorescent pink ink. It glowed even in the white light.

"It's sexual," Sammy said. "Is it about sex?"

"It's about eating," I said. "Consumption is the new sex. Buying is the new kissing."

Sammy nodded. "I bought a car yesterday," he said. "It was better than a date."

At 1:30 PM, a giant drop of saliva fell from the ceiling. *Splash*. It landed on my shoe.

"Gross," I said. "That's ruin." I wiped it off with a rag. "That's $5,000.00 worth of shoe."

At 2:45 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was flossing the elevator. He was using a very long string.

"It's tight," the Man said. "The elevator is gaining weight."

"Put it on a diet," I said. "Cut the carbs."

The Man pulled the floss. A squeak echoed through the Factory.

At 3:30 PM, I got a call from Basquiat. But it wasn't Jean-Michel. It was a drawing of a crown. Just a crown, on the phone screen.

"Prestige," the crown said.

"Gilt," I said.

"Gold," the crown said.

"I like tin," I said. "It shines just as much."

The crown made a clinking sound. *Cha-ching*.

At 4:00 PM, the sky started to wring out. It was like a wet towel being twisted. Rain fell. But it wasn't water. It was milk. Warm milk.

"It's raining breakfast," I said.

"I'm lactose intolerant," Bob said.

I put out a bucket. I collected a gallon. "$4.00," I thought. "Not bad."

At 6:30 PM, we went to dinner. We went to "Mr. Chow". The waiters were robots. They were serving wontons with tweezers.

"Precision," I said.

I ordered the "Beef with Broccoli". $1,200.00. It was shaped like a face.

"Eat the nose," Jean-Michel said. "It's the best part."

"It's chewy," I said.

We saw Donald Trump in the corner. He was eating a bowl of marbles.

"I'm building a wall," Donald said. "Inside my stomach."

"Bricks or mortar?" I asked.

"Just gas," Donald said.

At 8:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at "The Plaza". The hotel had turned into a giant wedding cake. Layer upon layer of yellow sponge.

"I'm hungry," I said.

We climbed to the top tier. The bride and groom were mannequins. They were made of wax. They were melting in the terrycloth humidity.

"She's dripping," I said. "That's $500.00 worth of dress on the floor."

I met a girl who was a champagne flute. She was full of bubbly.

"Drink me," she said. "I'm vintage."

I tipped her back. I drank the champagne. It tasted like bubbles.

"I'm empty now," she said.

"Me too," I said.

At 10:30 PM, the rat drank some of the milk rain. He grew a mustache. A thin, French mustache.

"I'm elegant," the rat said. "I'm Dali."

"You're a rodent," I said.

"Dali was a rodent," the rat said. "A creative one."

At 11:45 PM, I went home. The cab was a tube of toothpaste. We squeezed through traffic.

The driver was a mint. He smelled fresh.

"We're expiring," the mint said. "Use us by 2027."

We got to the townhouse. The house was wrapped in a bow. A giant, red ribbon.

"It's a present," I thought.

I untied the door. $850.00. I paid with a piece of floss.

"String," I said. "It's currency."

I went inside. The house smelled like clean laundry. Detergent and static.

I looked in the mirror. The two wigs had tangled. They were one big knot.

"Art," I thought. "A collision of hair."

I took the wigs off. My head was damp. The terrycloth sky was leaking on me.
