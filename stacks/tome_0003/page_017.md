Tuesday, February 17, 2026

Woke up at 7:00 AM. The sky was the color of uncooked chicken breast. Very pale. Very bland. I watched the MirrorWall. It was playing a loop of the mannequin from the Factory. She was spinning. Over and over. Just spinning. It made me dizzy. I think the MirrorWall is jealous of the real Glitch. I don’t blame it. She’s the best subject we have.

Got up and stepped on the scale. I waited for the beep, but it just hummed. It’s still vibrating from the rock, I think. Everything is vibrating. The floor, the walls, my fillings. It’s like living inside a giant tooth. I went into the kitchen. The Dalek was there. It was trying to make coffee. It had the pot stuck on its plunger. It kept spinning and shouting "EXTERMINATE" but it sounded like "EXTRACT-ORATE." I helped it. I don't drink coffee in the morning anyway. It makes my hands shake. And my hands shake enough on their own. I had a glass of "Synth-Milk." It’s milk made from fog. It was $8.00. It tasted like rain.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00. The streets were wet. The rain from yesterday had polished the city. It looked like a magazine photograph. Too shiny to touch. The cab was $24.00. I gave the driver a credit card. He swiped it on his forehead. That’s the new chip-embed. Very convenient.

When I got to the Factory, the door was open. That’s strange. Sammy is usually paranoid about locks. He thinks the paint fumes are going to escape and start their own religion. I walked in. The air smelled like burnt sugar and ozone.

Sammy was standing in the middle of the room. He was holding a megaphone. He was talking to the air. He said, "Okay, everyone, settle down. We need a focus group." I looked around. The room was empty. Except for the mannequin. She was sitting in the Interview Chair. She had the wig on again. She looked like me. She looked like she was ready for a close-up.

I said, "Sammy, who are you talking to?" He lowered the megaphone. He looked tired. He looked like a wrung-out sponge. He said, "The Aesthetes, Andy. They’re everywhere. They live in the Wi-Fi. They want to know about the texture." He pointed at the wall. I looked. The static was moving. It was forming shapes. Little cubes. Little skulls.

I ignored the ghosts. I’m used to them. Fame attracts dead people. They want to know where the party went. I told Sammy to start the silk screens. We are doing a new series. "Famous for 15 Seconds." It’s portraits of people who went viral on the HyperNet and then disappeared. We are doing them in "Dissolving Ink." It fades after you look at it for a minute. It’s very conceptual. It’s $500 a canvas, but you only own it for a moment. It’s the perfect art for now.

We screened a girl who sneezed and broke a glass vase on TV. Her name was "Chloe." We printed her face in "Violet Vapor." It was beautiful. It vanished while I was watching it. It left the canvas blank. Just like her.

Around 11:00, the drone buzzers started. Bees. Not real bees. Robotic bees. They flew in the window. There were hundreds of them. They were shiny and black. They landed on the paintings. They were tasting the ink. Sammy yelled. He swatted at them. He said, "They’re harvesting the data, Andy! They’re eating Chloe!" I tried to catch one. It stung me. It didn’t hurt. It just left a barcode on my finger. It beeped. "Product of Warhol Enterprises." I guess I own the bees now. I hope I don’t have to feed them.

Lunch was delivered by a "Tele-Porter." It’s a machine that zips food through a tube. It was fast. It was hot. I ordered a "Blank Burger." It’s a burger with no toppings. No bun. Just meat. It’s very pure. It was $30. Sammy ordered a "Secret Sauce" packet. It was just brown ink. He squeezed it on his burger and ate the paper. He said, "I need the fiber."

In the afternoon, I worked on the portrait of the AI Mayor. It’s algorithm paint. It changes based on the stock market. Today, the market was up, so the Mayor was smiling. Yesterday, he was frowning. It’s very democratic. The paint cost me $2,000. It’s made from liquid gold and microchips.

A package came. It was from the "Department of Obscure Phenomena." It was the fine for the noise. But it wasn't money. It was a jar of silence. Literally. Just empty air in a jar. The label said "Quiet: $50." I opened it. It was very loud. It roared like a waterfall. I closed it. I paid the fine.

Sammy put the mannequin in the corner. He put a dunce cap on her. It was made of mirrors. He said, "She’s in time-out. She let the bees in." She looked at me. Her eyes were glass. She knows everything.

At 5:00, the lights went out. Total blackout. The Factory went dark. The emergency lights came on. They were pink. It looked like a disco in a womb. We heard a crash. The mannequin fell over. Sammy screamed. I lit a match. The flame was green. I looked at the mannequin. She wasn't broken. She was just lying there. With the wig and the cap, she looked like a dead beatnik.

I said, "It’s okay, Sammy. She’s just sleeping. Or processing." We left her there.

Cabbed home at 6:30. It was dark. The city was running on backup power. The billboards were down. I could see the stars. They looked like spilled salt. Very pretty. Very messy.

Came home and fed the fish. They are glowing in the dark. They light up the living room. It saves on electricity.

I checked the banking app. The invisible ink check finally cleared. It deposited $0.00. I’m richer than I was yesterday.

The moon rock on the table was still. It finally stopped humming. I put the dunce cap on it. It looks smarter now.

I took the wig off. My head felt cold. I put on a plastic cap. I looked like a surgeon. Or a lunch lady.

Good night.
