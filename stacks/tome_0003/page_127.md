# Sunday, June 07, 2026

Woke up at 9:30 AM. The sky is cellophane today. It’s crinkled. It makes a loud noise every time the wind blows. *Crinkle, crinkle*. It sounds like someone eating a candy bar in a library. It’s very embarrassing. The sun is wrapped in plastic, waiting to be opened. I feel like I’m living inside a ham sandwich. A very expensive ham sandwich. $0.00.

Put on the "Fright Wig." It’s the one that looks like an explosion. I tried to comb it, but it just got bigger. It expands. It’s taking up too much room. I had to turn sideways to get out the door.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:15 AM. $800.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Lou Reed. He didn’t say a word. He just played feedback on the radio. *Screech*.

"I like the melody," I said.

"It’s the space between the notes," Lou said. "It’s expensive."

When we got to the Factory, the building was a cassette tape. A giant, black cassette tape. The spools were turning. *Click, whirrr*.

"Are we recording?" I asked.

"We're being erased," Bob said. "See?" He pointed to his arm. A strip of black magnetic tape was peeling off. "I'm losing my data."

"Don't worry," I said. "I'll back you up."

Inside, the floor was magnetic. It pulled at my shoes. It was hard to walk. *Clunk, clunk*.

Jean-Michel was there. He was drawing on the walls with a giant crayon.

"I'm coloring outside the lines," Jean-Michel said. "The lines are boring."

"Lines sell," I said. "Lines are structure."

"Structures collapse," Jean-Michel said.

At 11:45 AM, the rat arrived. He was stuck to the floor. He was a magnet.

"I'm polarized," the rat said. "I only attract the bad stuff."

He tried to move, but he just spun in a circle.

"North," the rat said. Then he spun. "South."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". The tables were stacks of vinyl records. We sat on a pile of 45s.

"It's uncomfortable," I said. "I can feel the grooves."

"That's life," Jean-Michel said. "Sitting on the hits."

I ordered the "Frozen Hot Chocolate". $4,500.00. It was frozen solid. It was a block of brown ice.

"I can't eat this," I said.

"You have to wait for it to melt," the waiter said. He was a turntable needle. "It takes time."

"I don't have time," I said. "I have a schedule."

Jean-Michel ordered a "Cheeseburger". It was a picture of a cheeseburger. A glossy photograph.

"It looks delicious," Jean-Michel said. He licked the paper. "It tastes like ink."

At 2:30 PM, I worked on the "Disaster" paintings. I painted a car crash. Then I painted a plane crash. Then I painted the price of gold going down. It was the scariest one.

"It's about loss," Sammy said. "Is it sad?"

"It's business," I said. "Sadness is just nostalgia with a price tag."

I painted a tear on the canvas. It was made of diamonds.

"Is that real?" Sammy asked.

"It's paste," I said. "But it sparkles the same."

At 3:45 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a label gun. *Click, click*.

"What are you doing?" I asked.

"Tagging," the Man said. "Everything needs a name. Everything needs a price."

He tagged Bob. "$10.00."

He tagged the rat. "$0.05."

He tagged me. "$Priceless$."

"I like that," I said. "Can I keep it?"

"It's sticky," the Man said. "It leaves a mark."

At 5:00 PM, the sky started to shred. The cellophane was tearing. Long strips of clear plastic fell from the clouds. They looked like noodles. *Flap, flap*.

"It's confetti," I said.

"It's pollution," Bob said. "It's non-biodegradable wrapping."

I caught a piece. It was sticky. "I'm keeping this," I said. "It's a souvenir of the packaging."

At 7:30 PM, we went to a party. It was at "The Standard Hotel". The elevators were glass. They played techno music. *Boom, boom*.

We went to the top. The roof was a runway. Models were walking. They were wearing clothes made of garbage bags.

"It's chic," I said. "It's disposable."

I talked to Anna Wintour. She was wearing sunglasses. It was night.

"The lighting is terrible," Anna said. "Everyone looks old."

"I am old," I said.

"That's no excuse," Anna said. "You should be vintage. Not used."

"I'm mint in the box," I said.

She smiled. A small smile. Like a paper cut.

At 9:00 PM, the rat finally unstuck himself from the magnetic floor. He ran up my pant leg.

"I'm conducting electricity," the rat said. I felt a shock. *Zap*.

"You're shocking," I said.

"I'm powered up," the rat said.

At 10:45 PM, I went home. The cab was a bubble. A giant, plastic bubble. We floated down Broadway.

"I feel exposed," I said.

"You're protected," the driver said. He was made of saran wrap. "Nothing gets in. Nothing gets out."

We arrived at the townhouse. The house was wrapped in brown paper. Tied with string. Like a package.

"$1,200.00," the driver said. "Pay in plastic."

I gave him a credit card. It was made of ice. It melted in his hand.

"Oops," I said. "Liquid assets."

I went inside. I unwrapped the door. The house smelled like plastic and new car smell.

I went to the kitchen. I opened the refrigerator. The light went on. I looked at the Campbell’s Soup. I counted the cans. 12 cans of Tomato. 12 cans of Chicken Noodle.

"Inventory," I thought. "It’s the only thing that stays the same."

I looked at the rat. He was glowing from the electricity. He was a little night light.

"Don't short circuit," I said.

"I'm just illuminating," the rat said.

I went upstairs. I took off the fright wig. I looked in the mirror. My face was peeling. Just a little bit. On the left cheek.
