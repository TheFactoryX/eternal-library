Woke up at 7:00 AM. The room was spinning. Literally. The floor was rotating. It was a lazy Susan. I slid out of bed and landed in the closet. *Thump*.

I got dressed. It was hard because the hangers were swinging away from me. I finally caught a black turtleneck. I put it on. It felt like a second skin. Or maybe a first skin. I put on my jeans. I didn't bother with shoes. I just slid across the floor to the kitchen.

The cat was holding onto the counter with her claws. Her fur was blowing in the wind. The centrifugal force.

"Slow down," I told the room.

It didn't listen. It just went *Whirrrr*.

I poured a glass of tomato juice. The juice stuck to the side of the glass. It wouldn't come out. I had to lick the glass. *Lick, lick*. Like a dog.

I looked out the window. The buildings were spinning too. The whole city is a carousel. And I'm on the horse that goes up and down.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $38.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Mick Jagger. He had huge lips. He was chewing gum.

"Rough night?" Mick asked.

"I didn't move," I said. "The room moved for me."

"Groovy," Mick said. "Hang on."

He drove fast. We went around corners on two wheels. *Screeeech*. I held onto the strap. My knuckles turned white.

The Factory was quiet. Sammy was there. He was holding onto a column. He looked green.

"The floor is spinning," Sammy said.

"I know," I said. "It's the latest trend. Kinetic real estate."

"I'm going to be sick," Sammy said.

"Don't," I said. "It ruins the rugs."

I walked to the back. My feet made *scuff-scuff* sounds. The friction was hot. I sat down. I anchored myself to a chair with a bungee cord.

I looked at the painting from yesterday. The blur. It was spinning too. It was turning into a solid color. Just grey. Like a fog.

"Good," I thought. "Less is more."

I opened my portfolio. I had to do a portrait of the Mayor. The Mayor is a cloud now. A cumulus cloud. He floats over City Hall.

"How do you paint a cloud?" I asked Sammy.

"Use a spray bottle," Sammy said. "Watercolors. He's very transparent."

I took out my paints. I squeezed the tubes. *Pfffft*. Nothing came out. The paint was dry. Solid blocks of color. Like crayons.

"I can't work like this," I said.

"Scrape it," Sammy said. "Make shavings. Call it 'Snow in the Studio.'"

"Genius," I said.

I scraped the paint. *Scrape, scrape*. Blue shavings. Red shavings. They fell on the floor. They spun around. Like a tornado.

Lunch was at "The Oyster Bar" in Grand Central Terminal. But the terminal is underwater too. Just the waiting room. You have to take a glass-bottom boat.

I ordered the oysters. $85.00. They were served by a "Simulacrum" of Jackie Kennedy. She was wearing a pearl necklace made of bubbles.

"These are fresh," Jackie said. "They were clapping just a minute ago."

"I don't eat things that applaud," I said.

"Just swallow," she said. "Don't think."

I swallowed. *Slurp*. They tasted like the ocean. Salty tears.

I paid the bill. I left the money on the boat. The water soaked it. The bills turned to mush. "Paper mache," I thought.

Went to a party at 4:00 PM. It was for a magazine called *Dust*. "The Magazine for the Ages."

It was at a penthouse on Park Avenue. The elevator was glass. I watched the city go down. Or maybe we were going up. It's hard to tell when gravity is optional.

The party was full of people. They were all wearing grey. They were blending into the walls.

"Andy!" someone shouted.

It was a blur. I squinted. It was Truman Capote. The real one? Or the Simulacrum? I couldn't tell. He was holding a drink.

"Did you find the wig?" Truman asked.

"No," I said. "Did you?"

"It's on the moon," Truman said. "I saw it through a telescope. It's growing."

"Growing?"

"It's putting down roots," Truman said. "It's becoming a topiary."

"Amazing," I said. "I should sell tickets."

A waiter passed by. He had a tray of pills.

"Vitamins?" he asked.

"Only if they make me younger," I said.

"These make you older," he said. "It's more dignified."

"I'll take two," I said. I swallowed them dry. *Gulp*. They felt heavy in my stomach. Like stones.

I talked to a sculpture. It was a metal man. He was very shiny.

"Are you art?" I asked.

"I'm a bank account," he said. "My value fluctuates with the market."

"Nice to meet you," I said. "I'm Andy."

"I know," he said. "You're blue chip."

"Hopefully blue," I said. "I'm feeling a little green."

I left the party at 6:00 PM. I felt heavy. The pills were working. I felt my shoulders drop. My sagging skin sagged some more.

"I'm aging gracefully," I thought. "Or disgracefully."

I hailed a cab. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Elvis. He was wearing a jumpsuit. It was studded with rhinestones.

"Thank you," Elvis said. "Thank you very much."

"Home," I said. "The one that's not spinning."

"I can't stop," Elvis said. "I'm on a tour."

"Just drive around the block," I said. "Until I dissolve."

We drove. I watched the lights. They were streaks. Yellow and red. Like smeared lipstick.

I checked my phone. I had a text from Bob.

*I'm in the spam folder again. Send help.*

*Check the 'Promotions' tab,* I texted back.

*I did. I'm a coupon now,* Bob texted. *50% off.*

*That's good,* I texted. *That's business.*
