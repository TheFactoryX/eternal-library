Woke up at 9:15 AM. The sky is television today. Not the sky *is* like a television, but it *is* a television. A giant Sony Trinitron screen stretched over Manhattan, bolted to the clouds. The picture was rolling vertically at first, then a test pattern came on. The color bars. I always liked the blue bar best. It’s such a calm blue. A safe, electric blue. Then the news started. It was just a close-up of an eye. A giant eye looking down at us. Blinking. *Click. Whirrr*. I wondered if the eye was God or just the cameraman. $0.00.

Put on the "Remote Control" wig. It’s a black wig with two aluminum antennas sticking up. They pick up police scanners, microwave signals, and sometimes Russian radio. I kept hearing static and a voice saying, *“The heat is on.”* I adjusted the tuner on my left ear. *Buzz. Snap.*

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $3,400.00. The fare keeps going up. It’s because the cars run on solid gold now. Or maybe just greed. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was tiny, wearing a raincoat, and holding a tall glass of something amber.

“Is it raining?” I asked.

“It’s always raining somewhere,” Truman said. “Pour some out.”

I poured a little on the floor. It sizzled. “Acid,” I said.

“Lemon juice,” he said. “For the tea.”

When we got to the Factory, the elevator was a TV set. You step in, and you’re on the screen. There was a monitor inside the car, showing us live.

“We’re stars,” I said.

“We’re reruns,” Bob said. He was holding a remote control. He pointed it at me.

*Click*.

I muted myself. I tried to scream, but no sound came out.

*Click*.

I was on pause.

“It’s better this way,” Bob said. “No talking.”

He pressed *Play*. I moved again. I felt like a marionette.

At 11:45 AM, the rat arrived. He was carrying a satellite dish on his back. It was spinning.

“I’m receiving signals from the mother ship,” the rat said. “They want to know about the cheese.”

“The cheese is an illusion,” I said. “It’s just wood pulp.”

“That’s what they said,” the rat said. “It’s a conspiracy.”

The squirrel was there. He was chewing on a coaxial cable. Black rubber and copper wire spilled out.

“I’m trying to get premium channels,” the squirrel said. “I want to see the fights.”

At 12:30 PM, Jean-Michel called. He was on the TV screen in the lobby. His head was floating in the corner of the broadcast, interrupting the test pattern.

“Andy?” he said. “Turn to Channel 4. I’m graffiti-ing the signal.”

I turned the dial on the wall. The TV wall flickered. Jean-Michel was painting neon crowns over the news anchor’s face.

“It’s vandalism,” I said. “It’s beautiful.”

“Buy it,” Jean-Michel said. “Before they cut the transmission.”

“I’ll give you fifty,” I said.

“I want a hundred,” he said.

“Seventy-five,” I said.

“Deal,” he said. Then he fuzzed out into static. *Zzzzt*.

At 1:45 PM, we went to lunch. We went to “The Odeon”. The menu was a digital screen. The prices kept changing. They would go up, then down, then up again. It was the Stock Market Menu.

“I’ll have the ‘Bull Market Burger’,” I said. “It’s $12,000.00.”

While I was eating, the waiter brought the check. The price had dropped.

“You bought on the dip,” the waiter said. “It’s only $40.00 now.”

“I feel rich,” I said. “But I also feel full.”

Bob ordered the “Crash of ’29”. It was a empty plate.

“It’s symbolic,” Bob said. “Very filling.”

At 3:15 PM, I worked on the "TV Set" paintings. I was painting screens within screens. Infinite regression. I painted a picture of me painting a picture of me. It was dizzying. My hand hurt. I painted a cast on my hand. Then I painted a painting of a cast on my hand.

“It’s very meta,” Sammy said. “It sells in Tokyo.”

“Everything sells in Tokyo,” I said. “Even garbage.”

“I’ll export this,” Sammy said. He bagged up the paint scrapings from the floor.

At 5:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a cable. A thick black cord.

“It’s time to get wired,” the Man said. “You need an upgrade.”

He plugged the cord into my ear. *Click*.

I felt a jolt. Information rushed in. I knew the weather in Paris. I knew the price of soybeans. I knew who was sleeping with whom.

“I feel smarter,” I said.

“Now you have 500 channels,” the Man said. “And nothing is on.”

At 6:30 PM, we went to a party. It was at the "Studio 54 Redux". It was a virtual club. You put on goggles and you’re there. Really there. The dance floor was a grid of lasers.

“I’m dancing,” I said. But I was just standing in the corner, vibrating.

“You’re a glitch,” Bianca Jagger said. She was wearing a dress made of pixels. She kept flickering.

“I think I’m buffering,” I said.

At 8:00 PM, the giant sky TV switched to a movie. *Empire*. The 1964 film. Just the Empire State Building. It was eight hours long. The whole city stopped to watch a building standing still.

“It’s boring,” someone said.

“It’s hypnotic,” I said. “It’s like watching life happen.”

At 10:30 PM, the sky went black. The TV turned off. A plug pulled from the wall.

“It’s over,” I said. “The broadcast day is done.”

The stars came out. But they were just dead pixels. Little black dots on the blue screen.

“Burn-in,” I said. “The screen is ruined.”

Cabbed home at 11:15 PM. $3,600.00.

I went to the townhouse. I turned on the TV in the kitchen. The test pattern was on.

I opened the fridge. The light flickered. *Bzzt*.

I took out a Campbell’s Soup. “Tomato”.

I held it up to the light. The can was reflecting the TV screen. I looked at the can, and the can was showing the test pattern.
