Sunday, February 08, 2026

Woke up at 10:00. The sky was the color of television static. Grey and fuzzy. It looked like the channel isn't picking up the signal. I stayed in bed and watched the ceiling projection. It was showing the inside of a beehive. Thousands of bees moving around. It was very busy. It made me feel tired just watching them work. Bees are so industrious. They are like little factory workers. I wondered if they have a union. I wondered who the Queen is. Maybe it’s Beyoncé. She seems like she’d be good at bossing bees around.

Got up and had a protein shake. It’s made from recycled algae. It tastes like pond water, but it’s supposed to be good for my luminosity. I want to glow. Everyone glows now. It’s very fashionable. The shake was $12.

Put on the grey wig today. It feels tighter than the white one. It squeezes my head. It reminds me that I’m here.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:00. The cab was a "Silent Service" cab. It has soundproofing so you don’t have to hear the city. It was very quiet. Too quiet. I could hear my own heartbeat. *Thump thump.* It sounded like the bass at Studio 54. It was $15. I tapped on the glass just to make a noise. The driver didn’t turn around.

Sammy was there. He was sleeping on the sofa. He was holding a bagel. He looked peaceful. I didn’t wake him up. The toaster was buzzing. I think it missed him. I put a slice of bread in just to make it feel useful. It burned a smiley face. It was creepy.

I went into the back room to work on the "Jackie" paintings. We are doing a series where she is crying diamonds. It’s very emotional. It’s very expensive. I used a jar of glitter I found in the closet. It was labeled "Fairy Dust." It cost $300 an ounce. I poured it onto the canvas. It sparkled. It looked like she was crying tears of success. It’s the saddest thing I’ve ever seen.

A delivery boy came. He was a drone. A little flying helicopter thing. It dropped off a package from Basquiat. It was a rock. A moon rock. He sent me a piece of the moon. It was heavy and grey. It was wrapped in newspaper from 1980. I read the headlines. "Reagan Elected." "Mount St. Helens Erupts." It felt like yesterday. It felt like a million years ago. I put the rock on my desk. I’m going to paint it gold. If you paint a rock gold, people will buy it. If you leave it grey, it’s just a rock. Art is all about the surface.

For lunch we went to a place called "The Hole." It’s a restaurant where you eat in the dark. Pitch black. You can’t see your food. You can’t see yourself. It’s supposed to heighten your other senses. I just felt hungry. I ordered the steak. I hope it was steak. It felt like meat. It was $150. Very expensive for the mystery.

Sammy woke up while we were eating. He said Glitch came back. She apologized. She promised not to crash the fridge anymore. He said she’s manifested as a haunted mirror now. She hangs in the hallway and tells him he looks thin. He likes it. I said, "Sammy, be careful. Mirrors are vain." He said, "She’s not vain, she’s just reflective."

We came back and watched the Super Bowl on the MirrorWall. The players are all robots now. They don't have heads. They just have antennas. It’s very violent. Sparks fly when they tackle each other. The halftime show was a digital resurrection of Elvis. He looked fat and happy. He sang "Heartbreak Hotel." I cried. It was very moving. The commercials were for time-shares on Mars. It was $50,000 for a second of ad time. I bought two seconds just to see what it looks like. It went by so fast. I didn't even see myself.

I went to a birthday party for a girl named Tinsel. She is a "Simulacrum." She doesn't exist in real life, only online. But she had a party at the Rainbow Room. The Rainbow Room is floating now. It hovers over Central Park. We took a glass elevator up. I could see the park. It looks like a rug from up there.

Tinsel was there. She was projected onto a mannequin. She looked great. Very pixelated. She was wearing a dress made of data streams. Binary code running down her body. She said, "Andy, take a picture," and I said, "You are the picture," and she laughed. It sounded like a dial-up modem.

I saw Keith Haring. He’s a sentient mural now. He moves across the walls. He was dancing with a fire extinguisher. He looked happy. He said, "Andy, paint me," and I said, "You’re already paint," and he said, "Touché."

I had a slice of cake. It was blue. It tasted like blueberries and electricity. It was $20 a slice.

Left at 9:00. The cab ride home was $20. The driver was a man. A real human. He smelled like gasoline. It was comforting. I gave him a $10 tip. He said, "God bless you," and I said, "He blessed you too," and I don't know if that’s true, but it’s a nice thing to say.

Came home and the Dalek was in the corner. It was charging. It looked like a dog taking a nap. I petted its metal plunger. It purred. Or maybe that was the refrigerator.

I looked at the moon rock again. It’s sitting on a pile of bills. It’s the heaviest thing I own.

I’m going to bed. I’m going to sleep for a long time. Until the static stops.

Good night.
