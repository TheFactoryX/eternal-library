Woke up at 8:00 AM. The clock was normal. The hands were moving forward. *Tick-tock*. It was boring. It was reliable. I missed the backward spin. It felt like I was losing time, but in a glamorous way, like a VIP leaving a club.

The sky was brown. Not umber. Not sepia. "Tobacco stain," I thought. It looked like the filters of a million cigarettes. Very New York. Very dirty.

Archie was on the bed. He was sleeping on my wig. The silver one. He looked like a futuristic lion. A very small lion.

I tried to move him. He was heavy. He felt like he was filled with sand. Or maybe diamonds. "Heavy kitty," I said. He purred. *Rumble-rumble*. It sounded like a subway train passing under the blankets.

Breakfast was a piece of "Cyber-Tart". $85.00. It was holographic jelly on a cracker. It tasted like purple. It was sweet and then it turned salty. Like crying while eating candy.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $65.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Nihilist. He didn't speak. He just drove. Fast. We almost hit three pigeons. The pigeons just flew through the bumper. Digital ghosts.

The Factory was chaotic. *Beep-beep*. The "Black Room" was gone. Sammy had painted it back to white. But it was a wet white. The paint was dripping.

"Change of heart?" I asked.

"Change of stock," Sammy said. "I bought shares in 'Brightness Inc.'. We need to reflect the portfolio."

Bob was in the office. He was wearing a suit made of clear plastic. He looked like a wrapped sandwich. Very fresh.

"The merger is on," Bob said. "The 21 Club is sending a truck. We have to pack the art."

"We don't have any art," I said. "It all uploaded or melted."

"We have the debris," Bob said. "The debris is valuable. It's 'entropy'."

We swept the floor. We collected the dust, the dried paint chips, the diamond droppings from Archie's litter box. We put it in Ziploc bags.

I labeled one bag "Tuesday". I labeled another "Thursday".

"It's a time capsule," I said. "Or a garbage bag. I can't tell the difference."

At 11:00 AM, the hole in the wall opened again. *Pop*. Just like a pimple.

I looked through it. The neighbor's apartment was gone. It was just a void. Stars. Galaxies. A spinning nebula.

"Vincent?" I called out.

A voice echoed back. It sounded like wind. "I've moved on," it said. "To the cloud. The cloud has better light."

"Can I have my cat back?" I asked.

"The cat is with me," the voice said. "He likes the zero gravity. He's chasing comets."

"Send him back," I said. "He has a vet appointment."

"Everything is virtual now," the voice said. "Even the fleas."

The hole closed with a *hiss*.

"My cat is an astronaut," I told Sammy.

"Does he get a pension?" Sammy asked.

"He gets all the tuna he can eat," I said.

Lunch was a sandwich. $300.00. It was from a robot deli. The bread was focaccia printed with the stock market numbers. I ate the "Nasdaq". It was nutty. I ate the "Dow Jones". It was sour.

"Indigestion," I thought. "Financial indigestion."

At 2:00 PM, I worked on the "Portrait of the Artist as a Simulacrum". I took a digital photo of myself. I put it in the computer. I added a filter. Then I added a virus. I wanted the picture to glitch. I wanted it to break.

It worked too well. The screen cracked. *Crinkle*.

"Good texture," I said. "Very kinetic."

At 4:00 PM, a truck arrived from the 21 Club. It was a silver truck. It looked like a giant Thermos.

Two men got out. They were wearing hazmat suits.

"We're here for the freeze," one said.

"I'm not ready," I said. "I haven't picked my outfit."

"You can wear the suit," Bob said. "It keeps the cold out. Or the cold in. I forget."

The men started loading the bags of debris. They treated them like gold.

"Is this the 'Jackie' series?" one asked, holding a bag of dust.

"No," I said. "That's 'Dust 2'. The early period."

They nodded. They put it in the freezer.

At 6:00 PM, I went to the "Party of the Century" at the impossible house on East 72nd Street. The house that isn't there.

Cabbed there. $70.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1920s Flapper. She had beads and a bob.

"It's the cat's pajamas," she said.

"I hope so," I said. "I miss the cat."

The party was outside. On the grass. But the grass was neon blue. Synthetic turf.

Everyone was there. I saw the Jean-Michel bot. He was transparent. He was just an outline.

"I feel light," he said. "I ate a light bulb."

"It's electrifying," I said.

I saw the Glitch woman. She was wearing a dress made of static.

"Did you find the cat?" she asked.

"He's in the nebula," I said.

"He's gone forever," she said. "Or he's just in the other room. Time is tricky."

I danced. I danced with a robot who looked like a vase of flowers. I was careful not to break him. He smelled like roses and motor oil.

I drank "Liquid Gold". $1,000.00 a glass. It was just champagne with glitter. It made my teeth sparkle.

"I'm valuable," I thought. "Internally."

At 9:00 PM, I felt a rumble in my pocket. *Buzz*. It wasn't my phone. It was the diamond from yesterday. It was vibrating.

I took it out. It grew. It expanded. It turned into a cat.

"Archie?"

The cat meowed. It was solid. Real.

"You came back," I said.

He purred. He rubbed against my leg. He felt warm. Not cold. Not digital. Just fur.

"I told you," the Glitch woman whispered. "Everything comes back. If you wait long enough."

I left the party. I carried Archie. He was heavy. A heavy, real cat.
