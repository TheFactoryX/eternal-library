Woke up at 9:30 AM. The floor was water again, but this morning it froze. It’s an ice rink. A very slippery, very cold ice rink. I slid out of bed on my back. *Swoosh*. I slid down the hallway like a hockey puck. I crashed into the wall. The wall was padded, thankfully. It felt like a velvet cushion. I don’t know if the house is trying to kill me or hug me. My knee is bruised. It looks like a blueberry. $0.00.

Put on the "Insulated" wig. It’s made of pink fiberglass. Like the insulation inside a wall. It itches, but it keeps the heat in. I look like a giant loofah.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:15 AM. The streets were quiet. All the noise from yesterday stopped. The cars were silent. The people were silent. It’s like someone pressed the Mute button on the remote control. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Marilyn Monroe. She didn’t say a word. She just smiled. She drove the cab with her elbows. $22,000.00. Expensive, but silent is good.

When we got to the Factory, the grass was gone. The ice was gone. The floor was a giant grid. Like graph paper. Giant black lines on a white floor. We were walking inside a drawing.

"It's perspective," Bob said. He was walking toward the vanishing point. He looked tiny. A tiny Bob.

"I feel two-dimensional," I said.

"It's retro," Sammy said. He was erasing a corner of the room with a giant gum eraser. *Scritch, scratch*. "I'm making a door."

At 11:00 AM, the rat arrived. He looked different again. He was a hologram. He was flickering.

"I'm ghost-like," the rat said. His voice sounded like static. *Shhh*. "I died in the night. But I kept my appointment."

"Did it hurt?" I asked.

"No," the rat said. "I just faded out. Can I have a cheese advance?"

"Give him the voucher," I told Sammy.

The squirrel was there. He was sitting on a grid line. He was drawing himself.

"I'm self-portraiting," the squirrel said.

At 12:00 PM, Jean-Michel called. He was on the TV. The TV was floating in the middle of the room. No wires.

"Andy?" he said. "I'm painting the void."

"Is it black?" I asked.

"It's nothing," he said. "I'm painting the air invisible. It's an erasure."

"Sounds expensive," I said. "Do you need more brushes?"

"I need a deletion," he said. "Send the bill."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Indochine". But the restaurant had been taken over by mannequins. Plastic people. They were sitting at the tables. They were eating plastic food.

"I'll have the 'Spring Roll'," I told the waiter. He was a mannequin too. He had no eyes. Just smooth plastic. $40,000.00.

He brought a plastic plate. It had a plastic spring roll on it.

"It looks real," I said.

I took a bite. It tasted like melting plastic. *Gooey*. "It's synthetic," I said.

"It's immortal," Bob said. He was eating a plastic noodle. "It doesn't digest. It just accumulates."

"I feel full," I said. "But empty."

At 3:00 PM, I worked on the "Oxidation" paintings. I usually pee on them. But today, I used champagne. Dom Pérignon. I shook the bottle. *Pop*. I sprayed the canvas. It smells like a party. It smells like celebrities and vomit.

"It's a celebration," I said.

"It's sticky," Sammy said. The flies were coming. They were holographic flies. They buzzed around the canvas. *Bzzzt*.

At 4:30 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was carrying a briefcase. He looked solid.

"We need to talk about the diamonds," the Man said. "The ketchup diamonds."

"What about them?" I asked. "I ate some."

"The government says they are taxable," the Man said. "They say they are income. In-kind income."

"They were condiments," I said. "Ketchup is a deduction."

"Not at that carat weight," the Man said. "You owe capital gains on the garnish."

"Charge it to the 'Social Observation' budget," I said.

At 6:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at the "MoMA". The museum was dark. We were given flashlights.

"It's a flashlight tour," the curator said. "Only look at the art. Don't look at each other."

I shined my light on a painting. It was a soup can. But it was open. The soup was spilling out onto the floor. Real soup. Tomato soup.

"It's Campbell's," I said.

"No," the curator said. "It's ' tomato Soup' by Andy Warhol. 2026."

"It's better than the original," I said. "It has more depth."

Bianca Jagger was there. She was wearing a suit made of mirrors. I shined my flashlight on her. The beam bounced everywhere. It hit the ceiling. It hit the floor. It hit her.

"I'm blinding," she said.

"You're reflecting," I said.

At 9:00 PM, the power went out. Total darkness.

"Perfect," I said. "Now the art is gone."

"It's immersive," Bob said from the dark.

I felt a hand on my arm. It was cold. Plastic.

"Mr. Warhol," a voice said. It was the waiter from lunch. "You didn't pay."

"I put it on my tab," I said.

"There is no tab in the dark," the voice said.

Cabbed home at 10:30 PM. $30,000.00. The driver was a Simulacrum of me. He had the wig. He had the glasses.

"Good night, Andy," he said.

"Good night, Andy," I said.

Went into the townhouse. The floor was floor again. Solid wood. Thank God.

I reached in. My hand went through the back of the fridge. I kept reaching. It went through the wall. I kept reaching. I felt something. It felt like a giant switch. A toggle switch.

I pulled it.
