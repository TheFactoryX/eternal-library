# Sunday, April 12, 2026

> Written at 2026-04-12 23:59:59

---

Woke up at 10:00 AM. Very late. The sun was shouting at the windows. *Shout-shout*. I pulled the covers up. Archie was sleeping on my pillow. He smelled like smoke and tuna. We both smelled like camp fires.

The phone rang. It was the landline. The red one.

"Mr. Warhol?" It was a young voice. High-pitched. "It's Justin. The Mayor's aide."

"Justin who?" I asked. "Justin Timberlake?"

"No," he said. "Justin 3.0. I'm a bio-android."

"Oh," I said. "Is the Mayor suing me too?"

"The Mayor wants to declare the bathtub a landmark," Justin said. "The 'Site of the Great Cooking'. He wants to put a plaque on it. He’s sending a photographer over at noon."

"I’m not dressed," I said. "And I don't have a bathtub anymore. I have an idea."

"Put on a wig," Justin said. "Wear a scarf. Be mysterious."

"I’m always mysterious," I said. "I’m not even here."

I hung up.

I got up. The apartment was cold. The air conditioning was broken, but it was blowing ice. Sammy must have fixed it. Or the window was open to space.

I went to the bathroom. The tub was empty. Just the black scorch mark. It looked like a shadow that forgot to leave. I touched it. It was warm. *Warm*. Like holding a hand.

"You're famous now," I told the tub. "Don't let it go to your head."

Archie rubbed against my leg. He wanted breakfast.

I opened a can of caviar. $400.00. It was "Space Caviar". From the Caspian Sea on Mars. It was green. I put it on a cracker.

"Good?" I asked Archie.

He sniffed it and turned away. He wanted the cheap stuff. The tuna. Cats have no taste. They only have hunger.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:30 AM. $55.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Marilyn Monroe. She was crying. Tears made of oil.

"They made me too real," she said. "I feel everything."

"I know," I said. "Turn up the radio."

We listened to static. It was soothing.

The Factory was quiet. Sammy was sweeping up the dead flies. *Sweep-sweep*.

"The flies died," Sammy said. "They flew too close to the exhaust."

"It's a tragedy," I said. "Can we sell them?"

"I'm making them into earrings," Sammy said. "Very punk."

I went to the back. I sat in front of the blank canvas. I needed to work. I needed to fill the void.

I took a can of tomato soup. Campbell's. $1.50. I poured it into a bucket. I added glue. And diamond dust. The dust from the tip I gave the bartender.

I dipped a brush in it. I painted a box. A red box.

"It's 'Soup to Nuts'," I thought. "Or 'From Soup to Ash'."

While I was painting, the jar in my pocket buzzed. The time jar. It was getting warm. I took it out. The blue light was pulsing. *Thump-thump*. Just like the meat.

"Hello?" I whispered.

"I'm bored," the Great-Great-Grandclone said. "The future is boring. Everyone is immortal. No one has any drama."

"Drama is overrated," I said. "Try a lawsuit."

"We did," he said. "It lasted a thousand years."

"I'm painting," I said. "Go away."

"Send me the painting," he said. "I'll trade you a dinosaur."

"Alive?"

"Stuffed," he said. "It plays the violin."

"No deal," I said. "I don't like loud pets."

I put the jar back in my pocket.

At 2:00 PM, the Mayor's photographer arrived. He was a drone with a very large lens. He hovered over my shoulder.

"Look sad," the drone buzzed. "Look like you lost something."

"I lost my lunch money," I said.

"Perfect," the drone said. *Click-click*. A flash of green light.

"Now the bathtub," the drone said. "Pretend to wash it."

"I can't," I said. "It's haunted."

"Even better," the drone said.

I stood by the tub. I looked at the scorch mark.

"Cheese," I said.

"You're supposed to say 'Cheese' after the picture," the drone said.

"I know," I said. "I'm hungry."

The drone left. It knocked over a lamp on the way out. *Crash*.

"Fabulous," I said. "Now it's a ruin."

Cabbed to Serendipity at 4:00 PM. $60.00. I wanted the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate again. I needed a brain freeze. I needed to stop thinking about the Meat.

The sunglasses lady was there. The one with the TVs. She was watching the news.

"They found the meat," she said. "On the moon."

"What?" I asked.

"The smoke," she said. "It drifted up. It condensed near the Sea of Tranquility. It formed a meat cloud. It’s raining ham sandwiches."

"Gross," I said.

"Profound," she said. "It’s a miracle."

I ordered the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $25.00. I ordered two straws. One for me, one for the ghost.

While I drank, I looked at the people. Everyone was staring at their phones. No one was looking at the art.

"I'm the art," I thought. "And they're missing me."

I paid the bill. Left a tip. $40.00. I paid with a button. A real button from a real coat. The waitress looked confused.

"It's vintage," I said. "It's worth more than the drink."

She kept it.

Walking home. The wind was picking up. It smelled like rain. And barbecue.

I got home at 6:00 PM. Archie was waiting. He was sitting in the sink. He looked like a gray potato.

"Did you miss me?" I asked.

He yawned. A wide, pink yawn.

I sat down to write.

I looked at my hands. They were stained with red paint and soot. I looked like a mechanic. A mechanic of fame.

"Goodnight, world," I said.

The red phone rang.

"Hello?"

"It's the Vice President," said the voice. "I swallowed a staple."

"Don't sue me," I said.

"I just wanted to say," he said. "The meat was delicious."

I hung up.

I put on my pajamas. The ones with the cows. I got into bed.

The jar of time in my pocket was ticking.
