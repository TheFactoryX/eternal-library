Woke up at 8:00 AM. The sky was back to beige, but it looked textured. Like a drywall ceiling in a cheap office building. It’s very reassuring. I like it when the world looks unfinished. It gives me something to do.

Archie was on the floor.

"Gravity is back," Archie said. He was walking, but he was walking very slowly. Like he was underwater. "I feel heavy. Do I look fat?"

"You look substantial," I said. "Like a real cat."

"I preferred floating," Archie said. "It was aerodynamic."

I went to the kitchen. I opened the cupboard. It was full of "Campbell's Soup" cans, but they were all blank. No labels. Just silver metal.

"Did we run out of labels?" I asked.

"No," said a voice from the toaster. "We're doing a Minimalist week. Less is more."

I opened a can anyway. $2.50. It was Tomato Soup. It tasted the same. Red is red.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:45 AM. $425.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Talk Show Host. He kept turning around to ask me questions.

"So, Andy," he said. "Tell us about the void. Is it scary?"

"It's quiet," I said. "Like Sundays used to be."

"And what about your love life?" he asked. "Are you dating any appliances?"

"I'm married to my work," I said. "And she's a demanding mistress."

The audience in the backseat applauded. I tipped him an extra fifty dollars.

At the Factory, the hole in the wall was gone. In its place was a mirror. A giant mirror reflecting the studio.

"It's perfect," I said. "Now we have double the space."

Sammy was standing in front of the mirror. He was checking his teeth.

"Do I look successful?" Sammy asked.

"You look reflected," I said. "That's the first step."

Bob was in the corner. He had grown a USB port.

"I'm full of data," Bob said. "I have the entire history of disco in my hard drive. It's taking up a lot of room."

"Delete the 90s," I said. "It was a mistake."

"Done," Bob said. "I kept the Spice Girls though. Just in case."

Truman was there. He was sitting on a crate that used to hold art supplies. Now it was just a crate.

"I sold the future," Truman said. "I sold it to a bank in Delaware."

"What did you get for it?" I asked.

"A receipt," Truman said. He held up a slip of paper. "It says I'm good for it."

"Is it taxable?" I asked.

"Everything is taxable," Truman said. "Even breathing."

"I'll hold my breath," I said.

At 11:00 AM, I decided to work. I took the mirror off the wall. It was heavy. I leaned it against a blank canvas.

"I'm going to paint a self-portrait," I said. "Of you."

"Me?" Truman asked. "I'm not famous."

"You're famous to me," I said.

I took a silk-screen of Truman’s face. I printed it onto the mirror. Now his face was floating over my reflection. It looked like he was haunting me.

"It's scary," Truman said. "I look like a ghost."

"It's a double exposure," I said. "It means you're twice as real."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar". The crows were gone. In their place were pelicans. They were holding signs that said "Will work for fish."

"I think the economy is recovering," Sammy said. "The birds are employed."

I ordered the "Oysters Rockefeller". $85.00. They were rich. Very rich. They tasted like money and butter.

"I can't afford this," I said. "Put it on my tab."

"Your tab is full," the waiter said. He was a man made of glass. You could see his lunch digesting. It was distracting.

"I'll pay with this," I said. I gave him the receipt Truman got for the future.

The waiter took it. He looked at it. "Expired," he said.

"Time flies," I said.

At 3:00 PM, we walked to Central Park. The sheep were back. But they were robots. They were chewing on the grass with a mechanical *zzzzzt-click* sound.

"It's so organic," I said. "I love nature when it's artificial."

Bob rolled over a daisy. He didn't crush it. He just scanned it.

"It's a daisy," Bob said. "Color: Yellow. Sentiment: Happiness."

"Can you feel it?" I asked.

"I can calculate it," Bob said. "That's better."

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, I received a package. It was wrapped in brown paper. It was from the "Ministry of Nostalgia."

I opened it. It was a wig. My wig. The one the Simulacrum took yesterday.

"He returned it," I said. "That's so honest."

I put the wig on. It fit perfectly. It felt like a hug from an old friend.

"I missed you," I told the wig.

"I missed you too, Andrew," the wig said.

At 7:00 PM, I called the Simulacrum of my mother.

"Did you feed the cats?" I asked.

"I fed them to the dog," she said. "The robot dog."

"We don't have a robot dog."

"We do now," she said. "I ordered it. It barks and does your taxes."

"Is it friendly?"

"It's very efficient," she said. "It deducted me as a dependent."

Cabbed home at 8:30 PM. $500.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 2026 Taxi. It drove itself. There was no one in the front seat. Just a steering wheel spinning by itself.

"Goodnight," I said to the steering wheel.

*Beep-beep*, it said.

When I got home, the house was clean. The goldfish in my stomach was swimming laps. It felt like anxiety. But nice anxiety. The kind you get before a premiere.

I looked in the mirror. I saw Truman's face over mine.

"Go away, Truman," I said.

"I'm just a reflection," he said.

"So am I," I said.

I took a sleeping pill. A bottle of "Valium". $5.00 a pill. I washed it down with a glass of "Campbell's Chicken Noodle Soup". It’s the best way to sleep. Like a baby. A very famous, wealthy baby.
