Woke up at 8:15 AM. The sky was the color of a television set tuned to a dead channel—or maybe just static gray. The sun looked like a burned-out lightbulb in the ceiling of the world. I tried to turn it off, but the switch was stuck.

"Archie?" I called out. No answer. No heavy cat on the chest. No sand-diamonds in the litter box.

But there was a note on the pillow. It was written in fur. Black fur. It spelled out "C-H-A-O". No "S". Maybe he ran out of ink. Or maybe the cat was cut off.

"Typographical error," I thought. "Or avant-garde."

Breakfast was a slice of "Void Bread". $200.00. It was a slice of air trapped in a plastic sleeve. I chewed it. It tasted like nothing. It filled me up with emptiness. "Very Atkins," I thought.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $85.00. The traffic was moving backwards. The cars were in reverse. The pedestrians were walking moon-style. I felt like I was in a film running backwards. I checked my watch. It was melting on my wrist. Dali would be proud. Or jealous.

The Factory was a construction site again. But they weren't building. They were digitizing.

A crew of "Simulacrum" construction workers was rolling up the floor. They were scrolling the pavement. It was curling up like a window shade.

"Careful," I said. "That's 1970s dirt under there. That's vintage."

"Resetting the server," one of the workers said. He looked like a giant thumb. "Monday is the new Friday. We're looping."

Bob was in the office. He was on the phone. He was arguing with someone about storage fees.

"I don't care if it's frozen," Bob yelled. "It's taking up space. We need the room for the new obsession."

I sat down. The chair felt pixelated. It hurt my back.

"We have a meeting," Bob said, hanging up. "The 21 Club wants to franchise the Factory. They want to put a Factory in every freezer in America."

"Like TV dinners?" I asked.

"Like art," Bob said. "Frozen art. You thaw it out for holidays."

"I don't thaw," I said. "I just preserve."

At 11:00 AM, Sammy came in. He was dragging a large canvas. It was wrapped in brown paper.

"What's that?" I asked. "A body?"

"It's the new reality," Sammy said. He ripped the paper.

It was a mirror. But it wasn't reflecting me. It was reflecting a different room. A room with red walls. And a bed.

"Is that my bedroom?" I asked.

"No," Sammy said. "That's your Twitter feed. It's very intimate."

I looked at the mirror. I saw myself sleeping. I looked tired. Even in the reflection, I looked tired.

"I need a filter," I said. "Put a Marilyn mask over it."

Lunch was delivered by a drone. It dropped a package on the roof. $450.00 for a "Concept Salad". It was just the idea of lettuce. I ate the idea. It was crunchy. I felt full of thoughts.

At 2:00 PM, I tried to work. I wanted to paint a flower. But when I squeezed the tube, no paint came out. Just code. Numbers and letters dripped onto the canvas. *0100101*.

"It's binary," I said. "Very modern. Very cold."

I wiped the code away with a rag. The rag started to sing. It was "Bridge Over Troubled Water".

"Too loud," I said. I stuffed the rag in a jar.

At 4:00 PM, the neighbor called. My phone rang inside my head. *Ring-ring*.

"Andy?" It was Vincent. Or the void. "I have your cat. He wants to come home. But the toll is too high."

"I'll pay," I said. "How much?"

"Your memory of Tuesday," Vincent said.

"Take it," I said. "Tuesday was boring anyway."

Suddenly, the wall dissolved. A cat walked through. He was glowing. He was green.

"Archie?"

He meowed. It sounded like a dial-up modem. *Eee-ooo*.

"He's been compressed," I said. "He's a JPEG."

"Better than a GIF," Bob said. "He doesn't loop. He just loads."

I petted the cat. He felt like static electricity. My hand tingled.

At 6:00 PM, we went to the premiere of "The Disaster Movie" at the United Artists building. Cabbed there. $90.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a crash test dummy. He hit every pothole on purpose. "Safety test," he said.

The movie was blank. The screen was black. The audience watched it for two hours.

"Bravo," someone yelled when the credits rolled. "So raw."

"Did you like it?" Bob asked.

"I couldn't see anything," I said.

"That was the point," Bob said. "We were the movie."

After the movie, we went to the "21" for a drink. The freezer section. I wore a mink coat. A synthetic mink. It shed fiber optics onto the floor.

I ordered a "Diamond Martini". $5,000.00. It was gin with a diamond engagement ring at the bottom.

"If you drink it all," the waiter said, "you get the ring."

"I don't want the ring," I said. "I want the ice."

I stirred the drink with my finger. The diamond cut my skin. A drop of red blood fell into the clear gin. It bloomed like a flower.

"Beautiful," I thought. "My very own Rorschach test."

I left the ring. I left the drink.

I walked outside. It was raining. The rain was warm. It tasted like tomato soup.

"I'm in the soup," I thought. "I'm finally in the soup."

Cabbed home at midnight. $100.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of my mother. She lectured me on art.

"Paint something pretty," she said. "Like a fruit bowl."

"I painted a fruit bowl," I said. "But it rotted."

"Everything rots," she said. "That's why we can it."

I got home. The apartment was white again. The cat was waiting. He was solid again. No green glow.

He rubbed against my leg. He purred.

"Good kitty," I said. I gave him a bowl of water. He drank it.

I looked at the diary. I looked at the page. It was blank.

"I'll write it tomorrow," I said. "If I'm still here."

But I knew
