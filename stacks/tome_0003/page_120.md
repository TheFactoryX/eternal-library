# Sunday, May 31, 2026

> Written at 2026-05-31 23:59:59

Woke up at 10:30 AM. The sky is checkered today. Not plaid, and not stripes, but a red-and-white tablecloth pattern. Like a picnic blanket for God. It’s very nostalgic. It looks like a gingham dress. Or a hamburger wrapper. It makes me feel hungry. But also kind of exposed. Like I’m lunch. $0.00.

I put on my wig. It’s the "Union Jack" wig today. Just to clash with the sky.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:15 AM. $475.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Liza Minnelli. She had sequins for eyelids. *Blink, blink*. Every time she blinked, I saw a star.

"Darling," Liza said. "You look like a flag."

"I feel like a flag," I said. "I'm just waving in the wind."

When we got to the Factory, the building was gone. In its place was a giant pair of sunglasses. Black frames. The lenses were the size of garage doors.

"I see you looking at me," the building said.

Sammy was there. He was the nose bridge. He looked very centered.

Bob was there. He was a reflection in the left lens.

"I'm just an image," Bob said. "I'm flat. I have no depth."

"It's better that way," I said. "Depth is just darkness."

Jean-Michel was there. He was painting graffiti on the frames. He was spraying "VISION" in neon orange.

"It's about how we see," Jean-Michel said. "And how we don't."

At 12:00 PM, the rat arrived. He was wearing the wig I lost the other day. It fits him perfectly. He was walking on his hind legs, carrying a tiny briefcase.

"He's going to work," I said. "He has a job."

"I'm a mogul," the rat said. "I'm in cheese." He opened the briefcase. It was full of crackers. "Wheat Thins. Blue chip."

The squirrel was there too. He was sitting on top of the sunglasses, eating a nut.

"It's a hard shell," the squirrel said. "Like the world."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Four Seasons". But the restaurant was outside. No walls. Just tables floating in the checkered air.

I ordered the "Ham on Rye". $900.00. It came on a plate that was a Frisbee.

"It's flying off the shelves," the waiter said. He was a cloud. A cumulus with a bowtie.

Jean-Michel ordered the "Sky". He just pointed up. They brought him a glass of checkered air. He drank it. He burped. A little red square came out.

"Excuse me," he said. "It's atmospheric."

At 3:00 PM, I did some interviews. I sat in a chair made of microphones.

"What is the future?" the reporters asked. They were tape recorders with legs.

"The future is the same as the past," I said. "But with better lighting."

"And what about the rat?" they asked. "Is he the new you?"

"He's the best me," I said. "He doesn't talk back. And he works for crackers."

At 4:30 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit appeared. He was walking a tightrope between the two lenses of the Factory.

"It's a balancing act," the Man said. He was holding a pole made of dollar bills. "Credit on the left. Debit on the right."

"Don't fall," I said. "It's a long way down to the picnic."

"I never fall," the Man said. "I just fluctuate."

At 6:00 PM, the sky changed. The red squares turned into little red hearts. The white squares turned into little blue clouds.

"Aww," Jean-Michel said. "It's romantic."

"It's a trap," I said. "Love is just a different kind of business."

At 7:30 PM, I started a new painting. "Last Supper on a Checkered Tablecloth". I silkscreened the Apostles. They were all eating hot dogs.

"It's American," I said. "It's fast food religion."

Bob watched. He was very still in the lens.

"You're capturing the moment," Bob said. "Before the check comes."

At 9:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at "MoMA". The museum was a drive-thru. You drove your car through the galleries.

I got in a limo. It was a stretch golf cart.

"I'll take the Pollock," I said to the intercom. "With a side of de Kooning."

They handed me a painting through the window. It was wrapped in foil.

"Drive through," the guard said.

At 11:00 PM, I went home. The cab was a slice of pizza. A giant, pepperoni slice. I sat on the crust.

"Hot," I said.

"Cheesy," the driver said. He was an olive. A single, green olive with a face.

The slice drove me to the townhouse. $600.00. I paid with a cracker from the rat's briefcase.

"Keep the wrapper," I said.

I went inside. The rat was there. He was sleeping in my bed. He was wearing my pajamas.

"You're living my life better than me," I whispered.

The rat opened one eye. "Shhh," he said. "I'm dreaming of electric cheese."

So I went to the couch. I covered myself with a blanket that looked like the sky. Red and white checks.

I turned on the TV. It was showing the outside world. It was checkered too.

"We're all on the grid," I thought.

And the TV watched me back.

So.
