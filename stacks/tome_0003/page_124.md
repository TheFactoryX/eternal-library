Woke up at 10:05 AM. The sky is corduroy today. Fine-waled, vertical ribbing in a dusty mauve. It’s very tactile. It looks like the walls of a rec room in 1974. Or a very cheap pair of pants. The light has to struggle through the ridges. It comes down in stripes, like a barcode. I feel like I’m inside a television set. A broken one. $0.00.

Put on the "Silver Elvis" wig. It’s heavy. It weighs ten pounds. It pulls my neck forward. I have to walk looking up.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:00 AM. $750.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was very small. He was sitting on phone books to see over the wheel.

"It's a textured day," Truman said. "Everything is nubby."

"I like nubby," I said. "It hides the dirt."

When we got to the Factory, the building was a rectangle. Just a simple, black outline. The inside was white, the outside was black. It looked like a negative. Like a film strip reversed.

"Very stark," I said.

Bob was there. He was a rectangle too. A tall, thin box.

"I've been compressed," Bob said. "I'm two-dimensional."

"Can you breathe?" I asked.

"I don't need to breathe," Bob said. "I need to be framed."

Jean-Michel was there. He was painting shapes on the floor. Circles, squares, triangles.

"I'm making a map," Jean-Michel said. "To the exit."

"There is no exit," I said. "It's a one-way street."

At 11:30 AM, the rat came in. He looked flat. He had been ironed.

"I was pressed this morning," the rat said. "I'm crisp."

"Do you starch?" I asked.

"Only the collar," the rat said.

He climbed up my leg. He felt like a piece of paper.

At 12:45 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Four Seasons". The pool was empty. It was drained. There were tiles on the bottom. Big, white squares.

"It's minimalist," I said.

I ordered the "Glorified Rice". $300.00. It was just rice and sugar. But it was glorious.

Jean-Michel ordered a glass of ice water. "It's structural," he said.

At 2:00 PM, I worked on the "Oxidation" paintings. I usually use urine, but today I used corduroy. I painted with the fabric. It left stripes on the canvas.

"It's a weave," I said. "A DNA weave."

Sammy watched. "It looks like a sofa," he said.

"Art is a sofa," I said. "Something to sit on and watch the world go by."

At 3:30 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a pair of scissors. He was cutting the air. *Snip, snip*.

"What are you doing?" I asked.

"Trimming the edges," the Man said. "The world is fraying."

"Be careful," I said. "You might cut a hole in the gravity."

He snipped a thread near the window. The window fell out. *Crash*.

"Oops," the Man said. "That's $500.00."

At 4:45 PM, I got a call from Yoko Ono.

"Hello?" I said.

"Breathe," Yoko said.

"I am breathing," I said.

"No," Yoko said. "Listen to the air."

I listened. The air sounded like corduroy rubbing together. *Swish, swish*.

"It's fashion," I said.

"It's the sound of the future," Yoko said.

At 6:00 PM, the sky started to pill. Little balls of mauve fuzz fell from the sky. It was a blizzard of lint.

"It's snowing fabric," I said.

Bob opened his mouth. He tried to catch the lint. "I'm eating the texture," he said.

"It tastes like dust," I said.

At 8:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at "The Morgan Library". The books were talking. They were reading themselves aloud.

*Blah, blah, blah*.

"It's noisy," I said.

I met a woman who was a lampshade. She was made of pleated silk.

"Don't touch the bulb," she said. "I'm hot."

"I like hot," I said. "It's warm."

"I cost $1,200.00," she said.

"You're expensive," I said. "I'll just look."

At 10:30 PM, the rat got tired of being flat. He crawled into a glass of water. He drank the water. He puffed up. He looked like a marshmallow.

"I'm three-dimensional again," the rat said. "I'm deep."

"You're just wet," I said.

At 11:45 PM, I went home. The cab was a loom. A giant, mechanical loom. We weaved the street as we drove. The wheels were shuttles.

*Click-clack, click-clack*.

"I'm making a rug," the driver said. He was a spool of thread.

We arrived at the townhouse. The house looked like a button. A big, round button in the mauve darkness.

"$900.00," the driver said. "Pay in thread."

I gave him a piece of my wig.

"Thanks," he said. "Nice texture."

I went inside. The lint was everywhere. It covered the furniture. It looked like grey snow.

"Cozy," I thought.

I went to the kitchen. I opened a can of Campbell's Soup. Cream of Mushroom. I ate it standing up. It was lumpy. Like the sky.

"Gee," I thought. "Everything is connected."

I looked at the rat. He was drying off. He looked fuzzy again.

"You're nubby," I said.

"I'm in style," the rat said.

I went upstairs. I took off the Elvis wig. My scalp was striped. Red lines from the wig cap.

I looked in the mirror. I looked like a piece of meat. Wrapped in plastic.

"Good," I thought. "Fresh."

So.
