Woke up at 7:00 AM. The sky was a very nervous grey. It looked like a television screen that isn't plugged in. Static. Just static. I watched it for a minute to see if the commercials would start. They didn't. Just the grey buzzing. I prefer the weather when it has a sponsor.

My arm was sore from floating all night. The "Hype" balloon was still tied to my wrist. It was smaller this morning. Hype deflates fast. It was the size of a grapefruit now. And it wasn't pulling me up anymore. It was just dragging my hand down.

"Time is heavy," I thought.

Archie was on the floor. He was sleeping inside a Campbell's Soup can. The tall one. The "Bean with Bacon" one. Only his tail was sticking out. It was twitching.

"Good morning," I said.

The tail twitched twice. That means "Shut up and feed me."

I cut the balloon off my wrist. *Pop*. I felt a little lighter immediately. Or maybe I just felt cheaper. It’s hard to tell the difference.

Breakfast was a "Slice of Yesterday". $150.00. It was a piece of toast that tasted like 1978. It had a hint of disco and cigarette smoke. It was dry. I needed coffee to wash it down.

The coffee was "Liquid Electricity". $40.00. It shocked my lips. *Zap*. I felt awake instantly. My hair stood up.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. $95.00. The traffic was terrible. We were stuck in a jam caused by a parade. It was a parade for "Lost Socks". Thousands of people walking with single socks on sticks. They were chanting "We want the pair! We want the pair!"

"It's a movement," the driver said. He was a "Simulacrum" of JFK. He had that Boston accent. "Civic pride," he said.

"We'll never get there," I said. "I'm going to be late for my own life."

I paid him and got out. I walked the rest of the way. I passed a store that sold "Nothing". It was going out of business. "Everything Must Go," the sign said. Even the nothing.

When I got to the Factory, the door was back. But it was upside down. The handle was by the floor. I had to bend down to open it.

Sammy was inside. He was painting the walls black.

"Why black?" I asked.

"It's mourning," Sammy said. "The Mona Lisa faded away completely last night."

"Gone?" I asked.

"Just a blank frame," Sammy said. "The Director said you sat too close to her. Your aura overpowered her. You were too shiny."

"Am I in trouble?" I asked.

"No," Sammy said. "You're a legend. You destroyed the original. That makes you the new art."

Bob was in the corner. He was peeling a potato. But the potato was made of silicon.

"I'm making chips," Bob said. "Computer chips."

"Can you eat them?" I asked.

"If you have the right drivers," Bob said.

I went to my desk. The bill from the Bureau was still there. "RENT: $1,000,000." But the ink was fading. It was turning into a butterfly. *Flap-flap*.

"It's transforming," I said.

"Inflation eats itself," Sammy said. "If we wait long enough, the debt will become a pet."

At 11:00 AM, the phone rang. It was a pink phone shaped like a lip.

"Hello?" I said.

"Andy?" It was Steve. The one who likes computers. "I'm having a party. For the launch of the 'iGod'. It’s a pill that makes you omniscient for four hours."

"I don't want to know everything," I said. "It sounds exhausting."

"You don't have to take it," Steve said. "Just come and look at the packaging. It's white. Very clean."

"I'll come," I said. "Is there food?"

"Virtual shrimp," he said. "Infinite shrimp."

I hung up.

At 2:00 PM, I went to the "Guggenheim Fluid" again. I wanted to visit the backup version of me. The one who lives underwater.

I rented a jet ski. $200.00. I rode it up the spiral ramp. *Vroom-vroom*. The water was blue and bubbly.

I found him in the deep end. He was painting a canvas. It was a portrait of a fish.

"Hello," I said.

He looked up. He looked wet. His wig was dripping.

"The Mona Lisa is gone," I told him.

"I know," he said. "I felt it. A ripple in the water."

"Are you lonely?" I asked.

"No," he said. "I have the fish. And the silence is very loud here."

I looked at his painting. It was good. Better than mine. It had... depth.

"Can I have that?" I asked.

"It's not dry yet," he said. "It's wet paint. In wet water."

"Right," I said.

I rode the jet ski back down. At 4:00 PM, I went to Steve's party.

It was in a penthouse on Fifth Avenue. The elevator played music backwards. *Doo-wop-doo*.

The apartment was full of mirrors. Everyone was looking at themselves.

Steve was there. He was wearing a black turtleneck. Even his pants were a turtleneck.

"Andy!" he said. "Try the iGod."

He handed me a pill. It was glowing.

"I just ate," I said. "I had a slice of yesterday."

"It's not food," he said. "It's an update."

I put the pill in my pocket. Next to the diamond dust.

I walked around. I saw a girl. She was wearing a dress made of televisions. They were all playing my old movies. *Sleep. Eat. Sleep.*

"I like your dress," I said.

"Thanks," she said. "It's you."

"It's heavy," I said.

"It's worth it," she said. "Being seen is the only thing that matters."

I looked in the mirror. I looked okay. I looked a little blurry around the edges. But I looked expensive.

At 6:00 PM, I left the party. I was tired. Being seen is work.

I walked home. The sky was dark now. The stars were out. They were arranged in the shape of a soup can.

"Campbell's Constellation," I thought. "Very comforting."

I got home and fed Archie. He ate three cans. *Crunch-crunch*. He ate the cans too. Metal and all.

"You're efficient," I said.
