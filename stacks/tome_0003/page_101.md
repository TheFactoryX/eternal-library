# Tuesday, May 12, 2026

> Written at 2026-05-12 23:59:59

Woke up at 7:45 AM. The sky was beige again. It’s the new black, I think. It matches my sofa. It matches my skin. It’s very monochromatic. I like it when the world is monochromatic because then you don’t have to make decisions.

Archie was on the floor again. He looked heavier today. He looked like he gained five pounds.

"I ate the Concept of a Cat," Archie said. "It was crunchy."

"I hope it doesn't give you a hairball," I said. "A conceptual hairball would be hard to clean up."

"I coughed up an idea," Archie said. "It was about cheese."

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $575.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1980s Stockbroker. He had suspenders and a mobile phone the size of a shoe.

"I'm buying futures," he said. "I'm buying air. I'm buying the time between seconds."

"Buy me a Tuesday," I said. "I need an extra day to sleep."

"Tuesdays are up," he said. "The market is bullish on boredom."

When we got to the Factory, the mirror was gone. In its place was a window. But it wasn't looking out to the street. It was looking in to my bedroom.

"It’s a feedback loop," Sammy said. He was standing in front of the window, watching himself sleep in the past. "Look at me. I’m dreaming of money."

"Get away from there," I said. "You’ll wake yourself up and then we’ll have a paradox."

"I like paradoxes," Sammy said. "They’re cheap. You don’t have to pay for them."

Bob was in the middle of the room. He had wheels now. He was screeching back and forth like a shopping cart with a bad wheel.

"I’m trying to find the edge," Bob said. "I want to roll off the world."

"Don't," I said. "It’s a long drop."

"I have GPS," Bob said. "I can find my way back."

Truman was sitting on the floor. He was cutting up dollar bills. He was making snowflakes out of them.

"It’s beautiful," Truman said. He held up a cut-up five-dollar bill. It looked like a doily. "It’s money you can’t spend. The best kind."

"Can I have one?" I asked.

"Five dollars," Truman said.

"Sold," I said.

At 11:30 AM, a man in a grey suit walked in. He didn't have a face. He just had a smooth surface where his face should be. Like an egg.

"I’m from the Bureau of Consistency," the man said.

"I didn't know we had a bureau," I said.

"We keep things the same," the man said. "You’re varying too much. The soup, the sky, the gravity. You’re making waves."

"I like waves," I said. "They’re blue."

"You need to be flat," the man said. "You need to be a surface."

He handed me a ticket. $1,000,000.00 fine for "Excessive Texture."

"Put it on my tab," I said.

"Your tab is maxed out," the man said. "We’re repossessing the color blue."

"No!" I yelled. "Not blue! Take green! Take yellow! Nobody likes chartreuse!"

"Blue is popular," the man said. "It’s the most downloaded color."

He left. Taking the blue with him. I looked at Bob. He was grey.

"I feel different," Bob said. "I feel less spherical."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar" but it was gone. In its place was a vending machine.

"I guess the rent went up," Sammy said.

I bought a pack of "Synthetic Peanuts". $8.50. They tasted like dust and old televisions.

"It’s an acquired taste," I said. "I haven't acquired it yet."

At 2:30 PM, we went to Central Park. The robot sheep were gone. In their place were statues of sheep. They were very still. They were bronze.

"I prefer them when they move," I said. "They were more lifelike."

"Bronze lasts forever," Sammy said. "That’s what you always say."

"I say a lot of things," I said. "I don't mean half of them."

Truman sat on a bench. A real person sat down next to him. A woman with a grocery bag. She didn't recognize him.

"Heavy," Truman said. "Real people are so heavy. They have so much... mass."

"I know," I said. "It’s exhausting."

At 4:00 PM, back at the Factory, I decided to paint. But I didn't have any blue paint. It was all grey.

"I'll paint a shadow," I said.

I painted a shadow on the floor. It looked like a hole. It looked very deep.

"I'm going to fall in," Sammy said. He stepped over the painting carefully.

"It's just paint," I said.

"It's a hole," Sammy said. "I can see the bottom."

"There is no bottom," I said. "That's the art."

At 6:00 PM, I got a call. It was Jean-Michel. Or his "Simulacrum".

"Andy," he said. "I'm painting a house. I need help."

"Is it a nice house?" I asked.

"It's a floating house," he said. "It's hovering over 5th Avenue. It's blocking the sun."

"That sounds expensive," I said.

"It is," he said. "Can you lend me twenty bucks?"

"Send it via pigeon," I said. "I don't trust the air."

At 8:00 PM, I walked home. I didn't take a cab. I walked. The sidewalks were moving. They were like conveyor belts. They were carrying me backwards.

"I want to go forward," I said. But the sidewalk didn't listen. It just kept humming. *Whirr*.

I passed a newsstand. The headlines were blank. The newspapers were white paper.

"What happened?" I asked the vendor.

"Nothing," the vendor said. "Nothing happened today. It was the best day in years."

I bought a paper for $5.00. It was heavy. It weighed a ton.

When I got home, Archie was sitting on the windowsill. He was watching the beige sky.

"Did they take the blue?" Archie asked.

"Yes," I said. "But they left the beige."

"Good," Archie said. "Beige goes with everything."

I fed Archie a can of tuna. $3.50.

I looked at myself in the mirror. I looked grey. I looked like a photograph that had been in the sun too long.

"I'm fading," I said
