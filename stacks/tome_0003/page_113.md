# Sunday, May 24, 2026

> Written at 2026-05-24 23:59:59

Woke up at 9:00 AM. The sky was gone. Just silver. A solid sheet of hammered silver. It looked like the inside of a cigarette case. Or an Andy Warhol Brillo box, but 50 feet tall and smooth. I knocked on the window. *Clink, clink*. It sounded hollow. "We're in the box," I said. "I knew we would end up here eventually." It was very shiny. I had to squint. My reflection looked back at me, but it was younger. It was the 1960s me, wearing a leather jacket. "You look cheap," I told him. He nodded. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $150.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Jackie Kennedy. She was wearing a pink pillbox hat that was actually a satellite dish. It was rotating.

"I'm picking up signals from Hyannisport," Jackie said. "They're having a clam bake."

"I miss clams," I said.

"I miss everything," she said.

When we arrived, the Factory had turned into a laundromat. An all-night, all-day silver laundromat. The machines were spinning, but they weren't washing clothes. They were washing ideas. The suds were pink. The water was blue.

"We're cleaning up the act," Sammy said. He was folding a giant sheet of transparent film. "It's the rinse cycle for the 20th century."

"I liked it dirty," I said.

Bob was there. He was back to being a pyramid, but this time he was made of televisions. Small ones. iPod screens. They were all playing static. White noise.

"I'm blank," Bob said. "I'm waiting for the input."

I put my hand on one of the screens. It was warm. Like a cat. Or an engine.

At 11:00 AM, the squirrel walked in. He was dragging a giant vacuum cleaner. It was a "Hoover". It was bigger than him.

"I'm cleaning up the gold," the squirrel said. "It's too heavy."

Jean-Michel was there. He was in the corner. He wasn't gold anymore. He was black and white again. He looked like a photocopy. A Xerox.

"I faded," Jean-Michel said. "The light exposure was too high. I lost my resolution."

"You look crisp," I said. "You look like the news."

"I feel like paper," he said. "Thin paper."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Four Seasons". But the pool was empty. The restaurant was empty. There were no tables. Just a single spotlight in the middle of the room. And a man standing in the light.

It was Henry Geldzahler. But he was a statue. A marble statue. White marble with blue veins.

"He's curating the silence," the waiter said. The waiter was a shadow. He didn't have a face.

"How much for the silence?" I asked.

"It's $2,000.00," the waiter said. "Per minute."

"I'll take five minutes," I said. I gave him my credit card. It was a piece of metal I found on the floor.

We sat on the floor and watched Henry stand there. He didn't move. He was perfect.

"It's the best show I've seen all year," I said. "Very static."

"It's a still life," Jean-Michel said.

At 3:00 PM, I tried to paint. But the silver sky had leaked into the room. The air was silver. My brushes were silver. The paint was silver.

"So I painted a silver canvas," I said. "With silver paint. On a silver easel."

"It's invisible," Bob said. "It's camouflaged."

"That's the point," I said. "If it's invisible, it can't get hurt. It can't get damaged. It can't age."

"I'll buy it," the Man in the Grey Suit said. He appeared out of the silver air. "I'll give you a million dollars."

"Invisible money?" I asked.

"No," the Man said. "Real money. Silver dollars."

"Sold," I said.

At 5:00 PM, the Bucket of Chicken rolled in. He was rusting. He had holes in him.

"I'm leaking," the Bucket said. "I'm rusty."

"That's oxidation," I said. "It's natural."

"I don't want to be natural," the Bucket said. "I want to be chrome."

The squirrel climbed up the Bucket and started polishing him with his tail.

"Shine, shine," the squirrel said.

At 7:00 PM, we ordered dinner. "Chinese Food". $600.00.

The delivery boy brought a box. It was a Chinese takeout box. White cardboard with a red wire handle.

I opened it. It was empty.

"It's air," the delivery boy said. "It's the air from Beijing. 1988."

I took a deep breath. It smelled like coal and bicycles.

"Delicious," I said. "Tastes like history."

At 9:00 PM, the lights flickered. The silver sky outside cracked. A huge piece of it fell off. *Crash*. It landed on 5th Avenue. Underneath the silver was... nothing. Just black. Empty black. Or maybe it was just the night sky.

"We broke the world," Jean-Michel said.

"We didn't break it," I said. "We unwrapped it."

I looked at my wrist. I wasn't wearing a watch. I never wear a watch. But I could feel time ticking. *Tick, tock*. It was coming from Bob.

"Bob, what time is it?" I asked.

"It's 15 minutes ago," Bob said. "Or tomorrow. It depends on the station."

I put on my coat. It was silver leather. Heavy. Like armor.

"I'm going home," I said.

"To the box?" Jackie asked. "The cigarette case?"

"Yes," I said. "I need to be put back in the pack."

I walked outside. The street was covered in silver shards. They crunched under my feet. *Crinkle, crunch*. It sounded like Christmas. Or a wrapper being opened.

I picked up a piece of the sky. It was sharp. I put it in my pocket.

"Gee," I said. "It's beautiful."

And then I hailed a cab. But it wasn't a cab. It was a shopping cart. Pushed by the wind.

"Perfect," I said. And I got in.
