Woke up at 7:00 AM. The sky was the color of a Band-Aid. *Beige*. It was raining again, but it wasn't water. It was confetti. Little squares of plastic paper were sticking to the window. They were colored silver and pink.

"It's raining money," I thought. "But not the spendable kind."

I got out of bed. My feet stuck to the floor. The puddle from the snow had dried into a glue. I peeled myself up. *Peel-rip*.

Archie was in the living room. He was crouched low. His tail was twitching. He was stalking something.

"Breakfast?" I asked.

He looked at me. His eyes were wide. He looked guilty.

I walked into the kitchen. There was a hole in the wall. A perfectly round hole. The size of a marble.

I looked closer. It went all the way through to the hallway. I could see the neighbor's door. The neighbor's door has six locks.

"Archie," I said. "Did you eat the building?"

He meowed. It was a tiny meow. A squeak.

I checked his mouth. No teeth marks. But he smelled like ozone. Like the subway. He must have licked the hole.

I fed him the Space Caviar. $400.00. He finally ate it. He likes it if he thinks he stole it.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $55.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote.

"Somebody has to talk," he said. "It's too quiet."

"I'm observing," I said. "I'm working on my silence."

"Boring," he said. "Did you hear about the moon?"

"It's raining soup," I said.

"It's worse," he said. "The soup hit the solar mirrors. It's reflecting. We have tomato sunspots."

I looked out the window. The sun looked red. *Ketchup-red*. It made the buildings look like they were blushing.

"It matches my wig," I said.

The Factory was loud. *Buzz-saw*. Sammy was cutting the floor. He was using a chainsaw. He was making spirals.

"It's a labyrinth," Sammy yelled over the noise. "To trap the ideas."

"Did you trap any?"

"Just dust," he said.

I went to the back room. The phone was ringing. It was the black phone. The private line.

"Hello?"

"Andy?" It was Jean-Michel. Or at least, a Jean-Michel bot. "I'm downtown. The virus is selling for $5,000."

"Good," I said. "Keep the change."

"I need a quote," he said. "For the catalog. 'Art is what you can get away with'?"

"That's Marshall McLuhan," I said. "Say something about soup."

"Soup is what you can't get away with," he said. "It's heavy."

"I like that," I said. "Write it down."

I hung up. I looked at my canvas. The "Soup to Nuts" painting. The soup was drying. It was cracking. *Crinkle-crackle*. It looked like a dry lake bed.

I took a marker. I drew a map of America on the cracks.

"It's a road map," I thought. "For hungry people."

Lunch was a peanut butter sandwich. $12.00. The bread was blue. It was made of blueberries. It tasted like a pillow.

I sat on a box of Brillo pads. A girl walked in. She was wearing a dress made of telephones. The rotary kind.

"Can I use the phone?" I asked.

"I am the phone," she said. She dialed her waist. *Ring-ring*. "Hello? Yes, he's here. He's eating blue bread."

"Who is it?" I asked.

"It's the future," she said. "They want their dust back."

She spun around and walked out. The bells jingled.

At 3:00 PM, I got a delivery. A crate. It was leaking. *Drip-drip*. The label said "FRAGILE: HANDLE WITH DREAMS".

I opened it with a crowbar. *Pop*.

Inside was a rock. A gray rock. But it was humming. A low C-note.

Attached was a note. *Note*.

"Andy. This is a piece of the Moon. The 'Soup Crater'. It froze instantly. It smells like oregano. Love, David."

I touched the rock. It was cold. Colder than the apartment.

"Oregano," I said.

I put it in my pocket. It made my leg vibrate. I felt like I had a motor inside.

At 6:00 PM, I went to a party. It was at the "Top of the Standard". But the building is a coil now. It looks like a spring. A slinky.

I took the elevator. It went sideways.

The party was for "The Day the Art Cooked". Everyone was there. Bob was there. He was wearing a suit made of receipts.

"Did you settle?" I asked.

"We paid him," Bob said. "We gave him the scorch mark. He's wearing it as a tie."

"Fabulous," I said. "Recycling."

I ordered a drink. A Campbell's Soup-tini. Tomato vodka and salt. $35.00.

A woman came up to me. She was gray. She looked like a statue.

"I'm the Meat Art," she said.

"You look great," I said. "Did you lose weight?"

"I evaporated," she said. "It was very detoxing."

"I bet," I said. "Do you want a drink?"

"I only drink smoke," she said.

I introduced her to the Jean-Michel bot. They talked about texture. I stood in the corner and watched the red sun go down. It looked like a penny dropping into a slot machine.

*Jackpot*.

I left at 9:00 PM. The air smelled like oregano. The whole city smelled like a pizza.

Cabbed home. $50.00. The driver was silent. He had no mouth. Just a speaker.

When I got in, Archie was sitting by the hole in the wall. He had pushed a toy mouse through it. A gift.

"Good boy," I said.

I took the moon rock out of my pocket. I put it in the empty food bowl.

"There," I said. "Dinner."

Archie sniffed it. He licked it. *Zap*. A tiny spark jumped from the rock to his nose.

He jumped back. *Poof*. His fur stood up. He looked like a dandelion.

"Electric," I said. "Very modern."

I washed up. I looked in the mirror. My face looked different. Older. Or maybe just newer. The cracks were showing.
