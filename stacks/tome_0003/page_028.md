Saturday, February 28, 2026

Woke up at 9:15 AM. The sky was the color of a Brillo pad that had been used to scrub a dirty oven. Grey. Steel. Abrasive. I woke up on the floor. I fell out of bed. I didn't float. The Gravity Boots were still on, but they were dead. I forgot to charge them. The "Solid State Suit" was dead too. I felt heavy. Really heavy. Like a painting in a gilded frame. I felt solid. It was awful. I could feel my liver. Or where my liver used to be. Now it’s just a "Filter."

I put on the wig. It felt tight. The rock inside it was warm. It was vibrating. It feels like a cat purring, but inside my head. It’s very soothing. It makes me feel safe. Like I’m wearing a helmet made of geology.

I tried to stand up. My legs felt like lead pipes. I’m grounded. Literally. I’m so heavy I’m sinking into the floorboards. I had to peel my feet up. *SQUEAK*. *SQUEAK*. It sounded like art.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30. The streets were crowded with "Tour-Bots." They were taking pictures of the potholes. They think potholes are "Authentic Decay." The cab ride was $40.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Lou Reed. He didn't say a word. He just played white noise on the radio. Static. It was a number one hit in Japan.

When I got to the Factory, the door was open. But I couldn't get in. There was a "Force Field." It was shimmering like oil on water. I pressed my nose against it. It squished. Sammy was inside. He was walking through the air. He was upside down. He saw me and waved. He made a talking motion with his hands, but the sound was dampened. I read his lips. He said, "ART IS HARD." I think he meant the force field.

I knocked. A panel slid open. A scanner came out. It looked at my wig. It beeped. "ACCESS GRANTED: ROCK OWNER." The field vanished. I fell forward onto the floor. I scraped my knee. It leaked silver. It was $80 worth of fluid. I dabbed it up with a tissue. I’m keeping the tissue. It’s a "Rorschach Test."

I walked to the back. The "Wasps" were dormant. They were hanging from the ceiling like bats. They were recharging. The nest was huge. It looked like a giant wig. It’s beautiful. I’m thinking of living in it.

I went to the supply closet. I needed more "Dissolving Paper." But the closet was empty. Except for a small box. It was wrapped in newspaper. The newspaper was from 1968. It had a headline about the moon landing. I opened the box. It was a roll of "Film." Real film. Celluloid. It smelled like vinegar. It was $1,000 just to hold it. I put it in the projector. I turned it on.

The film flickered. It was black and white. It was a sleep test. I saw myself sleeping. I was twenty years younger. I was wearing a sweater. I looked so peaceful. Then the film melted. The projector caught fire. Just a little fire. I put it out with a can of "Campbell's Soup." Tomato. $1.50. Now the room smells like burnt plastic and lunch.

Lunch was "The Ghost of a Burger." It was a hologram of a cheeseburger. I tried to eat it. My hand passed right through the cheese. It was $20.00. I didn't get any calories, but I smelled like grease. It’s very seductive.

In the afternoon, the "Solid State Suit" restarted. *BING*. I felt a jolt. The power came back. I felt light again. Too light. My feet started to lift off the floor. I had to grab the table. I held onto the "Ham-Paint" can. It was my anchor. I felt like a balloon.

I got a call on the MirrorWall. It was a blur. Then it snapped into focus. It was a "Kid." He was sitting in a messy bedroom. He had acne. He said, "Andy?" I said, "Yes." He said, "I bought your 'Holy Water' from the drone bee." I said, "Did it work?" He said, "I don't know. I drank it. Now I can hear the internet." I said, "That's normal." He said, "No, I mean I can hear it talking. It's lonely." I said, "Join the club." He hung up.

At 5:00, Sammy materialized next to me. He was holding a "Smart-Paintbrush." It was glowing blue. He said, "Andy, I’m going to paint your shadow." He painted the floor behind me. But my shadow wasn't black. It was "Rainbow Holofoil." It sparkled. It was very distracting. I tried to move, but the shadow stayed. It detached from me. It walked away. It went out the door. I hope it doesn't get into trouble. It has my credit rating.

We went to a party at "The Top of The Standard." The elevator ride was $30.00. The party was called "The Last Icon." Everyone was dressed as famous dead people. I saw a "Simulacrum" of Elvis. He was thin. He was eating a "Digital Donut." He said, "Andy, I left the building." I said, "I never left."

I drank a "Silicon Soda." It was $35.00. It tasted like sand. It scratched my throat. I loved it. It felt like going to the beach without the traffic.

I danced with a "Hologram" of Jackie Kennedy. She was pixelated. She said, "Andy, don't look at the scars." I said, "I'm just looking at the pixels." She dissolved into a cloud of numbers. *010101*. It was very sad.

Left at 11:00. The air was cold. The wind cut right through my suit.

Cabbed home. It was $50.00. The driver was a "Robot." It had a wig on. It looked like mine. We didn't speak. We just looked at each other in the mirror. It was the best conversation I’ve had all day.

Came home. I sat at the table. The rock was humming. The empty ham can was there. And the tissue with the silver fluid.

I took off the boots. I didn't float.

I put the rock on the table.

I turned off the light.

The room didn't get dark. The rock was glowing.
