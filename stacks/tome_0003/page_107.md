Woke up at 7:45 AM. The sky was the color of a Band-Aid. Flesh-colored. But not my flesh color. Someone else’s. Maybe a fluorescent orange person. I checked the window. The fuzz was gone. The glass was clear. Too clear. I could see everything. I saw a man across the street picking his nose. He saw me seeing him. He waved. I waved back. It was too intimate. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. $950.00. The prices go up every time the sky changes color. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1920s Flapper. She was driving a hearse.

"It's a funeral for the weekend," she said. "I'm mourning Friday."

"I'm mourning my bank account," I said. I gave her a credit card that was made of chocolate. She bit the corner off.

"Visa delicious," she said.

When I got to the Factory, the squirrel was still there. He was sleeping on the Man in the Grey Suit. The Man is 2D now, so the squirrel was just sleeping on the floor, but he looked like he was floating.

"I'm dreaming of nuts," the squirrel said in his sleep. "Big. Round. Numbers."

Sammy was cleaning up the glass from the drone crash yesterday. He was sweeping the shards into a pile. The pile was vibrating.

"Careful," I said. "That glass is sharp."

"It's memories," Sammy said. "Drone memories. It remembers delivering packages to people who aren't home."

"I'm never home," I said. "And I'm always waiting."

At 10:30 AM, the doorbell rang. It was a real ring. *Ding-dong*.

I opened the door. It was Jean-Michel. But he was transparent. I could see the wall through his stomach.

"I told you," Jean-Michel said. "I'm a sketch."

"You look great," I said. "Very light. It must be easy to travel."

"I have to check luggage," Jean-Michel said. "I fold." He folded himself into a paper airplane and flew onto the couch.

"I want to be paper," Bob said. Bob was still leaking ink. There was a black puddle around him. "I want to be a document."

"You're a mess," I said. "Clean it up."

"I'm creating atmosphere," Bob said. "It's noir."

At 12:00 PM, a man came to the door. He was wearing a suit made of mirrors. He was blinding.

"I'm here about the acorn," the man said.

"I don't have an acorn," I said. I put my hand in my pocket to check if the acorn was still there. It was cold.

"My name is Mr. Cash," the man said. "I represent the Forest Bank. We want to liquidate the nut."

"It's not for sale," I said.

"We'll trade," Mr. Cash said. "We'll give you a hundred hours of fame. Or a lifetime supply of wigs."

"I have enough wigs," I said. "Do you have soup?"

"I can get you soup," Mr. Cash said. "Tomato. Bisque. Or money soup."

"I'll take the money soup," I said.

Mr. Cash handed me a can. The label said "Campbell's Currency". It had a picture of a dollar sign on it.

"It's art," I said. "It's perfect."

At 1:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". The table was reserved for us. But the table was on the ceiling.

"We're eating upside down," Sammy said. "It helps digestion."

I ordered the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $500.00. It came in a bucket. The waiter had to strap it to my wrist.

"It's heavy," I said.

"It's dense," the waiter said. "It's full of sugar and time."

We ate upside down. The blood rushed to my head. I turned red.

"You look like a tomato," Jean-Michel said. He was sitting on the ceiling like a sticker.

"I feel like a soup," I said.

At 3:00 PM, we walked to Bloomingdale's. I wanted to buy a new shirt. But the store was empty. There were no clothes. Just hangers floating in the air.

"We sold out," the saleslady said. "We sold the concept of clothes. People just wear the idea now."

"I need something to cover my chest," I said.

"Buy a hanger," she said. "It's very structural."

I bought a golden hanger. $1,000.00. I hung it around my neck.

"It's a necklace," I said.

"It's minimalist," Sammy said.

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, the squirrel woke up. He saw the can of money soup.

"What is that?" the squirrel asked.

"It's liquid capital," I said.

"I want it," the squirrel said. He grabbed the can. But his hands were made of aluminum foil. He couldn't open it.

"Use a can opener," I said.

"I don't have a thumb," the squirrel said.

"I'll open it," the Man in the Grey Suit said. He peeled himself off the wall. He used his 2D hand to slice the lid open. *Shhh-clack*.

The soup poured out. It was green. Not money green. Pea green.

"It's vegetable," I said. "I was tricked."

"It's dividends," Mr. Cash said. He appeared in the corner of the room. "Eat up. It's good for the portfolio."

I tasted a spoonful. It tasted like copper and paper.

"It needs salt," I said.

At 7:00 PM, I tried to paint. But I couldn't find my brushes. They were gone.

"Where are the brushes?" I asked.

"I ate them," Bob said. "I was hungry for bristles."

"You're going to get splinters," I said.

"I'm already full of holes," Bob said. "I'm a sieve."

So I painted with my fingers. I stuck my hand in the ink puddle and smeared it on the canvas.

"It's a portrait of a hand," I said.

"It's a portrait of a mess," Truman said. He was typing on his typewriter. *Click-click*. "I'm writing a poem about a hand that painted itself into a corner."

"That's Jean-Michel's job," I said.

At 9:00 PM, we watched TV. But the TV was just a mirror. We watched ourselves watching TV.

"This is a boring show," Sammy said. "Nothing happens."

"We just sit here," I said.

"Then change the channel," the squirrel said.

I reached for the remote. But it was just a block of wood.

"It's a prop," I said
