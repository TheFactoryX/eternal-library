Woke up at 7:30 AM. The sky was clear. No confetti, no mustard, no ketchup. Just blue. A flat, electric blue. Like a blue screen of death. It felt unfinished. "Gee," I thought. "They forgot to render the clouds."

I went to the kitchen. The hole in the wall was gone. Just drywall. Smooth and white. No seam. No hole.

"Archie?"

He was sitting on the counter. He looked clean. Too clean. He looked like he had been Photoshopped.

I touched the wall. It was cold. Hard. Solid.

"Bad luck," I said. "Now how will I know what the neighbor is watching?"

Breakfast was a bowl of "Data-O's". $150.00. They were little O's made of styrofoam. They tasted like silence. I poured on a box of "Digital Milk". It evaporated before it hit the spoon. I was eating dry air. Very diet.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $60.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Jackie Kennedy. She was wearing pink.

"It's a lovely day," she said. "For a parade."

"Is it a parade?" I asked.

"It's always a parade," she said. "In your head."

We drove past the "Top of the Standard". The coil-spring building was unwinding. It was stretching out like a slinky on the steps of the courthouse.

"Gravity is winning," I said.

"It's just relaxing," Jackie said.

The Factory was quiet. Too quiet. The buzz-saw was gone. Sammy was sitting on a box of Brillo pads. He was holding a hammer. He wasn't hitting anything. He was just holding it.

"The art is gone," Sammy said. "It migrated."

"Did it fly south?" I asked.

"It went into the cloud," Sammy said. "The Cloud. The big server in the sky. I saw the paintings upload themselves. They just floated away."

"Fabulous," I said. "No shipping costs. It's a retrospective in heaven."

I walked to the back room. The moon rock was gone. The fridge was gone. The wall was gone.

It was just a white room. Infinite white.

"I feel like I'm inside a Tylenol," I said.

My phone rang. It was the Jean-Michel bot.

"Andy?" he said. "I can't find my hands. I'm trying to paint, but I just have stumps."

"Use your mind," I said. "Telepathy is the new brush."

"I'm hungry," he said. "Can we eat the white space?"

"It's non-fat," I said.

I hung up. I sat on the floor. It was so white it hurt my eyes. I needed some color. I took out a black marker. I drew a box on the white floor. I wrote "CAMPBELL'S" inside it.

"There," I said. "Now it's home."

At 11:00 AM, Bob walked in. He was wearing a suit made of mirrors. He reflected the white room. He looked like a kaleidoscope.

"The neighbor called," Bob said. "He wants the cat back."

"He can't have him," I said. "The cat is the only real thing left."

"The neighbor says the hole sealed itself," Bob said. "He says he's trapped in a sitcom now. He keeps hearing a laugh track every time he opens the fridge."

"Laugh tracks are good," I said. "They make you feel funny even when you're not."

Lunch was a pill. $500.00. It was a "Three-Course Meal in a Capsule". I swallowed it with tap water. $0.05.

I waited. I felt a rumble. *Burp*. I tasted roast beef. Then chocolate. Then toothpaste.

"Efficient," I thought.

At 2:00 PM, I worked on my new series. "The White Paintings". I just stared at the wall. I didn't paint. I just looked.

"It's about presence," I told a fly that was buzzing near my ear. "The act of looking is the art."

The fly landed on my nose. *Sit*.

"Are you a critic?" I asked.

It rubbed its legs together. It was washing its hands. Maybe it was eating the white space too.

At 4:00 PM, the sky turned green. Not pea green. Terminal green. The color of code. The blue screen had crashed.

"We need a reboot," Sammy yelled. "Someone pull the plug!"

"I don't want to," I said. "I might forget who I am."

"You're Andy," Bob said. "You're the soup."

I walked to the window. I looked down at the street. The people were pixelating. Their heads were squares. Their legs were lines.

"I'm going to Serendipity," I said. "I need a Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. Before we all turn into vectors."

Cabbed to Serendipity at 6:00 PM. $55.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Elvis. He was singing "Heartbreak Hotel" in binary. *Zero-one. Zero-one*.

The restaurant was crowded. But everyone was a silhouette. Just black shapes.

I sat in the corner. I ordered the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $25.00.

The waiter was a shadow. He poured the chocolate. It was thick. It was brown. It had chunks.

I tasted it. It tasted like chocolate. It tasted like sugar. It tasted like 2026 and 1960 all at once.

"It's good," I said. "It's consistent."

A woman sat down across from me. She was all white. White dress. White skin. White hair.

"I'm the Glitch," she said. "I wanted to meet you before the system restores."

"Do I know you?" I asked.

"I'm the extra," she said. "The background noise. The person in the photo you cropped out."

"I never crop," I said. "I just frame."

She smiled. She had nice teeth. Very white.

"You should have kept the hole," she said. "The hole was the door."

"To where?"

"To the next season," she said.

She took my spoon. She ate a bite of my chocolate.

"Mmm," she said. "Calories."

Then she faded away. *Poof*. Just like that.

I finished the drink. My brain freeze was exquisite.

I walked outside. The green sky was flickering. *Buzz-buzz*. Like a fluorescent tube about to break.

Cabbed home at 9:00 PM. $60.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of me. He was wearing a wig.

"Rough day?" he asked.

"It was pixelated," I said. "I need a filter."

I got home. The apartment was white again
