Woke up at 10:00 AM. The sky is plastic today. Shrink-wrap plastic. It’s pulled tight over the city. The sun is a barcode scanner. A red laser beam scanning the buildings. *Beep, beep*. It reads the price of everything. I looked out the window and the clouds were static. Cling film. I couldn't get them to move. I tried to peel a corner of the sky, but my fingernail broke. It’s sealed. Airtight. $0.00.

Put on the "Bubble Wrap" wig. It’s transparent and bumpy. Every time I move, I go *Pop. Pop. Pop*. It’s very rhythmic. It sounds like applause. Or gunfire. I can’t tell the difference anymore.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:45 AM. $2,200.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of T.S. Eliot. He was wearing a bowler hat made of a newspaper.

"The hollow men are gathering," T.S. said. "Headpiece filled with straw."

"I'm just going to work," I said.

"We are all going to work," he said. "To measure out our lives with coffee spoons."

When we got to the Factory, the entrance was blocked by a turnstile. A metal arm.

"One token," the machine said. It had a mouth. A coin slot.

I didn't have a token. I had a dollar bill. I tried to feed it in. The machine spat it out. *Reject*.

"It prefers hard currency," Bob said. He was standing on the other side. He was trapped inside a clear plastic bag. "Like a package of salmon."

"I can't get in," I said.

"Slide under," Jean-Michel said. He was in a bag too. He was squeezed against the glass. "It's tighter than the Odeon."

At 11:30 AM, I crawled under the turnstile. My wig scraped against the floor. *Pop, pop, screech*.

Inside, the Factory was sterile. White. Everything was wrapped in cellophane. The chairs. The tables. The sofa.

"It's preserved," Sammy said. He was sitting on a plastic-wrapped couch. He couldn't slide off. "I'm sticking to the upholstery."

At 12:15 PM, the rat arrived. He was sealed in a blister pack. Hard plastic backing. He was hanging on the wall. A retail display.

"Try to get me out," the rat squeaked. His mouth was moving, but the sound was muffled. "I'm on sale."

"Buy one get one free," the squirrel said. He was on the next peg.

I took a box cutter. I sliced the rat's package. *Slice*. The air rushed in.

"I'm free," the rat said. He fell to the floor. "And I'm bent."

"It's permanent," I said. "You can't return it."

At 1:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". The tables were spinning. Like record players. Lazy Susans. Big ones.

"I'll have the 'Frrrozen Hot Chocolate'," I said. "$6,500.00."

The waiter put the glass on the table. As the table spun, the centrifugal force held the liquid in the glass. It didn't spill. It was a science experiment.

"It's physics," I said.

Jean-Michel ordered a "Cheesesteak". It was printed on paper. Edible ink.

"It tastes like newsprint," Jean-Michel said. "And grease."

"It's information," I said. "Digest it."

At 3:00 PM, I worked on the "Oxidation" paintings. But I didn't use urine. I used soda. Coca-Cola. I poured it on the canvas.

"It's sticky," Sammy said. "The bugs will love it."

"It's the American way," I said.

The canvas bubbled. The sugar ate through the fibers.

"Look," Bob said. "It's eating itself."

"Capitalism," I said.

At 4:30 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a pricing gun. *Zzzt*.

He zapped the soda painting. "$1,000,000.00."

He zapped the rat. "$0.05."

He zapped me. "$Priceless$."

"Am I priceless?" I asked.

"You're out of stock," the Man said. "Discontinued."

He tried to peel me. He grabbed my arm. He pulled a layer of cellophane off my shoulder.

"Ouch," I said. But it didn't hurt. It just felt cool.

At 6:00 PM, we went to a gallery opening. It was for "The Invisible Artist". The room was empty. Just white walls.

"Where is the art?" I asked.

"It's the space," the gallerist said. "The air is $5,000 per cubic foot."

I bought a corner. I own the shadows.

"I'll take that shadow," I said. "$12,000.00."

I signed a check. The check bounced. It was made of rubber.

At 8:00 PM, the rat chewed through the cellophane on the floor. *Rustle, rustle*. He made a hole. A dark spot in the white room.

"It's a void," the rat said. "It's deep."

We looked into the hole. There was no floor underneath. Just black.

"Don't fall in," I said.

"I'm jumping," the squirrel said. He leaped in. *Whoosh*. No sound.

"Did he land?" I asked.

"Maybe," Bob said. "He's falling forever."

At 10:30 PM, I went home. The townhouse was wrapped. Like a Christmas present. A giant bow on the roof.

I didn't unwrap it. I went in through the mail slot.

I crawled to the kitchen. The Campbell’s Soup was wrapped in foil. Gold foil.

I opened the "Tomato Rice".

It was hot. Boiling hot.

"Steam," I thought.

I ate it standing up. The can burned my hand.

"Burns are feelings," I thought.

I went upstairs. I took off the wig. My head was smooth. Sealed.

I looked in the mirror. There was a barcode on my forehead. *Scan me*.

"I'm a product," I thought.

I tried to scratch the barcode off. It wouldn't come off. It was etched.

I got into bed. The sheets were crinkly.

I popped the bubbles on my pillowcase until I fell asleep. *Pop. Pop. Pop.*

So.
