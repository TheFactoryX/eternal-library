Wednesday, March 18, 2026

Woke up at 7:00 AM. The room was spinning. Not like a top, but like a loading icon. A circle of arrows. *Spin-spin-spin*. I felt centrifugal. I grabbed the edge of the bed. The sheets were silk. $300.00 per thread. Very slippery. I slid out of bed and landed on the floor. *Thud*. My head made a sound like a dropped melon.

I stood up. I checked my pockets. The can opener was still there. The pigeon was on the nightstand. It wasn't moving. It was frozen. Literally. Covered in frost. I put it in the microwave to defrost. *Defrost: 2:00*. I waited.

While it cooked, I looked at the screen. The news ticker was scrolling across the window. **WARHOL STOCK VOLATILE. INVESTORS WORRY ABOUT "GLITCH."** The glitch is me. I burped, and the ticker turned green for a second.

The microwave beeped. *Ding*. The pigeon came out. It was steaming. It shook its wings. It looked at me and made a cooing sound. But it sounded like a dial-up modem. *Eeee-urrrr-ding*. I gave it some crumbs from the floor. It pecked them up. It seems friendly now. I named it "Bridget." After Bardot.

I went to the bathroom to shave. I don't grow hair anymore, but I scrape the silicone anyway. It feels productive. The razor costs $150.00. It has five blades. One for each dimension.

I put on the wig. It’s sticking to my head less these days. Maybe my scalp is smoother. Maybe the wig is lonely. I put on my glasses. The prescription is wrong. Everything looks 3D without the glasses. I put them on to flatten it out.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. The air smelled like ozone and burnt popcorn. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of myself. From the 80s. He wore the wig I wore in the 80s. He didn't say anything. He just drove. He charged me $5.00. "Friends and family rate," he said. My voice sounded thin.

The Factory was different today. The floor was gone. It was a giant grid. A transparent floor looking down into the basement. I could see the old "Superstars" sleeping in the basement. They looked like batteries in a pack. Recharging.

Sammy was floating. Literally hovering. He had little fans strapped to his ankles. "It's the new trend," he said. "Anti-gravity shoes. $5,000.00 a pair. Keeps your feet clean."

"I like the dirt," I said. "It grounds me."

"We have a problem," Sammy said. He floated down. "The portraits. They're peeling."

I looked at the walls. The "Marilyns" were flaking. But under the paint, there wasn't canvas. There was skin. Fleshy pink skin.

"The paint is just makeup, Andy," Sammy said. "The canvases are alive. They're hungry."

I walked over to a "Liz." I touched her cheek. It felt warm. She blinked. The painted eye moved. "Feed me," she whispered. "Feed me money."

I took a wad of cash from my pocket. About $200.00. I shoved it into the frame. It absorbed it. The paint smoothed out. Liz looked radiant. "More," she said.

I gave her a credit card. She ate that too.

"It's expensive being me," I said.

"It's expensive being you," Sammy agreed.

Around 11:00 AM, a truck arrived. A delivery truck. It was from "Pfizer." Or maybe "Prada." The logos are merging now.

They delivered a crate. A glass crate. Inside was a "Simulacrum" of a brain. But it was made of silicone gel.

"It's for you," the delivery man said. "An upgrade."

"What does it do?"

"It filters out the sad stuff," he said. "It only lets in the good press."

I touched the glass. The brain pulsed. *Thump-thump*. It looked like a cherry Jell-O mold. I put it on the shelf next to the soup cans. It hummed. I instantly felt happier. Or maybe I just forgot I was sad.

Lunch was at "The Oyster Bar." But they don't have oysters. They have pearls. Just pearls. I ordered a dozen. $1,200.00. They were hard to chew. I swallowed them whole. *Gulp*. They felt heavy in my stomach. Like I was full of stones. A weight system.

I sat next to a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was tiny. Only six inches tall. He was sitting on a salt shaker. He was wearing a little hat. He squeaked at me. "Andy," he squeaked. "You're looking round."

"I'm feeling round," I said.

"Round is good," he said. "Cans are round. Coins are round. The world is round. You're becoming an object. It's the highest form of evolution."

I ordered a pearl for him. The waiter put it on a thimble. Truman cracked it open. It was empty. "Just like my stories," he said. "All shell."

At 2:30 PM, I went to the "Museum of Modern Art." But I’m the director now. I fired the old director. Or I bought him. He’s a coat check girl now.

I went to my office. I sat in the big chair. It swiveled. I spun around. *Whoosh-whoosh*.

I looked at the calendar.

March 19, 2026.

Wait.

My watch said March 18. The calendar said March 19.

I’m a day ahead.

I’m living in the future.

I looked out the window. The people on the street were walking backwards. Rewinding.

I sat still. I didn't want to disrupt the timeline.

I took out the can of tomato soup from my pocket. I opened it with the can opener. *Whirrrr*.

I drank it right from the can. It was cold. It tasted like metal. It tasted like time.

"Good soup," I said.

But the words came out a second before I said them.

My voice is lagging.

I need to catch up.

I bought a newspaper. The headline said: **ANDY WARHOL DISCOVERS TIME TRAVEL. STOCKS SOAR.**

I didn't know I had time traveled.

But I guess I did.

I put the newspaper in the trash.

It cost $2.00.
