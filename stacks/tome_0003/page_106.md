Woke up at 8:00 AM. The sky was green. Not a nice green. A green like pea soup that’s been left out in the sun. Or like a bruised banana. I think the atmosphere is finally molding. It’s very organic. I checked the window. The glass felt fuzzy. Like a peach. I licked it. It tasted like ozone and pennies. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $800.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Diana Vreeland. She was wearing purple glasses and driving from the back seat.

"Style!" she shouted every time we hit a bump. "Poise! Attitude!"

"Where are the brakes?" I asked.

"Brakes are for people who don't trust their trajectory," she said. We parked by jumping the curb. It was very dramatic. I gave her a twenty and she ate it.

"Delicious," she said.

When I got to the Factory, the puddle was back. The Man in the Grey Suit had reconstituted himself. But he was flat. He was 2D. Like a cutout. He was stuck to the wall again, but he was sideways.

"I fell," the Man said. "Gravity is so aggressive today."

"You look thinner," I said.

"I'm streamlined," the Man said. "I'm modern art now."

Sammy was trying to vacuum the floor, but the vacuum was screaming.

"It doesn't like the dust," Sammy said. "It says the dust has rights."

"Let the dust unionize," I said. "I'm not negotiating with dirt."

Bob was in the corner. He was vibrating again. *Hummmmm*. But he was leaking ink. Black ink was dripping out of his ears.

"I'm over-flowing," Bob said. "I have too many thoughts."

"Think less," I said. "It's cheaper."

At 11:00 AM, the doorbell rang. It wasn't a ring. It was a cough. *Cough-cough*.

I opened the door. It was the squirrel from the park. But he was big now. He was the size of a dog. And he wasn't wearing a suit. He was wearing a tuxedo made of aluminum foil.

"I'm here for the acorn," the squirrel said. "I need the capital."

"I ate it," I said. I lied. I still had it.

The squirrel gasped. He clutched his chest. "You consumed the future?"

"I had a snack," I said.

"Then I must work," the squirrel said. He marched into the Factory. He grabbed a paintbrush. "I will paint my debt away."

He started painting on the wall. He painted a giant nut. It was very photorealistic.

"It's a Warhol," the squirrel said. "It's a Squirrel-hol."

"I'll sue," I said. "I'll sue you for acorns."

At 1:00 PM, we went to brunch. We went to "Balthazar". It was full of people waiting in line. But they weren't people. They were mannequins.

"Is this the line?" I asked a mannequin.

It didn't answer. It just stared with glass eyes.

"It's silent disco dining," the host said. He was real. He had a mustache. "Everyone eats inside their own head."

"Table for five," I said. "Me, the dust, the cutout, the ink-leaker, and the squirrel."

"We don't serve squirrels," the host said.

"He's a conceptual artist," I said.

"Oh, why didn't you say so," the host said. "Right this way."

We sat down. I ordered the "Eggs Benedict". $200.00. They came out on a flat screen. I had to eat them with a stylus.

"It's digital cholesterol," Truman said. He was typing on his giant typewriter. *Click-clack*. "I'm writing a menu for the starving. The starving for attention."

"It tastes like pixels," I said. "Very crunchy."

The squirrel ordered the "Nuts and Berries". They brought him a bowl of computer chips and blueberries.

"It's hardware," the squirrel said. "Crunchy."

At 3:00 PM, we went to the Museum of Modern Art. There was a new exhibit. "The Retrospective of Things That Almost Happened".

We walked through rooms full of empty canvases.

"This is the painting I almost made in 1974," I said. "I like this one better. It's cleaner."

"Look at this one," Sammy said. He pointed to a pedestal with nothing on it. "This is the sculpture I almost made yesterday."

"It's profound," I said. "It's full of potential energy."

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, Jean-Michel called.

"Andy," he said. "I peeled myself off."

"You're unstuck?"

"I'm free," Jean-Michel said. "But I left a layer behind. I'm thinner now. Less opaque."

"Can you still paint?"

"I can only paint outlines," Jean-Michel said. "I'm a sketch."

"Come over," I said. "We'll trace you."

While I waited, I looked at the wall where the squirrel had painted the giant nut. The Man in the Grey Suit was trying to eat it. But he couldn't open his mouth. He was 2D.

"It's torture," the Man said. "I'm starving in the shadow of lunch."

"Eat the paint," I said. "It's good for the soul."

"I hate acrylic," the Man said. "It has no aftertaste."

At 7:30 PM, I worked on my portrait. I took the aluminum foil from the squirrel's tuxedo. I smashed it into a ball. Then I took a photo of it. Then I silk-screened the photo of the ball onto the ball.

"It's a loop," I said. "It's a comment on recycling."

"I'm dizzy," the ball said.

At 9:00 PM, we ordered dinner. "Thai food". $150.00.

The delivery boy was a drone. But it crashed into the window. *Smash*.

"It's abstract delivery," I said.

We ate the Pad Thai on the floor. The noodles glowed in the dark.

"I think they're radioactive," Sammy said.

"It adds flavor," I said. "It's a spicy kick."

I looked at the clock. It was melting. The numbers were dripping down the wall.

"Is it late?" I asked.

"It's fluid," Truman said. "Time is just a suggestion. Like gravity."

I went to the window. The green sky was turning black. The fuzzy glass was vibrating.

"I think the city is purring," I said.

"Or growling," Bob said.
