# Tuesday, May 19, 2026

> Written at 2026-05-19 23:59:59

Woke up at 6:30 AM. The sun was up, but it was square. A big, yellow square in the sky. It looked like a Post-it note from God. Or maybe just a very large pixel. I checked the window. The glass was gone. Just the frame. The air outside was thick, like gelatin. I could see the pigeons flying, but they were flying very slowly. Swimming through the air. I stuck my hand out the window. My hand felt sticky. I pulled it back in. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:45 AM. $850.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was tiny and wearing a scarf.

"More ice, darling," he kept saying to the passenger seat, even though nobody was there. "It's melting."

"Are you real?" I asked.

"I'm as real as the next sentence," Truman said. I gave him a twenty. He turned it into a paper crane and flew away.

When I got to the Factory, the floor was wet. But it wasn't water. It was time. The puddle from the Man in the Grey Suit had evaporated, but it left the floor feeling... wobbly. When I walked, my footprints stayed there for a second before disappearing.

"Don't step on the cracks," Sammy said. He was balancing on a table. "The seconds between the seconds are deep. You'll fall in."

"I like falling," I said. "It's the rushing that scares me."

Bob was sitting in the middle of the room. He wasn't leaking ink anymore. He was leaking numbers. *1, 2, 3*. They dripped out of his ears and hit the floor with a *plink* sound.

"I'm calculating my worth," Bob said. "But I keep dividing by zero."

"Don't do that," I said. "You'll explode."

"I'm infinite," Bob said. "And empty."

The squirrel was sitting on top of Bob's head. He was wearing the golden hanger I bought yesterday as a crown.

"I'm the King of Nutopia," the squirrel said. "Bow down."

"I don't bow," I said. "I crouch."

At 10:00 AM, the doorbell rang. It wasn't a ring. It was a cash register sound. *Cha-ching*.

I opened the door. It was the Man in the Grey Suit. But he was different now. He was 3D again. But he was made of origami. He was sharp. Paper sharp.

"I folded myself," the Man said. "It saves space."

"You look creased," I said.

"I'm complex," the Man said. He stepped inside and left a paper cut on the floor. "I'm here for the audit."

"We're not having an audit," I said. "We're having a Tuesday."

"Same thing," the Man said. He pulled out a clipboard made of razor blades. "Sign here. Sign your life away."

"I already did that in 1968," I said.

"Do it again," the Man said. "It's a renewal."

Jean-Michel floated in through the window. He was still a sketch.

"I smell paper," Jean-Michel said. "Can I draw on you?"

"You can draw on me," the Man said. "But use a check."

At 12:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Russian Tea Room". But it wasn't Russian anymore. It was Martian. The waiters were green antennas.

"We serve vacuum," the waiter said. "And dust."

"I'll have the Borscht," I said. "Is it red?"

"It's the color of silence," the waiter said.

The Borscht came in a lightbulb. $400.00.

"How do I eat it?" I asked.

"Screw it in," the waiter said.

I screwed the lightbulb into a socket on the table. The room glowed red.

"It's delicious," I said. "It tastes like beets and electricity."

The squirrel ordered the "Table". He ate the corner of the table.

"It's oak," the squirrel said. "Very woody."

At 2:45 PM, a truck pulled up outside. It was from "Celebrity Storage". Two men brought in a giant crate.

"What's in the box?" Sammy asked.

"It's my shadow," I said. "I lost it in 1985. I finally found it in a warehouse in New Jersey next to some old Brillo boxes."

They opened the crate. Inside was a flat black shape. It looked like me, but darker.

"It's heavy," Jean-Michel said.

"It's my dark side," I said. "Or maybe it's just a stain."

The shadow crawled out of the box. It stood up. It didn't have a face.

"Hello," I said.

The shadow didn't say anything. It just mimicked me. I raised my hand. It raised its hand.

"It's a copycat," the squirrel said. "Sue him."

"It's me," I said. "It's better than me."

At 4:00 PM, I tried to work. I sat in front of a canvas. I wanted to paint a portrait of the Shadow. But every time I looked at it, it disappeared.

"Stop looking at it," Truman said. He was typing on a typewriter made of smoke. "Art requires peripheral vision."

I looked away. I looked at Bob. Bob was still dripping numbers. The numbers were piling up around him. A mountain of integers.

"I'm rich," Bob said. "I'm a billionaire."

"It's just math," I said. "Spend it."

"I bought a vowel," Bob said. "I bought a 'U'."

So I painted the Shadow while looking at Bob. I used a sponge. I just slapped the paint on.

"It looks like a bruise," Sammy said.

"It's a masterpiece," I said. "It's 'Portrait of the Artist's Void'."

At 6:00 PM, Mr. Cash came back. He was wearing a suit made of receipts.

"I heard you found your shadow," Mr. Cash said. "I'll give you a million dollars for it."

"It's not for sale," I said.

"I'll give you two million," Mr. Cash said.

"Okay," I said. I shook his hand. The shadow detached itself from my feet and walked over to Mr. Cash. It looked good in a suit.

"A pleasure doing business with nothing," Mr. Cash said. He gave me a check. The check was made of glass. "Don't break it."

At 8:00 PM, we ordered dinner. "Pizza". $200.00.

The pizza came in a box, but the box was empty.

"It's thin crust," the delivery boy said. "Invisible crust."

"It's diet pizza," I said. I ate a slice of air. It was filling.
