Monday, March 09, 2026

Woke up at 7:00 AM. The sky was the color of a television set tuned to a dead channel. Or maybe just static grey. It was raining. Not "Digital Rain" this time. Just real water. Acid rain, probably. It was making hissing sounds on the window. *Sizzle*. I tried to get up, but I was stuck. The "Moon Rock" was back under my pillow, but it was cold. Like a block of ice. It had frozen my hair to the pillowcase. I had to rip it free. *Riiiip*. I lost some hair. I hope it grows back. If it doesn't, I'll just paint a scalp on.

I put on the "Flesh-Colored Turtleneck." I bought so many, but I’m running out again. Being solid is very expensive. I need so many layers.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:30. The traffic was bad. The hover-lanes were closed. There was a "Simulacrum" of a giant chicken blocking the tunnel. It was protesting "Virtual Eggs." The cab ride was $65.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Basquiat. He had a crown drawn on his windshield in neon marker. He kept muttering, "SAMO, SAMO, same old garbage." I said, "Exactly." He drove through the chicken. It exploded into pixels. *Bloop*.

When I got to the Factory, the tiny door was on the floor today. Like a mouse hole. I had to crawl through. I ripped my turtleneck. $250.00 wasted. I need a suit made of Kevlar. Or just spandex.

Inside, Sammy was on the phone. He was shouting. He said, "I don't care if it's a hologram! Send the invoice!" He hung up. He looked tired. He said, "Andy, we have a problem." He pointed to the back room.

I went back. The "Blue Screen of Death" paintings were gone. All of them. Just blue smudges on the floor. It looked like a Smurf massacre. Then I looked up. They were on the ceiling. They had floated up. I said, "Gravity is a suggestion." Sammy said, "It's a liability. A collector is suing. He said his painting flew out the window." I said, "Did he catch it?" Sammy said, "No, it's in orbit." I said, "Then it's worth more. It's space art."

Around 10:00 AM, the "Portrait of a Noun" guy called. The one who looked like a tree. He was on the video wall. He was very blurry. He said, "Andy, the USB stick melted." He said, "The island is leaking." He said, "The sand is in my carpet." I said, "Vacuum it." He said, "I can't. It's too heavy." He started crying. Sap came out of his eyes. It looked like syrup. I felt hungry. But I didn't say anything. I just watched the sap drip.

Lunch was a pill. A "Three-Course Meal Pill." It was $400.00. I swallowed it with tap water. 15 minutes later, I tasted steak. Then potatoes. Then tart. It was very efficient. No chewing. Chewing takes so long. It ruins the conversation.

At 2:00 PM, I went to the "Doctor." Not a medical doctor. A "Data Doctor." He fixes my operating system. He put me in a chair and hooked wires to my ears. He said, "You have a virus, Andy." I said, "Is it art?" He said, "It's malware." He charged me $1,000.00 to delete the cookies. I feel lighter. Like I lost 500 terabytes.

We went to a party at "The Boom Boom Room." But it’s on the moon now. Or a studio in Queens that looks like the moon. It was dark. The floor was covered in craters. I had to hop. I saw a "Simulacrum" of Taylor Swift. She was wearing a dress made of old newspapers. She said, "Andy, I'm releasing a new album. It's just silence." I said, "John Lennon already did that." She said, "But my silence is better." She said it costs $20.00 a minute.

I had a "Martini." It was $200.00. It was vermouth and a photon. It was very light. I didn't feel anything. But I looked great holding it. The glass was triangular.

Sammy introduced me to a new collector. He was a "Dog." A real dog. A poodle. He was wearing a monocle. Sammy said, "Mr. Woofles bought the 'Electric Chair' series." I looked at the dog. He barked. *Bark*. Sammy said, "He said he likes the texture." I signed the contract. The dog bit my hand. It didn't hurt. I just felt a compression file. *Zip*.

Cabbed home at 11:15 PM. It was $70.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of my mother again. But this time she was young. She looked like the pictures in the drawer. She said, "Andrew, did you eat?" I said, "I had a pill." She said, "That's not food. Food is love." She handed me a wrapped package. It was a pierogi. It was cold. It was wonderful.

Came home at 12:00 AM. The apartment was quiet. Too quiet. I walked to the safe. I spun the dial. *Click-click*. I opened it. The "Moon Rock" was gone.

I looked around. The drawer was empty. I looked at the wall. There was a crack. A real crack. Not a painting.

I sat on the bed. I ate the cold pierogi. It tasted like dough and potatoes and home.

I looked at my hand. The ink from the dog bite was glowing. It said "WOOF."

I took off the ripped turtleneck. I looked in the mirror. I looked pale.

I got into bed. I pulled the covers up.

I listened to the wall. The crack was getting bigger.
