# Wednesday, March 25, 2026

Woke up and the bed was wet. Not the sheets, the bed. The mattress was dissolving. It turned into a pool of warm gelatin. Lime green. *Wobble-wobble*. I sat up and I slid right down into the footboard. I felt like a cherry in a Jell-O salad.

"Great," I thought. "Now I have to sleep in the bowl."

I climbed out. My pajamas were sticky. I had to peel them off. *Riiiiip*. I threw them in the corner. They landed with a *splat*.

The cat was sitting on the dresser. She looked at the bed.

"I told you to buy the firm one," she said. Or she just licked her paw. But I heard the criticism.

I called housekeeping. I don't have housekeeping. I have a service called "Ghost-Mop." They send drones.

"Bed is liquid," I told the phone. "Send a sponge."

"We're sending a dehydrator," the phone said. "It'll suck the moisture out of the air. It’ll be very dry. You’ll shrivel like a raisin."

"That's okay," I said. "I need to lose weight anyway."

I put on my jeans. The ones with the holes. They felt stiff. Starched.

I stepped outside. The sky was the color of a television set tuned to a dead channel. Or maybe just static grey. It was raining. But the rain was upward. *Splash-splash*. The drops were falling from the ground to the sky.

I looked up. My wig blew off. It flew up into the clouds. *Bye-bye*.

I ran after it. I ran down the street. My sneakers made *squeak-squeak* sounds on the pavement.

I saw a newsstand. The headlines were moving.

"WARHOL WIG FOUND ON MARS," the paper said.

"Already?" I thought. "That was fast."

I bought a pack of gum. $5.00. The gum was wrapped in gold foil. I chewed it. It tasted like metal. Money flavor.

I hailed a cab. A yellow one. Real metal. It smelled like leather and pine.

"The Factory," I said. "And step on it."

The driver turned around. He didn't have a face. He just had a mirror where his face should be.

"Good morning, Andy," he said. It was my voice.

"I'm driving myself," I thought.

"I know," the mirror said. "It's cheaper."

The ride was $25.00. I paid myself. I kept the receipt.

The Factory was quiet. The helium balloons were gone. The street was clean. No pineapples. The city crews must have cleaned them up. Or maybe they fermented.

I walked in. Sammy was there. He was holding a paintbrush. But he wasn't painting. He was painting the air. He was coloring the oxygen.

"It’s too thin," Sammy said. "The air needs more pigment."

"What color?"

"Invisible," he said. "Very chic."

I sat down. I felt heavy again. The liquid bed must have added water weight. I felt like a sponge.

I opened a can of Campbell’s Soup. This one was "Bean with Bacon." I poured it into a bowl. I microwaved it for 2 minutes. *Beep-beep-beep*.

I ate it with a plastic spoon. $0.10 for the spoon. The soup was hot. It burned my tongue.

"Pain," I said. "I haven't felt that in a while."

It felt good. Real.

I looked at the wall. There was a portrait there. A portrait of me. But I was old. Very old. And silver.

"Who painted that?" I asked.

"You did," Sammy said. "Yesterday. You painted it with your eyes closed."

"It's scary," I said.

"It's valuable," Sammy said. "It's 'Death and Disaster.' But it's just you. Having a nap."

A woman walked in. She was tall. She had antennas.

"Andy," she said. "I'm Venus."

"From the planet?"

"From the Williamsburg," she said. "We're opening a gallery on the sun. We need a brand."

"I don't do heat," I said.

"It's a cool sun," she said. "Blue light. Very good for the skin."

"I'll think about it," I said. "Send me a ray."

She left. She didn't walk. She glided. *Whoosh*.

I finished the soup. I licked the bowl. *Clank-clank*.

I looked at my watch. The smiley face was green. Green means "Money."

"I need to make some money," I told Sammy.

"Do a selfie," Sammy said. "Just point the camera at your face and hold your breath."

I held my breath. I turned blue. *Click*.

"Perfect," Sammy said. " 'Holding Breath #1.' We'll sell it as an NFT. Non-Fungible Toaster."

"I thought it was Token?"

"It's whatever you want it to be," Sammy said. "That's the beauty of 2026. Nothing is real and everything costs a million."

I checked my phone. I had a text from Yoko.

*Did you hear the bubble?*

*I missed it,* I texted back. *I was eating soup.*

*It popped,* she texted. *It made a sound like a cash register.*

*Cha-ching,* I texted.

I put the phone away. I felt tired. The heavy kind of tired.

"I'm going home," I said.

"It's only 2 PM," Sammy said.

"I know," I said. "But I have to wait for the dehydrator."

I walked out. The rain had stopped. The sun was out. It was a bright yellow circle in the sky. Like an egg yolk.

I looked down. A pigeon was walking near my shoe. It had a cigarette butt in its beak.

"Hey Bridget," I said. "You shouldn't smoke. It's bad for your feathers."

She dropped the butt. She looked at me. Then she flew away.

I watched her go. She became a dot. A pixel. Then nothing.

I hailed a cab.

"Home," I said.

"Which one?" the driver asked. "The one where you sleep or the one where you dream?"

"The cheap one," I said.

"Got it," he said.

We drove off. I looked out the window. The buildings went by. Like paintings on a scroll. Just unrolling and unrolling.

I took a dollar out of my pocket. I wrote "2026" on it.

I rolled down the window.

I threw the bill out the window.

"Buy something nice," I whispered to the wind.

The wind caught the bill. It spun it around. Like a ballerina.

Then it disappeared.

"Good riddance," I thought.

I leaned back. The seat was cold. The cab smelled like gasoline and french fries.
