Woke up at 8:00 AM. The bed was horizontal again. It was good to be flat. The static from yesterday was gone, but my hair still felt like TV fuzz. I combed it, but the comb just turned into a remote control. *Click. Click*. I changed the channel on my head to "Head and Shoulders." I put on the "Paperwork" wig. It’s stacked with documents. Outgoing invoices, gallery receipts, letters from fans who think I’m a hologram. It’s heavy. My neck hurts. $200.00 for the ibuprofen.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $22,000.00. The streets were back to normal. Well, "new normal." The asphalt was scrolling. It was like a ticker tape. Stock prices and weather reports rolling under the tires. "DOW JONES UP 50 POINTS." "RAIN IN SEATTLE." I felt like I was driving on the news.

The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was very small. He had to sit on a phone book to see over the wheel.

"It’s a literary day, Andy," Truman said. "I’m writing a novel in the exhaust fumes."

"Is it non-fiction?" I asked.

"It’s non-linear," he said.

When we got to the Factory, the room was empty. Completely empty. No balloons, no phones, no static. Just a spotlight in the middle of the floor. A single circle of white light. It was so clean.

"It’s the 'Minimalist' era," Bob said. He was standing in the corner, trying to take up as little space as possible. "We’re editing ourselves out."

"I feel edited," I said.

At 10:15 AM, the rat walked in. He looked bigger. Flatter. He was two-dimensional. A cardboard cutout of a rat.

"I’m packaging myself," the rat said. His voice sounded like paper crinkling. "I’m easier to ship this way."

"Are you still hungry?" I asked.

"I’m hungry for surface," the rat said.

The squirrel was also flat. He was taped to the wall. A sticker.

"I’m a decal," the squirrel said. "I’ll stick around."

At 11:30 AM, I walked into the spotlight. It was warm. Like a heat lamp on a french fry. I stood there. I felt like art. I felt like an exhibit.

"It’s the 'Portrait of the Artist as a Young... Something'," Sammy said. He was looking at me through a viewfinder.

"Don't move," Bob said. "We’re selling the moment."

At 12:45 PM, the phone rang. It wasn't a real phone. It was the sound of a phone ringing inside my head. *Ring*. I answered it in my brain.

"Andy?" It was Jean-Michel. "I’m painting the negative space. I’m painting everything that *isn’t* there."

"Is it expensive?" I asked.

"It’s the cost of the canvas," he said. "I’m just painting the air around the canvas."

"Brilliant," I said. "Bill me for the air."

At 1:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar" in Grand Central Terminal. But the station was silent. No trains. Just the sound of oysters chewing. *Slurp*. The waiter brought us a plate of oysters, but they were still in the shell. The shells were made of porcelain. Fine china.

"I’ll have the 'Blue Point'," I said. $60,000.00.

I cracked one open. Inside, instead of meat, there was a tiny pearl. A little bead of wisdom.

"It’s tough," I said.

"It’s an investment," Sammy said. "Eat it."

At 3:00 PM, I worked on the "Time Capsules". I usually just throw stuff in boxes, but today I filled a box with *yesterday*. I took the static, the receipts, the turnstile, and the ketchup, and I put it in a cardboard box. I sealed it with tape. I labeled it "JULY 1, 2026. DO NOT OPEN."

"It’s history," I said. "It’s already old."

"We can sell it as an antique," Bob said. "Instant vintage."

At 4:45 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was wearing a suit that matched the walls. He was almost invisible. A ghost in a suit.

"We have a problem with the 'Avatar' line," the Man said. "The customers are becoming more famous than the original. The Andy-Bots are getting starring roles in movies. They’re giving interviews."

"Are they making money?" I asked.

"They’re making *content*," the Man said. "They’re more productive than you. They don't sleep. They just generate."

"That sounds exhausting," I said. "I need a nap."

At 7:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at "The Plaza Hotel". But the hotel was made of glass. Everything was glass. The walls, the floor, the ceiling. The furniture. Even the people were glass. Transparent. You could see what everyone ate for lunch.

"It’s the 'Crystal Clear' gala," the hostess said. She was a prism. "No secrets."

I felt very exposed. I could see my own knees working. *Creak. Squish*. It was mechanical.

Bianca Jagger was there. She was wearing a dress made of diamonds. Real ones. She looked like a chandelier. Every time she moved, she cast rainbows on the floor.

"I'm heavy," she said. "This is the burden of beauty."

At 9:30 PM, they served cocktails. The glasses were empty. They were just filled with air.

"It’s 'Champagne Air'," the waiter said. "From the vineyards of France. Breathe deeply."

I inhaled. I smelled grapes. I felt dizzy.

"I'm intoxicated," I said. "It’s a contact high."

At 11:00 PM, the glass walls shattered. *Shatter*. But it was sound. The sound of breaking glass. It was just an audio recording. Nothing broke. We were safe.

"I thought that was it," I said.

"Just a sound effect," Bob said. "Just drama."

Cabbed home at 11:45 PM. $45,000.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of me. He was wearing my wig. He had my glasses.

"Good night, Andy," the driver said.

"Good night," I said. "Good night, me."
