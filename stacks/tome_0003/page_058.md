Woke up at 8:45 AM. The cat was still glowing. A soft, radioactive blue. It made it easy to find the bathroom. I didn't have to turn on the light. I just followed the cat. *Meow-glow*. I brushed my teeth with the glow-in-the-dark toothpaste. $12.00. It tastes like peppermint and uranium.

I looked at the moon rock. It was sitting on the nightstand. It looked smaller. I think it's evaporating. Or maybe it's just hungry.

"Good morning, rock," I said.

It didn't say anything. Rocks are very quiet. That's why I like them. They don't interrupt.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $40.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Abraham Lincoln. He was very tall. His hat hit the roof every time we hit a bump. *Thump-thump*.

"Four score and seven dollars ago," Abe said. "That will be the fare."

"Inflation," I said.

"It's the economy," Abe said. "It's booming. It's loud."

The Factory was wet. The "Indoor Rain" from yesterday had leaked into the ventilation system. It was raining inside. Just in the center of the room. A vertical cloud.

Sammy was holding an umbrella. He was standing over the silkscreens.

"The ink is running," Sammy said. "Everything is blending. The portraits are melting."

"Let it melt," I said. "It's 'Dissolve.' It's the new series."

I walked to the back. I was wearing my trench coat. I kept it on. My socks were wet. *Squish-squish*.

I sat down to work. I had a commission from a "Simulacrum" of Elizabeth Taylor. She wanted a portrait of herself, but as a diamond.

"She wants to look hard," Sammy said.

"She is hard," I said. "She's a diamond."

I started drawing. I used a silver pen. It scratched the paper. *Scritch-scratch*.

I checked my phone. Another text from Bob.

*I'm a Buy Now button. Click me.*

*I'm saving you,* I texted back.

*I'm going to a warehouse in New Jersey,* Bob texted. *The shipping is free.*

*That's tragic,* I texted.

I put the phone away. The rain in the Factory stopped suddenly. *Snap*. The sun came out. But it was a fake sun. A projection from the ceiling. It buzzed. *Bzzzzzt*.

"It's noon," Sammy said. "Time for lunch."

Lunch was at Serendipity 3. But it's now in the basement of a bank. The vault.

I ordered the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $25.00. It was served by a "Simulacrum" of Rudolf Nureyev. He was wearing a tutu and a guard's uniform.

"Is it safe?" I asked.

"It's secure," Rudolf said. "You have to enter a PIN to drink it."

I typed in my birthday. *Beep-beep*. The straw unlocked. I sipped. It was cold. It froze my brain. *Brain-freeze*.

"Exquisite," I said.

"We have a new dessert," Rudolf said. "It's called 'The Golden Parachute.' It's made of spun gold and edible stock options."

"I'll take two to go," I said. "For the cloud."

I paid the bill. I signed the receipt. My signature looked like a scribble. A valuable scribble.

We went back to the Factory. There was a crowd outside. Tourists. They were taking pictures of the door.

"It's just a door," I told them.

"It's the Door," one of them said. "He walked through it."

I signed a few autographs. "Andy." "Andy." "Andy." My hand hurt. Repetition is work.

Inside, the moon rock was gone.

"Where is it?" I asked.

"The cat ate it," Sammy said.

"The cat ate the moon?"

"Just swallowed it," Sammy said. "She's heavy now. Very dense."

I looked at the cat. She was lying on the floor. She looked like a black hole. Light was bending around her.

"She's going to have kittens," I said. "Moon kittens."

"Or asteroids," Sammy said. "Be careful when you pet her. The gravity is strong."

I didn't pet her. I just watched her. She purred. *Rumble-rumble*. It sounded like an earthquake.

I worked on the Diamond Liz. I made it sparkle. I used glitter. $500 worth of glitter. It got everywhere. In my hair. In my ears. I looked like a disco ball.

At 6:00 PM, a "Simulacrum" of Bob Dylan came by. He was wearing a mask. A paper mask.

"I wrote a song about you," Bob said.

"Is it fast?" I asked.

"It's a waltz," Bob said. "3/4 time. Very slow."

"I'll speed it up," I said. "I'll listen to it on 2x speed."

"You'll miss the point," Bob said.

"I don't want the point," I said. "I want the chorus."

He played a note. *Pluck*. It was sad.

"Beautiful," I said. "How much?"

"Free," Bob said. "But you have to wear the mask."

I put on the mask. It smelled like stale coffee and harmonicas.

Cabbed home at 8:00 PM. $55.00. The driver was a robot. Just a metal box with wheels. No face. Very efficient.

"Goodnight, Mr. Warhol," the robot said. Its voice was like Siri.

"Goodnight, metal," I said.

I walked into the apartment. The cat was in the corner. She was glowing brighter. A blinding blue light.

I sat on the couch. I turned on the TV. The news was on. The weatherman was a cloud. He was raining on the map.

"Expect heavy showers tonight," the cloud said. "And maybe some moon dust."

I opened my diary. I wrote the date. March 30. The pen ran out of ink. The moon rock must have sapped the energy.

I used a pencil. It was grey. Like the future.

I thought about the wig on the moon. I thought about Bob in the spam folder. I thought about the cat.

"I'm tired," I thought. "But being tired is good. It means you did something."

Even if that something was just watching the cat eat the moon.

I closed the book. *Thud*.

The light in the other room flickered. The cat meowed. It sounded like a transmission.

"Over and out," I said.
