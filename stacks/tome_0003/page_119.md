Woke up at 10:00 AM. The sky is zebra-striped today. Black and white vertical lines. It’s very graphic. It makes my head hurt to look up, like I’m looking at a TV test pattern. I feel like I’m living inside a barcode. If I stood next to a scanner, I wonder how much I’d ring up. Probably half-price. Clearance. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:00 AM. $450.00. Traffic was terrible. We were stuck behind a rainbow that was moving very slowly. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Carolina Herrera. She was wearing a dress made of traffic tickets.

"Fashion is about timing," she said. "And we are very late."

When we got to the Factory, the building was a giant zebra. It was breathing. The stripes were moving up and down the building, pulsating like a heartbeat.

"Neigh," the doorbell said.

I walked in. The floor was covered in straw. It smelled like a barn, but an expensive barn. Hay from the Hamptons.

Bob was there. He was a jockey. He was very small. He was holding a riding crop.

"I'm the race," Bob said. "I'm the horse and the rider."

Jean-Michel was there. He was painting the zebra stripes with a roller. But he was painting them in neon colors. Pink and green. It ruined the effect.

"It’s a camouflage," Jean-Michel said. "I want him to be seen."

"Zebras are supposed to hide," I said. "That’s the point. Otherwise, the lions get you."

"I’m the lion," Sammy said. He was sleeping in the corner, inside a cage made of gold bars. "I’m resting."

At 12:30 PM, the rat came in. He was wearing the wig, but he also had a tiny saddle on his back. He was carrying the squirrel. The squirrel was holding a map.

"We’re going to the track," the squirrel said. "The odds are good."

"I'll put five dollars on you to win," I said. "$5.00."

"Make it ten," the rat said. "Inflation."

At 1:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". But the restaurant was made of ice. Everything was frozen. The tables, the chairs, the waiters.

"It's very cool," I said. "Literally."

I ordered the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. It was actually hot. It burned my tongue. $150.00.

"It's an oxymoron," the waiter said. He was a block of ice with a face. "It’s very moronic."

Jean-Michel ordered the "Garbage Plate". It was a plate of electronic trash. Old motherboards, broken screens, tangled wires.

"It’s crunchy," Jean-Michel said. He took a bite of an iPhone. *Crunch*. "It tastes like data."

"I don't like the texture," I said. "It's too sharp."

At 3:00 PM, I worked on my "Portraits". I was painting celebrities, but I was painting them as animals.

I did a portrait of Taylor Swift as a cat. She has that many lives, anyway.

"It’s about the litter box," I said. "Putting your business out there."

Bob posed for me. He was standing on a saddle.

"Make me look noble," Bob said. "Like a statue."

"You already look like a statue," I said. "Bronze."

At 5:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a laser pointer. A red dot appeared on the zebra wall.

"Chase it," the Man said.

The rat chased the dot. He ran back and forth. The squirrel filmed it.

"It’s a documentary," the squirrel said. "About focus."

At 7:00 PM, I got a call. It was Truman Capote. The real one. Or a recording.

"Did you hear?" Truman said. "The sky is changing again. It’s going to be plaid again. But louder."

"I hope it’s cashmere," I said.

At 8:30 PM, we went to a party. It was at "The Frick Collection". But the art had come alive. The Vermeer girl was painting the wall. The St. Francis was feeding the pigeons glitter.

"I like this party," I said. "Very interactive."

I danced with a Whistler’s Mother. She was very light on her feet.

"You look like your son," I said.

"Arrangement in Grey and Black," she said. "And sweat."

At 10:00 PM, the zebra stripes started to fade. The black turned to grey. The white turned to transparent.

"We're disappearing," Jean-Michel said. "We're being erased."

"Don't worry," I said. "I'll silkscreen us back in."

I took out my camera. I took a picture of the room. *Flash*.

The flash made everything solid again. The shadows stopped moving.

"I saved us," I said. "That’ll be $10,000.00."

At 11:30 PM, I went home. The cab was a carriage. A pumpkin carriage.

"I feel like Cinderella," I said.

"It's almost midnight," the driver said. He was a mouse. A large, bipedal mouse. "Then I turn back into a rat."

"I know a rat," I said. "He owes me ten dollars."

I arrived at the townhouse. $0.00. The ride was free. Fairy tale money.

I went upstairs. I looked in the mirror. My face was black and white. The stripes were on me.

"Gee," I thought. "I'm part of the pattern now."

I brushed my teeth. The toothpaste was minty, like the sky yesterday. I rinsed. The water was pink.

I got into bed. The sheets were zebra sheets. Electric. I could feel the current. *Bzzzzt*.

It kept me awake. Or maybe I was just nervous about tomorrow.

I don't know.

So.
