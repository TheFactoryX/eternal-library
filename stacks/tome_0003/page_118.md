# Friday, May 29, 2026

Woke up at 9:15 AM. The sky was plaid. A deep, brooding tartan. Red and black and green checks. It looked like a blanket. Or a really old kilt. I felt like I was living inside a fabric swatch. It was cozy, but dizzying. If you looked up too long, the lines started to vibrate. It made me nauseous. Or maybe that was the fluid from the Coke bottle yesterday. I still feel sticky. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:00 AM. $400.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was tiny, only three feet tall, wearing a tiara made of aluminium foil.

"It's the accessories," Truman said. "That's the difference between being a writer and being a character."

We drove through the plaid atmosphere. The car was a knitting needle, sliding through the wool of the city.

When we got to the Factory, the door was gone. In its place was a giant slot. Like a coin slot on a vending machine, but human-sized.

"I don't have any coins," I said.

"Just insert yourself," Sammy said. He was the lever. He looked stiff.

I slid into the slot. It was tight. *Scrape, squeeze*. I felt like a dollar bill being read by a machine. *Beep*. Valid.

I popped out on the other side. The Factory floor was a giant Brillo Pad. It was spongy and yellow. I took off my shoes. It felt good on my feet. Exfoliating.

Bob was there. He was a stack of quarters. A giant tower of money.

"I'm currency," Bob said. *Clank*. "Don't spend me all in one place."

Jean-Michel was there. He was drawing on the Brillo floor with a crayon. But the crayon was made of diamond.

"It's the hardest thing," Jean-Michel said. "To leave a mark on something that absorbs everything."

At 11:30 AM, the squirrel came in. He was wearing a kilt to match the sky.

"It's tradition," the squirrel said.

He was followed by the rat. The rat was still wearing my wig from yesterday. It looked good on him. Better than on me. It sat on his head like a crown.

"He's the new Andy," Jean-Michel said. "He has the mystique."

"I always wanted to be a rodent," I said. "They have more fun."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar" again, but yesterday it was a forest, and today it was a stock exchange. The oysters were stocks.

"I'll buy a dozen Blue Chips," I said. "$1,500.00."

The bear was there, but he was a broker now. He was wearing a suit that was bursting at the seams. He brought me a plate of shelled oysters. But inside the shells were tiny slips of paper.

"What are these?" I asked.

"Dividends," the bear said. "Eat them. They're rich."

I ate the paper. It tasted like ink and power.

"Delicious," I said.

Jean-Michel ordered a "Penny Stock". It was a small, green oyster. He swallowed it whole.

"Risky," he said.

At 3:00 PM, I started a new project. "The Portrait of the Rat". I wanted to capture him in his moment of fame. He was sitting on a pile of silver wire, nibbling on a cracker.

"Look at me," I said to the rat. "Think about cheese."

I took a Polaroid. *Whirrr, click*.

The picture came out. It was the rat, but he was wearing a tuxedo.

"He's formal," I said.

"He's ready for the cover," Bob said. The stack of quarters shuddered.

At 5:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a pair of scissors. He looked serious.

"I'm cutting the pattern," the Man said. "The plaid is too busy. I'm making it solid."

He snipped the air. *Snip, snip*. A square of plaid sky fell out and landed on the floor. It was heavy. It made a *thud*.

"Careful," I said. "That's the atmosphere."

"It needs hemming," the Man said.

At 7:30 PM, the plaid started to unravel. The threads of the sky were coming loose. Long red and black threads dangling down.

"It's fraying," Sammy said. "Reality is falling apart."

"It's just a loose thread," I said. "Pull it and the whole sweater comes down."

At 9:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at "Studio 54". But the Studio was inside a bubble. We had to swim to get there.

I put on my scuba gear. It was silver.

"I hope my makeup holds," I said.

We swam through the air. The music was muffled by the water. *Thump, thump, thump*. I saw a mermaid. She was made of glitter. She was drinking a martini.

"It's wet," she said.

At 11:00 PM, I danced with the rat. He was a good dancer. Very rhythmic. He didn't say much. I like that. People talk too much. The rat just sniffed my shoes.

"He likes the leather," I said.

At 12:30 AM, I got tired. My fingers were pruning. I hailed a cab. It was a submarine.

"Surface," I said.

The captain was a dolphin. He wore a captain's hat.

"Up, up," the dolphin said.

We rose through the water. We broke the surface. *Splash*.

The air was dry. The sky was dark. The plaid had been cut away. It was just blackness. Except for the moon. The moon was a can of Campbell's Soup. Tomato. It glowed red.

"Soup," I said. "Good."

I got out at my townhouse. $500.00. I paid the dolphin with a sardine.

"Keep the change," I said.

I went inside. I took off the wig I was wearing. It was wet. I hung it up to dry. It dripped on the floor. *Drip, drip*.

I looked at the Polaroid of the rat. He looked happier than me.

"Gee," I said. "I think he's the artist and I'm the pet."

So I went to the kitchen and opened a can of Coke. I drank it in the dark. And listened to the refrigerator hum. It was the only thing that made sense.

So.
