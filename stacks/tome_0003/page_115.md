Woke up at 6:00 AM. The black sky outside had turned into television static. Just grey and black fuzz. *Krrshhh*. It was making a noise. A white noise. Like the air conditioner in a cheap motel in the Mojave. I tried to find a station, but there was nothing on. Just the sound of the universe dying. Or maybe it was just the traffic on the West Side Highway. It’s so hard to tell the difference between entropy and rush hour these days. I put on my wig to muffle the sound. It helps. The hair absorbs the waves. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 7:15 AM. $250.00. The fare keeps going up. I think the meter is measuring something else. Maybe it measures anxiety. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Liberace. He was wearing a cape made of cellophane.

"You look translucent," I said.

"I'm wrapped," Liberace said. "I'm preserving my freshness."

He drove backwards. We went from the Upper East Side down to the Factory in reverse gear.

"I like going backwards," I said. "You don't have to look at where you're going."

When we arrived, the Factory was a parking lot. Empty. Just white lines painted on silver asphalt. My art supplies were parked in the spaces. The paint cans were cars. The brushes were parking meters.

"We're in a pause," Sammy said. He was a traffic cone. Bright orange. "We're waiting for the culture to shift."

"I hope it shifts to automatic," I said.

Bob was there. He was a microphone. Just the mesh head on a stand. No cord.

"I'm listening," Bob said. *Feedback squeal*. "Can you hear the plumbing?"

"I can hear the money," I said.

Jean-Michel was spray-painting the air. He was painting clouds on the static sky.

"It needs color," Jean-Michel said. "It's too monochromatic."

"Black and white is a color," I said.

"It's a lifestyle," he said.

At 10:00 AM, the squirrel ran in. He was being chased by a Roomba. The Roomba was hungry.

"It wants to vacuum my aura," the squirrel said. He scrambled up a parking meter.

"It just wants to be full," I said. "I know how it feels."

At 12:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". But the tables were all telephones. We had to sit at booths that were just giant phone receivers.

"You have to talk to eat," the waiter said. He was a dial tone.

I sat on the mouthpiece. I ordered the "Frrrozen Hot Chocolate". $900.00.

It came in a tall glass, but it was steaming hot and frozen solid at the same time. It was a paradox.

"Drink it before it melts," the dial tone said.

"How can it melt if it's hot?" I asked.

"Physics is expensive," the tone said.

I drank it. It burned my tongue and gave me brain freeze.

"It's stimulating," I said.

Jean-Michel ordered a burger. It was a hologram. He ate the light.

"It's fattening," he said. "Photons have calories."

At 3:00 PM, I met with a collector. She was invisible. Just a floating pair of white gloves and pearls.

"I love your new work," the gloves said. They clapped. *Smack, smack*. "The 'Black Static' series. Very noir."

"It's just what I see," I said.

"I'll take three," she said. "I'll pay in silence."

"I prefer cash," I said. "Or shoes."

"I have gold shoes," she said. "But they're imaginary."

"I'll take them," I said. "I can wear them in my imagination."

At 5:00 PM, I started a new portrait. I took a Polaroid of the static sky. *Whirrr, click*. The picture came out grey.

"It's a picture of nothing," I said.

"It's a masterpiece," Bob said from the stand. "It's the ultimate abstraction."

I put it in a frame. A silver frame. Now it was something. It was Art.

At 7:30 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came back. He was carrying a shovel.

"I dug a hole," the Man said. "All the way through the world."

"What did you find?" I asked.

"Tuesday," the Man said. "It was underneath Monday all along."

"I hate Tuesdays," I said. "They feel like a hangover without the fun of the party."

"That's why they're durable," the Man said.

At 9:00 PM, we ordered dinner. "TV Dinners". $1,100.00.

The delivery boy brought trays. But the trays were just slabs of dry ice. They were smoking.

"It's 'Salisbury Steak'," the boy said. "But the cow is digital."

I poked the food. It disappeared into pixels.

"I think it's done," I said.

We ate the cold air. It tasted like aluminum and nostalgia.

At 11:00 PM, I looked at the sky. The static was clearing. The blackness was fading. I saw a pinprick of light. A star.

"Look," I said. "A celebrity."

Everyone looked up.

"Make a wish," Jean-Michel said.

"I wish for a Coca-Cola," I said. $0.50.

The star twinkled. And then a can fell out of the sky. *Plonk*. It landed right in my hand.

It was warm.

"Good," I said. "I hate the cold."

I popped the tab. *Fizz*. It sounded like the static. I drank it. It tasted like sugar and bubbles and time passing.

"Gee," I said. "It's the real thing."

I put the empty can on the silver ground. It rolled away. I watched it go until it was just a dot. A pointillist dot on a beige canvas.

"I think I'm ready for the commercial," I said.

Bob squealed with feedback. "We're live," he said.

So I smiled. And waited for the fade to black.
