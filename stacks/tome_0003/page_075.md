Woke up at 6:15 AM. *Click-click*. The sound was the lock on the door spinning. It was spinning by itself.

The sky was yellow. *Mustard yellow*. It looked like someone had spilled a giant jar of French’s on the city. Very condiment-y. I liked it. It felt like a hot dog day.

Archie was in the hallway. He was halfway through the hole in the wall. His back half was in my apartment. His front half was in the neighbor’s. He was stuck.

"Archie?" I said.

His tail twitched. *Swish-swish*. Dust fell on the rug.

I pulled his legs. *Pop*. He came out. He was holding a slip of paper in his mouth. He looked proud. He looked like a hunter.

I took the paper. It was a check. Made out to "Cash". For one million dollars.

"Good boy," I said. "That's better than a dead bird."

I gave him a treat. $5.00. It was a "Cheez-Borb". A spherical cheese puff. He ate it in one bite. *Gulp*.

I put the check in my pocket. I wondered what the neighbor wanted. Maybe he was paying rent. Or maybe it was hush money for the noise.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $50.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1950s Housewife. She had pearls and an apron.

"Buckle up, dear," she said. "Traffic is a nightmare."

She was right. The streets were jammed with trucks. They were all delivering "Red Rain". It was being bottled and sold as "Tomato Pop". $10.00 a bottle.

"It's selling out," the driver said. "Everyone wants to drink the sky."

We inched forward. *Creep-creep*. I watched the people on the corner. They were licking the sidewalks. Just a little taste. To see if it was Campbell's.

The Factory was humid. *Steam-steam*. The air conditioning was leaking.

I walked in. Bob was there. He was wearing a suit made of sponges.

"It's the humidity," Bob said. "I'm absorbing it."

"Good," I said. "We can squeeze you out later."

I went to the back. The moon rock was in the fridge. I opened the door. The light was out. The rock was glowing. It was the only light in the room. It looked like a red egg.

"I think it's hatching," I said.

"Better call the Pope," Bob said. "Or maybe the Heinz company."

I took the rock out. It was warm. It felt like a heart. I put it on the table. I took a silkscreen of Marilyn Monroe. I laid it over the rock. I taped it down.

"Now she's incubating," I thought. "She's going to hatch out of the stone."

Lunch was a hot dog. $250.00. It was from a cart on the corner. The vendor was a robot with no ears.

"Ketchup or mustard?" he asked.

"Both," I said. "To match the sky."

I ate it fast. The bun was soft. The meat was crunchy. Very textured.

At 2:00 PM, the hole in the wall at home called me. My pocket rang. *Ring-ring*.

I answered it.

"Hello?"

"Mr. Warhol?" It was the neighbor. His voice sounded far away. "Your cat is in my apartment again."

"He likes the view," I said.

"He's staring at my television," the neighbor said. "The TV is off. He's staring at the static. He's purring so loud he's vibrating the floor."

"He's conducting electricity," I said. "Don't touch him. You'll get a shock."

"I'm already shocked," the neighbor said. "My rent just went up. The building is alive."

"Pay it," I said. "It's cheaper than moving."

I hung up.

At 4:00 PM, I started a new painting. I took the check Archie brought. I glued it to a canvas. I took a pot of black ink. I poured it over the check. *Splat*. I turned the check into a Rorschach test.

"It looks like a butterfly," I said.

"It looks like a debt," Bob said.

"It's 'Assets to Ashes'," I said. "Very financial."

I sold it to the robot horse from the 21 Club. He paid $2 million. He said he wanted to eat it. I told him paper gives him a stomachache, but he wouldn't listen.

At 6:00 PM, I went to the opening of "The Hole". It was at the neighbor's apartment. The neighbor is an artist now. His name is Vincent Van Go-Go. He wears shoes on his hands.

I took the elevator. The button was wet. Someone had spilled soup on it.

The apartment was packed. There was no furniture. Just the hole. Archie was sitting on a pedestal. He looked like a statue. He was wearing a bow tie.

"Archie," I said. "You're famous."

He blinked. *Click*.

"It's a commentary on barriers," Vincent said. His voice was muffled because he was holding a glass of champagne in his foot. "The wall separates us, but the hole connects us. The cat is the bridge."

"Deep," I said. "How much for the cat?"

"He's not for sale," Vincent said. "He's the installation."

"He's mine," I said. "I fed him."

"He belongs to the art now," Vincent said. "Get your own cat."

I felt a little jealous. I wanted to be the bridge.

I drank some "Tomato Pop". It was fizzy. It tasted like metal and sugar.

I saw Bianca. She was wearing a dress made of the yellow sky.

"Andy," she said. "Did you bring the soup?"

"I am the soup," I said. "I'm broth."

We danced. The floor was sticky. The music was low. It was the sound of the moon rock humming. *Hum-hum*.

I left at 8:00 PM. I couldn't look at Archie. He was too popular. It reminded me of the old days. When Edie was the star and I was just the camera.

I walked outside. The sky was turning purple. *Grape jelly*.

Cabbed home at 8:30 PM. $45.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a taxi driver from 1975. He had a mustache and an opinion.

"The Mets are losing," he said.

"They're winning," I said. "They're playing a different game."

He looked at me in the mirror. "You're weird, mac."

"I'm rich," I said. "It's the same thing."
