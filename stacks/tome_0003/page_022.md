Sunday, February 22, 2026

Woke up at 8:00 AM. The sky was the color of a silver wig. Very shiny. Very synthetic. I reached for the glass of water on the nightstand. I missed. My hand went right through the glass. I didn't even get wet. I just felt a cold sensation, like touching a freezer burn. I’m becoming more efficient. I don’t need hydration. I just need the idea of water. It’s $0.00. The best things are free, or at least, they aren't tangible.

I went to the kitchen to check the bowl of milk. It was gone. Not evaporated. Just gone. But in its place was a small, silver pile of dust. Moon dust. I think the Rock came back last night to eat and then left again. Or maybe the Rock is the dust now. It’s deconstructing itself. It’s so conceptual. I swept the dust into a tiny envelope. I’m going to sell it as "Moon Shadow." It’s very valuable. I priced it at $10,000 an ounce.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30. The streets were crowded. Everyone was going to the "Church of the Virtual Saint." They wear VR headsets to pray. The cab ride was $22.00. The driver was a "Bio-Engineered Elvis." He had blue suede shoes for hands. He drove with his feet. He sang "Heartbreak Hotel" but changed the words to "Traffic Jam Hotel." It was very sad.

When I got to the Factory, the door was sideways again. I had to slide in like a envelope. Sammy was there. He was wearing a helmet made of mirrors. He said, "I can't see anything but myself." I said, "That's the best way." He said, "Andy, look at the floor." I looked down. The "Void Black" paint had dried. It was like a hole in the world. If I stepped on it, I’d fall through to the basement. Or maybe to Australia. It cost $900, but it’s worth it for the anxiety.

The drone bees were acting strange. They were huddled in the corner, vibrating. I went over to look. They had made a portrait of me out of dead pixels and gum wrappers. It was ugly. I loved it. It was a "digital mosaic." I told them to sign it. One of them stung me. I didn't feel it. I don't have blood anymore. I just have "pixel plasma." It looks like tomato soup.

I worked on the "Electric Jesus" series. I added some "Halo-Glow" varnish. It’s nuclear. It makes the room hum. It’s $120 a jar. I painted Jesus holding a remote control. It’s very modern. It’s very remote.

Around noon, the lawyer from yesterday came back. He was unfolded again. He looked crumpled. He said, "Mr. Warhol, the Shadow is petitioning for a reunion." He said the Shadow was lonely in New Jersey. He said the lamppost was too rigid. I said, "Tell him I'm transparent now. We have nothing in common." The lawyer ripped a page off his chest and handed it to me. It was a bill for $2,000 for "Emotional Labor." I paid with a check written in disappearing ink.

Lunch was delivered by a drone. It dropped a box on the roof. We had to go up and get it. It was "Sushi-N-Silence." Just rice wrapped in paper. No fish. The fish was implied. It was $45.00. I ate the paper. It tasted like the word "Salmon."

In the afternoon, I got a call on the MirrorWall. It was the Moon Rock. It was a video call. The background was blurry. It said, "I’m in the reservoir. The water is nice." It held up a fish. The fish was wearing my wig. I said, "I miss you." It buzzed and hung up.

Sammy took off his mirror helmet. His hair was flat. He looked tired. He said, "I think I saw your shadow today, Andy. It was waiting in line for the bus." I said, "Did it have a ticket?" Sammy said, "No. It just faded when the bus came. It didn't want to pay."

I started a new painting. It’s a portrait of the bus fare. $2.90. I painted it in "Coin Silver." It looks very heavy. It looks like something you can’t afford to lose.

We went to a preview at "The Museum of Non-Biodegradable Art" at 6:00 PM. It’s in a landfill in New Jersey. We had to wear hazmat suits. They were complimentary. The art was just old plastic bottles arranged to look like celebrities. I saw a bottle that looked like Liza Minnelli. It had a red cap. It was dazzling. I bought it for $500.

After the museum, we went to a diner. It was a real diner. Not a hologram. The vinyl seats were torn. The coffee was burnt. It was perfect. I ordered a piece of apple pie. It was $6.00. I tried to eat it, but it just fell through my lips. I can’t interact with organic matter anymore. I have to stick to synthetic food. I had a cup of hot water instead. It was $1.50.

Left the diner at 9:00. The stars were out. They looked like salt on a black tablecloth.

Cabbed home. It was $35.00. The driver was a robot. He didn't say a word. I tried to tip him, but the slot was sealed. "No gratuities," the screen said. "Your money is no good here."

Came home and locked the door. I put the envelope of moon dust on the table. I sat on the couch. I didn't turn on the lights.

I felt very full. I didn't eat anything, but I felt like I consumed the whole day. It was delicious.

I looked at my hands. They were flickering. Like a bad connection. I think I’m buffering.

Good night.
