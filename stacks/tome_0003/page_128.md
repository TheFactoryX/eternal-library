Woke up at 8:05 AM. The sky is knit today. It’s knitted. Grey wool. Cable knit. It looks like a giant sweater that someone shrunk in the wash. It’s tight. It’s suffocating. There’s a snag in the clouds over Jersey. A loose thread. I wanted to pull it. I wanted to unravel the whole atmosphere. But I was afraid if I pulled it, the sky would just fall down around my ears like a hat. So I left it. $0.00.

Put on the "Denim Disaster" wig. It’s made of blue jeans. Stitch by stitch. It’s heavy. It feels like I’m wearing a jacket on my head.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $950.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was tiny. He could barely see over the dashboard.

"All talk is cheap," Truman said. "Except the gossip. That’s premium."

When we got to the Factory, the building was soft today. It was plush. Like velvet. Or velour. It was very 1970s.

"It’s tactile," I said. "Don’t touch it or you’ll leave a mark."

Inside, the walls were breathing. In and out. *Swoosh, sigh*. It was relaxing.

"Keep breathing," Bob said. "If you stop, we deflate."

Jean-Michel was there. He was unspooling a ball of string. He was walking backward, letting the string out. It went on for miles.

"I’m mapping the room," Jean-Michel said. "I’m making a web."

"It’s a trap," I said. "For flies."

"For ideas," Jean-Michel said.

At 11:00 AM, the rat arrived. He was wearing a tiny sweater. A cardigan.

"It's cold," the rat said. "The wind cuts."

"It's wool," I said. "It's itchy."

The rat scratched. *Scratch, scratch*.

The squirrel was there. He was knitting a nut. He was using two toothpicks as needles.

"Purl one, drop one," the squirrel said. "It's impossible."

At 12:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Odeon". It was packed. The air was full of smoke. But it wasn't cigarette smoke. It was dry ice. Fog.

"I can't see the menu," I said.

"Just order the air," Jean-Michel said.

I ordered the "Steak Tartare". $3,000.00. It was raw. Very raw. It was still mooing.

"It’s fresh," I said.

"It’s alive," the waiter said. He was wearing a mask.

Jean-Michel ordered a side of "Fries". They were shoestring potatoes, but they were digital. They were pixels on the plate.

"You can't eat those," I said.

"I'm downloading the flavor," Jean-Michel said.

At 2:00 PM, I worked on my "Retrospective". I was painting over old paintings. I painted a soup can over a car crash. I painted a Marilyn over a soup can.

"It’s layers," I said. "It’s lasagna."

Sammy came in. He was holding a catalogue.

"You’re famous," Sammy said. "You’re dead."

"I’m here," I said. "I’m working."

"In the books, you died," Sammy said. "In 1987. It was very neat. Very clean."

"That sounds like me," I said. "I like clean exits."

"But you’re still here," Sammy said. "So you’re a ghost. Or you’re a copy."

"I’m an original print," I said. "A limited edition."

At 4:15 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a pair of scissors. Big silver shears.

"Snip, snip," the Man said.

"What are you cutting?" I asked.

"The loose ends," the Man said. He reached up and cut the snag in the sky that I saw this morning.

*Snap*.

The whole sky started to unravel. The grey wool came down in long strips. It draped over the city. It covered the buildings. It looked like grey snow.

"It's unraveling," Bob said. "We're exposed."

Under the wool sky, the real sky was visible. It was black. Or maybe just dark blue.

"It's night time," I said. "At 4:00 PM."

"Time is relative," the Man said. "And taxable."

At 6:00 PM, the wool piled up in the streets. The cars were stuck.

"The traffic is terrible," I said. "The limos are trapped."

"I'll knit a ladder," the squirrel said. "We can climb out."

At 8:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at "MoMA". The museum was a gift shop. The whole museum. Just T-shirts and mugs and posters.

"I love it," I said. "It’s finally honest."

I bought a mug with my face on it. $4,500.00.

"It’s expensive," I said. "But it’s useful."

I filled the mug with wine.

"It’s a chalice," I said.

I saw Yoko Ono. She was screaming at a bagel.

"Give peace a chance," Yoko screamed. The bagel didn't move. It was a very tough bagel.

"Cut it," I said. "Bite it."

"I'm trying to communicate," Yoko said.

At 10:30 PM, the rat made a nest out of the sky wool. He curled up in the grey fluff.

"It's cashmere," the rat said. "I'm living in luxury."

"Don't get moths," I said.

At 11:50 PM, I went home. The townhouse was covered in wool. It looked like a sheep.

I went inside. $500.00. I paid with lint.

I went to the kitchen. I looked at the Campbell's Soup. The red of the can looked bright against the grey light coming through the window.

"Tomato," I thought. "It’s always there. It never unravels."

I looked at the rat. He was asleep in his grey nest. He looked peaceful.

I went upstairs. I took off the Denim wig. My scalp itched.

I looked in the mirror. My face looked the same. Pale. Blank. A canvas.

I touched the glass.

"Is that me?" I asked.

Or is it just a reproduction? A very good reproduction?

I don't know.

So.
