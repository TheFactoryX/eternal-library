Thursday, February 19, 2026

Woke up at 7:15 AM. The sky was the color of a bruised peach. Purple and yellow. Very sore looking. I reached for the MirrorWall to check the weather, but I remembered I don't have a reflection anymore. The screen was just black. It felt like looking into a very deep hole. I waved my hand in front of it. Nothing. Just the void. It was very peaceful. Like being dead but without the inconvenience.

Got out of bed and I didn't make a shadow. I looked down at the floor. The sun was coming through the blinds, making stripes on the carpet, but I didn't have a stripe. I looked like I had been cut out of the picture and pasted on top. It’s very clean. It’s very two-dimensional.

I went to the kitchen. The moon rock was on the table. It was vibrating again. It had slid across the table during the night and knocked over the salt shaker. There was a pile of salt where it used to be. It looked like a little beach. I swept it into the trash. The rock hummed at me. It sounded hungry. I poured some of the "Synth-Milk" on it. It drank it. Or maybe it just absorbed it. The milk vanished.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:30. The streets were shiny. It rained glitter last night. Not real glitter. Nano-plastic glitter. It gets in your eyes and makes you see stars. The cab was $20.00. The driver was a new model. He didn't have a face, just a screen showing a grid. He drove very fast. He said he was running on "Algorithm Aggression." I tipped him $5.00 to be nice.

Sammy was at the Factory when I got there. He was in the office, wearing sunglasses. It was dark inside. He said, "Andy, don't turn on the lights." I said, "Why?" He said, "The color red gives me a headache now." He said the "Neon Vomit" ink from yesterday gave him "Color Synesthesia." He said he can hear numbers. He said, "Don't say seven. Seven screams like a teakettle."

I went into the main room. The drone bees were sleeping on the rafters. They hang upside down like bats. They are very cute. They are metallic and fuzzy. The mannequin—Glitch—was sitting in the middle of the floor. She had the party hat on from yesterday, but she had also acquired a necklace. It was made of Saltine crackers. I don't know who put it there. Maybe the bees. Or maybe she walked to the deli. I took the crackers. I was hungry. They were stale. I ate them anyway. It was $0.00.

I worked on the "Electric Jesus" paintings. I added some "Static Spray." It makes the paint look like it’s moving even when it’s not. It makes you dizzy if you look at it too long. It’s $75 a can. It’s worth it. Religion should make you dizzy.

Around noon, a woman materialized in the room. She wasn't a ghost. She was a "Holo-Influencer." She was six feet tall and transparent blue. She was promoting a new brand of "Digital Skin." She said, "Mr. Warhol, your pores are so... retro. So analog." She touched my face. Her hand went right through my cheek. It felt like a cold wind. She took a selfie of us. Or just of me, while she was the filter. She disappeared in a puff of binary code. My phone buzzed. She tagged me. I owe her $20 for the exposure.

We ordered lunch from "The Cyber-Deli" again. I had a "RAM-EN." It’s noodles made of memory foam. They expand in your stomach but they have no taste. You have to add your own memories to make it flavorful. I thought about 1965. The noodles tasted like silver paint and speed. It was $18.00. Sammy just had a glass of water. He said the water tasted like the color blue. He said it was very loud.

In the afternoon, I worked on the portrait of the AI Mayor. The stock market crashed at 2:00 PM. The painting started to cry. Black ink ran down the canvas. It ruined the suit. I had to wipe it off. I used a silk scarf. It cost $120, but now the scarf has a picture of a crying politician on it. It’s art. I’m going to wear it to the opening tonight.

A man from the "Department of Obscure Phenomena" came by. He was wearing a suit made of bubble wrap. He popped every time he moved. It was very distracting. He said, "Mr. Warhol, we detected a Shadow Anomaly." I said, "I sold it." He said, "You can't sell your shadow. It's a Class B essential organ." I said, "I don't need it. It was dragging me down." He fined me $100 for "Unauthorized Detachment." I paid him. He popped when he walked out the door.

Before I left, I checked the moon rock. It had stopped humming. It was glowing. A soft, pink light. It looked like a nightlight. I put the wig back on it. It looked like a girl ready for a prom. Or a funeral. I don't know.

I left at 6:30. The sun was setting. The sky was the color of a Campbell's Tomato Soup can. Red and white. Very appetizing. I walked to the corner. Since I don't have a shadow, the light wouldn't change for me. The streetlights didn't turn on when I walked under them. The city didn't notice me. It was very private.

Cabbed home. It was $22.50.

Came home and sat in the dark. I looked in the mirror. Still nothing. Just the wall behind me. I felt thin. I felt like a piece of paper.

I checked the date. Tomorrow is Friday. Friday is for parties. I hope someone remembers to invite me. Or maybe I'll just invite the moon rock.

Good night.
