# Tuesday, April 21, 2026

> Written at 2026-04-21 23:59:59

Woke up at 7:00 AM. The sky was a lovely shade of "Tiffany Blue." Not the box, the girl. The Breakfast at Tiffany's girl. It made me feel like I should put on pearls and stare at a window. But I just put on my jeans and a wig. The "Campbell's Soup" wig. It’s red and white and looks like a labeled can if you look from above.

Archie was on the floor. He was chasing something. A laser dot. But I didn't have a laser.

"Who's shining that?" I asked.

Archie looked at me. He looked guilty. Or maybe hungry. "Meow," he said. "Feed-me."

"Okay," I said. "But the laser is extra."

Breakfast was a "Bagel & Lox" pill. $400.00. It was chewy. It tasted like fish and cream cheese, but in a chemical way. Like eating a perfume ad. I washed it down with some water. The water was fizzy. It was "Champagne Water". $25.00 a bottle.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. $70.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Aristotle Onassis. He was wearing a captain’s hat.

"The market is up," Aristotle said. "Way up. It's in the stratosphere."

"Is that good for art?" I asked.

"It's good for walls," he said. "White walls are a commodity."

The Factory was bustling. *Buzz-buzz*. The door was open. A real breeze was coming in. It smelled like car exhaust and pretzels. A real New York smell.

Sammy was in the front room. He was putting up shelves. But the shelves were floating. They weren't attached to the wall. They were just hovering there.

"It's magnetic," Sammy said. "I turned off the gravity in this corner. It saves on brackets."

"Fabulous," I said. "But what if the art floats away?"

"Then it's not art," Sammy said. "It's a balloon."

I went to the back. Bob was on the phone again. He looked stressed. His plastic suit was crinkling.

"Andy," he said. "We have a situation. The 21 Club deal is off. The freezers failed. They melted."

"Did the art melt?" I asked.

"Everything melted," Bob said. "It's a puddle. A very expensive puddle. We need to mop it up."

"We can sell the water," I said. " 'Abstract Expressionist Water'. $1,000 a bottle."

"You're a genius," Bob said. "I'll get the bottles."

At 11:00 AM, a man came to the door. He looked normal. Too normal. He was wearing a gray suit. He had a briefcase.

"I'm from the Bureau of Reality," he said. "We had a report of a glitch."

"I'm not a glitch," I said. "I'm a diarist."

"We'll see," he said. He opened his briefcase. It was full of rulers. He measured the room. He measured me. He measured Archie.

"You're 5-foot-11," he said. "In reality, you're supposed to be 5-foot-10."

"I'm wearing shoes," I said. "Platform shoes."

He made a note. "Platform shoes. Noted. Carry on."

He left. He measured the doorframe on the way out. *Squeak-squeak*.

"That was close," I thought. "Almost deported to Real Life."

Lunch was delivered by a kid on a bicycle. A real kid. Human. He looked terrified. He handed me a bag.

"$200.00," he said.

I gave him two hundred-dollar bills. They looked like Monopoly money. He took them anyway.

I ate a sandwich. It was a "Ham & Cheese". But the ham was pink and the cheese was yellow. It tasted like a memory. Like a lunchbox.

At 2:00 PM, I started a new portrait. It was a portrait of the guy from the Bureau of Reality. I wanted to make him look like a comic strip.

I used the "Roy Lichtenstein" filter on the printer. It printed his face in dots. Big red dots. *Ben-day dots*.

"I can hear the ocean," I said. The paper was loud. *Shhh-shhh*.

At 4:00 PM, the hole in the wall opened again. *Pop*.

I looked over. Vincent's eye was there. Just a giant eye.

"Hey," Vincent said. "Did you get my cat?"

"I have him," I said. "He's chasing lasers."

"Keep him," Vincent said. "I'm upgrading to a dog. A digital dog. It doesn't need to walk."

"I don't think I can keep him," I said. "He eats too much."

"Feed him data," Vincent said. "He loves spreadsheets."

The hole closed. *Zip*.

At 6:00 PM, I went to the opening of "The Mop Bucket Show" at the Gagosian. Cabbed there. $85.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Grace Jones. She was wearing a helmet.

"Don't touch the fro," she said.

"I wouldn't dream of it," I said.

The gallery was full of water. Well, not full. There were buckets everywhere. Silver buckets.

"It's about the clean-up," the gallerist said. "It's a meta-commentary on the spill."

"Who spilled?" I asked.

"Everyone," he said.

I saw a lot of famous people. I saw Madonna. She looked 20. She looked 60. She looked like a hologram.

"I love your soup can," she said to me.

"It's not a can," I said. "It's a pill."

"Same thing," she said. "We all get consumed."

I drank some wine. It was red. $15.00 a glass. It tasted like grapes. Real grapes.

"I miss the fake stuff," I thought. "It had more kick."

At 9:00 PM, I met Bob and Sammy at Serendipity 3. We had "Frrrozen Hot Chocolate". But it wasn't frozen. It was boiling hot. $50.00.

"It's a reversal," Sammy said. "Hot chocolate that’s hot."

"It burns the tongue," Bob said. "It feels like something."

I sipped it slowly. It hurt. But it was a good hurt. A "rich" hurt.

We talked about the merger.

"We should buy the sky," Bob said. "The sky is the only thing left."

"The sky is free," I said.

"Not anymore," Bob said. "The clouds are rent-controlled. The blue is for sale."

"I'll take two blues," I said. "And a cloud with a view."
