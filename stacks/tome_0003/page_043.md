Sunday, March 15, 2026

Woke up at 9:00 AM. My mouth tasted like copper. Or battery acid. I must have eaten the whole thing. The tomato. There were seeds in my teeth. I swallowed them. I hope a tree doesn't grow in my stomach. I’d be a terrarium.

I got out of bed and my foot went *click*. Like a camera shutter. I looked down. My skin was smooth. Too smooth. No hair. No pores. Just a beige surface. I tried to pinch my arm. It made a *beep* sound. I think I’m digital now. Or maybe I’m just clean. I couldn’t find the wig. My head was bare. Shiny. I looked like a lightbulb.

Breakfast was difficult. I poured a bowl of "Corn Flakes." $12.00. When I poured the milk, the milk didn't splash. It hovered over the flakes in little white spheres. Zero gravity milk. It’s trendy. I had to chase the spheres with my spoon. It was like trying to catch tadpoles. I gave up and ate the flakes dry. They crunched. *Crinkle-crunch*. They sounded like static.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. The sun was too bright. It was a perfect circle in the sky. No clouds. Just blue. Very saturated blue. Like Photoshop blue. The cab ride was $40.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1964 Ford Mustang. He had a grille for a face. He purred. I said, "Nice engine." He said, "I get 5 miles to the gallon."

The Factory was buzzing. Literally. The air was vibrating. Sammy was there. He was solid again. High definition. He looked great. younger. He had a new suit. It was made of mirrors. I saw myself reflected a thousand times. I looked distorted.

"Big day, Andy," Sammy said. "The IPO."

"The what?"

"The Initial Public Offering. For You."

"For me?"

"We're selling shares. Of Andy Warhol." He handed me a ticket. "You're incorporated. 'Andy Warhol, Inc.' We're listing on the NASDAQ at noon."

I felt lightheaded. Or maybe I was just buffering. "Do I get any money?"

"You are the money," he said.

Around 11:30 AM, the "Superstars" started arriving. They were all wearing numbers. Their stock prices. A "Simulacrum" of Edie Sedgwick was there. She was floating three inches off the ground. She looked like a balloon. If she popped, would she make a sound? She said, "Andy, I’m up five points." I said, "I’m still in pajamas." I wasn't. I was wearing jeans. But they felt like pajama pants.

We went down to the floor. The trading floor. It used to be a disco. Now it was a giant screen. Numbers scrolling. Green. Red. Green. Red.

At 12:00 PM, the bell rang. *DING*.

The screen flashed: **WARHOL**.

It opened at $1,000.00.

The room cheered. Confetti fell. It was made of shredded money. Real money. I picked up a scrap. It was a $100.00 bill. shredded. I tried to tape it together. I wasted $5.00 worth of tape.

The price went up. $1,500.00. $2,000.00.

I saw a "Simulacrum" of David Bowie. He was the Thin White Duke again. He was buying shares. He said, "Andy, I want to own a piece of your ear." I said, "Which ear?" He said, "The left one. It listens better."

I sold him my left ear for $50,000.00. He didn't detach it. It just transferred ownership. Now my left ear belongs to David Bowie. I can still hear out of it, but I hear his music sometimes. *Rebel Rebel*. It’s distracting.

Lunch was at "The Four Seasons." But the pool was empty. They drained it. They are growing money trees in the planters now. Little saplings with dollar bills for leaves. The hostess was a "Simulacrum" of Diana Vreeland. She wore glasses the size of dinner plates. She said, "Pink is the new beige. And money is the new pink."

I had the "Soup Du Jour." It was tomato. Of course. It cost $5,000.00 a bowl. It was very thick. It tasted like the future. Salty and metallic.

I sat next to a "Simulacrum" of Bob Dylan. He was whispering. He said, "The times they aren't changing. They've already changed. We're just waiting for the software to update." He handed me a harmonica. It was made of glass. I played it. *Hooooo*. It sounded like wind.

After lunch, I did an interview with a "Simulacrum" of Barbara Walters. She was made of questions. Literally. Little question marks floating in a dress. She asked, "Andy, are you happy?"

I said, "I don't know what that word means anymore."

She asked, "Do you feel real?"

I said, "I feel expensive."

She asked, "What do you want for the future?"

I said, "More. I want more of everything. And less. I want less gravity."

The interview aired immediately. On the inside of my eyelids. Every time I blinked, I saw my face. Saying "I feel expensive." It’s a good slogan. I should put it on a t-shirt.

At 4:00 PM, the stock closed at $10,000.00 a share. I was a billionaire. But I didn't feel any richer. I felt the same. Just *beepier*. My hand made a *beep* when I picked up my fork.

I walked home. I wanted to see the city.

The streets were crowded. People were wearing masks of my face. The "Andy Mask." $29.99. It was the best-selling item at the Halloween store. It’s not Halloween. But every day is Halloween now. I saw a kid wearing an Andy mask. He was crying. He said, "I can't see." I said, "Neither can I."

It started to rain. But it wasn't water. It was glitter. Gold glitter. It stuck to everything. The cars. The dogs. The bums. It made the world look like a jewelry box. I opened my mouth to taste it. It tasted like plastic and ambition.

I stopped at a bodega. I bought a "Coca-Cola." The old kind. In a glass bottle. It was warm. It cost $2.00.
