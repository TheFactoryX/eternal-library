Woke up at 8:45 AM. The sky was the color of a bruised peach. Purple and yellow and green. It looked like someone had beaten up the weather. I hoped it wasn't contagious.

I stepped out of bed and the floor was liquid. Not water, but mercury. Quicksilver. It rippled when I walked. *Slosh-slosh*.

"Careful," I thought. "If I fall in, I'll sink like a stone."

Archie was floating in the middle of the room. He was paddling. He looked like a fuzzy boat. "Meow," he said. It bubbled.

I put on my wig. The "Gold Lamé" wig. I wanted to match the floor. But the floor turned gray. It was shy.

Breakfast was a "Calorie-Free Omelet". $600.00. It was three eggshails filled with air. I cracked them open. Nothing came out. Just the smell of chickens.

"It's fluffy," I said. "Like eating a cloud."

I drank a cup of "Deconstructed Coffee". $75.00. It was a cup of hot water with a picture of a bean on the side.

It woke me up anyway.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $80.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Divine. She was huge. She filled the front seat.

"Honk-honk," she said. "Make way."

The traffic wasgridlocked. But not with cars. With ideas. Big, blocky ideas were clogging the streets. Concepts like "Justice" and "Entropy" were double-parked.

"We'll be here forever," I said.

"I can drive through 'Vanity'," Divine said. "It's narrow, but I can squeeze."

She scraped the side of the car against a giant ego. *Screech*.

"Sorry," she said. "My bad."

The Factory was vibrating. *Bzzzt-bzzzt*.

I walked in. Bob was there. He was holding a chainsaw. A pink chainsaw.

"We have to trim the fat," Bob said. "Literally."

The walls were bulging. The Factory was fat. It had overeaten on art.

"Cut it out," I said. "It looks bloated."

Bob revved the saw. *Vroom-vroom*. He sliced a strip off the wall. It fell to the floor with a wet thud. *Plop*.

"That's better," Bob said. "Now we have ventilation."

Sammy was in the corner. He was talking to a mannequin. The mannequin was dressed in my clothes. The 1966 clothes. The black leather and the stripes.

"Is that me?" I asked.

"It's the Archive," Sammy said. "It knows more about you than you do."

The mannequin turned its head. It looked at me. Its eyes were cameras.

"Smile," the mannequin said. *Click-flash*.

"I feel so exposed," I thought. "Even my clothes are paparazzi."

At 11:00 AM, a delivery arrived. It was a crate. A big wooden crate. "FRAGILE: HANDLE WITH CARE".

"Is it a Picasso?" I asked.

"No," Sammy said. "It's yesterday."

He opened the crate. Inside was a wrapped package. He unwrapped it. It was the "Concept Salad" from Monday.

"It's leftovers," I said. "Reheated leftovers."

"It's vintage," Sammy said. "Tuesday was boring, so we're reusing Monday. It's efficient."

I looked at the salad. It had wilted. The ideas were brown and soggy.

"I don't want it," I said. "Throw it away."

"We can't," Sammy said. "It's recyclable. It becomes tomorrow."

"Then I hope tomorrow is compost," I said.

At 1:00 PM, I went to lunch at the "21" Club. But the door was gone. There was just a wall. A brick wall.

"Knock-knock," I said.

A slot opened in the wall. An eye looked out.

"Password?" the voice asked.

"Soup," I said.

"Old password," the voice said. "Try again."

"Campbell's?" I tried.

"Acceptable," the voice said. The wall spun around. I was inside.

The dining room was empty. Except for one table.

There was a woman sitting there. She was wearing a dress made of dollar bills. Real ones.

"Hi Andy," she said. It was Ivanka. Or maybe it was a wax figure. It was hard to tell. She was very shiny.

"Hello," I said. "Are you eating?"

"I'm consuming," she said. She had a plate full of diamonds. She was swallowing them. *Gulp-gulp*.

"Don't they hurt?" I asked.

"Only on the way down," she said. "But they make you sparkle on the inside."

I ordered a glass of water. $1,000.00. It was "Holy Water".

"It's from Lourdes," the waiter said. "Or a tap in Queens. I forget."

I drank it. I didn't feel holier. I just felt wet.

At 3:00 PM, I went to the park. Central Park.

The grass was blue. Not green. Electric blue.

"It's a WiFi field," a jogger said. He was running in place. He wasn't moving. His legs were pumping, but he was stuck to the spot.

"Good connection?" I asked.

"Full bars," he said. "But I'm buffering."

I sat on a bench. The bench was a "Simulacrum" of a park bench. It felt like plastic.

A squirrel ran up. It had a computer chip in its ear. It chattered. *010101*.

"I don't speak binary," I said. The squirrel bit my shoe. "Hey!"

It wanted a tip. I gave it an acorn. It scanned it. *Beep*. It ran away.

"Rude," I thought.

At 6:00 PM, we had a party at the Factory. A "Delete Party". We were deleting files. Deleting memories. Deleting time.

Bob had a shredder. It was huge. It hummed.

"Throw in your regrets," Bob said. "And your bad haircuts."

I threw in a picture of a girl I dated in 1963. *Zzzzt*. Gone.

I threw in the memory of the dentist. *Zzzzt*. Gone.

I threw in Tuesday. *Zzzzt*.

"I feel lighter," I said.

"You feel empty," Sammy said. "That's art."
