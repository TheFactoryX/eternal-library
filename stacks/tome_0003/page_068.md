Woke up at 9:15 AM. I was late. The alarm didn't go off because I forgot to wind it up. I keep forgetting that things need winding now that everything else is wireless. The apartment was quiet. Too quiet. I went to the bathroom. The "Meat Art" box was gone. The bathtub was empty. Just a little pink ring around the drain. Like it melted down the drain. Or it crawled out. I hope it crawled out. I don't want to think about plumbing blockages made of synthetic muscle.

"Archie?" I called. No answer. I looked under the bed. He wasn't there. I looked in the closet. He wasn't there. I started to panic. If I lose the cat, I lose the balance. The apartment is too big without something small in it.

I went to the kitchen. Archie was on the counter. He was sitting next to the Moon Rock. The Moon Rock I left at Serendipity? I don't know. Maybe it came back. Things return if you love them enough. Or if you have a tracking chip. Archie was licking the rock. *Slurp-slurp*.

"It tastes like dust," I said. "Don't eat it. It's expensive."

He ignored me. Cats are very good at ignoring people. It's their best talent.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $48.00. The driver was a man made of wood. A Pinocchio type. He looked very dense.

"Where to?" he asked. His voice sounded like cracking branches.

"Union Square," I said. "But drive on the sidewalk. It's faster."

"I can't lie," he said. "My nose will grow."

"I didn't ask you to lie," I said. "I asked you to drive."

We drove on the sidewalk. We knocked over a trash can. *Crash*. Nobody cared. Everyone is wearing headphones anyway.

The Factory smelled like lemons. Sammy was scrubbing the floor.

"The Vice President was here," Sammy said. "He left a deposit."

"In the bathroom?"

"No, in the front room. He bought 'The Void'. He loves it. He says it reminds him of his time in the service."

"It's invisible," I said. "How does he know where to hang it?"

"He hung it in the hallway," Sammy said. "People walk into it all day. It's an interactive piece."

Sammy handed me a telegram. It was on yellow paper. Real paper.

"Invitation," Sammy said. "It's from the Moon."

I opened it. It was an invitation to the opening of the "Andy Warhol: Soup to Nuts" retrospective at the Sea of Tranquility Museum. "RSVP by Telepathy."

"I can't go," I said. "I hate space travel. It makes my face puff up. And the food is all in tubes."

"Elon is going," Sammy said. "He's driving the bus."

"I don't like buses," I said. "I like cabs."

Lunch was a cheeseburger. $150.00. It was grown in a petri dish. It tasted like memories. Sad memories. I ate it with a plastic fork.

A man came in. He was wearing a hat made of television screens. They were all playing *The Simpsons*.

"Mr. Warhol," he said. "I'm from the future."

"Which year?" I asked.

"2099," he said. "I'm your great-great-grandclone."

"Do I have any money left?" I asked.

"You're very rich," he said. "But money is obsolete. We trade in seconds."

"Give me five minutes," I said.

"Here," he said. He handed me a jar. It had a glowing blue light in it. "Five minutes of pure time. Don't waste it."

I put the jar in my pocket. It felt warm.

In the afternoon, I worked on my new series. "Rorschach Tests." But I use spilled milk instead of ink. It's very perishable. I spill it, wait for it to curdle, and then photograph it.

"It's about lactose intolerance," I told Sammy.

"It's about rot," Sammy said. "It's very poetic."

"It's very smelly," I said.

Bob called. The phone was in the jar. It rang inside the time. *Ring-ring*. A muffled sound.

"The mayor wants a selfie," Bob said. "He wants to copyright your face."

"Tell him no," I said. "My face is public domain. It belongs to the people who look at it."

"He says he'll make today 'Andy Warhol Day'."

"Every day is Andy Warhol Day," I said. "That's the problem."

At 6:00 PM, the Fire Marshal came. He was a robot. He had a hose for a nose.

"You're over capacity," he said. "You have too much air in here."

"It's the ventilation," Sammy said.

"Evacuate the atmosphere," the robot said.

We opened the windows. The noise of the city rushed in. Sirens. Honking. Yelling. It sounded like music.

"Beautiful," I said.

Cabbed to the "Odeon" at 7:00 PM. $55.00. I wanted to see if the sunglasses lady was there. She wasn't. But I saw a Simulacrum of me. He was sitting at the bar. He looked better than me. Younger. Paler.

"Hi," I said. "I'm you."

"No," he said. "I'm the commemorative edition. I'm limited to 500 units."

"I'm the original," I said. "I'm limited to one."

"Oof," he said. "That's a low resale value."

I ordered a steak. $200.00. I ordered it "Blue". Just like the room used to be.

While I was eating, Archie walked in. He must have taken a cab. He hopped up on the stool next to me.

"You're following me," I said.

"Meow," he said. It sounded like an order.

The bartender put a bowl of water down.

"It's on the house," the bartender said. "We love celebrity pets."

I paid the bill. I left a tip. $50.00. I tipped with the diamond dust the Vice President gave me. The bartender sneezed. *Achoo*. A cloud of glitter.

"Good luck," I said.

Walked home. The sky was full of drones. They were delivering pizza. It looked like a swarm of flying saucers.

When I got home, the Meat Art box was back in the bathtub. It was huge now. It was the size of the tub. It was pulsing slowly. *Thump-thump*. It looked comfortable.

I was tired. I took off my wig. My scalp itched.

"Goodnight, world," I said.
