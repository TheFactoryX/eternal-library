Woke up at 8:45 AM. The sky is polka-dotted today. Giant yellow dots on a blue field. It’s very playful. Like a bikini. Or a vintage dress worn by someone very famous who is now dead. It makes the world look like a piece of fabric waiting to be cut up. I tried to count the dots, but I got dizzy. It’s like looking at money. Too much to count. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $550.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Bianca Jagger. She was wearing a dress made of taxi receipts.

"It's expensive to be seen," Bianca said. "The meter is always running."

When we got to the Factory, the building was back to normal. Silver walls. No sunglasses, no zebras, no Brillo pads. It was just a building. It felt wrong. It felt empty. I missed the illusion.

"I feel heavy," I said. "Like a real person."

"Don't worry," Sammy said. He was standing by the door. He looked solid. "We're still superficial."

I walked in. The floor was linoleum again.

Bob was there. He was a man today. Just a regular guy in a suit. He looked boring.

"I'm the businessman," Bob said. "I'm the art dealer."

"You look like a bank," I said.

"I am a bank," Bob said. "Invest in me."

Jean-Michel was there. He was painting the silver walls black. He was covering up the shine.

"It's too bright," Jean-Michel said. "I need some shadow to define the edge."

At 10:30 AM, the rat came in. He was wearing a tiny business suit. A grey pinstripe. He was carrying a suitcase.

"I'm going to the office," the rat said. "I have a meeting."

"Where do you work?" I asked.

"Wall Street," the rat said. "I'm high frequency." He twitched his nose. "I smell cheese futures."

At 11:00 AM, I checked my mail. There was a bill from the hospital. $25,000.00. For the fluid. For the observation. I put it in the pile with the others. The pile is getting high. It’s a sculpture of debt.

At 12:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar" again. I like the pattern of the tiles. It’s like being inside a computer chip.

I ordered the clam chowder. $200.00. It was red. Like the sky was yesterday.

"It's Manhattan," I said. "It tastes like real estate."

Jean-Michel ordered a dozen oysters. He didn't eat them. He arranged them on the table.

"It's a city," Jean-Michel said. "A shell city."

"I lived there," I said. "It's gritty."

At 2:00 PM, I started a new painting. It was a portrait of the rat. But not as a rat. As a businessman. I called it "The Rat as CEO". I silk-screened his face onto a canvas of stock ticker tape.

"He's the new idol," I said. "He works for cheese and he never sleeps."

"Make me rich," the rat said. He was sitting on my shoulder. He smelled like money. Or maybe just fur.

At 3:30 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a stamp.

"Approved," the Man said. He stamped the floor. *Thud*.

"What is that?" I asked.

"June," the Man said. "It's official. You can turn the page now."

At 4:45 PM, I got a phone call. It was from the future.

"Hello?" I said.

"Buy gold," the voice said. "And wigs. Lots of wigs."

"Is this me?" I asked.

"No," the voice said. "This is your legacy. I'm calling to collect."

"I'm busy," I said. "I'm making art."

"Art is just a receipt," the voice said. And then it hung up.

At 6:00 PM, the sky started to change again. The polka dots began to melt. They dripped down the air like yellow rain.

"It's pollen," Bob said. "It's spring."

"It's paint," I said. "The world is frescoed."

At 7:30 PM, we went to a party. It was at "The Whitney". The museum looked like a ranch. It was the "Biennial of the Beef".

We walked in. There were hay bales covered in glitter. Cowboys were reading poetry.

"I like this," I said. "It's very tactile."

I met a cow. She was a critic.

"I give it a B-plus," the cow said. "It needs more milk."

"Maybe I'll paint you," I said.

"I'm already immortal," the cow said. "I'm a steak waiting to happen."

At 9:00 PM, I danced with Jean-Michel. We didn't move much. We just stood there and blinked.

"This is the post-dance," Jean-Michel said. "It's a commentary on movement."

"It's exhausting," I said. "I need to sit."

At 11:15 PM, I went home. The cab was a roller skate. A giant, silver roller skate. We rolled down the avenue. *Whoosh*.

"My feet hurt," I said.

"It's friction," the driver said. He was a wheel.

I arrived at the townhouse. $600.00. I tipped the wheel an extra $100.00 because he was round.

I went inside. The rat was back. He was sitting at my desk. He was writing in my diary.

"Hey," I said. "That's my life."

The rat looked up. "I'm just editing," he said. "You wrote too much about money. It's vulgar."

"Money is the most interesting thing," I said. "It's the only thing that's real."

"Maybe," the rat said. "But cheese is the only thing that matters." He ate the page. *Crunch*.

"I'll write it again tomorrow," I said.

I went to the kitchen. I opened a can of Campbell's Soup. Chicken Noodle. I ate it cold. It was slimy.

"Gee," I thought. "I wish I was a Simulacrum."

I looked in the mirror. I was wearing a wig made of yellow dots.

"Good," I said. "I match the sky."

I turned out the light. The yellow dots glowed in the dark.

I waited for the dreams to start. I wanted to dream of electric sheep and stock options.

But I couldn't sleep. The rat was typing too loud.

*Click, click, click*.

So.
