Woke up at 9:00 AM. The clock was blinking. *Blink-blink-blink*. It wasn't showing the time, it was showing a ticker tape of stock prices. NASDAQ is up. Soup is down. I tried to turn it off, but the button was just a painting of a button. A trompe l'œil. Very annoying. I had to get out of bed to see what time it was. The room was blue. The moon rock on the table has turned the whole apartment into an aquarium. Everything is floating. Even the dust motes look like plankton. It’s very pretty. It’s very calm.

Fed the cat. $4.00 for the food. He didn't eat it. He just sniffed it and turned his nose up. He’s on a hunger strike. He wants the moon again. I tried to tell him that the moon is just a rock, but he won’t listen. He’s an artist. He only eats concepts.

I got dressed. My black jeans felt tight. Maybe the moon rock is radioactive and I’m swelling. Or maybe I just ate too much invisible cheeseburger yesterday.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $45.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote.

"Did you hear the news, Andy?" Truman asked. He had a very high voice.

"What news?"

"I bought the pain," Truman said. "All of it. The Factory is empty, so I bought the pain."

"Is it heavy?"

"Terribly," he said. "But it goes with my shoes."

The Factory was open. The sign was gone. Just a rectangle of cleaner silver paint where the sign used to be.

Sammy was there. He was moving things back in. He was carrying a sofa, but he was carrying it with his mind. *Hover-hover*. It was very efficient.

"Where did you get the furniture?" I asked.

"I rented it," Sammy said. "It's 'Phantom Furniture.' It looks solid, but if you sit on it too long, it disappears. It saves space."

I went to the back room. I had an idea. A business idea. Since the Simulacrums are striking and I’m broke and I own the moon, I need to diversify.

I set up a table in the front room. I put a chair on it. The red chair from the kitchen. I put a sign on it: "AUTHENTIC ANDY WARHOL EXPERIENCE. $50.00 FOR 15 MINUTES."

"We're selling the void," I told Sammy.

"Brilliant," Sammy said. "People love nothing."

The first customer was a tourist. He was wearing a t-shirt that said "I <3 NY." He gave me a credit card. *Swipe-beep*.

"Now what?" he asked.

"Sit," I said. "I'll watch you."

"You?"

"Yes. Me. The real Andy. I'll watch you exist. It validates you."

He sat down. He looked nervous. He picked his nose. I stared at him. I didn't blink. I stared for fifteen minutes.

"Time's up," I said.

"That was amazing," he said. "I felt... perceived."

He left. $50.00 in the jar.

A woman came in. She wanted the package with the selfie.

"That's extra," I said. "$100.00."

She paid. We took the picture. I didn't smile. She smiled too much.

"Can I put my arm around you?" she asked.

"No," I said. "You'll wrinkle the suit."

I took the photo. *Flash*. It blinded me for a second.

Lunch was a slice of pizza. $5.00. It was cold. I like cold pizza. It tastes like leftovers, and leftovers are free in your mind.

In the afternoon, the phone rang. It was Bob.

"I have a deal," Bob said. "Bezos wants the moon. He wants to turn it into a server farm."

"It's not for sale," I said. "The cat needs it."

"He'll give you Amazon Prime for life," Bob said. "And a rocket."

"I don't want a rocket," I said. "I have a cab."

"Hold out for a blue checkmark," Sammy whispered. "Those are valuable again."

"I want a blue checkmark," I told Bob.

"You can buy those for $8.00," Bob said.

"Not mine," I said. "I want the one that says I'm Verified Forever."

"I'll see what I can do," Bob said. *Click*.

I went back to work. I sat in the chair. I watched the empty room. A "Simulacrum" of Bianca Jagger walked in. She wasn't a customer. She was on strike, but she was bored.

"Can I sit?" she asked.

"It's $50.00," I said.

"I'm a muse," she said. "You don't charge muses."

"I charge everyone," I said. "Business is business."

She laughed. A little silver sound. She sat on the table. She didn't pay.

"I hear you're selling time," Bianca said.

"I'm selling attention," I said. "It's the rarest thing in the world."

"It is," she said. "Look at us. We're fading."

She looked translucent. I could see the wall through her shoulder.

"You need a recharge," I said.

"I need a drink," she said.

We went to the bar down the street. It’s called "The Error." All the glasses are cracked. It’s the style.

I bought her a digital martini. $12.00. It was just an image of an olive on a screen.

"Cheers," she said.

"Cheers," I said.

She vanished at 5:00 PM. *Zap*. Just a sparkle where her hand used to be.

Cabbed home at 7:30 PM. $38.00. The driver was a nobody. Just a guy. It was refreshing. He didn't say a word. He just drove. We stopped at a red light. It stayed red for a long time. *Red-red-red*.

"Is it broken?" I asked.

"It's thinking," the driver said.

We waited. I counted the money in my pocket. $240.00. A good day. Better than painting.

Got home. The apartment was still blue. The cat was asleep on the moon rock. He looked like a sphinx. A hairy sphinx.

I took the wig out of the box. The fiber optic wig from Bob. I put it on. It lit up the room. *Blip-blip*. I looked in the mirror. I looked like a spaceship.

"Hello, Andy," I said.

"Hello, Andy," I said back.
