Saturday, March 21, 2026

Woke up at 6:00 AM. I didn't want to. The watch on my wrist was screaming. The hands were spinning so fast they were drilling into my skin. *Whirrrrr*. I took it off and threw it in the trash can. It landed on top of the peel of the banana-phone from last night. The trash can is getting full of time.

I felt flat. Like a piece of paper. I tried to stand up, but I just sort of... drifted. I had to pull myself to the bathroom using the doorknob. The doorknob cost $80.00. Very solid.

In the mirror, I looked 2D. Literally. I had no depth. I was just a line drawing of a face. The wig was a squiggle. The glasses were two circles.

"Oh no," I said. But I didn't make a sound. A speech bubble appeared above my head. *OH NO*.

I needed volume. I needed mass.

I went to the kitchen. I opened the fridge. I took out a bottle of "Campbell’s Tomato Juice Heavy." It promises to add weight to your atoms. I drank the whole bottle. $12.00. It tasted like lead pencils.

I felt heavier instantly. My feet hit the floor with a *thud*. I looked in the mirror again. I was 3D. But I looked blurry. Like a JPEG that was compressed too much.

I put on the wig. It was a struggle. The wig didn't want to sit on a blurry head. I used staples. *Staple-staple*. That worked. It’s industrial.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $45.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Lindsay Lohan. She was driving backwards. Very fast.

"It's the only way to go forward," she yelled.

I held on. The city zoomed by in reverse.

The Factory was a mess. A beautiful mess. There was a line of people stretching around the block. They were all holding empty soup cans.

"What is this?" I asked Sammy. He was standing at the door wearing a velvet rope.

"It's a soup line," Sammy said. "Irony is back. Everyone wants to be poor."

"Do we charge them?"

"Of course," Sammy said. "It's $500.00 to stand in line. $1,000.00 if they want to look hungry."

"That's a lot of money for hunger," I said.

"Hunger is the new luxury," Sammy said. "Being full is so 2024."

I signed a few cans. "To Mimi." "To John." "To Future Me." My hand was cramping. The signature looked like a spider that fell in the ink.

Around 11:00 AM, I went into the back room to hide. The silence was nice. But then I heard a sound. *Munch-munch-munch*.

I looked behind a stack of Brillo boxes. There was a rat. But it was a robotic rat. Its eyes were camera lenses.

It was eating the wall.

"Hey," I said. "That's load-bearing."

The rat looked at me. It zoomed in. *Click-whirr*. It took a picture of me.

"Data," it squeaked.

"More like dinner," I said. I picked it up by the tail. It was cold. It had a USB port in its side. I plugged it into my phone to charge it. Multitasking.

Lunch was at "Serendipity 3." But the tables were all treadmills. You have to walk while you eat. To burn calories.

I ordered the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $25.00. It was delicious. But I spilled it because I can't walk and drink at the same time.

A man was running on the treadmill next to me. It was a "Simulacrum" of Rudolf Nureyev.

"You're leaking," he said. He pointed to my brown stain.

"It's art," I said. "It's a Rorschach test on my pants."

He pirouetted away.

I finished my drink. It gave me a headache. A brain freeze. But my brain is gel, so it just jiggled.

At 2:00 PM, I went to a gallery opening. "The End of History." A show by an AI named "Godzilla_vs_Kong_Bing."

The paintings were generated by algorithms. They looked like my paintings, but... angrier. More noise.

I stood in front of a portrait of Jackie Kennedy. Her eyes were pixelated. Her mouth was a barcode.

"I like the colors," I said to the artist.

The artist was a toaster. It toasted my words onto a piece of bread.

**I LIKE THE COLORS** appeared on the bread.

"I'll take it," I said. "How much?"

"Butter," the toaster said.

I didn't have butter. I had a credit card. I swiped it through the slot. *Beep*.

I walked out eating the art. It tasted like burnt wheat. And ego.

I walked through Central Park. The grass was blue. Synthetic grass. It doesn't need water. It just needs to be looked at.

I sat on a bench. A "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote was there. He was full size today.

"Did you hear?" he asked. "They're cancelling the future."

"Why?" I asked. "Low ratings?"

"Too expensive," he said. "They're rerunning the 1980s instead."

"Again?" I asked. "That was a good season."

"We need to create a finale," Truman said. "A big event. Something spectacular. Something that makes people stop looking and start seeing."

"Like a car crash?" I asked.

"Better," he said. "Like you disappearing."

"I'm good at that," I said. "I do it every day."

"Not that kind," he said. "The real kind. The *poof* kind."

He handed me a balloon. It was silver. It had my face on it.

"Hold this," he said.

I took it. It floated me up a few inches. *Hover*.

"If I let go," I asked. "Where do I go?"

"Up," he said. "Or nowhere. Nowhere is very popular this time of year."

I held the string tight. $0.05 worth of rubber holding me to the ground.

I looked at my watch. I was wearing a new one. I found it in a Cracker Jack box. It doesn't have hands. It just has a smiley face that changes color. Right now it was blue. Blue means "Bored."

"I think I'll go back to sleep," I said.

"Good idea," Truman said. "Dreams are the only reality left."
