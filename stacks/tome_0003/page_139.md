Woke up at 10:00 AM. The sky is plastic today. Shrink-wrapped. There’s a seam running right down the middle, from the Hudson to the East River. Someone took a big heat gun to the city and sealed it tight. The sun is trapped inside a bubble of thermoformed plastic. It’s distorted. Wavy. Like looking through a soda bottle. Or like my glasses after I’ve fallen asleep in them. The air smells like manufacturing. Fresh PVC. $0.00.

Put on the "Transparency" wig. It’s made of Lucite. Hard, clear plastic. I can’t see the hair, but I can see the scalp underneath. It looks like I have no head, just a brain bobbing in a jar. It’s a good look for the immune system. Very sanitary.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:45 AM. $3,100.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Jackie Kennedy. She was wearing a pink suit, but it was made of bubble wrap. *Pop, pop*.

"Going to Dallas?" I asked.

"It's a conspiracy," she said. "But the fare is still five dollars."

She gave me a receipt made of cash register tape. It was three miles long.

When we got to the Factory, the doors were sealed. Clear packing tape. Criss-crossed. I couldn't get in.

"It's preservation," Bob said. His voice was muffled. He was inside, wrapped in plastic. Like a leftover sandwich. "Keep it fresh."

"We need air," I said.

"Air causes oxidation," Bob said. "We're archiving the moment."

At 11:30 AM, I found a seam. I ripped the plastic. *Creeeeak*. It tore like a scab. I climbed inside.

The room was filled with inflated art. Balloon animals. But they were huge. Ten-foot-high poodles and giraffes. They floated against the ceiling. Bumping into the lights.

"It's a birthday party," I said.

"Happy Birthday," the animals said. They were helium-filled. Their voices were high and squeaky.

"Who's birthday?" I asked.

"Yours," they said. "Again."

"I don't want presents," I said. "Just money."

At 12:00 PM, the rat arrived. He was deflated. Flat. A 2D rat. He had to walk sideways. He looked like a drawing.

"I lost my volume," the rat said. "I'm two-dimensional."

"Lay off the helium," I said.

The squirrel was there. He was bouncing. He swallowed a balloon. He floated up to the rafters.

"Gravity is for people who can't fly," the squirrel said.

At 1:15 PM, Jean-Michel Basquiat called. He was on speakerphone. The speakerphone was inside a balloon.

"Andy?" he said. *Muffle, muffle*. "I'm tagging the moon."

"Is it lucrative?" I asked.

"It's very space," he said.

"Bring me back a rock," I said. "A moon rock. I’ll paint it silver."

At 2:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". The tables were covered in plastic tablecloths. Sticky.

"I'll have the 'Frrrozen Hot Chocolate'," I said. "$9,500.00."

It was a block of ice. Solid.

"It's frozen," I said. "I can't drink it."

"You have to wait for it to melt," the waiter said. "It’s about global warming."

"It's taking too long," I said. "I'm thirsty."

Bob took a knife and chipped the ice. *Crack, crack*. We ate the shavings.

"It's crunchy," I said. "Like snow."

"I wish it was money," Bob said.

At 3:30 PM, I worked on the "Abstract" paintings. I bought a drippy machine. An engine that drips paint. I turned it on.

*Whirr. Drip. Splatter*.

It made a mess on the floor.

"It's a masterpiece," Sammy said. "It has movement."

"It's a dirty floor," I said. "Call the cleaning lady."

"The robot is broken," Sammy said. "It choked on a paint can."

At 5:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a stapler. A big industrial stapler.

"We need to tag this," the Man said. "Price it."

He stapled a tag to my ear. *Kachunk*.

"Ouch," I said.

"It says 'Sold'," the Man said. "To the collection in Zurich."

"Do I get a cut?" I asked.

"You get the frame," he said.

At 6:30 PM, we went to a gallery opening. It was for "The Last Painting Ever". Everyone was there.

The painting was just a mirror. A giant mirror.

"It's a self-portrait of the viewer," the artist said.

"I hate it," I said. "I look terrible."

"You look honest," the artist said.

"I look old," I said. "I need filters."

At 8:00 PM, the plastic wrapping on the sky started to shrink. The sun was getting squeezed. It turned red. Then purple.

"It's vacuum packed," I said.

"I can't breathe," Jean-Michel said.

"It's just art," I said.

At 10:45 PM, I went home. The townhouse was wrapped in cellophane. It crinkled when I touched it. *Crinkle, crinkle*.

I went inside. The plastic wrap was everywhere. On the furniture. On the cat.

I opened the fridge. The light was wrapped in plastic.

I took out a Campbell’s Soup. "Chicken Noodle".

I tried to open the can. But the lid was plastic. It was welded shut.

"It's display only," I thought.

I looked at the label. The soup looked delicious.

"I'll just look at it," I thought. "Looking is free. Eating costs calories."

I stared at the can for an hour.

My stomach growled. *Grrr*.

"Shhh," I said. "You're ruining the composition."

I went upstairs. I lay on the bed. The sheets were plastic. Hot and sticky.

I felt like a piece of cheese. Or a collectible doll.

"Mint in box," I thought.

I closed my eyes. But even my eyelids felt wrapped.

I don't know.

So.
