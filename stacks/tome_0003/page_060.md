# Wednesday, April 01, 2026

> Written at 2026-04-01 21:45:12

Woke up at 7:00 AM. I thought I was dead. I really did. I waited for the white light. But it was just the cat. Archie is glowing so bright he looks like a fluorescent tube. *Buzz-buzz*. I had to wear sunglasses to make coffee. The coffee was black. At least I think it was. With the sunglasses on, everything looked like a movie from the 50s. Technicolor.

Checked the mirror. I was back. Solid. I pinched my cheek. *Ouch*. It left a red mark. I have skin again. The subscription must have gone through. The bank called while I was brushing my teeth.

"Payment received," the machine said. "You are current until 2030. Would you like to renew the 'Celebrity' package? It includes better lighting and automatic hair."

"No," I said. "Just the basic. I like the shadows."

"It's $500 extra," the machine said.

"No," I said.

" charged anyway," the machine said. *Beep*.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $35.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Liza Minelli. She was singing while she drove. Very loud. The windows were rattling.

"Start spreading the news," she sang. "I'm leaving today."

"I'm not leaving," I said. "I'm arriving."

"That's show business," Liza said. "You always arrive when you leave."

The Factory was chaotic. Or maybe organized. I couldn't tell the difference. Sammy was there. He looked solid again. He was wearing a tie made of DNA. It was squirming.

"April Fool's," Sammy said. "The cat didn't actually eat the moon."

"It felt real," I said.

"That's the best joke," Sammy said. "The reality."

He handed me a newspaper. The *New York Times*. The headline was: "ANDY WARHOL BUYS MOON. CAT CONFUSED."

"I didn't buy it," I said.

"You paid for the rock," Sammy said. "Possession is nine-tenths of the law. You own the moon."

"Do I have to pay taxes on it?"

"Space tax," Sammy said. "It's astronomical."

I went to the back room. The floor was sticky. The "Indoor Rain" had turned to glue. Everything was sticking to everything. A Brillo box was stuck to my shoe. *Clunk*. I walked like I was limping.

I started a new painting. It was a portrait of the glue. Just a big, sticky square. I called it "Social Adhesion."

"It captures the moment," Sammy said.

"It's messy," I said.

"It's America," Sammy said.

Lunch was at The Oyster Bar. I took the subway. $2.90. The subway car was empty except for a robot janitor. It was mopping the floor. It mopped over my shoes. *Squeeeak*. Now my shoes are clean. And sticky.

I met Jean-Michel. Or a "Simulacrum" of Jean-Michel. He was wearing a crown made of circuit boards.

"Happy April Fool's, Andy," Jean-Michel said. "They canceled the future."

"When?" I asked.

"Next Tuesday," he said. "We're going back to 1985. I'm bringing the boomboxes."

"I like 1985," I said. "The money was greener."

We ordered the pan roast. $24.00. It came in a bowl shaped like a satellite dish.

"Do you think the cat is okay?" I asked.

"The cat is a god now," Jean-Michel said. "Cats were always gods. Now they just have the lighting to match."

He paid the bill. He used a credit card made of silver. He melted it to pay. *Sizzle*. The waiter was impressed.

After lunch, I went to a gallery opening. It was for an artist named "The Algorithm." The painting was just a printer spewing out paper. *Whirrrr-clank*. The paper piled up on the floor.

"It's a commentary on volume," the gallerist said.

"It's a mess," I said.

"It's valuable," she said. "Each sheet is $10,000."

"How many for the whole pile?"

"You can't afford the pile," she said. "Only Bezos can afford the pile."

I bought one sheet. $10,000. I signed it "Andy Warhol." Now it's worth $20,000. I flipped it. I sold it to a tourist in the lobby.

"April Fool's," I said.

"I don't get it," the tourist said.

"That's the art," I said.

Cabbed home at 6:00 PM. $40.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of me. The fourth time.

"Rough day?" he asked.

"I bought the moon," I said.

"Expensive," he said.

"I got a deal," I said.

I walked into the apartment. It was dark. Very dark. The cat was in the corner. He wasn't glowing anymore.

"Archie?"

He meowed. A regular meow. *Meow*. He looked like a cat. Just a small, black cat. The rock was gone. The glow was gone. He jumped on the couch. He purred. It was warm. Not radioactive. Just warm.

I felt sad. The magic was gone. Or maybe it was just hidden.

"I liked the light," I said.

He curled up into a ball. He went to sleep.

I turned on the TV. The news was on.

"Reports of a giant space cat descending over New Jersey," the anchor said. "Scientists are baffled."

I looked at the cat on the couch. He looked innocent.

"You're projecting," I said.

I opened a can of tuna. $1.50. I put it on a plate. He ate it. *Crunch-crunch*.

I sat down to write. The pen worked. Blue ink. My favorite.

I looked at the calendar. April 1. The joke is that it's all real.

"I'm tired," I thought.

I closed the diary. *Thud*.

The cat sneezed. *Achoo*.

A small piece of moon rock fell out of his nose.

"Keep it," I said.

I turned off the light.

"Goodnight, moon."

I slept.
