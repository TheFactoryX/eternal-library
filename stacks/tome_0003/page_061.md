# Thursday, April 02, 2026

> Written at 2026-04-02 22:10:33

Woke up at 8:15 AM. The alarm didn't go off. Or maybe it went off and I just didn't hear it because the silence was so loud. The cat was sleeping on my face. Heavy. Very heavy. He felt like a bag of flour. Or gold. I pushed him off. *Thud*. He didn't wake up. He just purred in his sleep. A diesel engine.

I walked to the window. The sky was grey. Flat. Like a canvas that hasn’t been primed. I looked for the moon. I couldn't see it. Maybe the cat really did eat it. If he did, I hope he chews it better than the tuna. I found a piece of the moon rock in his sneeze yesterday. I put it in ajar on the shelf next to the wig. It’s vibrating. *Bzzzt*. It’s nice to have a conversation piece that actually speaks back, even if it’s just buzzing.

I had coffee. Instant. $0.50. It tasted like brown water. I miss the good stuff. I miss the Future. But the Future is expensive and I’m saving up for the new "Celebrity" package the bank mentioned. I want better lighting. I want to look good for the satellites.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $42.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Marilyn Monroe. She was crying.

"What's wrong?" I asked.

"I'm timeless," she said. "But I'm running late. Timelessness takes so much time."

I tipped her extra. $5.00. "Don't cry," I said. "It ruins the makeup."

"Oh honey," she said. "The tears are part of the look."

The Factory was dry. The indoor rain was gone. The glue was gone. It was just a big, empty room with silver walls. It looked like the inside of a thermos. I felt cold. Like a soup that’s been left out.

Sammy was on the phone. He was arguing with a computer.

"I don't want a refund," Sammy said. "I want a refund of the refund."

He hung up.

"The Simulacrums are on strike," Sammy said. "They want residuals. Every time someone thinks about them, they want a quarter."

"That's a lot of thinking," I said.

"We're going to go broke just thinking about them," Sammy said.

I went to the back. I decided to work on the "Disaster" paintings. But disasters are hard to find now. Everything is a disaster, so nothing is. It’s all just... weather.

I painted a car crash. $300 for the paint. It looked pretty. Like a rainbow.

"It needs more red," Sammy said.

"Red is expensive," I said. "I'll use pink."

"Pink is the new red," Sammy said.

Lunch was at the Carnegie Deli. Or what used to be the Carnegie Deli. It’s now a "Carnegie Simulation." You don't eat the pastrami. You just look at it through a glass case. It’s cheaper.

I paid the cover charge. $15.00.

I looked at the sandwich. It was huge. Six inches of corned beef.

"I remember when I could eat this," I told the waiter. He was a hologram. He flickered.

"I remember when I was real," the waiter said. "April Fool's."

"I thought that was yesterday," I said.

"Every day is a fool's day in the service industry," he said.

I didn't eat. I just looked. It was delicious. Visual calories are the best kind. They don't make you fat. They just make you hungry for things that aren't there.

I bought a postcard. $1.00. It was a picture of the Factory before it was silver. When it was just a factory. I wrote "Wish you were here" and mailed it to myself. When it arrives, it will be a nice surprise. "Look! I was here!"

Back at the Factory, a package arrived. It was from Bob.

"Open it," Sammy said. "It might be a bomb."

"It's probably a Buy Now button," I said.

I opened it. It was a wig. A blonde wig. But it was made of fiber optic cables. It lit up. *Red-Green-Blue*.

"He wants me to be a TV," I said.

"You're already a TV," Sammy said. "This is just 4K."

I put the wig on the mannequin. It looked like me, but smarter.

"I'll save it," I said.

Cabbed home at 7:00 PM. $50.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a taxi driver from 1975. He had an accent and he didn't know where he was going. We went in circles. *Round-and-round*. It felt like the past. I liked it. We passed the same street corner three times. It looked exactly the same every time. Repetition.

"Keep the change," I said when we finally got there.

"There is no change," he said. "Only repetition."

"Exactly," I said.

Went upstairs. The cat was on the couch. He looked normal. He was licking his paw.

I looked at the jar on the shelf. The piece of moon rock had stopped vibrating. It was dark. Grey.

I turned on the TV. There was a documentary about the 21st century.

"They loved their screens," the narrator said. "They loved them more than people."

I looked at the cat. I looked at the jar. I looked at the wig.

"I love my screens too," I thought.

I opened the diary. My hand felt solid. The pen scratched the paper. *Scratch-scratch*.

It was a quiet day. A boring day. Boring is good. Boring means you're safe.

I wrote the date. April 2.

I checked my bank account. The balance was zero.

"Tax day," the screen flashed. "Paid in full."

So I'm broke again. But I have the moon rock.

I closed the book. *Thud*.

The cat stretched. He looked like a question mark.

"Meow," he said.

"Echo," I said.
