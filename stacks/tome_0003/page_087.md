Woke up at 7:30 AM. The sky was back to being blue. Or at least, it was blue when I first looked. But when I blinked, it turned a sort of rusty orange. Like the inside of a tomato soup can that’s been left open too long. I decided to keep the blinds closed. Reality is so changeable these days. It’s better to just look at the wall. The wall is always off-white.

My feet hurt from climbing the stairs at the Chelsea last night. I looked down at them. My left foot was blurry. It had a low-pixel count.

"I need an upgrade," I thought.

Archie was sitting on my chest. He stared at me. His eyes were dial-up modems. *Eee-ooo-ring*.

"Get off," I said. "You're heavy."

He jumped off, leaving little paw prints on the blanket. But the paw prints were just text. They read: *FEED ME*. *FEED ME*. *FEED ME*.

Breakfast was a "Vintage Egg". $400.00. It was an egg from 1982. It was preserved in amber. I had to crack the amber with a hammer. The egg inside was gray. It tasted like history and cholesterol. It was delicious.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $80.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a taxi driver from 1975. He had a mustache and a heavy accent.

"Where to, mac?" he asked.

"The Factory," I said. "But drive around the block first. I want to see if the city is still there."

We drove. The buildings looked like they were breathing. Expanding. Contracting.

"Look at that," the driver said. "Real estate is alive."

We got to the Factory. The doors were gone. Just a hole where the doors used to be.

"Guess we're open," I said.

I walked in. The floor was covered in shoes. Thousands of shoes. Loafers. Boots. High heels. They were all walking in place. *March-march-march*.

Sammy was sitting on a ladder, counting them.

"Is this an installation?" I asked.

"It's a distraction," Sammy said. "The Bureau is auditing us today. We need to look productive."

Bob was in the corner, trying to be solid. He was holding a magnet.

"Is that working?" I asked.

"I feel heavier," Bob said. "But I might just be remembering gravity."

I went to the back room. I took the jar of "Time" I bought yesterday. I opened it. I sprinkled a little on my canvas. A big, white canvas. *Poof*.

The paint dried instantly. It aged beautifully. Cracks appeared. It looked like a masterpiece from the Renaissance.

"Antiques are so easy now," I thought.

At 11:00 AM, the Bureau agents arrived. They were wearing suits made of mirrors. I couldn't look at them directly. I just saw myself over and over.

"Andrew Warhol?" asked the main agent. His voice sounded like static.

"That's me," I said.

"We received a complaint," he said. "You are exceeding your bandwidth. You are too famous."

"I can't help it," I said. "Fame sticks."

"You need to delete some memories," he said. "To make room for the current users."

He held up a syringe. It was labeled "FORGETFULNESS".

"I like my memories," I said. "Even the bad ones. Especially the bad ones. They make good stories."

"Compliance is mandatory," he said.

Just then, a woman walked in. It was Bianca. Or a scan of her. She looked fabulous.

"Andy!" she screamed. She ran over and kissed me on the cheek. Her lipstick was neon pink. $1,000.00 an ounce.

"Don't delete him," she told the agent. "I'm wearing him to the Met Gala."

The agent paused. He recalculated. "Bianca Jagger. A-list. Priority access."

He put the syringe away. "Carry on, Mr. Warhol. But keep your resolution low."

They left.

"Thanks, Bianca," I said.

"Don't mention it, darling," she said. "By the way, I love the shoes. Very 'Grounded'."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". It’s exactly the same as it was in the 60s. The waitresses are rude. The menu is sticky.

I ordered a Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $50.00.

It tasted like sugar and ice.

"I can't feel my toes," I said.

"It’s the freeze ray," Sammy said. "They keep the kitchen at absolute zero."

I looked out the window. A cloud was shaped like a giant shoe. It was hovering over the Empire State Building.

"I hope it doesn't rain," I said. "It might storm shoelaces."

At 3:00 PM, I did a portrait. Of a girl named "XØA-9". She’s a pop star from the moons of Saturn. She has three eyes, but she asked me to only paint two.

"I want to look accessible," she said.

"Sure," I said.

I silk-screened her face. It was green and slimy. It looked great.

"It’s very human," she said. "Does it come with a soul?"

"Souls are extra," I said. "$500.00."

"I'll take two," she said.

She wrote me a check. It bounced before it hit the table. The check turned into a butterfly and flew away.

"I'll send the crypto later," she said.

At 6:00 PM, I came home.

I went to the bathroom. I washed my face. The soap was made of champagne bubbles. $75.00.

I looked in the mirror. My reflection was looking at a phone.

"Hey," I said. "Pay attention."

The reflection looked up. "Sorry," it said. "I was doomscrolling."

"Me too," I thought.

Archie was eating his tuna. He had opened another five cans. There was a mountain of silver fish on the floor.

"You're going to get sick," I said.

He ignored me. He purred. It sounded like a dial-tone. *Beeeeeeeeep*.

I put on my pajamas. They were silk. $200.00. Very slippery.

I got into bed. The jar of Time was on the nightstand. It was glowing.

I took a pinch. I rubbed it on my eyes.

"Goodnight, New York," I said.

The city lights flickered. The sky turned off.

"Goodnight, Campbell's."

And for a second, just a second, I felt real.
