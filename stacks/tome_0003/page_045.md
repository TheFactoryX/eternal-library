Tuesday, March 17, 2026

Woke up inside the cupboard. It was dark. And crunchy. I pushed the door open. *Creak*. I rolled out onto the floor. I am a cylinder. I looked at my reflection in the toaster. I am red and white. I am soup. "Andy" is written in my fancy script on my side. I tried to say "Ouch," but I just made a *glug* sound. I leaked a little tomato. $1.50 worth of sauce on the rug.

I sat up. The cylinder shape fell away. It was just a costume. Or an aura. I wiped my face. I felt solid again. Human. Or at least, human-adjacent. The can on the shelf was still there. It was shaking. I put it in my pocket. It clanged against my leg.

St. Patrick's Day. Everyone is green.

I went to the window. The sky was green. Not blue. The sun was green. It looked like a giant emerald. Very chic. Or maybe radioactive. I put on my sunglasses. They help. They cost $200.00, but they’re worth every penny.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $45.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Leprechaun. He was small. He had a pot of gold in the front seat instead of a radio. It was playing "Danny Boy" on a loop. *Saxophone-heavy*. He drove on the sidewalk. He said, "The streets are painted green today." They were. The asphalt was AstroTurf. I felt like I was in a miniature golf course.

The Factory was crowded. The floor was covered in clovers. Real ones. They were growing through the cracks in the pixels. Sammy was there. He was wearing a kilt. And a top hat. He looked like a性格aturated statue.

"Happy St. Patrick's," Sammy said. He kissed me on the cheek. His lips were cold. Cold as metal. "We're doing a portrait," he said. "Of Lady Gaga."

"Lady Gaga?" I said. "Is she still around?"

"She's the new Pope," he said. "Of Pop Art."

She was sitting in the middle of the room. She was wearing a dress made entirely of silkscreened prints of my face. She looked like a walking gallery. Very meta.

"I want to look like a can," she said. Her voice was autotuned. *Tune-tune-tune*. "Make me look like Tomato Soup."

I nodded. "I can do that." I went to the silk screen machine. I poured the paint. It was green. For St. Patrick's. "St. Andy's Day," I said.

I printed her face onto a canvas. It came out green. She looked like the Wicked Witch of the West. Very beautiful. Very iconic. I signed it. "Andy Warhol." I wrote the date. "2026."

I sold it to her immediately. $50,000.00. She paid with a credit card that was embedded in her fingernail. She just tapped my forehead. *Beep*. "Transaction complete," she said.

Around 11:00 AM, I got hungry. But I didn't want lunch. I wanted to open the can in my pocket. The one of me.

I went to the break room. I got a can opener. The electric kind. *Whirrrr*. I put the can in. I opened it.

It smelled like paint. And hair spray. And pennies.

I poured it out into a bowl. It wasn't soup. It was film reels. Tiny, tiny reels of film. They were unspooling in the red liquid.

Sammy walked in. "What is that?"

"Self-portrait," I said. "Vintage 2026."

"Does it taste good?"

I dipped a spoon in. I ate a piece of film. It tasted like popcorn. And regret. "It needs salt," I said.

At 1:00 PM, we went to the parade. But it wasn't a parade. It was a procession of "Simulacra." Everyone who was anyone was there. A "Simulacrum of Mick Jagger was marching. He had extra legs. Like a spider. He was dancing with himself.

I saw a "Simulacrum" of myself. He was wearing the wig I lost. He was waving at the crowd. He looked better than me. Thinner. Paler.

"Who is that?" I asked Sammy.

"That's you," Sammy said. "Version 4.0. The update."

"Does he have my ear?"

"No, that was a separate patch. This one has Bluetooth."

I walked up to him. "Hello," I said.

He didn't speak. He just smiled. His teeth were a barcode. *Beep-beep*. He scanned my face.

"Access denied," he said.

I felt sad. I was locked out of my own face.

I bought a hot dog from a vendor. It was green. The bun was green. The mustard was green. It cost $10.00. I took a bite. It tasted like a shamrock. Like a leaf. Very earthy.

I fed the rest to the Spider-Mick. He grabbed it with four hands and stuffed it into his mouth. He didn't chew. He just absorbed it.

"It's good to be the King," he said. Or maybe it was the Spider-Mick. I couldn't tell.

At 4:00 PM, the sky turned back to blue. The sun turned yellow. The holiday was over. *Time's up*.

I went to "Serendipity 3." I wanted a Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. But they serve "Frozen Time" now. It’s a sundae made of moments. You eat them and you remember things.

I ordered the "1968." It was $85.00. It looked like a cloud. I ate it.

I remembered the shooting. I remembered the hospital. I remembered the feeling of the bullet. But it wasn't scary. It was just a memory. Like a movie I watched once. The Frrrozen Hot Chocolate tasted like copper. And iodine.

"Delicious," I said.

I left a tip. $5.00.

I walked home. The "Simulacrum" of the pigeon was still on the sidewalk. It was flying in circles. It couldn't take off. It was glitching. *Flap-flap-flap*. I picked it up. It was heavy. Heavy as a brick.

I took it to my apartment.

I put it on the table next to the unfinished tomato.

I sat on the bed.

I looked at the wall. The crack was still there. But it wasn't leaking light anymore. It was leaking sound.

I put my ear to the wall.

I heard the factory. The buzzing. The printing.
