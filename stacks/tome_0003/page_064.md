Woke up at 10:00 AM. Late for me. Usually, the light hurts, but today the light was blue. The whole apartment was blue. The moon rock in the jar on the shelf has turned the room into an underwater aquarium. I felt like I should be swimming to the bathroom. *Swish-swish*. The cat was floating by the window. Just an inch off the floor. He looked comfortable. He’s evolving. I think he’s turning into a balloon. I tried to grab him, but my hands are full of static electricity. *Zap*.

I looked in the mirror. I was still wearing the fiber optic wig. It was dim now. A dull grey. The batteries died while I was sleeping. Or maybe I just dreamt in black and white. I took it off. My hair was flat. Pressed down like a dried flower.

I ordered coffee from the drone. It dropped it through the mail slot. *Thud*. $4.00. It was cold. It’s always cold now. I drank it anyway. It tasted like battery acid and money.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:30 AM. $38.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a young Bob Dylan. He was playing the guitar.

"The times they are a-changin'," he sang.

"Not fast enough," I said. "I'm still paying."

The Factory was busy. Not with people, but with *Things*. Sammy had hired "Atmosphere Actors." They stand in the corner and project mood.

"I got a deal on 'Nostalgia'," Sammy said. "It's cheap because nobody wants to remember yesterday."

The room smelled like silver spray paint and burnt hair. It smelled like success.

"I have a meeting," I said. "With the Vice President."

"Of what?"

"Of Vice."

I sat in the red chair. I was ready for the "Authentic Andy Warhol Experience." But today, I was the customer.

A man walked in. He was wearing a suit made of mirrors. He reflected everything. I saw myself in his knees. I looked short.

"Mr. Warhol," he said. "I represent the new currency."

"I like the old currency," I said. "The green paper."

"It's obsolete," he said. "We're moving to 'Moments'. You are rich in Moments."

"Can I buy a hamburger with a Moment?"

"Not a hamburger," he said. "But you can buy the *memory* of a hamburger. It's more filling."

He handed me a coin. It was warm. It was beating. *Thump-thump*. Like a heart.

"Don't drop it," he said. "If it stops, you're broke."

I put it in my pocket next to the moon rock. My pocket felt heavy. It felt like a uterus.

"We want to franchise you," Mirror Man said. "We want to put an Andy Warhol in every living room. A small one. A pet one."

"I'm not a pet," I said. "I'm a person. Mostly."

"People are hard to maintain," he said. "People need to pee. The Andy Warhol Automaton runs on electricity and gossip."

"I'll think about it," I said. "Do I get a royalty?"

"You get a percentage of the attention."

That sounded good. Attention is the best currency. You can spend it anywhere.

He left. *Jingle-jingle*. I watched him go. I felt like I sold something, but I didn't know what.

Lunch was a peanut butter and banana sandwich. $250.00. I bought it from a street vendor who claimed he was Elvis Presley’s avatar.

"Is it good?" I asked.

"It's uh-huh-huh," he said.

I ate it. It tasted like peanut butter and loneliness.

In the afternoon, I worked on the "Disaster" paintings. The Simulacrums were still striking, but they were picketing outside. They were holding signs: "OCCUPY WALL STREET... IN 3D."

I painted a car crash. I used the moon dust I scraped off the cat. It sparkled. It glowed.

"It needs more blood," Sammy said.

"Blood is messy," I said. "I'll use red nail polish. It's cheaper and it dries faster."

I painted a big, red smear across the canvas. It looked like a lipsticked mouth.

"It's beautiful," Sammy said. "It's so violent."

"It's just fashion," I said.

At 5:00 PM, the "Atmosphere Actor" in the corner glitched. He was supposed to be "Melancholy," but he slipped into "Existential Dread." The room got very dark. The windows rattled.

"Turn it down," I shouted. "I can't work in this much depth."

Sammy hit him with a broom. *Bonk*. The Actor went back to being mildly sad.

A package arrived. It was from Taylor. The Simulacrum I met yesterday.

I opened it. It was a wig. But it wasn't a hair wig. It was a wig made of fur. Cat fur.

"She shed for you," Sammy said.

"That's disgusting," I said. "I love it."

I put it on. It was soft. It tickled. I looked like a lion. A very tired lion.

I decided to go to the movies. I wanted to see something real. Or at least something that pretended to be real.

Cabbed to the Ziegfeld at 7:00 PM. $50.00. The theater was empty. I was the only one there.

"I bought out the house," I told the usher. "I want privacy."

"It's just you, Mr. Warhol," the usher said. "Nobody else wants to see this movie."

The movie was called "The Last Party on Earth." It was a documentary about 1985. Everyone looked so young. Everyone looked so alive.

I ate popcorn. $12.00. It was pink. Strawberry flavor. It tasted like cotton candy and chemicals.

I watched the screen. I saw myself in the background. I was holding a camera. I was watching them.

"I was there," I thought. "And I'm here. It's the same thing."

Walking home. It was raining. Digital rain. *Pixel-pixel-pixel*. The drops didn't wet the pavement. They just illuminated it. The gutters glowed.

I walked past a man sleeping on a grate. He had a sign: "Need Cash for Upgrades."

I gave him the coin the Mirror Man gave me. The beating heart coin.

"Here," I said. "Buy a Moment."

He took it. He held it to his ear. He smiled.

"It sounds like the ocean," he said.

"That's not the ocean," I said. "That's business."
