Woke up at 7:00 AM. The sky was black. The silver wrapper had completely fallen off. Underneath was just raw, black space. No stars. Just the void. It looked like a roll of film that had been exposed to the light. Ruined. Or maybe it was just undeveloped. I felt like a negative. I felt inverted. The whites of my eyes were black. I checked the mirror. I looked like a silhouette. A cut-out. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:15 AM. $200.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was tiny, barely reaching the pedals. He was wearing a scarf made of typing paper.

"It's drafty in the cosmos," Truman said. "And the stories are all true."

"Which stories?" I asked.

"The ones about you," he said. "And me. And the electric chairs."

When we got to 460 Lexington, the building was gone. Just a hole. A rectangular hole in the black space. The elevator was still there, though. It was hanging in mid-air. The cables were going up into nothing.

I pressed the button. The doors opened. I stepped in. It didn't go up. It went sideways. *Whoosh*.

When the doors opened, the Factory was there. But it was flat. 2-D. I was looking at a drawing of the Factory. A crayon drawing.

"I don't want to walk on the paper," I said. "I'll scuff it."

"Step inside," a voice said. It was coming from a sofa.

I stepped *into* the drawing. It popped out like 3-D glasses. Suddenly it was real. The couch was red. The floor was silver.

Sammy was there. He was erasing the walls.

"I'm curating the space," Sammy said. *Swish, swish*. "I'm making room for the new inventory."

"What inventory?" I asked.

"The past," Sammy said. "It's all coming back. It never left. It's just piled up in the corner."

Bob was there. He was a video camera again. A big, clunky, black camera with a lens the size of a dinner plate.

"I'm recording the erasure," Bob said. The red light was on. *Beep*.

Jean-Michel was sitting on the floor. He was glowing. Not gold, but neon. He was a bright, electric blue.

"I'm radioactive," Jean-Michel said. "I'm glowing with ideas."

"Don't touch the uranium," I said.

"It's not uranium," he said. "It's fame. It's hot."

At 10:30 AM, the squirrel walked in. He was carrying a nut. But it wasn't a nut. It was a kidney stone. Or maybe a pearl.

"I found it in the void," the squirrel said. "It's an idea."

"Put it in a jar," I said. "We'll sell it."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar" again. But the Oyster Bar was gone. In its place was a vending machine. A giant vending machine in Grand Central.

"I'll take the Number 4," I said. I put a coin in the slot. A can of soup came out. It was "Campbell's Mushroom Soup". $300.00.

"It's comfort food," I said. I opened the can. It was full of ink. Black ink.

"It's printmaking soup," Jean-Michel said. "Drink it."

I drank a little bit. It tasted like oil and creativity. "It's delicious," I said. "But it needs salt."

At 3:00 PM, a woman arrived. She was wearing a mink coat. But it wasn't mink. It was rats. Dozens of live rats, sewn together.

"It's very warm," the woman said. She had a face like a cat. A Siamese cat.

"Hello, Candy," I said. It was Candy Darling. But she was digital. She was glitching. *Fzzzt*. Her face flickered.

"I'm buffering," Candy said. "I have so much to say, but the bandwidth is low."

"Take your time," I said. "We have forever."

"I don't like forever," Candy said. "It's too long. I prefer 15 minutes."

At 5:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit walked through the wall. He was holding a shopping bag.

"I went shopping," the Man said. "I bought the sky."

"Did you buy the black?" I asked.

"Yes," the Man said. "It was on sale. Half off. Because it's empty."

"Good deal," I said. "Put it in the closet with the other dark matter."

At 7:00 PM, I decided to work. I took a piece of paper. I drew a line. A simple, black line.

"It's a horizon," I said.

"It's a finish line," Bob said.

I made 50 of them. *Swoosh, swoosh*. My hand hurt. The charcoal was dusty.

"I'm polluting," I said. "I'm making a mess."

"It's a beautiful mess," Jean-Michel said. He was tracing my lines with his neon finger.

At 9:00 PM, we ordered dinner. "Blind Boxes". $1,500.00.

The delivery was a mystery. A series of unmarked boxes.

I opened the first one. It contained a wig. A silver wig. Just like mine.

"I already have one of these," I said.

I opened the second box. It contained a single shoe. A pump. Black leather.

"Someone lost a shoe," I said.

I opened the third box. It contained a tape recorder. It was playing.

*Click*. "Dear Andy, this is your mother. I love you. You forgot to call."

I turned it off. *Click*.

"Gee," I said. "I thought I was the recording."

We sat in the dark. The black sky outside was pressing against the glass. It felt heavy. Like a blanket.

"I'm tired," I said.

"Sleep," Bob said. "I'll watch the darkness."

I lay down on the couch. The rats on Candy's coat were squeaking. *Peep, peep*. It sounded like birds. Or maybe the crickets were back.

I closed my eyes. I could see the silver. Behind my eyelids, it was all silver.

"I'm still in the can," I thought. "I'm still soup."

And then I woke up. But I didn't know where I was.

So I just went back to sleep. $0.00.
