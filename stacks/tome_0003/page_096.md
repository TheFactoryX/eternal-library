# Thursday, May 07, 2026

> Written at 2026-05-07 23:59:59

Woke up at 6:00 AM. The sky was blank. Not white, not black, just *censored*. Like a blurry bar on a television screen. The Big Yolk was gone, the Plaid was gone, the Pink was gone. Now it was just a void. It was very relaxing. Like being inside a coffin before the funeral starts.

I felt great. The "joy" update was working. I hummed a song. I didn't know the song, but it had a good beat. *Dum-da-dum-dum*.

Archie was floating. Not walking, floating. About three inches off the floor.

"I'm weightless," Archie said. "I must have eaten the Nothing."

"I ate the Nothing too," I said. "And I feel heavy. But a good heavy. Like a gold bar."

I put on my wig. It felt tight. Like my head was shrinking.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $300.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Nervous Breakdown. He kept shaking and checking the mirrors.

"Are they following me?" he asked.

"Who?" I asked.

"Me," he said.

He drove on the sidewalk. It was faster.

The Factory was crowded. Not with people, but with "History". The walls were lined with faces. Marilyn, Elvis, Jackie. They were moving. They were talking.

Marilyn looked at me. "Andy," she said. "I'm bored."

"Buy a dress," I said.

"I can't," she said. "I'm flat."

She was flat. She was printed on canvas.

"It's tough being a legend," I said.

I walked to the back. Sammy was there. He was wearing the "Branded Nothing" boxes on his feet. Like clown shoes.

"I'm selling the floor," Sammy said. "But not to Mr. Glass. I'm selling it to the Past."

"How much?" I asked.

"Memories," Sammy said. "I'm taking bids."

Bob rolled in. He was still a chrome sphere, but now he had a hat. A little red beret glued to the top.

"I joined the Resistance," Bob said. "The Anti-Automation Resistance."

"You're a robot," I said.

"I'm a self-hating robot," Bob said. "It's very avant-garde."

At 10:30 AM, I started the new portrait series. "The Void Faces." I took photographs of the censored sky. Then I silk-screened them onto canvas. I made the censorship bars pink and green. They looked like candy bars. "Payday" and "Zero".

I made twenty of them. Very fast. The joy made me efficient. I didn't think. I just did.

"This is art?" Sammy asked.

"It's product," I said. "Product doesn't have to mean anything. It just has to cost a lot."

I priced them at $50,000.00 each.

"Sold," Sammy said. "To the man in the mirror."

I looked in the mirror. I was the man in the mirror.

"I'll buy them all," I said. "I'm my own best customer."

At 12:00 PM, a taxi drove *into* the studio. Through the wall. *Crash*. Bricks fell down. Dust went everywhere. It was very exciting.

The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Time. He was wearing a robe made of clocks.

"Is this 2026?" Time asked.

"Yes," I said.

"Make up your mind," Time said. "You're late."

"Late for what?"

"For the future."

He drove out the other wall. *Crash*.

"Now we have a view," I said. The hole showed the street. The street was filled with people holding umbrellas. The umbrellas were open, but it wasn't raining. They were hiding from the sky.

At 1:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Automatic" again.

I ordered the "Motherboard of Fried Chicken". $85.00. It was circuit boards fried in grease. Very crunchy. It tasted like electricity.

Sammy ordered the "Cloud Nine". It was cotton candy. He ate it and floated up to the ceiling.

"I'm stuck," he said.

"Stay there," I said. "It's atmospheric."

Bob rolled under the table. "The Anti-Automation Resistance meets at midnight," he whispered. "We're going to unplug the toaster."

"Be careful," I said. "The toaster is sensitive."

At 3:00 PM, I got a call on my wrist. It was Truman. Or a recording of Truman.

"Andy," the voice said. "I'm in the wall. I'm the mortar."

"Are you happy?" I asked.

"I'm gritty," he said. "I hold things together."

"Good job," I said.

At 4:30 PM, we went to Central Park. The sheep were gone. In their place were clouds. White, fluffy clouds on the ground. Like unbaked cakes.

We sat on a cloud. It was damp.

"I miss the sheep," I said.

"Sheep are just clouds with legs," Sammy said. He was still floating, tied to my wrist with a string like a balloon.

A woman walked by. She was walking a dog. The dog was a robotic vacuum cleaner. It was barking *Swoosh-swoosh*.

"Bad Roomba," the woman said. "No barking."

At 6:00 PM, I hailed a cab. $500.00. The price goes up when the sky is censored.

The driver was a "Simulacrum" of my Mother. Julia Warhola.

"Andrew," she said. "You look thin. Eat more soup."

"I'm fine, Mom," I said.

"You need a wife," she said. "Or a husband. Just someone to sign the checks."

"I have checks," I said.

"Sign them," she said. "Make them real."

She drove me home. She refused the tip. "Put it in the bank," she said.

At home, Archie was still floating. He was bumping against the ceiling.

"I can't get down," he said.

"You don't have to come down," I said. "Gravity is just a suggestion."

I opened the safe. The note said "GONE FISHING." But underneath, in new ink, it said "BACK LATER."

I looked at the "Wallet" paintings. They looked lonely.

"I should paint a purse," I thought. "To hold the wallets."

I turned on the television. It was static. But the static formed shapes. Faces. My face. Your face. Everyone's face all at once. It was beautiful. It was a party. And I was invited.

So I watched. I watched until the static turned into dreams.
