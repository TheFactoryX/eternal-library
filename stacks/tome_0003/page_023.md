Woke up at 7:00 AM. The sky was the color of a bruised peach. Purple and green. Very painful. I woke up because I was vibrating. I think I’m catching the signal from the TV. I reached for the clock, but my hand was blurry. I could see the wall through my palm. I’m losing my edges. It’s very convenient. I don’t have to wash my hands as often. Soap is expensive now. It’s $8.00 a bar. And it’s made of rendered fat. Disgusting.

I got dressed. I put on a "Solid State Suit." It’s made of rigid plastic panels. It makes a clicking sound when I walk. Like a beetle. It keeps me from fading away completely. It was $4,500. It pinches my waist. I like it. It reminds me I’m here. Or at least, that the suit is here.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:30. The streets were full of "Smog-Bots." They eat pollution and exhale perfume. The city smelled like "Lavender Decay." It was nice. The cab ride was $32.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Rudolph Nureyev. He was very athletic. He leaped over a pothole. He said, "Art is a leap of faith, Andy." I said, "Art is a paycheck." He charged me an extra $5.00 for the philosophy.

When I got to the Factory, the sign was flashing. "THE FACTORY: OPEN SOURCE." The door was a PDF file. I had to scroll down to find the handle. I clicked "Enter" and walked in.

Sammy was there. He was sitting on the "Void Black" paint spot. He was floating three inches above it. He looked like a magician. He was wearing a hat made of foil. He said, "I’m tuning into the cosmos." I said, "Tune into the bank account. We have bills."

The drone bees were dancing. They were flying in a figure-eight pattern. They were carrying tiny buckets of glitter. They were spelling out my name on the floor. "ANDY." But it was backwards. Or maybe I was backwards. I checked the mirror. I was backwards.

I went to the back room. The mannequin—Glitch—had moved. She was standing by the window. She was wearing the scarf again. But she was also wearing a raincoat. It was raining inside the room. Just a small cloud over her head. It was sprinkling on her plastic face. It was very dramatic. I told her to wipe her feet. She didn't answer. She just buzzed.

Around 10:00, a man came in. He wasn't a paper lawyer. He was a "Collector." He was wearing a suit made of human hair. It was shaved. It was blonde. He looked like a golden poodle. He said, "Mr. Warhol, I want to buy your shadow." I said, "It’s in litigation." He said, "I don't want the real one. I want the rights to the *idea* of it." I said, "That will cost you." He wrote me a check for $25,000. It was made of chocolate. I ate it. It tasted like money.

I worked on the "Famous for 15 Seconds" series. I did a portrait of the $2.90 bus fare again. But this time, I added a tear. It was "Digital Tears." It cost $60.00 a tube. It looks very wet but it’s dry. Very poignant.

Lunch was "Synth-Pasta." It’s pasta made of synthetics. It doesn't digest. It just sits in your stomach and vibrates. It keeps you full. Forever. It was $15.00. I had two bowls. Sammy had a "Glass of Time." He said it tasted like yesterday.

In the afternoon, I got a call on the MirrorWall. It was the Moon Rock. It was sitting on a pier. It said, "I’m thinking of becoming a statue." I said, "Statues don't move." It said, "I know. Isn't that great?" I said, "It’s very 19th Century." It buzzed and disconnected. I think it’s lonely. I felt a pang in my chest. Or maybe that was just the synth-pasta settling.

At 5:00, the "Solid State Suit" started to beep. Low battery. I had to stand very still to conserve energy. I stood in the corner of the Factory. I looked like a piece of furniture. A very expensive coat rack. Three people hung their coats on me. I didn't mind. It felt good to be useful. And famous. They took selfies with the coats.

At 7:00, I went to a party at "The Oyster Bar." It’s not underwater anymore. It’s in space. It’s on a satellite. We had to take a "Space-Elevator." It’s just a long rope. You hold on and they pull you up. It was $100.00. My gloves got dirty.

The party was hosted by a "Digital Diva." She’s a pop star who is just a frequency. You can’t see her, you can only hear her. She was singing a song about taxes. It was very catchy. Everyone was dancing. The zero gravity was free. But the drinks were expensive. I had a "Cosmopolitan." It was $85.00. It had actual stars in it. Or maybe they were just sparkles.

I floated around the room. I saw a "Hologram" of Mick Jagger. He was drinking battery acid. He said, "Andy, you look stiff." I said, "I’m preserving myself." He laughed. His laugh sounded like a fax machine.

I left at 9:30. The ride down was faster. I dropped my wallet. It fell into the atmosphere. It burned up. I hope someone finds the ashes.

Cabbed home. It was $40.00. I had to pay with a "Favor." I promised the driver I’d put him in a movie. He said, "Make sure I have good lighting."

Came home at 10:30. The apartment was cold. The window was open. The envelope of moon dust was gone. But on the table, there was a small stone. It was grey and heavy. It wasn't the Moon Rock. It was a kidney stone. I think it’s a gift from the Moon Rock. It’s very personal. I put it in a Plexiglas box. I titled it "The Remains of the Day."

I took off the suit. I plugged it into the wall. It lit up. It was charging.

I looked in the mirror. I was faint. But the kidney stone was solid.
