Woke up at 8:45 AM. I couldn't tell if I had woken up or if I had just finished loading. The iGod pill was still on the nightstand. I hadn't taken it. I was afraid that if I knew everything, I’d know exactly how much time I had left, and knowing the time is the worst thing you can do. It ruins the surprise. So I just swallowed a glass of water instead. $35.00. The water was "Imported from the Paleolithic Era". It tasted like rocks and dinosaurs. Very hearty.

The sky was bright yellow. Not like the sun. The sky itself was yellow. Like a big sheet of yellow felt. Or maybe like a Yolk. It felt cozy. Like being inside an egg.

"I’m the chick," I thought. "Or the yolk. I don't know."

Archie was staring at the wall again. He was meowing at a crack in the plaster.

"What is it?" I asked.

He looked at me. His eyes were loading bars. *30%... 40%...*

"He's buffering," I thought. "Probably downloading a new behavior."

I gave him a tin of "Solidarity Tuna". $45.00. It’s tuna that sticks together. Very political.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $110.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Futuristic Car. He didn't speak, just honked a synthesized tune. *Beep-beep-beep-boop*.

The Factory was quiet. Too quiet.

The upside-down door was right-side up again. But the walls were gone. Just the girders holding up the roof. It looked like a ruin. Or a skeleton.

"Where are the walls?" I asked.

Sammy was sitting on a crate. He looked tired. He was wearing a suit made of Velcro.

"We sold them," Sammy said. "We sold the vertical space."

"Who bought it?" I asked.

"A cloud," Sammy said. "A cloud company. They need the storage."

"So we're just... open air?" I asked.

"We're an 'Open Concept' workspace now," Sammy said. "It's very trendy."

Bob was floating by. He was holding a rake. He was raking the air.

"What are you doing?" I asked.

"Leaves," Bob said. "But there aren't any. So I'm raking the nothing. It's very Zen."

I looked at the floor. The shoes were gone. Sold too, I guessed. It was just concrete. I felt naked. I felt exposed.

"I need to work," I said. "I need to make something to fill the space."

I went to my desk. It was the only thing left.

I took out the acetates. I decided to make a portrait of the Sky. The Yellow Sky. I wanted to capture that Yolk feeling.

I mixed the paint. Nickel Yellow. Cadmium Yellow. Banana Yellow.

At 11:00 AM, a drone flew in. It was the shape of a worm. It coughed up an envelope.

I opened it. It was an invitation.

"THE FIRST ANNUAL MET GALA FOR THE UNSEEN."

"Black Tie Optional. Existence Mandatory."

"It's tonight," Sammy said. "You have to go."

"I don't have anything to wear," I said. "And my wig is flat."

"Wear the air," Sammy said. "Wear the space."

"I can't afford that," I said. "The air is rent-controlled."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We walked to a "Pop-Up Restaurant" that was literally popping up. It was an inflatable castle. It bounced when we walked. *Boing-boing*.

We sat on air mattresses.

I ordered the "Carbonara Foam". $85.00. It was just air. Tasted like pepper and pig.

"It’s light," I said. "I could eat this forever."

"You'd starve," Sammy said. "But you'd die thin."

Bob ordered the "Silence". It was free. He just sat there and didn't make a sound.

"I love the ambiance," I said. "It’s so empty."

At 3:00 PM, I finished the painting. It was just a big yellow square. *Yellow*. It looked like the sky outside. Or like a block of butter.

"It's a masterpiece," I thought. "It goes with everything."

A man walked in. He didn't have a body. He was just a suit. A suit walking by itself. It was filled with wind.

"Mr. Warhol," the suit said. "I'm the Breeze. I represent the Weather."

"What can I do for you?" I asked.

"I'd like to buy that painting," the suit said. "I want to hang it in the sky. To cover the grey days."

"How much will you pay?" I asked.

"I'll blow you a kiss," the suit said.

"I'll take it," I said. "A kiss is worth more than money these days. Money is heavy. Kisses are light."

The suit kissed me. It felt like a draft. A cold draft.

"Perfect," I said.

At 6:00 PM, I got ready for the Gala.

I put on my tuxedo. $800.00. It was made of denim. Blue jeans formal. Very American.

I put the iGod pill in my pocket for luck.

I took a cab to the Met. $150.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of my mother. She kept telling me to wear a hat.

"You'll catch a cold," she said.

"I'm immortal," I said. "I just get pixelated."

The Gala was on the Roof Garden. But the roof was gone. They had taken the roof off.

The stars were hanging on strings. Low. Like chandeliers.

I saw Bianca. She was wearing a dress made of mirrors. She looked like a disco ball.

"Andy," she said. "You look so solid."

"I feel heavy," I said. "I sold the walls."

"Selling out is buying in," she said.

We walked into the crowd. Everyone was invisible. You just saw empty tuxedos and empty gowns floating around. Clinking glasses. *Clink-clink*.

"I love this party," I said. "It's so discreet."

At 9:00 PM, they gave out the awards.

The award was "Most Invisible."

The winner was a ghost. A real ghost. From 1890.

He floated up to the podium. He didn't say anything. He just moaned.

"Very articulate," I thought.

I felt a rumble in my pocket. The iGod pill was vibrating.

I took it out. It was warm.

I looked at the yellow sky. It was turning purple.

"I don't want to know," I thought. "I just want to look."
