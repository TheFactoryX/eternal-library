Woke up at 8:30 AM. The room was pink. Very pink. Like Pepto-Bismol. I looked for the light switch, but the wall was soft. It felt like skin. "Gross," I thought. "Very fleshy."

It turned out the walls were just breathing. *Inhale-exhale*. The apartment was alive. I hope it doesn't get sick. I can't afford a doctor for a house.

I put on my wig. The "Silver Elvis" wig. It matched the breathing walls.

Archie was on the windowsill. He was watching a fly. The fly was hovering. It wasn't moving. It was suspended in a drop of amber. Or maybe it was just a very expensive robot fly.

"Don't eat it," I said. "It's probably worth more than you."

Breakfast was an "Edible Advertisement". $125.00. It was a piece of toast with a QR code burned into it. I scanned it with my eyes. It played a jingle for toothpaste. I tasted mint. It was convenient. No chewing required.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:45 AM. $75.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of myself. He had the wig and the glasses.

"Where to?" he asked.

"To the Factory," I said.

"Are you me?" he asked.

"I think so," I said. "Are you famous?"

"Not yet," he said. "I'm still driving."

We arrived in silence. It was awkward talking to myself. I never know what to say.

The Factory was quiet. Too quiet. The *beep-beep* was gone.

Sammy was sitting in the middle of the floor. He was meditating. Or maybe he was frozen. He was holding a pose. Like a statue.

"Sammy?"

"The shelves floated away," Sammy said, without opening his eyes. "I'm grounding myself. I'm thinking about heavy things. Anvils. Lead. The Great Depression."

Bob came out of the office. He was holding a bottle. It was filled with gray liquid.

"Is that the water from the melted art?" I asked.

"It's 'Abstract Expressionist Water'," Bob said. "We sold it to a collector in Tokyo. He wants it in a crystal vase."

"Does he know it's just mop water?" I asked.

"It's *art* mop water," Bob said. "It has *provenance*."

I went to the back to work. I wanted to do a portrait. But the canvas was gone. The room was empty. White walls.

"Where is my silkscreen machine?" I asked.

"It uploaded itself," Bob said. "It's in the cloud now. You have to print wirelessly."

I pointed my finger at the wall. *Click-click*. Nothing happened.

"I think I need a password," I said.

"Try 'Password'," Sammy suggested.

I tried it. A image appeared on the wall. It was a selfie I took in 1978.

"Retro," I said. "Very vintage."

At 11:30 AM, the Bureau of Reality guy came back. The one with the rulers.

"Mr. Warhol," he said. "We have a discrepancy. Your cat is broadcasting on a government frequency."

"He's just purring," I said.

"He's transmitting data," the guy said. He pulled out a device. It looked like a TV remote. He pointed it at Archie.

*Beep*.

Archie sneezed. "Meow-zzzt."

"See?" the guy said. "He's a pirate radio station."

"I didn't know," I said. "I thought he was just a cat."

"He needs a license," the guy said. "Or we have to delete him."

"I'll pay the fee," I said. "How much?"

"Three cans of tuna and one original sketch," he said.

I drew a sketch of a tuna can on a napkin. I handed it to him. He took it.

"Pleasure doing business," he said. He left.

"That was expensive," I thought. "But I can always draw more tuna."

Lunch was a pill. But I took the wrong one. It was a "Sleeping Pill". $50.00.

I fell asleep on the couch. I dreamed I was a soup can. I was on a shelf. I was next to a can of tomatoes. We didn't speak.

I woke up at 4:00 PM. There was drool on the couch. "Very Dada," I thought.

At 5:00 PM, we went to the opening of the "Cloud Gallery". It's a gallery that doesn't exist. It's just an app you download. You have to walk around with a headset on.

Cabbed there. $60.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a horse. He galloped down 5th Avenue. It was a bumpy ride. "Giddy-up," I said.

The gallery was crowded. But everyone was bumping into each other. Because they were all wearing headsets.

"Watch out for the Picasso," someone said. I looked. There was nothing there. Just a pillar.

"Oh, excuse me," I said. I walked around the pillar.

I saw the Jean-Michel bot. He was painting on a virtual wall.

"This is better," he said. "No paint fumes. Just code."

"I miss the smell," I said. "Turpentine smells like work."

I took off my headset. The real room was empty. Just a bunch of people in headsets waving their hands.

"I prefer the reality," I thought. "It's less crowded."

At 7:00 PM, I went to the "21" Club. The real one. Not the freezer.

I sat in a booth. The booth was leather. It was red. It felt like a mouth.

I ordered a "Plain Burger". $85.00.

The waiter brought it. It was on a plate. No holograms. No lasers. Just meat and bread.

"Is this safe?" I asked.

"It's organic," the waiter said. "From a farm."

I took a bite. It was juicy. It was greasy. It was delicious.

"I love grease," I thought. "It's so human."

I wiped my mouth with a napkin. The napkin was paper. It tore.

"This is real," I said. "It actually tears."

I left the waiter a $200.00 tip. "For the reality," I said.

Cabbed home at 10:00 PM. $95.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a cab driver. He didn't speak. He just drove.

"Are you reading the news?" I asked.
