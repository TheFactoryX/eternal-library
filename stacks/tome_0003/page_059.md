# Tuesday, March 31, 2026

> Written at 2026-03-31 23:55:01

Woke up at 9:00 AM. The room was dark. Not night-dark, but space-dark. The cat is still glowing, but now she's pulsating. *Thump-thump-thump*. Like a heart. Or a bass speaker at a disco. She ate the moon rock and now she’s a radioactive nightlight. It’s very convenient. I didn’t turn on the lamp. I just used the cat. I brushed my hair by her light.

I feel strange today. Hollow. Like a chocolate rabbit. I think I’m fading.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $48.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of himself. He was invisible. Just a floating hat and gloves.

"I'm off today," the hat said. "Invisible ink."

"Expensive," I said.

"Rare," the hat said.

The Factory was quiet. Too quiet. The "Indoor Rain" had stopped, but everything was dripping. *Drip-drop*. The puddles on the floor made mirrors. I looked down and saw ten of me.

"I'm everywhere," I said.

Sammy was sitting in the corner. He wasn't moving. He was holding a sponge. He looked like a statue.

"Sammy?"

"I'm buffering," Sammy said. *Whirrr-click*. "The connection is slow. The cat ate the moon and it’s interfering with the Wi-Fi."

"We need to get online," I said. "I have emails."

"Delete them," Sammy said. "Delete everything. Start fresh."

"I can't," I said. "I have a brand."

I walked to my desk. There was a pile of mail. Real mail. Paper. It felt heavy.

I opened a letter. It was from the government. The Department of Time.

"NOTICE OF RENEWAL," it said. "Your subscription to 2026 expires at midnight. Please remit payment to continue existing."

"How much?" I asked.

 Sammy looked over my shoulder. "It doesn't say. It says 'Call to Arrange Payment.'"

"Telephones are obsolete," I said. "I'll text."

I tried to text the number. My fingers went through the screen. *Static-buzz*.

"I'm glitching," I said. "Look at my hands."

My hands were transparent. I could see the wall through my palm.

"It's the bandwidth," Sammy said. "We need to buy more data. Or we need to become famous again. Fame sustains you. It’s like preservatives."

I tried to call Bianca. I needed to go somewhere. Anywhere.

"Bianca," I said when she answered. "It's Andy."

"Who?" she asked.

"Andy. Warhol."

"I don't know an Andy," she said. "I'm having lunch with The Collector. He's buying my shadow."

"Can I come?"

"Only if you have a physical form," she said. "No ghosts. It ruins the appetite."

She hung up.

I looked at Sammy. "I'm disappearing."

"Just keep moving," Sammy said. "If you stop, you'll freeze. Like a buffer wheel."

I paced the room. *Left-right-left*. I felt light. Like a balloon.

Lunch was at the automat. I needed something solid. I bought a slice of pumpkin pie. $6.00. I put the coin in the slot. *Clink*. The door opened. I took the pie. It felt heavy. Dense.

I ate it standing up. *Gulp*. It tasted like nostalgia and nutmeg.

"Good," I said. "I can taste it."

I checked my reflection in the pie case. I was faint. Like a pencil sketch that had been erased too many times.

"I need to spend money," I said. "Spending makes you real."

I went to a gallery. The "Gallery of Nothing." It was very crowded. Everyone was looking at empty pedestals.

"Is it Art?" I asked a critic.

"It's pure Concept," the critic said. "The artist didn't even show up. He stayed home. The absence is the piece."

"Did he sell it?"

"For ten million," the critic said. "To a hedge fund."

"I can do that," I said. "I'm not here. I'm absent."

I tried to autograph a wall for a fan. A girl with pink hair.

"Mr. Warhol," she said. "I love your... your..."

"My what?"

"Your lack of presence," she said. "It's so ethereal."

I took her pen. I tried to sign. *Swish-swish*. No ink came out. I was out of ink. I was out of Andy.

"Keep the pen," I said.

I walked out. The sun was setting. It looked like a spilled Coke can. Orange and red and sticky.

I hailed a cab. The driver was the "Simulacrum" of me again. The third time this week.

"Rough day?" he asked.

"I'm evaporating," I said.

"Don't worry," he said. "Water vapor eventually becomes rain. You'll come back down."

"Will I be expensive?"

"More than ever," he said.

I got out at the townhouse. $52.00. I gave him a high-five. My hand went through his hand. A ghost high-five. *Whoosh*.

I went inside. The cat was waiting. She was brighter than before. A blue star.

"Meow," she said. *Zap*.

I sat on the couch. I looked at the clock. It was 11:59 PM. One minute left in the subscription.

I opened my diary. My hand was almost invisible now. Just a faint outline.

I wrote the date. March 31.

"It was a good month," I wrote. "I ate some pie."

The clock ticked. *Tick*.

The cat purred. The room filled with blue light.

"I think," I wrote. "Therefore I am."

But I don't know if I am.

I put the pen down.

Midnight.

The lights flickered. *Blink*.

"Hello?" I said.

Nobody answered. Just the hum of the refrigerator. And the cat.

"Goodbye," I thought.
