Woke up at 8:15 AM. The sky was a very pale grey. Like a dirty sheet. I think the city is washing itself. Or maybe it’s just fading away like an old newspaper. I looked in the mirror and my wig was on backwards. I didn’t fix it. It’s avant-garde to look backwards.

Archie was on the windowsill. He was a pile of dust, but he was vibrating. A little pile of vibrating grey dust.

"I’m buzzing," Archie said. "I’m radio-active."

"That’s nice," I said. "Can you pick up FM?"

"I only pick up static," Archie said. "It’s the best station. They play the same song all day. *Shhhhh*."

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $600.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1950s Housewife. She had a beehive hairdo and was smoking a cigarette that didn't burn down.

"It’s a long drag," she said. "Just like my marriage."

"I never married," I said. "I just dated."

"Same thing," she said. "Except you don't get the toaster."

When we got to the Factory, the walls were breathing. In and out. *Whoosh*. It was making me seasick.

"The rent went up," Sammy said. He was sitting on a box of Brillo pads. He looked very flat today. Like a cardboard cutout. "The building is trying to expand to make more room for the debt."

"I can't afford the heating bill if the lungs are working," I said.

Bob was in the corner. He was covered in yellow pollen. He looked like a giant tennis ball.

"I'm a flower," Bob said. "I'm blooming."

"It's very seasonal," I said. "Can we sell you?"

"I'm not for sale," Bob said. "I'm a rare species. I'm a *Techno-Organism*."

"I'll give you fifty bucks," I said.

"I'll take it," Bob said.

I looked up at the ceiling. The Man in the Grey Suit was stuck there. But he was peeling. His face was flaking off like dried paint.

"It’s itchy," the Man said. "I feel like I’m shedding."

"You’re exfoliating," I said. "It’s very expensive to get that done in a salon."

"I don’t want to be a fresco anymore," the Man said. "I want to be a sculpture. I want to be 3D again."

"Too late," I said. "You’re part of the architecture now. You’re the moldings."

At 11:00 AM, I started to work. I took the little dollhouse Jean-Michel sold me. I put it on the table. I decided to paint a portrait of it. But I needed a subject. So I put the squirrel inside the house. The squirrel in the suit.

"It’s a squirrel in a house," I said. "It’s the American Dream."

I silk-screened it. I made a hundred copies. Then I accidentally knocked over the bottle of ink. It spilled all over the floor. It was a huge black puddle.

"It’s a lake," Truman said. He was typing on his giant typewriter. *Clack-clack-clack*. "I wrote a story about a lake made of ink. A sailor drowned in sentences."

"Don't step in it," I said. "You'll get stained."

"I'm already stained," Truman said. "Life is a stain."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Four Seasons". It was empty. There were no tables. Just a giant pool of water in the middle of the room.

"We’re doing aquaculture," the Maitre D' said. He was wearing scuba gear. "The fish are the customers now."

"I’ll have the steak," I said.

"We only have plankton," he said. "It’s very trendy. It’s what the stars eat. The stars in the sky."

I ordered the plankton. $150.00. It was green and tasted like pond water.

"It’s earthy," I said.

"It’s gritty," Sammy said. "I feel like I’m eating the bottom of a fish tank."

"That’s the point," I said. "You are what you eat. If you eat the bottom, you become the bottom."

At 3:00 PM, we walked to Central Park. The robotic sheep were gone. In their place were topiary animals. They were bushes cut into the shape of wolves.

"It’s scary," I said. "Nature is scary."

"It’s manicured," Sammy said. "It’s safe. The wolves can’t bite. They’re leaves."

I sat on a bench. A woman walked by. She was pushing a stroller. But the stroller was empty. She was cooing at it.

"Who’s a good nothing?" she said. "You are! You’re such a good void!"

I looked in the stroller. It was just a shadow.

"It’s a Simulacrum baby," the woman said. "It never cries. It never grows up. It’s perfect."

"How much?" I asked.

"It’s priceless," she said. "But I’d trade it for a cigarette."

"I don't smoke," I said.

"Neither does it," she said. She walked away.

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, the ceiling started to leak. A drop of grey liquid fell on my head. *Plop*.

"It’s raining," I said.

"It’s the Man," Bob said. "He’s melting."

I looked up. The Man in the Grey Suit was dripping. He was turning into liquid.

"Go get a bucket," I said. "We can’t waste good Man."

Sammy got a bucket. He caught the drips. It filled up fast.

"What do we do with it?" Truman asked.

"We bottle it," I said. "We call it 'Essence of Bureaucracy'. We sell it to bankers. They’ll drink anything."

At 7:30 PM, Jean-Michel called.

"Andy," he said. "I’m stuck."

"Where?"

"I’m stuck in a painting," he said. "I painted myself into a corner. Literally. I used industrial adhesive."

"Can you move your arm?"

"No," Jean-Michel said. "But I look great. The brushwork is amazing."

"Do you want me to come get you?"

"No," he said. "Just send over some pizza. And a straw."

I hung up. I looked at the pile of dust that was Archie. He had made a little swirl. A little dust devil.

"I’m dancing," Archie said.

"Good," I said. "Save a dance for me."
