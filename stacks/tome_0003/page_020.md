Friday, February 20, 2026

Woke up at 9:30 AM. I forgot to set the alarm. Or maybe the alarm forgot to ring. The sky was the color of a Band-Aid. Beige. Very soothing. I lay in bed for a long time. I didn't want to step on the floor. I was afraid I would fall through. Without a shadow to hold me down, I feel lighter. Like a balloon. Like a plastic bag caught in an updraft.

I finally got up. I went to the kitchen. The moon rock was vibrating so hard it had walked to the edge of the table. It was teetering. About to fall. I caught it just in time. It felt warm. Like a cat. Or a toaster. I gave it a bowl of "Cereal-O’s." They’re circular data bits. They cost $12.00. It didn't eat them. It just hummed C-sharp.

I got dressed. I put on the "Invisibility Suit." It’s a suit made of tiny cameras. It films what’s behind you and plays it on the front. It was $40,000. It makes you look like a ghost. Or a window. I looked in the mirror and I saw the wall behind me. It was perfect.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was wearing a trench coat made of feathers. He squawked when he stopped at red lights. He said, "Andy, darling, you look so transparent." I said, "It’s the new look." The cab ride was $28.00. I paid with a "Crypto-Wafer." It tastes like chocolate but it’s worth $500. I didn't eat it. It’s bad luck to eat money before noon.

When I got to the Factory, the door was gone. Just a hole in the wall. I walked through. Sammy was there. He was painting the floor black. He was using a roller. He said, "I’m painting a shadow for you, Andy. So you don't float away." It was very sweet. But the paint was "Void Black." It absorbs all light. It cost $900 a gallon. If I stepped on it, I might disappear forever. So I hovered near the door.

The drone bees were awake. They were buzzing around the mannequin—Glitch. She was wearing the scarf I wiped the AI Mayor’s tears with. She looked very chic. Very mourning. The bees were polishing her surface. One of them landed on my nose. It didn't sting me. It just scanned my retina. It beeped. "Identity Confirmed. Famous Quotient: 15%." I guess I’m slipping. I need to do something more viral.

We worked on the "Famous for 15 Seconds" series. We did a portrait of a dog who learned to drive a car. We printed it in "Traffic Light Yellow." It was very bright. It hurt Sammy’s eyes. He was still wearing the sunglasses. He said, "Yellow sounds like a trumpet." He asked me to stop painting.

Lunch was a "Concept Salad." It’s just an empty plate with a picture of a salad on it. It’s vegan. It’s gluten-free. It’s $80.00. I had two. I wasn't hungry, but I liked the presentation. Sammy had a glass of "Silence." He said it tasted like snow.

Around 3:00, the man from the "Department of Obscure Phenomena" came back. He was wearing a suit made of static. He looked like TV noise. He said, "Mr. Warhol, we have a problem. Your shadow is suing you." I said, "For what?" He said, "For abandonment. It wants alimony. It wants to be attached to someone else." He said the shadow was currently dating a lamppost in Times Square. He said the settlement was $5,000. I paid him. I don't want the shadow coming back. It was too dark.

We prepared for the party tonight. It was the opening of "Dissolving Ink" at the "Gallery of Non-Events." It’s a gallery that doesn't exist. You just stand in an empty room and think about art. It’s very exclusive. Only people with high IQs can see the walls.

I took the moon rock with me. I put it in my pocket. It weighed me down. It kept me from floating into the stratosphere.

We arrived at 8:00 PM. The party was at "Studio 54-V." It’s the virtual version. You have to wear a headset to get in. But since I don't have a shadow, I just slipped through the firewall.

The room was full of Holograms. I saw a digital version of Bianca Jagger riding a mechanical cat. The cat was made of lasers. I saw a simulation of Halston. He was wearing a dress made of pixels. He said, "Andy, love the suit. I can see right through you." I said, "I know. It’s breathable."

I floated around the room. I didn't drink anything. I just absorbed the atmosphere. It tasted like ozone and perfume. The DJ was a "Algorithm." It played songs based on the stock market. When the market went up, it played "Disco Inferno." When it went down, it played "Raindrops Keep Fallin' on My Head."

I danced with a girl who was just a pair of lips floating on a screen. She didn't say anything. She just hummed. It was a good conversation.

At 10:00, the Dissolving Ink paintings were unveiled. We all looked at them. They were beautiful. They were images of people I didn't know. A boy eating a burger. A woman crying at a bus stop. And then, one by one, they faded. The canvas went white. The gallery curator clapped. He said, "Bravo! The ultimate commodity! Something you pay for and then lose!" Everyone cheered. They love losing things. It makes them feel rich.

I checked the moon rock in my pocket. It was glowing. It was glowing pink. I took it out. It floated above my head. It looked like a halo. A very heavy, vibrating halo.

I left the party at 11:00. I was tired. Being two-dimensional is exhausting.

Cabbed home. It was $30.00. The driver was mute. He didn't say a word. It was the best conversation I had all day.

Came home and sat on the couch. I took off the Invisibility Suit. I looked in the mirror. I was faint. Like a smudge on the glass.

I put the moon rock on the table. It stopped glowing. It went to sleep. I think it dreams of the moon. Or maybe it dreams of being a paperweight.

I opened the window. The city was loud. Sirens
