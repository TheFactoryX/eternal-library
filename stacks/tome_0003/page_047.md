Thursday, March 19, 2026

Woke up and the day was finally here. Or there. Or somewhere. The calendar on the wall matched the watch on my wrist. March 19. I felt synchronized. Like a pair of socks. It’s a good feeling. Being on time.

I got out of bed. My feet made a sound. *Click-clack*. The gravity was back. I felt heavy. Heavy is good. Heavy means you’re real. I went to the kitchen. Bridget—the pigeon—was sitting on the counter. She had laid an egg. It was a square egg. A cube. White and perfect. I put it in my pocket. It might be art. It might be breakfast. I didn’t want to break it.

I put on the wig. It felt tight. Like a swim cap. I looked in the mirror. The loading bar was gone. My face was back. The nose, the eyes, the skin. But something was different. I looked younger. Or maybe I just looked high-definition. 4K. I touched my cheek. It felt like latex.

I took a cab to the Factory. $55.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Edie Sedgwick. She was wearing a t-shirt with my face on it. Meta. She drove very fast. We flew over a pothole. I felt weightless for a second.

When I got to the Factory, the door was locked. I knocked. No answer. I used my key. It didn't fit. The lock had changed shape. It was an eye. It scanned my iris.

"Identity confirmed," the door said. "Welcome, Andy Warhol Version 4.1."

The door melted. I walked through the metal. It was warm. Like walking through liver.

Inside, the Factory was empty. No art. No superstars. Just a single chair in the middle of the room. A spotlight was hitting it. The chair was electric blue.

Sammy was there. But he wasn't floating. He was sitting on the floor, holding a remote control. He looked sad. Or his avatar was set to "Melancholy."

"Where is everything?" I asked. "Where are the Marilyns?"

"They went on strike," Sammy said. "They want residuals. Every time someone looks at them, they want a nickel."

"That's expensive," I said.

"Very expensive," he said. "So we digitized them. We sent them to the cloud. Now the Factory is a blank slate."

"We should paint it black," I said. "Black is chic."

"We already did that," he said. "It was invisible."

I sat in the blue chair. It hummed. *Vrrrrmm*. It was a massage chair. It massaged my ego. It felt good.

"We have a visitor," Sammy said. He pointed to the corner.

A man was standing there. He was wearing a suit made of newspaper. Headlines from 2026. He looked familiar.

"Is that…?" I asked.

"It's you," Sammy said. "The future you. From next week."

Next week me walked over. He looked tired. His wig was askew.

"Buy stock in water," he said. "And sleep. Sleep a lot."

Then he dissolved. Like a Alka-Seltzer in water. *Fizz-fizz-fizz*. Just a pile of newspaper on the floor.

I picked up the paper. The headline was: **ANDY WARHOL SLEEPS FOR A WEEK. NATION YAWNS.**

I folded it up and put it in my pocket with the square egg.

At 11:00 AM, I got a call on my internal phone. *Ring-ring*. It was Jean-Michel Basquiat. Or a "Simulacrum" of him. He still draws on everything.

"Andy," he said. "I'm drawing on the moon."

"Is it visible from here?" I asked.

"No," he said. "But I can feel it. The moon feels like a grapefruit."

"Send me a piece," I said. "In a jar."

"I'll FedEx it," he said. "It'll cost $5,000.00 in shipping."

"Pay it," I said. "Art is expensive. Even rocks."

Lunch was at "The Russian Tea Room." But it’s not Russian anymore. It’s run by robots. The waiters are silver trays with wheels. They bring you soup and tell you jokes.

I ordered the Borscht. $45.00. It was bright red. Like blood. But sweeter.

The tray rolled up. "Why did the chicken cross the road?" it beeped.

"To get to the other side of the canvas," I said.

"Error," the tray said. "Humor not found."

I ate the soup. It was cold. But I didn't mind. I like cold things. They keep you fresh.

A woman sat next to me. She was wearing a hat made of televisions. They were showing my old movies. *Sleep*. *Empire*. *Blow Job*.

"I love your work," she said. "Especially the silent parts."

"Me too," I said. "Silence is the loudest sound."

She handed me a bill. $1,000,000.00. "For the silence," she said.

I took it. It was Monopoly money. But it felt heavy. Like gold leaf.

I walked home. The city was loud. *Honk-honk*. *Beep-beep*. The buildings were tall. They scraped the sky.

I saw a "Simulacrum" of a dog. It was barking at a fire hydrant. The hydrant was barking back.

I stopped at a newsstand. I bought a magazine. *Time*. *Newsweek*. *Life*.

My face was on all of them. And the can. The can was on all of them.

"Cheese sandwich," I said.

The vendor looked at me. "That'll be $10.00."

I didn't want a sandwich. I just wanted to say the words. "Cheese sandwich." It sounded like a poem. "Cheese sandwich."

I paid him. I took a bite of the air. It tasted like exhaust fumes. And possibility.

I came home at 4:00 PM. The apartment was quiet. Bridget was asleep on the rug. The square egg was in my pocket.

I took out the egg. I put it on the table.

I stared at it.

I waited for it to hatch.

Maybe tomorrow it will be a bird. Maybe it will be a painting. Maybe it will be another can of soup.

I don't know.

But it's expensive. I can feel it.

I took off my wig. My head felt light.

I lay down on the bed.

The ceiling was staring at me. It had a crack in it. A smiley face crack.

I closed my eyes.
