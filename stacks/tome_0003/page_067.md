Woke up at 8:30 AM. The room was finally still. No spinning. No blue light. Just grey. The nice, safe grey of dirty cotton. I missed the blue already. It made getting up feel like swimming.

I stepped on something squishy. *Squish*. It was one of the "Meat Art" boxes. It must have followed me home from the Factory. It was small now, like a lost hamburger. It was nudging my ankle.

"Soup?" it asked. Its voice was tiny. Like a squeaker in a dog toy.

"Not yet," I said. I kicked it gently into the bathroom. "Go wait in the tub."

Archie was watching from the sink. He looked betrayed. He hates sharing his bathroom with commerce.

Ordered a bagel from the drone. $18.00. It dropped it, shattering the plate. I ate the bagel off the floor. It tasted like ceramic and sesame. The five-second rule applies to art, so it should apply to breakfast.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:00 AM. $45.00. The driver was a hologram of Truman Capote.

"More money, more problems," he said, drifting through the steering wheel.

"I have less money, same problems," I said.

The Factory was quiet. Too quiet. The bio-degradable boxes were gone. Sammy sold them all last night.

"To a collector in Tokyo," Sammy said. He looked tired. He was eating a candy bar. "He’s going to display them in a refrigerator. He calls it 'The Chill of Death'."

"Death is very commercial," I said. "Did we get paid?"

"Pure crypto," Sammy said. "We can buy a small island. Or a large yacht."

"I want a wig," I said. "A real one. Made of human hair. From a real person who is still alive."

"That's expensive," Sammy said. "Living people charge a premium."

We worked on the "Oxidation" paintings. But instead of urine, we used a new chemical mixture. Sammy bought it from a man who sells clouds. It turns the copper green, then purple, then invisible.

"It's 'Invisible Art'," Sammy said. "The ultimate status symbol. You buy it, and you can't see it. You just *know* it's there. It’s very intellectual."

"It’s very expensive air," I said. I splashed the mixture on the canvas. *Sizzle*. It smelled like rain.

Lunch was a tuna sandwich. $300.00. I ate it with the Taylor Simulacrum. She was wearing a yellow raincoat.

"Did you like the wig?" she asked.

"It was shedding," I said. "I looked like a cat exploding."

"That's texture," she said. "That's history."

She passed me a note. It was printed on gum wrapper.

"The Vice President is coming to dinner," she said. "Tonight. At the automat."

"I can't go," I said. "I have to watch my paint dry. It might turn invisible."

"He's bringing the tank," she said. "To pick you up."

At 4:00 PM, a noise like the end of the world shook the building. *Crash*. The window rattled.

I looked out. A tank was parked on 33rd Street. It was painted camouflage silver. It looked like a giant Hershey’s Kiss.

The Vice President stepped out. He was wearing a suit made of money. Not fabric with money prints, but actual dollar bills, glued together. He crinkled when he walked. *Crinkle-crinkle*.

"Mr. Warhol!" he shouted. "I'm here for the portrait!"

I went downstairs. I felt short. The street was blocked. Robots were taking pictures.

"I painted it on a toaster," I said. "Do you have the toaster?"

"I ate it," the Vice President said. "I wanted to consume the art."

"I hope you didn't get toast burn," I said.

"I got indigestion," he said. "It was delicious."

He handed me an envelope. It was heavy.

"A tip," he said. "For the fame."

I opened it. It was full of diamond dust. Just dust.

"Thanks," I said. "I can make another cat with this."

"Keep making things," he said. "America needs more things to put in the corner."

He got back in the tank. The tank crushed a parking meter. *Crunch*.

"Oopsy-daisy," he said.

He drove away. The street was scratched.

I went back upstairs. The "Invisible" painting was gone. The canvas was blank.

"Sammy," I said. "Where is the painting?"

"It dried invisible," Sammy said. "It’s perfect."

"I can't sell a blank canvas," I said. "People will think I'm lazy."

"We sell it as 'The Void'," Sammy said. "It's a mirror. It reflects the buyer's soul."

"I hope their soul is worth more than $300,000," I said.

Cabbed to Serendipity at 7:30 PM. $40.00. I wanted the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate again. I needed the sugar.

The robot host was there. The faceless one.

"Table for one?" he asked.

"Table for two," I said. "Me and my cat."

"We don't allow pets," he said.

"He's not a pet," I said. "He's a very small person in a fur coat."

He let me in. I sat in the corner. I ordered the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $22.00. I also ordered a bowl of milk for Archie. $5.00.

A girl came over. She had blue hair. Real blue hair, like the light from Sunday.

"Are you him?" she asked. "The Avatar?"

"I'm him," I said. "But I'm not digital. I'm mostly water."

"Can I take a selfie?" she asked. "For the feed?"

"Sure," I said. I made a face. A blank face. The face I make when I'm thinking about money.

*Flash*.

"Thanks," she said. "You look great. You look so... real."

"That's the problem," I said.

I drank the chocolate. It was cold. It made my teeth hurt. Archie lapped the milk.

Walking home. The sky was purple. The streetlights were humming. I looked in a shop window. They were selling wigs. My wigs. The Andy Warhol Collection. They were $5,000 each.

"Good," I thought. "I'm worth more than I cost."

I got home. The meat box in the bathtub had grown. It was the size of a dog now.

"Soup?" it asked.

"No," I said. "Go to sleep."

I took off my clothes. I put on the pajamas. They have pictures of soup cans on them. Very meta.
