Woke up at 9:45 AM. The sky is vinyl today. Black vinyl. It has grooves. Deep, concentric grooves spinning out from the sun. The sun is the label in the center. A bright yellow 45 rpm single. I looked out the window and the clouds were just dust. Dust in the grooves. It sounds static-y. *Shhh, pop*. Like listening to the radio underwater. I tried to find the track listing, but it was just white noise. $0.00.

Put on the "Silver Cloud" wig. It’s made of mylar pillows. It floats. It hovers two inches above my head. It’s very anti-gravity. I had to wear a lead necklace to keep me from drifting up to the ceiling.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $1,100.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Grace Jones. She was flat. Literally two-dimensional. A cardboard cutout driving a Buick.

"Am I a face?" Grace asked. "Or am I a record?"

"You're a cover," I said. "Glossy."

When we got to the Factory, the building was a jukebox. A giant Wurlitzer. Flashing lights. Glass tubes.

"I want to play B-7," Bob said. He pushed a button. The building changed shape. It morphed into a hamburger.

"I'm hungry," Bob said. "It smells like grease."

The building morphed again. It turned into a coffin.

"I'm sad now," Bob said.

"Don't push the buttons," I said. "We don't know the playlist."

Inside, the walls were spinning. The floor was a turntable. We were the needle.

"Walk carefully," Jean-Michel said. "If you skip, we repeat the same verse."

At 11:15 AM, the rat arrived. He was DJing. He had headphones the size of dinner plates.

"I'm scratching," the rat said. He moved his paws back and forth. *Wiki-wiki-wa*. "It's rhythmic."

The squirrel was there. He was breakdancing in the corner. He was spinning on his shell. It was impressive.

"That's acrobatic," I said. "Do that again."

"I can't," the squirrel said. "I'm dizzy."

At 12:45 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Balthazar". It was very French. The waiters wore stripes. They were all the same waiter. Clones.

"I'll have the water," I said. "$45.00."

"Sparkling or still?" the waiter asked. "Or fast or slow?"

"Still," I said. "I don't want to move."

Jean-Michel ordered the "Steak Frites". The plate came out and it was a collage. A picture of a steak glued to a picture of fries.

"It’s Cubism," Jean-Michel said. "I can’t eat it. It’s stuck."

I looked at my water. It was frozen in a block. A glass brick.

"I can't drink this," I said.

"You have to wait for the needle to drop," the waiter said. "Side B."

At 2:20 PM, I worked on the "Repetition" paintings. I painted the same chair 50 times. Then I painted the same chair 50 more times.

"It's the same chair," Sammy said.

"It's different," I said. "The price went up."

I painted a price tag on each chair. $500. $600. $700.

"Inflation," I said. "It's happening fast."

At 3:30 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a microphone. He was singing. But he wasn't singing words. He was singing numbers.

"Five, six, seven, eight," the Man sang. "Nine, ten, buy, sell."

"It's a hit," I said. "Catchy."

At 4:45 PM, the sky skipped. *Zzzzt*. The sun jumped to the other side of the horizon. It was suddenly night.

"Technical difficulties," Bob said. "The tone arm is bent."

I looked out the window. The stars were scratches. Light scratches.

At 7:00 PM, we went to a premiere. It was for a movie called "The Last Superstar". It starring a digital avatar of Taylor Swift and a hologram of me.

"We look good together," I said. "She’s shiny. I’m matte."

We walked the red carpet. But the carpet was moving. It was a conveyor belt.

"Keep walking," the security guard said. "You can't stop."

"I'm tired," I said. "My feet hurt."

"Fame is endurance," the guard said. "Walk."

We walked in place for an hour. The flashbulbs went off. *Pop, pop*. Like popcorn.

"I'm popping," I said.

At 9:30 PM, the rat fell asleep on the turntable floor. The needle dropped on his tail.

*Ouch*. He didn't wake up. He just snored in B-flat.

At 11:15 PM, I went home. The cab was a speaker box. We drove in stereo.

The townhouse was quiet. The house was a gatefold sleeve. I opened the front door like a book.

Inside, I took off the Silver Cloud wig. It floated up to the ceiling and stayed there. It’s a permanent installation now.

I went to the kitchen. I opened a can of Campbell’s Soup. Tomato.

I poured it into a bowl. It looked like red paint.

"I'm mixing my media," I thought.

I sat in the dark. The only light was the glow from the rat's tail.

I drank the soup. It was cold.

"Good," I thought. "It tastes like 1962."

I went upstairs. I lay in bed. The room was spinning. Slowly. 33 and a third revolutions per minute.

I closed my eyes.

I could hear the music. It was far away.

Is it me?

Or is it just a cover version?

I don't know.

Gee.

I hope they play the B-side.

So.
