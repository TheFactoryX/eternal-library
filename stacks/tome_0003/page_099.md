# Sunday, May 10, 2026

Woke up at 9:30 AM. I slept late. The "Melancholy" update is working great. I feel gray. I feel like a newspaper left out in the rain. The ink is running and I can’t read the headlines. It’s very romantic.

The sky was beige again, but the rip was taped up. Someone used silver duct tape. It looked like a scar. Or a bracelet. I like it when the sky has accessories.

Archie was on the ceiling. He was asleep. He looked like a bat. A very furry, expensive bat.

I went to the kitchen. The fish from the safe was swimming in the living room air. It’s beautiful. A goldfish with nowhere to go. It’s the perfect pet. It doesn’t need food, it just swims through the nothingness.

I opened a can of "Campbell's Tomato Soup". $2.50. I heated it up. I put the goldfish in the soup. I figured it needed to feel at home.

"Are you eating me?" the goldfish asked.

"I'm adopting you," I said.

I ate the soup. The goldfish swam in my stomach. It felt like a hug.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $500.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Taxi Driver. The Robert De Niro one. He kept looking in the mirror and talking to himself.

"You talkin' to me?" he asked.

"No," I said. "I'm thinking."

"You lookin' at me?" he asked.

"I'm looking at the meter," I said. "It's going up fast."

"You talkin' to my meter?" he asked.

"I'm talking to money," I said. "It's the only language I speak."

He dropped me off at the hole in the wall. It was taped up with the same silver tape as the sky.

The Factory was quiet. Too quiet.

Bob was in the corner. He had stopped rolling. He was just sitting there. A silver sphere. A giant marble.

"I'm full," Bob said. "I downloaded the internet. It’s boring."

"Delete it," I said.

"I can't," Bob said. "I'm stuck in a loop. I keep reading about celebrity divorces. It’s very depressing."

"Good," I said. "Now you're an artist."

Sammy was sitting on a crate. He looked sad. He was holding the Concept of a Cat box. It was silent.

"The cat ran away," Sammy said.

"It was a concept," I said. "Concepts don't run away."

"It was a *bad* concept," Sammy said. "It was a cat that didn't exist. So it left. To not exist somewhere else."

"Did you pay for it?"

"I signed a check," Sammy said. "But the check bounced. Because the cat wasn't real."

"Money isn't real," I said. "So it's a perfect match."

At 12:00 PM, I went to the back. The horse was gone. The painting of the horse was gone. Even the "Portrait of a Nobody" was gone.

"Where is the art?" I asked.

"The Past took it," Truman said. He was standing in the doorway. He looked solid. He didn't look like a ghost anymore. He looked like a person. He was wearing a suit that fit.

"I made a deal," Truman said. "I traded the art for the Present."

"We have the present," I said.

"Not the real one," Truman said. "This one is borrowed. I bought the future so we can keep living in the now."

"How much?" I asked.

"Everything," Truman said. "But don't worry. I put it on your tab."

"Good," I said. "I hate carrying cash."

At 1:30 PM, we went to Serendipity 3. The line was long. It was full of tourists. But they were blurry. Like they were low-resolution files.

"I think the server is lagging," Sammy said.

We got a table. I ordered the "Frrrozen Hot Chocolate". $15.00. It was cold. It was so cold it burned my tongue.

"I want to go to a party," I said. "I haven't been to a party in two days."

"Studio 54 is closed," Sammy said. "It's a data center now."

"Let's go anyway," I said. "We can dance with the servers."

At 3:00 PM, we walked to Studio 54. The disco ball was spinning. But there was no light. It was just a spinning sphere in the dark. It was very existential.

Inside, there were no people. Just rows of black blinking boxes. The servers were humming. *Whirr-click*.

"Is this the party?" I asked.

"It's the aftermath," Truman said. "The party is always over before you arrive."

"I'll have a drink," I said.

Bob rolled over to a server. He plugged himself in. *Zap*. He started to glow.

"I'm downloading the party," Bob said. "I can see the dancing. I can feel the beat."

"Can you see Bianca?" I asked.

"I see everyone," Bob said. "They're all pixels. Beautiful, colorful pixels."

At 5:00 PM, we went back to the Factory. The hole in the wall was peeling. The silver tape was curling up. Behind the tape wasn't the 19th century. It was just white. Blinding white.

"It's the Return to Zero," Sammy said. "The Reset."

"Are we going to disappear?" I asked.

"No," Truman said. "We're going to be famous. Forever. It’s very tiring."

I looked at the empty spot where the paintings used to be. I felt like painting. But I didn't have any paint. I only had time.

"I'll paint a clock," I said. "And sign it 'Andy Warhol'. And sell it for a million dollars."

"Sold," Sammy said. "To the man with the watch."

At 7:00 PM, I called home. I wanted to check on Archie.

"The cats are eating the furniture," my mother said. "The Sam's are hungry."

"Feed them the fish," I said. "The fish in the air."

"There are no fish in the air," she said. "Only cats."

"Gee," I said. "I must have eaten it."

Cabbed home at 8:00 PM. $600.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of me. He had a wig and glasses.

"Nice wig," I said.

"Thanks," he said. "I stole it from your head."

"Keep it," I said. "I have a million of them."

When I got home, the house was quiet. Archie was still floating. He was purring. The goldfish in my stomach was swimming. It tickled.
