Woke up at 7:30 AM. The sky was beige. Still beige. But it was a *shimmering* beige today. Like pearl nail polish. It’s very elegant. I think the Bureau of Consistency polished the city while I was sleeping.

Archie was gone. Just a dent in the pillow.

"Archie?" I said. Nothing.

I looked on the floor. A small pile of grey dust.

"Oh no," I said. "You were right. You were too subtle."

"I'm still here," the pile of dust said. It was a whispery voice. Like scratching on a record. "I'm just distributed now."

"Can I vaccum you?" I asked.

"No!" said the dust. "I'm atmospheric! I'm art!"

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. $550.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Nostalgia Critic. He was crying.

"It's all so derivative," he sobbed. "This traffic... it's just copying yesterday's traffic."

"I like repetition," I said. "It's like comfort food."

When we got to the Factory, the door was gone. Just a hole in the brickwork. The bricks were pulsing. *Thump-thump*. It was like walking into a stomach.

"We're digesting the day," Sammy said. He was standing in the middle of the room. He looked solid again. High definition. "I found a charger. I plugged myself into the wall."

"Do you feel full?" I asked.

"I feel electric," Sammy said. Sparks were flying off his shoulders. It was very festive. Very *Party-in-the-Box*.

Bob was in the corner. He wasn't grey anymore. He was vibrating. Making a humming sound. *Hummmmm*.

"I'm churning," Bob said. "I'm making butter."

"From what?" I asked.

"From motion," Bob said. "From the vibration of the earth."

"Can I put it on my toast?" I asked.

"It's digital butter," Bob said. "It has zero calories and infinite flavor."

"I'll take ten pounds," I said.

I looked at the wall where the bruise-painting was. The Man in the Grey Suit was still stuck there. But he had moved. He was climbing up the wall.

"It's steep," the Man said. "And the atmosphere is... wet."

"Keep climbing," I said. "If you reach the ceiling, I'll give you a bonus."

At 11:00 AM, I sat down to work. I took a piece of acetate. I decided to make a print of Archie. Or rather, the *idea* of Archie.

I silk-screened a pile of dust onto a canvas. Then I silk-screened a $500 bill over it. Then a can of soup.

"It's a triptych," I said. "Life, Money, Dinner."

"It's very poignant," Truman said. He was sitting on a giant typewriter. He was typing by jumping on the keys. *CLACK-WHAM*.

"I'm writing my memoirs," Truman said. "It's a tell-all. It tells everything I know about nothing."

"Who's the publisher?" I asked.

"The wind," Truman said. "I'm going to throw it out the window and see who reads it."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". The hostess was a hologram of a little girl.

"Table for four," I said.

"Do you have a reservation?" she asked. "Do you exist?"

"I have a credit card," I said. "That's better than existing."

We sat down. I ordered the "Frrrozen Hot Chocolate". It was $75.00. It was lukewarm.

"It's not frozen," I said.

"It's room temperature," Sammy said. "That's the new luxury. Temperature-neutrality."

I sipped it. It tasted like chocolate water.

"I miss the cold," I said. "Cold made you feel alive."

"Cold is inefficient," the straw said. It was a digital straw. It displayed the temperature of the liquid on the side. 72 degrees.

"It's perfect," I said.

While we were eating, Jean-Michel walked in. He was carrying a house. A small dollhouse.

"Did you find your floating house?" I asked.

"I found this one," Jean-Michel said. "It was on the sidewalk. Someone threw it out."

"It's nice," I said. "Is it rent-controlled?"

"It's very open," Jean-Michel said. "Look at the windows." He pointed to the holes in the roof. "Great light."

"Can I buy it from you?" I asked.

"Five hundred dollars," Jean-Michel said.

"Sold," I said. I gave him the cash.

At 3:30 PM, we walked to Central Park. The robotic sheep were back. But they were shearing each other.

"It's self-sustaining," I said. "I love capitalism when it's fuzzy."

I sat on a bench. A squirrel ran up to me. It was wearing a tiny suit. It held out a tiny hand.

"I don't have any nuts," I said.

"I have stocks," the squirrel said. His voice was high and squeaky. "I have acorns of blue-chip companies."

"I can't afford stocks," I said. "I'm an artist. I'm bad with money."

"Buy low," the squirrel said. He handed me a tiny acorn with the Apple logo on it.

"Is this real?" I asked.

"It's as real as you want it to be," the squirrel said.

I took the acorn. It felt heavy.

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, the Man in the Grey Suit reached the ceiling.

"I made it," he said. He was flat against the plaster. "Now what?"

"Now you're a fresco," I said. "You're part of the building."

"Do I get a break?"

"You get a break in 300 years," I said. "When the lease is up."

At 7:30 PM, I started work on a new portrait. I took the photo of the squirrel. I bleached it. I inverted it. So the squirrel was white and the suit was black.

"It's a negative," I said. "It's about how animals are people and people are... pending."

Bob rolled over. He was covered in yellow dust.

"I made pollen," Bob said. "I'm a flower now."

"It's very fertile," I said.

At 9:00 PM, we ordered pizza. "Dominos". $40.00. The delivery boy was a drone. It dropped the pizza through the mail slot.

*Splat*.

"It's abstract," I said.

I ate a slice. It tasted like cardboard and tomatoes. The best kind.

I put the little house from Jean-Michel on my desk. I looked inside
