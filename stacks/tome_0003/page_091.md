# Saturday, May 02, 2026

Woke up at 10:30 AM. Very late. The sky was the color of a television channel that doesn’t exist—a fuzzy grey static. It matched my hair perfectly.

I put on my glasses. The world was still in 2D. Flat. Like a pancake. Or a painting.

I reached into my pocket to check on the iGod pill. It was gone.

"Did I eat it?" I thought. "Or did it evaporate?"

I checked my tongue. It didn't feel omniscient. It just felt fuzzy. Like a peach.

I walked into the kitchen. Archie was sitting on the counter. He was vibrating. *Hummmmm*.

"Are you downloading something?" I asked.

He didn't answer. He just hummed louder. He was glowing faintly. A pale blue light.

I opened a can of "Zero-Calorie Air". $10.00. I poured it into his bowl. He didn't eat it. He just absorbed it through his pores. Very efficient.

Breakfast was a piece of "Leftover Birthday Cake". $75.00. It was dry. It tasted like frosting and wishes.

At 11:30 AM, the phone rang. *Ring-ring*. But it wasn't the phone. It was the toaster.

"Andy?" It was Truman. He sounded frantic. "I've been stuck in the closet for three days."

"Why?" I asked.

"The doors are gone," he said. "We sold them, remember? Now I'm just stuck in a door frame. Anyone can walk in. I need a door, Andy. I need privacy."

"We can buy you a curtain," I said. "A bead curtain. Like the 60s."

"Phony," he said. "I want a solid door. Oak."

"I'll see what I can do," I said.

I hung up the toaster.

Cabbed to the Factory at 12:15 PM. $105.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Marilyn Monroe. She was sad.

"Men don't realize that curves are just lines that got tired of being straight," she said.

"That's profound," I said.

"I read it on a billboard," she said.

The Factory was windy. Very windy. Without the walls, the air just rushed through. *Whoosh*. Papers were flying everywhere. My receipts were flying up to the ceiling.

"My accounting!" I yelled.

I ran around trying to catch them. "$4.00 for gum. $50.00 for a thought."

Sammy was holding onto a pillar. His hair was blowing in his face.

"We have a problem," Sammy shouted over the wind. "The painting you sold yesterday. The Yellow Square?"

"What about it?" I asked.

"The Breeze hung it up," Sammy said. "But it's blocking the sun. It's making the whole city yellow. People are getting jaundice just by looking out the window."

"Is that bad?" I asked.

"It's a health hazard," Sammy said. "The City is suing us for 'Visual Pollution'."

"But pollution is beautiful," I said. "It's part of the texture."

"Not when it's clogging the pores," Sammy said.

Bob floated by. He was tethered to a desk with a rope.

"I found a buyer for the rope," Bob said. "But I'm using it."

"Who is the buyer?" I asked.

A man walked in. He was wearing a suit made of balloons. Red balloons. He squeaked when he walked. *Squeak-squeak*.

"I'm Mr. Pop," the man said. "I need the rope. I need it to tie down my ego. It's floating away."

"It's not for sale," I said. "It's holding Bob."

"I'll give you $500.00," Mr. Pop said.

"Let him go," I told Bob.

Bob let go of the rope. He didn't fly away. He just hovered there. He’s very good at hovering.

Mr. Pop handed me a credit card made of bubblegum. $500.00. I put it in my pocket. It stuck to the lint.

At 2:00 PM, I went to the back. I wanted to work on a new portrait. Something to apologize for the yellow sky.

I decided to paint a "Shadow". Just a black rectangle.

I started painting. It was hard to paint shadow on a white floor. It kept looking like a hole.

"It's conceptual," I thought.

Just then, Archie walked in. He was still glowing blue.

He walked over to the shadow painting and sat on it. His blue light mixed with the black paint. It looked like outer space.

"Oh," I said. "It's better with the cat."

"I can charge admission for that," Sammy said. "$5.00 to look at the Space Cat."

"I'll look for free," I said.

At 4:00 PM, we got a delivery. It was a door. A big, heavy oak door.

"Truman sent it," the delivery man said. He was a "Simulacrum" of a delivery man. He looked very realistic.

"Where should I put it?" he asked.

"There's no wall," I said. "Just lean it against the air."

The man leaned the door against nothing. It stood up by itself. It looked like a magic trick. A door to nowhere.

"It's perfect," I said.

At 6:00 PM, I left the Factory. I walked to Serendipity 3.

I wanted a Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $25.00.

The street was quiet. The sky was still yellow.

I sat in the booth. The waitress was a robot named Betsy.

"What'll it be, hon?" Betsy asked. Her voice was static.

"The chocolate," I said. "And a straw."

Betsy brought the glass. It was smoking.

I looked out the window. I saw a bird. But it wasn't a bird. It was a flying fish. A goldfish with wings. It was swimming through the yellow air.

"It's beautiful," I thought. "But it's probably expensive."

I drank the chocolate. It gave me a headache. A good headache. A sugar headache.

I paid the bill. $35.00 with tip.

I walked home. The city looked like a black and white movie that had been colored in with a yellow highlighter.

I got home and checked the mail. There was a letter from the government.

"NOTICE OF OBSOLESCENCE."

It said I was scheduled for an update on Tuesday.

"I don't want an update," I thought. "I want to stay the same."

I went into the bedroom. The iGod pill was on the pillow. It hadn't disappeared. I must have put it there and forgot.

Archie jumped on the bed. He sat on the pill.

"Don't eat it," I said. "You'll know too much."

He purred. *Purr-purr*. He looked like he knew everything already.
