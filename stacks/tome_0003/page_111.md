Woke up at 8:30 AM. The sky was raining nickels. It sounded like wind chimes. A terrible, loud jingling. *Clink, clink, clink*. It sounded like money burning. I opened the window and a coin hit me on the nose. It left a dent. A tiny square dent. I picked it up. It wasn't a nickel. It was a chocolate coin wrapped in gold foil. But it was warm. It melted in my hand. "Inflation," I said. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:45 AM. $50.00. I paid with the chocolate foil. The driver licked it and said it was delicious. He was a "Simulacrum" of Aristotle Onassis. He had a mustache made of grey smoke.

"Did you bring the boat?" I asked.

"I am the boat," Aristotle said. "I'm sailing on the pavement of New York."

When I got to the Factory, the floor was covered in chocolate coins. The squirrel was swimming in them. Backstroke. *Swish, swish*.

"It's a Scrooge McDuck pool," the squirrel said. "I'm rich in cocoa."

"Don't eat the foil," I said. "It'll bind you up."

Bob was there. He wasn't a pyramid anymore. He was a line. A vertical line of light. He looked like a laser beam, but jittery.

"I'm streamlined," Bob said. "I'm a barcode. Scan me."

"What's your price?" I asked.

"Priceless," Bob said. "Or 99 cents. Depending on the aisle."

Sammy was sweeping up the chocolate with a dustpan made of a hubcap. The Man in the Grey Suit was standing in the corner. He was holding the laminated shadow.

"It's peeling," the Man said. He pointed to the shadow. The lamination was bubbling.

"It's a tan," I said. "It caught some rays."

At 11:00 AM, the cricket, Jim, chirped. *Chirp*. But it wasn't a chirp. It was a fax machine noise. *Brrrr-ding*.

"I have incoming," Jim said. "It's a telegram from the 80s."

A piece of paper shot out of Jim's mouth. It was curled.

I unfolded it. It was a press release. "ANDY WARHOL DEAD. AGAIN."

"Gee," I said. "I missed the funeral."

"You're the ghost," the Man in the Grey Suit said. "You're haunting the art world. It's very lucrative. Haunting fees are 20%."

"I don't feel like a ghost," I said. "I feel like a wig with a person attached."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". But it wasn't a restaurant. It was a shoe store.

"We serve sole," the waiter said. He was holding a loafer. "And heels."

"I'll have the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate," I said. "Do you have that?"

"We have the Frrrozen Hot Boot," the waiter said.

We sat on ottomans. The Bucket of Chicken ordered the laces. "I like the leather," the Bucket said.

I had the soup. It was shoe polish. Black polish. $900.00.

"It's tangy," I said. "Like leather and tears."

Jean-Michel was there. He was peeling more. His blue face was flaking off. Underneath was... gold. Actual gold leaf.

"I'm shining," Jean-Michel said. "I'm gilded."

"You're expensive," I said. "Don't touch the walls."

At 3:30 PM, a helicopter landed in the middle of the room. It was a toy helicopter, the size of a grape. A tiny woman stepped out. She was made of diamonds.

"I'm Liz," she said. "Liz Taylor. But the small version. The engagement ring version."

"Hello, Liz," I said.

"You look terrible, darling," Liz said. "You look like you've been dead for forty years."

"I feel great," I said. "I just ate shoe polish."

"That explains it," Liz said. "You need a spa treatment." She snapped her fingers. A team of microscopic dust mites came out with tiny sponges. They started cleaning my glasses. "There. Now you can see the future."

I looked through my glasses. The room was made of crystals. Everyone was a prism.

"It's beautiful," I said. "But sharp."

At 5:00 PM, I tried to work. I wanted to paint the rain. But the rain had stopped. The window was dripping chocolate syrup.

"It's sticky," I said.

Bob the Barcode spun around. "I have an idea," Bob said. "Make a portrait of the money."

"Money is boring," I said. "That's why I like it."

So I painted a portrait of the chocolate coin. I used real chocolate. I just smeared it on the canvas. Then I ate the canvas.

"It's delicious," I said. "It's my best work yet. 'Chocolate Eddy'."

"Can I have the foil?" the squirrel asked.

"You can have the wrapper," I said.

At 7:00 PM, I got a call from Basquiat. He was glowing gold.

"I'm too heavy," Jean-Michel said. "I can't move. I'm weighted down by my own value."

"That's art," I said. "You're an asset class."

"I miss being a sketch," he said. "I miss the paper."

I hung up the gold receiver. It was heavy.

At 9:00 PM, we ordered dinner. "Burgers". $250.00.

The delivery boy brought a bag of buns. No meat. Just buns.

"It's the buns," the delivery boy said. "The upper crust."

"I'll take the bottom," I said. "I prefer the foundation."

We sat in the dark and ate bread. The chocolate foil on the floor sparkled in the light of Bob's barcode. It looked like stars.

"It's a galaxy," I said. "A chocolate galaxy."

"I want to go to Mars," the squirrel said. "I hear they have nuts there."

"Mars is red," I said. "Like the sky on Tuesday."

"I'm bored," the Man in the Grey Suit said. "Let's play a game."

"What game?" I asked.

"Russian Roulette," the Man said. He pulled out a revolver. But the barrel was a lipstick. He spun it. *Click-click-click*. He pointed it at his head and pulled the trigger. *Click*.

"Cherry Red," the Man said. His lips were painted. "My favorite."
