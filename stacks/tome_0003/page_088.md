Woke up at 8:15 AM. The sky was a nice shade of silver today. Like the gelatinous inside of a can of Spam. Very smooth. Very uniform. I like it when the weather matches the food. It makes decisions easier.

I got up and checked my foot. It was still blurry.

"Pixellated," I thought. "I'm becoming a low-resolution JPEG of myself."

I rubbed some "High-Def Ointment" on it. $150.00. It smelled like lemons and mathematics. The pixels smoothed out a little. I could see my toenails again. That's important. If you can't see your toenails, you don't know where you are.

Archie was sleeping in the sink. He looked like a wet towel. A wet, expensive towel.

"Move," I said.

He didn't move. He just purred and generated a small cloud of data. It drifted up to the ceiling. *Float-float*.

Breakfast was a "Tab of Blandness". $50.00. I swallowed it dry. It cancelled out the excitement from yesterday. My heart rate slowed down to 1 beat per minute. Very efficient. I felt like a clock.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $85.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Little Richard. He kept screaming "WOOO!" every time we hit a green light. It was thrilling but exhausting.

"Good golly, Miss Molly!" he yelled as we parked.

I tipped him an extra $20.00 just to stop making noise.

The Factory was crowded. Very crowded.

People were waiting in line. They weren't people, though. They were "Fans". Not the kind that blows air, but the kind that screams. They were floating heads. Just heads, hovering at eye level.

"Is this the line?" I asked Bob.

Bob was eating a bagel. He looked much better today. The magnet worked. He was solid. He had texture.

"They're here for the signing," Bob said. "The 'Save the Factory' signing."

"What are we signing?" I asked.

"Air," Bob said. "We're signing air molecules."

"Oh," I said. "Business as usual."

I walked past the heads. One of them looked at me.

"Mr. Warhol," it said. "Can you sign my forehead?"

"I don't have a pen," I said.

"Just use your aura," it said. "It's worth more."

I pushed my way through the cloud of fans to the back.

Sammy was on the phone with the bank.

"No," Sammy was saying. "We can't deposit 'vibes'. We need dollars. Or credits. Or golden eggs."

I sat down to work. I had to finish the portrait of XØA-9. The Saturn pop star.

I took out the diamond dust. $4,000.00 worth of sparkle.

I poured it on the canvas. *Shhh-shhh*.

It stuck to the green slime paint. It looked like a disco ball on a bad day.

"Perfect," I thought.

At 11:30 AM, the mail delivery drone buzzed at the window. *Bzzzzzt*. It was a "Package of Consequences" from the Bureau of Reality.

Bob brought it over. "It's heavy," he said. "Like a brick."

I opened it. It was a bill.

"RENT FOR APRIL - $1,000,000.00."

"For what?" I asked. "The Factory is paid for."

"It's for the 'space'," Sammy said. "You're renting the void. You're renting the air. You're renting the time we occupy."

"That's expensive," I said.

"We need to sell something," Sammy said. "Sell the shoes."

"The shoes are walking," I said. "I can't catch them."

We looked at the floor. The shoes were still marching. *Step-step-step*. They were wearing out the floorboards.

"What about the cat?" I asked. "Can we rent Archie?"

"He's not an asset," Sammy said. "He's a liability. He eats too much tuna."

Just then, a girl walked in. She was wearing a dress made entirely of credit cards. They fluttered when she moved. *Swish-swish*.

"I'm here for the portrait," she said.

"It's done," I said. I pointed to XØA-9. She was shimmering on the canvas.

"Does she come with the frame?" the girl asked.

"The frame is extra," I said. "$500.00."

"I'll take it," she said. "I'll pay in 'Hype'."

"We can't use Hype at the grocery store," Sammy said.

"Yes you can," the girl said. "Hype is the new currency. It's inflation-proof."

She handed me a small balloon. It was filled with pink gas.

"Here," she said. "This is worth $2 million."

I took the balloon. It tugged at my hand. It wanted to go up.

"Don't let it pop," she said. "Or you'll become irrelevant instantly."

I tied it to my wrist. It made my arm float. I looked like I was waving at nobody.

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to the "Automat" again. I wanted another story.

I put in a quarter. $25.00.

The little door opened. There was a small scroll inside.

I took it out. It read: "The balloon took him away, and he never touched the ground again."

"That's funny," I said. "I just bought a balloon."

Sammy put in a quarter. He got: "The soup was cold, but he ate it anyway."

"Very literary," Sammy said.

We sat and ate our paper. It tasted like ink and prophecy.

At 3:00 PM, I got a call on my wrist. It was the "Metropolitan Museum of Art".

"Andy," the director said. "We have a problem. The Mona Lisa is fading."

"What happened?" I asked.

"She's lonely," he said. "She needs a companion. We need you to come over and sit next to her. Just for an hour. To boost her engagement metrics."

"Do I have to pay to get in?" I asked.

"We'll pay you," he said. "$10,000.00."

"I'll be right there," I said.

I left the Factory with my balloon. It was hard to walk. My hand was in the sky.
