Woke up at 6:00 AM. I couldn't sleep. The room was too grey. Without the blue, the grey doesn't have any contrast. It’s just... flat. Like a photograph that hasn't been developed yet. I feel unpainted.

Archie was on the bed. He was blending into the sheets.

"I'm disappearing," Archie said. "I'm becoming part of the pattern."

"Don't disappear," I said. "I can't afford a new cat. The exchange rate is terrible."

"I'm not disappearing," Archie said. "I'm just being subtle. It’s very chic."

I went to the kitchen. The "Campbell's Soup" cans were still blank. Just silver. I opened a can of "Campbell's Mushroom Soup". $2.50. I poured it into a bowl. It was grey. It looked like cement.

"Tastes like clouds," I said. "Grey, fluffy clouds."

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. $400.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Statistician. He had a graph for a face. The line was going down.

"Business is bad," the driver said. "Nobody is buying anything. They're just staring."

"Staring is free," I said. "It's the best bargain."

When I got to the Factory, the window was gone. In its place was a painting of a window. It was very realistic. But it was closed.

"I can't see out," I said. "I feel like I'm in a coffin."

"It’s a very nice coffin," Sammy said. He was sitting on the floor. He looked blurry. Like he was losing resolution. "I think I'm running out of pixels."

"Sit still," I said. "You'll last longer."

Bob was in the corner. He was completely grey now. He looked like a bocce ball.

"I have no color," Bob said. "I have no data. I'm just... waiting."

"Waiting is good," I said. "It’s what I do best."

Truman was there. He was holding a bucket of paint. It was a strange color. It was a color that didn't have a name. It was like a bruise.

"I stole this," Truman said. "From a rainbow in the 22nd century."

"Is it expensive?" I asked.

"It’s priceless," Truman said. "Which means it’s worthless. We can't sell it."

"I'll buy it," I said. "How much for the bruise?"

"Your shadow," Truman said.

"Sold," I said. "I don't use it anyway. It follows me everywhere. It’s very clingy."

At 11:00 AM, I started to paint. I painted over the grey wall. I used Truman's paint. It made a noise when it touched the wall. *Squelch*. It was a wet noise.

"What is it?" Sammy asked.

"It’s a Wednesday," I said. "It’s how Wednesdays feel."

"It looks like a headache," Sammy said.

"That’s because you’re looking at it with your eyes," I said. "You have to look at it with your wallet."

At 1:00 PM, the man in the grey suit came back. The one from the Bureau of Consistency. He looked at the wall.

"You have a smear," he said. "It’s... inconsistent."

"It’s art," I said.

"It’s a violation," he said. "You have to pay the fee."

"I paid the fee," I said. "I paid it with my shadow."

"Shadows are depreciating," he said. "They’re worth less than pesos."

He reached out to wipe the wall. But the wall grabbed him. The painting sucked him in. He became part of the bruise.

"Get me out," he said. His voice was muffled. Like he was underwater.

"You're part of the art now," I said. "You're famous. You're going to be in a museum."

"I don't want to be in a museum," he said. "Museums are for dead people."

"So is New York," I said. "And look how popular it is."

At 3:00 PM, we went to lunch. We walked to the MoMA Design Store. It wasn't a store anymore. It was an installation. The price tags were on the floor. They were huge. Giant numbers floating in the air.

"I'll take the '5'," I said. "It’s a nice number. Very symmetrical."

"That’s five hundred dollars," the cashier said. She was a mannequin. A very chic mannequin.

"I'll give you three," I said. "And a concept of a cat."

"Deal," she said.

I walked out with a giant number 5. It was heavy. It felt like a legacy.

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, Bob started to spin. He was spinning faster and faster.

"I'm a turbine," Bob said. "I'm generating power."

"Don't blow away," I said. "You're the only heavy thing we have."

"I'm generating blue," Bob said. A small puff of blue smoke came out of his USB port. It smelled like ozone and grape soda.

"Save it," I said. "We can bottle it."

"It’s just gas," Bob said.

"All art is just gas," I said.

At 7:00 PM, I called Jean-Michel. I wanted to tell him about the blue.

"I lost my house," Jean-Michel said. "It floated away. It’s somewhere over New Jersey now."

"Did you paint it?" I asked.

"I painted it black," he said. "It was too dark. It absorbed the light and it floated away like a solar balloon."

"Can you get a refund?" I asked.

"I'm keeping the receipt," he said. "I'm going to frame it."

At 8:30 PM, I walked home. The sidewalks were still moving, but they were moving slower. They were tired. The city was tired.

I saw a cloud. A real cloud. It was floating down 5th Avenue. It was bumping into the streetlights. It looked lost.

"Excuse me," the cloud said. "Is this the subway?"

"It’s the street," I said. "But you can take the train if you want. Just don't get your rain on the seats."

"Thanks," the cloud said. It drifted into a sewer grate. I guess it was going downtown.

When I got home, Archie was still on the bed. He was barely visible.

"I'm still here," Archie said.

"I know," I said. "I can hear you."

I took off my wig. My head felt cold. I looked at the giant number 5 in the corner. It looked back at me.

"You're worth more than me," I told the number.

"I know," the number said. "I'm timeless."
