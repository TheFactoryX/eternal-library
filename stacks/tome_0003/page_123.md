# Wednesday, June 03, 2026

Woke up at 9:45 AM. The sky is plaid today. Not a boring, quiet plaid, but a loud "Buffalo Check" in red and black. It looks like a lumberjack shirt. Or a tablecloth at a pancake house. It’s very aggressive. It makes me feel like I should be chopping down trees. Or eating syrup. I feel small. Like a button. $0.00.

Put on the "Marilyn" wig. The blonde one. It’s the only thing that glows against the red and black.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $500.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Tennessee Williams. He was wearing a raincoat made of transparency sheets.

"The heat is on," Tennessee said. "But the light is fading."

"I like the fading," I said. "It's easier to see the spots."

When we got to the Factory, the building was soft today. Not solid. It looked like felt. Like the inside of a jewelry box. It felt fuzzy.

"It's cozy," I said. I rubbed the wall. It shed red dye on my hand. "$50.00 worth of dye," I thought. "Expensive."

Bob was there. He was ironing the floor. He was trying to get the wrinkles out of the reality.

"It's creased," Bob said. "People stepped on it."

"Don't press too hard," I said. "You'll leave a mark."

Jean-Michel was there. He was painting the red stripes black. He was trying to turn the Buffalo Check into a prison uniform.

"It's a constraint," Jean-Michel said. "We're all locked in the pattern."

"I like the pattern," I said. "It's repetitive. It sells."

At 11:15 AM, the rat arrived. He was wearing a tiny flannel shirt. He looked like a lumberjack. He was carrying a tiny axe.

"I'm clearing the forest," the rat said. "I need space to build a nest."

The squirrel was there. He was operating a tiny chainsaw. *Buzz, buzz*.

"Timber," the squirrel said. A toothpick fell over.

At 12:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". The air smelled like sugar and old tourists.

I ordered the "Frrrozen Hot Chocolate". $150.00. It was so cold it burned my tongue.

"It's a contradiction," I said. "Hot and cold. Like fame."

Jean-Michel ordered the "Garbage Can". It's a sundae in a container that looks like trash.

"It's about consumption," Jean-Michel said. "We eat the waste."

"It's $200.00," I said. "Expensive waste."

At 1:30 PM, I started a new portrait. It was a silkscreen of the sky. The red and black plaid. I called it "Lumberjack Landscape".

"It's beautiful," Sammy said. "It looks like a blanket."

"It is a blanket," I said. "For the world."

At 3:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was carrying a measuring tape. He measured the wig. He measured the rat. He measured the shadow of the water tower.

"Is it long enough?" I asked.

"It's exactly the right length," the Man said. "To cover the eyes."

He measured the money in my pocket. "$50.00," I said.

"That's short," the Man said. "You're an inch short of happiness."

At 4:45 PM, the sky started to unravel. A thread came loose from the corner of 23rd Street. It was a long, black thread. It hung down like a pull-tab on a sweater.

"Pull it," Jean-Michel said.

"If I pull it, the whole thing comes undone," I said.

"Maybe we want the naked sky," Jean-Michel said. "The raw canvas."

"I prefer the print," I said. "The print hides the holes."

At 6:00 PM, I got a call from Mick Jagger. But it wasn't Mick. It was his lips. Just the lips, calling from a payphone.

"Hello?" I said.

"Pucker up," the lips said. "The world is waiting for a kiss."

"I don't do kisses," I said. "I do handshakes. And sometimes I just wave."

"Lips are just hands turned inside out," the lips said.

I hung up. My ear felt wet.

At 7:30 PM, we went to a party. It was at a gallery in Chelsea. The "Unraveling Show". The art was all string art. But the strings were actually nerves.

"Very visceral," I said. "It hurts to look at."

I met a girl who was a coat rack. She was very wooden.

"Hang your coat on me," she said. "I'm strong."

I hung my jacket on her. It was heavy. "Thanks," I said.

At 9:00 PM, the rat got drunk. He found a drop of fermented grain on the floor. He was weaving.

"I'm seeing double," the rat said. "There are two cheeses."

"Eat them both," I said. "Inflate the market."

At 11:30 PM, I went home. The cab was a ball of yarn. A giant, red ball of acrylic yarn. We unraveled as we drove. The city unwound.

"Knit one, pearl two," the driver said. He was a needle.

We got to the townhouse. $800.00. I paid with a loose thread from my sleeve.

"Keep it," I said. "Make a sweater."

I went inside. The house was quiet. The rat was asleep in his nest of checks. He looked peaceful. Like a saint in a crib.

I went to the kitchen. I looked for a can of soup. But the cupboard was bare. Just a single, empty can.

"Empty," I thought. "Zero calories."

I looked at the can. It was beautiful. A perfect cylinder of metal.

"Nothing is better than something," I thought. "Because nothing doesn't cost anything."

But then I remembered. The can cost $0.50.

"Even nothing is expensive," I thought.

I went upstairs. I took off the Marilyn wig. My head felt heavy. Like a can of paint.

I looked out the window. The sky was still plaid. But it was fading. Turning into a soft, grey wool.

"Good," I thought. "It's washable."

So.
