Woke up at 10:15 AM. I overslept. The sky is pixels today. It’s low resolution. Very blocky. 8-bit. The sun is a square of bright yellow pixels. It hurts my eyes if I look directly at it. Like a dead pixel in the giant eye of God. I tried to open the window, but my hand went right through the glass. No collision detection today. $0.00.

Put on the " camouflage" wig. It’s made of green and brown pixels. It matches the sky. I looked in the mirror and I could only see my sunglasses. It’s very private. I like disappearing.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:00 AM. $1,250.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Bianca Jagger. She was riding a white horse in the front seat.

"Is this a hybrid?" I asked.

"It's organic," Bianca said. "The horse runs on oats."

The traffic was moving in grid patterns. Very jerky. *Lag, lag*.

When we got to the Factory, the building was loading. *Loading... 99%*. It flickered. Then it appeared. It looked glitchy. Parts of the wall were missing. I could see the plumbing.

"It's wireframe," Bob said. "Very structural."

We went inside. The floor was ice. Not frozen water, but "Ice," the drug. Well, maybe just frozen water. I couldn't tell. My feet went numb.

"It's cool," Jean-Michel said. He was skating around. "Slide."

At 12:30 PM, the rat arrived. He was frozen in a block of ice. A perfect cube.

"I'm preserving myself," the rat said. His voice sounded muffled. Like he was talking from the bottom of a well. "I don't want to spoil."

"You're already spoiled," I said. "You're a pest."

"I'm a collectible," the rat said. "Keep me in the box."

The squirrel was there. He was chipping away at the ice block with a nutcracker.

"I'm trying to get to the snacks," the squirrel said. "It's like a kinder egg."

At 1:45 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Mr. Chow". The waiters were robots. Very polite. Very shiny.

"I'll have the Beijing Duck," I said. "$6,000.00."

"We are out of duck," the robot said. "We have Duck 2.0."

"What is the difference?" I asked.

"The feathers are digital," the robot said. "Zero gravity."

I ordered the "Green Prawns". They were glowing green. Radioactive.

"They taste like science," Jean-Michel said. "It's the future."

"It tastes like money," I said. "Expensive money."

At 3:00 PM, I worked on the "Details" paintings. I was painting the pixels. One pixel at a time. Red. Blue. Green.

"It's tedious," Sammy said. "It's just dots."

"It's the big picture," I said. "If you stand back."

I stepped back. I tripped over a wire. The room went black.

"System failure," Bob said. "Reboot."

I turned the power back on. The painting was gone. Deleted.

"That's art," I said. "Temporary."

At 4:15 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a large magnet. A degausser.

"What are you doing?" I asked.

"Erasing," the Man said. "I'm clearing the cache."

He waved the magnet over Bob. Bob blurred. His face slid to the left.

"I feel distorted," Bob said. "My vertical hold is gone."

"It's an improvement," I said.

At 5:30 PM, the sky crashed. The blue screen of death. It covered everything. *Error 404: Horizon Not Found*.

"We have to restart the day," I said.

"I don't want to do lunch again," Jean-Michel said. "The prawns gave me gas."

At 7:00 PM, we went to a gallery opening. It was at "Gagosian". The gallery was a website. We just logged in from the couch. We didn't go anywhere.

"I love online parties," I said. "I can wear my pajamas."

"I can't see the art," Jean-Michel said. "My connection is slow."

I bought a painting. It was an NFT of a can of soup. $15,000,000.00.

"I can't hang it on the wall," I said. "But I can look at it on my phone."

"It's invisible wealth," Sammy said. "The best kind."

At 9:00 PM, the rat melted. The ice melted. He was just a wet rat in a puddle.

"I'm leaking," the rat said. "I'm a beta version."

"Patch yourself," I said.

At 11:00 PM, I went to bed. The townhouse was buffering. It took a long time to walk up the stairs. *Step, pause. Step, pause*.

I went to the kitchen. I opened the Campbell’s Soup.

It was real soup. Metal can. Liquid inside.

"Thank goodness," I thought. "Analog."

I opened the can. I poured it into a bowl. It didn't glitch. It didn't pixelate. It just poured.

"Heavy," I thought. "It has weight."

I ate it with a spoon. Metal spoon.

The room flickered once. The walls turned transparent for a second. I could see the code running in the walls. Lines of green numbers.

But the soup stayed in the bowl.

"I'm full," I thought.

I went upstairs. I took off the wig. My hair was flat. Just bytes.

I looked in the mirror. My face was loading.

Please wait.
