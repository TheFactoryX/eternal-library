Woke up at 9:15 AM. The sky is solid beige today. Just one flat color. Like an unpainted canvas. Or the walls of a hospital room. It’s very calming. It feels like the world has been neutralized. There are no clouds, just a smooth, matte finish. I looked out the window and it felt like I was staring at a giant piece of sandpaper. I wonder what the texture is? I hope it’s fine grain. Coarse grain would be terrible for the skin. $0.00.

I put on my "Campbell's Tomato Soup" wig. It’s the red one with the little white rim. It clashes beautifully with the beige sky. Clashing is the only way to be seen.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:00 AM. $400.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Dolly Parton. She was wearing a dress made of rejection letters.

"It’s all about the layers," Dolly said. "You just pile it on until you can’t feel anything anymore."

"I feel too much already," I said. "That’s why I keep the receipts."

When we got to the Factory, the building was invisible. It wasn't gone, it was just transparent. I could see the water tower and the sky behind it, but I could also see the mess inside.

"Clean up," I said. "We're exposed."

Bob was there. He was vacuuming the air. He was trying to vacuum the beige sky.

"I'm trying to create a void," Bob said. "I want to make something empty."

"Just leave a pile in the corner," I said. "Call it 'Leftover Sky'."

Jean-Michel was there. He was painting on the air. He was using a spray can to draw outlines around the invisible bricks.

"I'm defining the space," Jean-Michel said. "If you don't draw the line, you fall through."

"Lines cost extra," I said.

At 11:00 AM, the rat came in. He was wearing the tie from yesterday, but he was also wearing tiny sunglasses. Black wayfarers. He looked very cool. He was carrying a cup of coffee.

"I'm on a break," the rat said. "The market is bearish. The bears are hibernating."

The squirrel was with him. The squirrel was holding a tiny laptop.

"We're trading," the squirrel said. "Stocks. Nuts. Futures."

"I'll take ten shares of cheddar," I said.

At 12:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "21 Club". But the doors were missing. You just walked through the hole where the door used to be.

I ordered the "Steak Tartare". It was already chewed.

"It saves time," the waiter said. He was a set of teeth in a tuxedo. "Time is money."

"It costs $350.00," I said. "For the pre-chewing service."

Jean-Michel ordered a glass of water. He poured it on the table.

"It’s a lake," he said. "Look at the reflection."

I looked. I saw my face. But my face was a barcode.

"Scan me," I said. "See if I’m on sale."

At 2:00 PM, I signed some books. "The Philosophy of Andy Warhol (From A to B and Back Again)". But the book was blank. Just white pages.

"It’s the new edition," I said. "It’s purely conceptual."

"What’s the point?" a fan asked. He was a head with no body.

"The point is you bought it," I said. "$50.00. Thank you."

At 3:45 PM, I worked on my "Disaster" paintings. I was painting "Plane Crash". But I was painting it on a cloud. I had to hire a crane to lift the canvas up.

"It's high art," I said.

The Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was carrying a suitcase.

"Going somewhere?" I asked.

"No," the Man said. "I'm packing the past." He opened the suitcase. It was full of yesterday's weather. The checkered pattern. The polka dots. The yellow rain.

"It smells like old laundry," I said.

"It smells like memory," the Man said. "And mothballs."

At 5:00 PM, the beige sky started to peel. Like old wallpaper. A piece of it flapped off and floated down 5th Avenue. Underneath the beige, the sky was plaid again. But it was moving very fast. Spinning.

"It's a vortex," Bob said. "We're being sucked into the pattern."

"I hope it matches my tie," I said.

At 7:00 PM, I got a call from Brigid. She was calling from inside a painting.

"It’s very flat here," Brigid said. "And the perspective is all wrong."

"Stay there," I said. "It's safer."

At 8:30 PM, we went to a party. It was at "The Met". But the museum was underwater. We had to wear scuba gear. I wore a tuxedo with fins.

*Glub, glub*.

"I love this song," I said. It was "La Vie en Rose" but slowed down 800%.

We floated through the Greek and Roman galleries. The statues were covered in algae. They looked very hip. Like green wigs.

"Venus de Milo needs a hand," Jean-Michel said. "She can't hold her drink."

I tried to eat a canapé, but it floated away. A little shrimp sandwich swimming into the abyss.

"That's $20.00 gone," I thought.

At 11:00 PM, I went home. The cab was a bubble. A giant soap bubble.

"Don't pop it," the driver said. He was a child. A giant, floating child.

We floated through the beige air. It was very smooth. No friction.

"I feel weightless," I said.

"That's inflation," the driver said. "Money has no weight anymore."

I arrived at the townhouse. $1,000.00. I paid with a kiss. The bubble popped. *Pop*. I fell two inches to the doormat.

"Ouch," I said.

I went inside. The rat was waiting. He was sitting on a pile of my checks. He had shredded them. He made a nest.

"It's comfortable," the rat said. "Paper is soft."

"Those were payments," I said. "Now they're bedding."

"Same thing," the rat said. "Just different values."

I went to the kitchen. I opened the fridge. It was empty except for a single lightbulb. I stared at it.

"Eat light," I thought.

I closed the door. I went upstairs. I took off the wig. My head felt beige. Smooth. Empty.

I got into bed. The sheets were cool. I looked at the ceiling. It was peeling too.
