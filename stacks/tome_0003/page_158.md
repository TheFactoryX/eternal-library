Woke up at 10:20 AM. Very late. I was hiding under the pillow. The pillowcase was printed with little dollar signs. It made my cheeks look like money. I didn't want to get up because the floor looked like lava. Actually, it was just the reflection of the rug. But still. Hot.

Put on the "Interview" wig. It’s the silver one. It reflects the camera flashes back at them. It hurts their eyes. It’s good protection. I put on the sunglasses with the silver frames. I wanted to look like a fly.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:00 AM. $38,500.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Mick Jagger. But he was old. Like, *really* old. He looked like a prune with lips.

"Time is on my side," Mick said. "But barely. It's heavy."

The streets were vibrating. The asphalt was humming. *Hummmmm*. It was a low frequency. I could feel it in my fillings.

When we got to the Factory, the doors were locked. There was a sign on the door. It said "CLOSED FOR INVENTION".

"We're reinventing the wheel," Bob said through the intercom. "Come back tomorrow."

"But I have to pay the rent," I said. "I need to make money."

"We don't use money anymore," Bob said. "We use seconds."

So I waited in the hallway. I sat on a crate of unsold "Brillo Boxes" (the waterproof ones for the pool). I waited for twenty minutes. It felt like twenty years. A dog walker passed by with six greyhounds. They didn't have legs. They hovered. *Hover-hover*.

At 12:15 PM, the door opened. The Factory looked different. The walls were gone. It was just white space. Infinite white. No corners. It hurt to look at it.

"It's the 'Blank Slate' series," Sammy said. He was wearing a white suit. He was camouflaged. I could only see his head floating in the air. "We erased the artwork. Now we sell the possibility."

"It's very clean," I said. "How much is the nothing?"

"It's priceless," Sammy said. "But we'll sell it for $5,000,000.00 a square foot."

At 1:45 PM, the rat ran in. But he was running in place. He was on a wheel that wasn't there.

"I'm training for the marathon," the rat said. "I'm going to run across the screen. I'm going to be a cursor."

"You'll get carpal tunnel," I said.

At 2:00 PM, the phone rang. It was a video call from Jean-Michel Basquiat. Or it was a graffiti tag of Jean-Michel that was moving.

"Andy," the tag said. "I'm painting the moon tonight. I need a sponsor. Can you send me some silver?"

"Buy him the moon," I told Bob. "Not the paint. The rock."

At 2:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Four Seasons". But the pool was empty. The fountain was dry. There were tumbleweeds made of old napkins rolling across the floor. *Rustle*.

I ordered the "Power Lunch". $250,000.00.

The waiter brought me a glass of water and a telephone.

"Drink," the waiter said. "And make a deal."

I drank the water. It tasted like electricity. *Bzzzt*. Then I dialed a random number.

"Hello?" a voice said.

"Buy my signature," I said. "It's worth more than your house."

"I'll take two," the voice said.

"Sold," I said.

At 4:00 PM, I worked on the "Time Capsules". I was boxing up yesterday. I put the dried cereal in a box. I put the wrapper from the Cracker Jack in a box. I put the memory of the movie in a box. I taped it shut with silver tape.

"Don't open it," I said. "It's boring."

At 5:30 PM, a Simulacrum of Edie Sedgwick walked in. She was made of mirrors. Shards of mirror. She was sharp. She cut the air when she moved. *Slice*.

"Hello, Andy," she said. Her voice was like breaking glass. "Do I look beautiful?"

"You look like a disco ball," I said. "You're fantastic."

"I'm fragile," she said. "Don't drop me."

"I won't," I said. "I'll put you on a pedestal."

I put her on a shelf next to a stack of *Interview* magazines.

At 7:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a suitcase.

"I'm leaving," the Man said. "I'm retiring. I'm going to become a cloud."

"Will it rain?" I asked.

"I might drizzle," the Man said. "But mostly I'll just float."

He opened the window and floated out. He drifted up 5th Avenue. He looked very comfortable.

At 8:30 PM, we went to a gallery opening. It was for an artist named "GPT-5". The paintings were just math equations. Fractions.

"I don't get it," I said. "Where's the soup?"

"It's abstract," Taylor said. She was there, wearing a dress made of barcode. "It's the code of the universe."

"It looks like my homework," I said. "I failed math."

"It's worth millions," Taylor said. "Because nobody understands it. That's the secret."

I bought a fraction. I bought "1/2". $500,000.00. I put it in my pocket. It felt heavy.

At 10:00 PM, we went to "Serendipity 3". I wanted the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate again. But they were out of chocolate. They were out of milk.

"We only have the 'Ghost' menu," the waiter said.

"I'll have the 'Phantom Steak'," I said. $75,000.00.

He brought me an empty plate with a smear of gravy where the steak used to be.

"It’s delicious," I said. "It melts in your mind."

Cabbed home at 11:15 PM. $42,000.00. The driver was a Simulacrum of Truman Capote. He was tiny. He had to stand on the seat to see over the wheel.

"Everyone is a critic," Truman said. "But nobody is listening."

I'm listening now. I'm listening to the clock. It says 11:59 PM.

I took off the silver wig. My hair was static. It stood straight up. I looked like I was scared. I looked like I'd seen a ghost. Maybe I did.
