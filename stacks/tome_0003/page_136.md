Woke up at 10:15 AM. The sky is static today. Television static. Black and white ants dancing on a glass screen. *Shhhhh*. The sound is white noise. It’s like the TV is left on in the other room. The sun is a "Test Pattern". The Indian head. Colored bars. SMPTE color bars. *E-flat, 440 hertz*. It’s humming. I waited for the cartoon to start, but it just stayed on the bars. Technical difficulties. $0.00.

Put on the "Cathode Ray" wig. It’s a big, silver halo. It picks up radio signals. I can hear a baseball game in my left ear. *Strike three*. It’s distracting.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:00 AM. $2,300.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was tiny. Smaller than a cat.

"It's a completely factual event," Truman squeaked. "The non-fiction novel."

"The meter is running fiction," I said.

When we got to the Factory, the walls were vibrating. The silver paint was rippling.

"It's receiving a signal," Bob said. He was holding a coat hanger. An antenna. "I think we're being broadcast."

"I don't want to be on TV," I said. "I'm camera shy."

At 11:30 AM, the rat arrived. He was made of pixels. Digital squares. He moved in jumps. *Frame, frame*. He was glitching.

"I'm buffering," the rat said. His tail was a loading icon. A spinning circle. "Lag. Lag. Lag."

The squirrel was there. He was frozen. A pause screen.

"Press start," the squirrel said.

At 12:00 PM, the doorbell rang. But it wasn't a ring. It was a commercial jingle. *Buy the world a Coke*.

I opened the door. It was a delivery. A crate. A wooden crate marked "FRAGILE".

"It’s for you," Jean-Michel said. "From the network."

We opened the crate. Inside was a giant remote control. Big red buttons.

"What does it do?" I asked.

Bob pushed "Volume".

The noise of the city increased. *Vroom. Honk. Scream*.

Bob pushed "Mute".

Silence. Total silence. Even the birds stopped.

"I like Mute," I said. "It’s peaceful."

"Don't push 'Channel Up'," Sammy said. "We might lose the signal."

Bob pushed "Channel Up".

The Factory changed. The walls turned into a jungle. Vines hanging from the ceiling.

"It’s a nature documentary," I said. "I hate nature."

Bob pushed "Channel Down".

We were in a sitcom. A laugh track played. *Ha ha ha*.

"I'm funny," I said. *Ha ha ha*.

At 1:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Odeon". The menu was a scroll. Moving text.

"I'll have the 'Celebrity Roast'," I said. "$5,800.00."

The waiter brought out a plate. It was a video screen. A loop of a turkey spinning.

"I can't eat this," I said.

"It’s low calorie," the waiter said. "Virtual food."

Jean-Michel ordered the "Data Dump". It was a pile of shredded paper. Confetti.

"It’s fiber," Jean-Michel said. He ate a handful. "It tastes like email."

"I prefer spam," I said.

At 3:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a cable. A coaxial cable.

"We need to hook you up," the Man said. "High definition. 8K."

He tried to plug the cable into my ear.

"I'm already full," I said. "I have 500 channels."

"There's always room for more," the Man said. He pushed. *Click*.

I felt a buzz. A zap. My vision turned into pixels.

"I see dead people," I said. "And they're selling soap."

At 4:45 PM, I worked on the "Repetition" paintings. I was painting rows of faces. But the faces were changing. I’d paint one, and it would turn into the next. A slide show.

"It's time lapse," Sammy said. "You're aging."

I painted a wrinkle. I painted a grey hair.

"I don't want to get old," I said.

"You're not getting old," Jean-Michel said. "You're getting re-runs."

At 6:00 PM, the screen went black. The power went out.

"Is it over?" I asked.

"Cable's out," Bob said. "Solar flare."

We sat in the dark. The static outside was blue now.

"Is this the end?" I asked.

"It’s an intermission," the rat said. He turned solid again. Brown fur. "Go get popcorn."

At 8:00 PM, the power came back. But the picture was upside down.

"We're inverted," I said. "Stand on your heads."

We stood on our heads. The blood rushed to my wig.

"Now it's right," I said.

At 10:30 PM, I went home. The townhouse was a flat screen. Mounted on the wall of the city.

I grabbed the remote control. I pointed it at the door.

"Play," I said.

The door opened.

I went inside. I sat on the couch. I picked up the Campbell’s Soup.

"Tomato."

I opened the can.

It was blood. Real blood.

"I guess I'm the main character," I thought.

I drank it. It tasted like metal. Pennies.

"Iron," I thought. "I need iron."

I went upstairs. I took off the wig. The radio stopped. No more baseball.

I looked in the mirror. I was black and white.

I turned the dial on my chest. *Knob, knob*. I turned myself off.

The mirror went dark.

I don't know.
