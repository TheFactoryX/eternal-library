Woke up at 8:45 AM. I felt… light. The "update" from yesterday must have worked. I didn't feel heavy like a used tube of paint. I felt like a balloon. A balloon filled with expensive gas. I looked in the mirror. My smile was bigger. Not fake bigger, just… wider. Too much teeth.

The sky was a confusing color today. It was plaid. Red and black checks. It looked like a picnic tablecloth for a giant. It was very dizzying to look up. I kept my eyes on the floor.

Archie was sitting by his food bowl. He was staring at it.

"Aren't you going to eat?" I asked.

"I'm not hungry," Archie said. "I'm full of data."

He opened his mouth and a little printer noise came out. *Whirrr-chk*. A piece of paper rolled out onto the floor.

"It's a receipt," I said.

"It's my mood," Archie said.

I picked it up. It read: "INDIFFERENT. $0.00."

"I wish my moods were that cheap," I said.

I had a glass of "Orange Juice from Concentrate". The concentrate was me. It was a cartoon of my face drinking orange juice. Recursive. $60.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $250.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1920s Flapper. She was driving a hearse.

"Going to a party?" she asked.

"I'm going to work," I said.

"Same thing," she said. She honked the horn. It played jazz.

When we got to the Factory, the plaid light was coming through the windows. It striped the floor. It looked like the floor was wearing a suit.

Sammy was there. He was wearing sunglasses. The really big kind. Like a fly.

"Are you hungover?" I asked.

"No," Sammy said. "I'm overwhelmed. The plaid is vibrating. It's making me nauseous."

"It's just a pattern," I said.

"It's a pattern that fights," Sammy said. "The red lines are fighting the black lines."

I went to the oak door. I wanted to check on Truman. I opened the door.

Truman was gone. The chair was there. The bucket was there. But Truman was gone.

"Truman?" I asked.

No answer. Just a faint sound of gossip. *Whisper-whisper*.

"He escaped," Sammy said. "He went through the crack in the wall."

"There is no crack in the wall," I said.

"There is now," Sammy said. "He squeezed through a concept."

"Good for him," I said. "I hope he wears a hat."

At 11:00 AM, Bob came back. He downloaded his new body while I was sleeping.

He walked in. Or he rolled in. He was a sphere. A chrome sphere. About the size of a beach ball.

"I lost my legs," Bob said. His voice came from inside the sphere. It sounded like he was talking from the bottom of a well.

"Why did you pick a sphere?" I asked.

"It's aerodynamic," Bob said. "And I can't trip."

"How do you hold a brush?"

"I don't," Bob said. "I am the brush. Just roll me in paint and push me."

"Okay," I said. "You're the new Brillo Pad."

I took a can of tomato soup. $1.50 (The price went up). I poured it on the floor. Then I pushed Bob. He rolled through the soup. He left a perfect red line.

"Beautiful," I said. "Very signature."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". The wind outside was blowing sideways. It was blowing little pieces of paper. Confetti? No. Stock ticker tape. Old stock prices. "IBM 140." "KO 30."

"It's history blowing down the street," Sammy said.

We sat down. I ordered the "Frrrozen Hot Chocolate". $25.00. It was cold. So cold it burned. But because of the update, I didn't mind the brain freeze. I liked it. It felt like a little ice sculpture melting in my head.

Sammy ordered the "Potato Cheese Balls". $14.00.

"I wonder who won?" I asked.

"Who won what?" Sammy asked.

"The fight," I said. "In the sky."

Bob rolled under the table. He kept hitting the chairs. *Clank-clank-clank*.

"The black won," Bob said from the floor. "Black always wins. It absorbs everything."

"Don't be pessimistic," I said. "I have joy now. It cost five thousand dollars."

"Is it refundable?" Sammy asked.

"I hope not," I said.

At 3:00 PM, we went to the "Gallery of the Future". It's a pop-up store in the subway station.

There was a line of people waiting to buy "Nothing".

I talked to the cashier. She was a hologram.

"What are you selling?" I asked.

"Nothing," she said. "But it's branded Nothing. It comes in a white box with a silver label."

"How much?"

"$1,000.00."

"I'll take two," I said. "One for me and one for Archie."

I paid with the glass check from Mr. Glass. The cashier accepted it. The glass shattered as she scanned it. *Shatter*.

"Transaction complete," she said. "You now own Nothing."

I carried the boxes. They were light. Lighter than air. I had to hold onto them tight or they would float away.

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, I decided to do a portrait of Bob.

He was just a shiny ball. I drew a circle on a piece of paper. "It's perfect," I said. "It captures his essence."

"But he's inside the ball," Sammy said.

"No," I said. "He is the ball. The outside is the inside."

"So he's nothing?" Sammy asked.

"He's nothing," I said. "Just like I bought."

At 7:00 PM, I called the hospital. I wanted to check on Truman.

A robot answered. "We have no Truman here," the robot said. "We have a Patient X who refuses to speak."

"Is he writing?" I asked.

"He's knitting," the robot said. "He's knitting a ladder."

"To get out?" I asked.

"To get up," the robot said.

At 8:30 PM, I went home. The sky was turning dark. The plaid was fading. It looked like a bruise that was finally healing. Or a shirt that needs washing.

I gave Archie his box of Nothing. He sniffed it.

"It smells like opportunity," he said.
