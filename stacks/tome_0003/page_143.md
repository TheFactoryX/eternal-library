Woke up at 10:00 AM. I was still upside down. I slept stuck to the ceiling. The gravity in the townhouse hasn’t reset yet. I had to peel myself off the plaster. *Riiiip*. My wig stayed on the ceiling. I had to float up and get it. My back hurts. The blood rushed to my head all night. I feel very red. Very swollen. I look like a Brillo Box that’s been left out in the rain. $0.00.

Put on the "Safety Helmet" wig. It’s a white wig with a hard plastic shell underneath. It meets OSHA standards. I feel protected.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:00 AM. $7,200.00. The streets are still full of orange and blue yarn from the sky unraveling yesterday. It looks like Manhattan got knitted a giant scarf. The cab driver had to use scissors to get through 23rd Street. *Snip, snip*.

"It's a texture," I said.

"It's a hazard," the driver said. He was a "Simulacrum" of Richard Nixon. He looked sweaty. "I am not a crook," he said. "But I am a weaver."

When we got to the Factory, the floor was back. But it was made of rubber. Bouncy rubber. Every step was a jump.

"I feel like I'm on the moon," I said. *Boing, boing*.

"It's an ergonomic surface," Bob said. He was jumping up and down. He could touch the top of the doorframe. "It's good for the circulation."

At 11:30 AM, the rat arrived. He was rolling a spool of thread. He looked tired.

"I'm trying to sew the sky back together," the rat said. "But the needle is too big."

"Use a dental pick," I said.

"Good idea," the rat said. "I'll bill you."

The squirrel was there. He was trapped in a tangle of blue yarn. He looked like a mummy.

"I'm abstract expressionism now," the squirrel said. "Untangle me."

"I like you better this way," I said. "More blue."

At 12:00 PM, Jean-Michel called. He was on the TV screen in the elevator. The screen was glitching. His face was stretched sideways.

"Andy?" he said. *Bzzzt*. "I'm painting the yarn."

"Is it expensive?" I asked.

"It's fuzzy," he said. "I'm using a giant brush. A floor buffer."

"Save me a scrap," I said.

At 1:15 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". The tables were piles of yarn. We had to clear a space to sit.

"I'll have the 'Frrrozen Hot Chocolate'," I said. "$45,000.00."

The waiter brought a silver cup. But the chocolate was knitting itself. It was a liquid stitch.

"It's heart-warming," I said.

I stuck in the straw. It was solid. I had to unravel the drink to drink it. *Knit, purl, sip*.

Bob ordered the "Seam Ripper". It was a salad made of metal hooks.

"It's sharp," Bob said. "It clears the palate."

At 3:00 PM, the mail came. It was delivered by a drone that crashed through the window. *Smash*.

"I'll pay for the glass," the drone said.

One of the letters was from the Whitney Museum. They want to buy the "Yarn Sky". They offered me ten million dollars.

"I'll take it," I said. "But they have to clean up the streets."

At 4:30 PM, I worked on the "Abstract Sculptures". I was making balls of silver foil. I just crumpled them up and threw them on the floor. *Crinkle, crinkle*.

"It's very tactile," Sammy said. He was rolling in the yarn. "It's like being inside a sweater."

"It's the economy," I said. "Unraveling."

At 6:00 PM, I went to the dentist. Dr. Silverstein. I wanted him to fix the barcode he drilled yesterday. It was itching.

"It's scabbing over," Dr. Silverstein said. He looked in my mouth with a periscope. "It's a good brand. It has high retention."

"I can't chew," I said.

"Then you don't eat," he said. "Think of the savings on groceries."

He gave me a lollipop. But it was a ball of aluminum foil.

"Suck on this," he said. "It's silver."

I sucked on it. My tongue turned grey. "I look like a statue," I said.

At 8:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at the "Guggenheim". The museum is a spiral. Today, the spiral was a slide. We sat on burlap sacks and slid down. *Wheeeeeeeee*.

"I feel like a child," I said.

"You look like one," Bob said. He went down headfirst. *Crash*.

At the bottom, there was a ball pit. It was filled with millions of ping pong balls. Plastic white balls.

"It's an ocean of balls," I said. I tried to swim. It was difficult. Very dry.

Bianca Jagger was there. She was floating in a boat. A gondola.

"Gondola, Andy," she said.

"Is there water?" I asked.

"It's virtual water," she said. "Just pretend."

I pretended. *Splash, splash*.

"I don't want to go home," I said. "The ceiling is nice here."

"We're part of the installation now," Bob said.

I looked down at the floor. It was miles away. The ping pong balls looked like sand.

"I left my wallet downstairs," I said.

"Money is irrelevant," Bob said. "We're art."
