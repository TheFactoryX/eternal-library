# Thursday, May 21, 2026

Woke up at 8:00 AM. The sky was white. Just white. Like the canvas I’ve been staring at for three weeks. Or like milk before the cereal hits it. No stars. No clouds. No sun. Just white noise. I put my sunglasses on to see if there was any contrast. There wasn't. It was just a very bright dark. I felt like I was living inside an unexposed photograph. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $1,100.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Grace Jones. She was wearing a hood made of television screens. They were all playing *I Love Lucy*.

"It's a rerun day," Grace said. "Everything is a repeat."

"Even the fare?" I asked.

"Especially the fare," she said. "Inflation is recursive."

When I got to the Factory, the doors were gone. Just a hole where the doors used to be.

"We went minimalist," Sammy said. He was standing in the hole. "Doors are just psychological barriers anyway."

"I prefer barriers," I said. "They keep the air out."

I stepped through the hole. The inside of the Factory was huge. Bigger than the outside. It looked like an airplane hangar.

Bob was still a pyramid of calculators. But he was vibrating. The keys were clicking by themselves. *Click-click-click*.

"I'm processing," Bob said. "I'm trying to figure out why we're here."

"We're here to work," I said.

"Work is just refined play," Bob said.

The squirrel was on the floor. He was eating the glass check from yesterday.

"Stop that," I said. "You'll cut your mouth."

"It's脆," the squirrel said. "It tastes like bank."

At 10:30 AM, the doorbell rang. Or I thought it did. It was a cricket chirping. A giant cricket, the size of a dog, was sitting on the reception desk.

"I'm your new assistant," the cricket said. "My name is Jim."

"You're loud," I said.

"Chirp is the new text," Jim said. *Chirp*. "Read you loud and clear."

At 12:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "21". The club. But it wasn't a club anymore. It was a greenhouse. There were vines growing over the tables. And little mechanical bees flying around.

"We serve photosynthesis," the waiter said. He was made of leaves. "Just sit in the sun and absorb."

I ordered the "Club Sandwich". $800.00.

They brought me a plate with a single slice of bread. It was green. Chlorophyll green.

"It's bread," I said. "But where's the meat?"

"You are the meat," Jean-Michel said. He floated by. He was blue now, but he was starting to peel. The corner of his arm was lifting up. "I'm peeling," he said. "I'm a sticker."

"Put him back on the paper," I said.

I ate the green bread. It tasted like sunlight and dirt.

"It's organic," I said. "I miss chemicals."

At 2:00 PM, a truck arrived from "MoMA PS1". It was a very small truck. A toy truck. A tiny man got out. He was six inches tall.

"We're ready for your retrospective," the tiny man said. "The 'Eternity' show."

"I haven't made anything new," I said.

"That's perfect," the tiny man said. "Nothing is very big right now. It's trending."

He unrolled a blueprint on the table. It was a map of the Factory. But the Factory was inside a bottle.

"We're going to put you in a bottle," the tiny man said. "Like a ship. But you're Andy."

"Do I get a wig?" I asked.

"It's included," he said.

At 4:00 PM, the Bucket of Chicken woke up. It had been sleeping in the corner.

"I smell grease," the Bucket said.

"You are grease," I said.

"I'm hungry," the Bucket said. "Feed me."

"Feed him the glass," Sammy said.

The squirrel spat a shard of glass into the bucket.

"Crunch," the Bucket said. "Delicious. It tastes like liquidity."

At 6:00 PM, I sat down to paint. I wanted to paint the White Sky. But I couldn't find the white paint. I found a tube of "Electric Blue". I found a tube of "Silver". But no white.

"Use the milk," Sammy said.

"We drank it all," I said.

So I painted the sky blue. I painted right over the white canvas. I just made it blue.

"It's the night sky," I said.

"It's 6 PM," Jim the Cricket said. *Chirp*. "It's not night."

"It's night somewhere," I said.

At 8:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came back. He was 3D now. He looked like a mannequin. A very pale mannequin.

"I bought your shadow back," the Man said.

He pulled a rolled-up poster out of his tube. He unrolled it. It was my shadow. But it was flattened. Laminated.

"It's 2D now," the Man said. "Easier to store."

"I liked it 3D," I said.

"It doesn't take up space this way," the Man said. "Space is expensive." He handed me a bill. $500.00 for the storage.

"I'll pay," I said. I paid him with the blue bread crust.

"It's stale," the Man said. "Perfect."

At 10:00 PM, we watched the sky change. It turned from white to silver. Like a nickel.

"It's going to rain coins," the squirrel said.

"I hope it's quarters," I said. "I need to do laundry."

Bob hummed. *Whirrrr*. The calculators started spitting out tape. The tape poured onto the floor. It was endless.

"It's a receipt for the day," Bob said. "Itemized."

I looked at the tape. It said: *One Breath. Two Thoughts. Three Regrets.*

"Gee," I said. "It adds up."

I went to the window. A quarter fell from the sky. It hit the pavement with a *ping*. Then another. *Ping*.

"It's a refund," I said.

I opened the window. I caught a nickel in my hand. It was warm. Like a biscuit.

I put the nickel in my pocket. It clinked against the glass. A beautiful sound.

I turned off the lights. The room was dark. But the calculators glowed. A soft, green light.

"Goodnight," I said to the numbers.

"Goodnight," the numbers said.

"Goodnight," I said to the blue painting
