Woke up at 6:00 AM. I didn't want to, but the room was vibrating. The "Ghost-Mop" dehydrator was still running. It made a *hummmmm* sound that rattled my teeth. The air was dry. Too dry. My skin felt like parchment paper. I cracked a smile and my cheek made a *crinkle* sound.

The bed was back to normal. Solid. The lime green gelatin had evaporated. The sheets were stiff, like starched jeans. I lay there and looked at the ceiling. There was a watermark shaped like a shoe. A high heel. A pump.

"Hello," I said to the shoe.

It didn't answer. It just dripped once. *Plip*.

I got up. The cat was on the floor, shedding. She looked like a dust bunny with eyes. I tried to pet her, but a static shock zapped me. *Zap*.

"We're both electrified," I said. "We're batteries."

She rubbed against my leg. *Bzzzt*. Now I'm charged.

I went to the kitchen. The Roomba was stuck in the corner. It was spinning in circles. *Whirr-whirr*. It looked confused. I poured myself a glass of tomato juice. The "Heavy" kind. $12.00 for the bottle. It tasted like iron and pennies. Good for the blood. I drank it down in one gulp.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. $35.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was very small. He had to sit on a phone book to see over the steering wheel.

"Everyone is talking about your wig," Truman said. "The Mars landing. It’s iconic."

"I miss it," I said. "My head gets cold."

"Wear a hat," Truman said. "Hats are the new wigs. Or maybe wigs are the new faces. Who knows anymore?"

The Factory was empty. No helium balloons. No pineapples. No crowds. Just a vast, white space. It looked like a computer screen before you turn it on. Blank.

Sammy was sitting in the middle of the floor. He was meditating. He was floating about three inches off the ground.

"Where is everyone?" I asked.

"They're downloading," Sammy said. "Everyone went digital today. It's 'Download Your Soul' Thursday. Very big in the meta-verse."

"I don't have a meta-verse," I said. "I just have this room."

"Lucky," Sammy said. "The server is down. Everyone is frozen."

I walked to the back. I wanted to paint. I picked up a brush. It felt heavy. Like a log.

I opened a can of orange paint. I spilled a little on the floor. It made a puddle.

"Look," I said. "A sunset."

"Don't step in it," Sammy said. "It's wet memory."

I stepped over it. I didn't want to remember today yet.

Lunch was at Serendipity 3. But the tables were gone. We had to eat standing up. It was very European.

I ordered the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $35.00. It was served by a "Simulacrum" of Rudolph Nureyev. He was wearing a tuxedo made of ice. He was melting a little. There was a puddle around his feet.

"Dance while you eat," Rudolph said. "It helps digestion."

I took a sip. It was cold. It gave me a headache. An ice cream headache.

"Ouch," I said.

"Pain is just weakness leaving the body," Rudolph said. "Or maybe it's just brain freeze."

I paid the bill. The money was wet from Rudolph’s puddle. The cashier didn't mind. She was a robot. She just scanned the bills and beeped. *Accepted*.

Went to a gallery opening at 3:00 PM. It was for a new artist named "XÆA-12." Or maybe that was just the name of the show. "Nothing, Nothing, Nothing."

The room was full of invisible sculptures. We had to wear special goggles to see them.

I put on the goggles. The room was full of pink neon spiders. They were dancing.

"Beautiful," I said. "Very scary."

A woman tapped me on the shoulder. It was Bianca Jagger. But she was half-horse. She had a horse’s body and a human head. A centaur.

"Andy," she said. "You look terrible."

"I'm dehydrated," I said. "And I lost my wig."

"I love it," she said. "It's very raw. Very vulnerable."

She trotted away. Her hooves made *click-click* sounds on the floor.

I took off the goggles. The spiders were gone. Just people in black clothes drinking white wine.

"Can I get you a drink?" a waiter asked. He was a "Simulacrum" of a young man. He looked like a runway model. Very symmetrical.

"Just water," I said. "Still."

"We only have sparkling," he said. "It's louder."

"Fine," I said.

He gave me a glass. It fizzed. *Fizz-fizz*.

I drank it. It tasted like air. Like nothing.

I checked my watch. The smiley face was blue. Blue means "Sad."

I took a cab home at 5:30 PM. $40.00. The driver was silent. He didn't have a face. Just a windshield.

I got home. The apartment was dark. The Roomba was still spinning in the corner. *Whirr*.

I sat on the couch. I turned on the TV. It was just snow. Static.

I watched the static for an hour. It was the best show on. It didn't demand anything. It just made noise. *Shhhhh*.

I ordered dinner from a place called "The Silent Kitchen." They deliver food in unmarked boxes.

I ordered a burger. $25.00.

It arrived 20 minutes later. I opened the box. It was a single sesame seed.

"Expensive," I said.

I ate the seed. It was nutty.

I looked at the cat. She was asleep.

"Goodnight," I said.

She opened one eye. *Meow*.

I turned off the TV. The room went dark. I sat there. I felt heavy. Full of seed.

I picked up the remote. I turned the TV back on.

*Shhhhh*.

It’s better than thinking.

So I watched. And watched. And the pixels got smaller and smaller until they were just dust motes.

Dust motes dancing.

I like dancing.

I closed my eyes and the dancing continued behind my eyelids. Orange and purple spots.

My own private screening.

I need to buy more tomato juice tomorrow.
