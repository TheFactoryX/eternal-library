Woke up at 9:30 AM. I overslept. The alarm didn't go off because time was stuck. I looked at the clock on the wall. The second hand was spinning backward. *Whoosh-whoosh*. It looked like a fan. I felt a breeze.

The apartment was white again. But a different white. "Eggshell white," I thought. Or maybe "Bone." I hoped it wasn't bone. Bones are so heavy.

I went to the kitchen. Archie was there. He was eating his breakfast. He looked solid again. Not a blur. He looked like a cat from a catalogue. A very expensive cat.

"Meow," he said. It sounded like a question. "Me-ow?"

"I don't know," I said. "I just work here."

I opened the fridge. It was full. Not full of food. Full of Campbell's soup cans. But they weren't soup. I picked one up. It was heavy. It rattled.

I opened the lid. It was full of diamonds. Hundreds of little diamonds. "Beanie Weenies," I thought. "Industrial diamonds."

"Bad investment," I said. "They're too hard to digest."

I took a pill for breakfast. $600.00. This one was "Coffee & Donuts". I swallowed it dry. I tasted glaze. I felt jittery. *Buzz*.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $75.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was very small. He had to stand on the seat to see over the wheel.

"Good morning, Andy," he said. "Did you hear the gossip? The sky is divorcing the clouds. It's a messy settlement. The clouds get the rain. The sky gets the blue."

"Who gets the gray?" I asked.

"The lawyers," Truman said.

The streets were wet. Not with rain. With mercury. *Silver puddles*. The cars were leaving trails like slugs. It looked very glamorous. Very sci-fi.

We passed a church. The sign outside said: "THE APOCALYPSE IS CANCELLED DUE TO LACK OF INTEREST."

"That's a relief," I said.

The Factory was loud. *Thump-thump*. Music was playing. It was the Velvet Underground. But it was a remix. The tempo was 500 BPM. It sounded like a buzzing bee. A giant bee.

Sammy was in the front room. He was painting the walls black. He was using a roller.

"I decided white is over," Sammy said. "Black is the new black. It absorbs the errors."

"Is it 'Black Black' or 'Midnight Jet'?" I asked.

"It's 'Void'," Sammy said. "It costs $4,000 a gallon. It's made from crushed black holes."

"Fabulous," I said. "Don't fall in."

I walked to the back. The office was dark. Bob was there. He was sitting in the dark. He was holding a flashlight. He was shining it under his chin.

"Andy," he said. "I have a proposition."

"Is it a business proposition or a lawsuit?" I asked.

"It's a merger," Bob said. "I'm merging with the building. The 21 Club wants to buy the Factory. They want to turn it into a cryogenic freezing center."

"For the soup?" I asked.

"For the celebrities," Bob said. "They want to freeze us. So we never get old. We just get cold."

"I'm already cold," I said. "My feet are freezing."

"We'll be assets," Bob said. "Investable ice cubes."

"I'll think about it," I said. "I have to check my schedule."

I went to my desk. I sat down. I took out my diary. I looked at the pen. It was leaking. The ink was red. It looked like blood.

I drew a line on the page. A long red line.

"It's a horizon," I thought.

Then the line moved. It crawled off the page. *Wiggle-wiggle*. It was a worm made of ink.

"Go away," I said. "I'm trying to record history."

The ink worm crawled into my ear. *Wiggle-wiggle*.

I heard a voice. It was inside my head. It sounded like the 1960s.

"Groovy," the voice said.

I shook my head. The worm fell out. *Plop*. It turned into a diamond.

"See?" I thought. "Everything turns into money eventually."

At 2:00 PM, the Jean-Michel bot came in. He was carrying a bucket of water. He was dripping.

"I washed the suit," he said. "The spray paint suit. It melted. Now I'm just 'Jean-Michel'. No bot."

"Is that good or bad?" I asked.

"It's abstract," he said. "I feel like a drawing."

He drank the water. He poured the rest over his head. He looked like a shiny stick figure.

"I want to do a portrait session," he said. "Polaroids."

"Machine's broken," I said. "It only takes pictures of ghosts now."

"We're all ghosts," he said.

"That's true," I said.

We took the photos anyway. I said "Click". He said "Cheese".

The photos came out blank. White squares.

"Perfect," I said. "Very minimalist. Very 8th grade."

At 6:00 PM, I went to the opening of "The Black Room" at the Factory. Sammy had finished painting. The room was pitch black. You couldn't see anything.

"Where is the art?" someone asked. It sounded like Bianca.

"It's the dark," I said. "It's $10,000 a ticket to sit in the dark. It's better than a movie. You can make up your own dialogue."

I sat in the corner. I couldn't see my hand in front of my face. I felt like I was inside a camera. Closed. Waiting for the flash.

I ate a canapé. It was on a plate. I couldn't see the plate. I almost ate the plate. It tasted like china. $45.00.

At 8:00 PM, the lights came back on. Blink. Blink.

Everyone was gone. Just me and the empty room. And a single red balloon floating in the corner.

"Party's over," I said to the balloon.

It bobbed. "Yes," it seemed to say.

Cabbed to "Ushers" on East 58th. $80.00. I had a craving for their "Chicken Pot Pie". But the menu had changed. It was just "Pot Pie". No chicken.

"Is it vegetable?" I asked.

"It's pot," the waiter said. "Clay. Baked clay."

"How much?" I asked.

"$200.00," he said.
