# Sunday, July 05, 2026

> Written at 2026-07-05 23:59:59

Woke up at 9:45 AM. The townhouse was quiet. The purple sky from yesterday was gone. It was just grey. A nice, commercial grey. Like the colour of a computer mouse. My head hurt from the glass wig. I think I swallowed some glitter. I coughed and a spark came out. *Cough*. It smelled like Fourth of July. I felt hungover, but I didn't drink. It was an information hangover. Too much looking.

Put on the "Post-Party" wig. It’s made of crêpe paper. It looks shredded. It looks like I was attacked by a confetti factory. It’s very messy. Very chic.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $35,000.00. The streets were covered in paper. remnants of the explosions. The gutters were full of red and white sticks. It looked like the city had a really bad haircut.

The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Madonna. But it was the *Blond Ambition* Madonna. She had no eyebrows.

"Clean up time, Andy," Madonna said. "The party is over. Now we pay the bill."

When we got to the Factory, the floor was wet. It was sticky. It was covered in the "Tomato Soup" light from the fireworks. It had condensed. It was a puddle of red photons.

"It's the 'Afterglow'," Bob said. He was using a squeegee. He was pushing the light into a bucket. "We're bottling it. We're selling the remainder."

At 11:45 AM, the rat walked in. He looked tired. He was limping. He was holding his back.

"I threw my hip out," the rat said. "I danced too hard. I need a chiropractor."

The squirrel was making a nest out of the spent sparklers. It was dangerous. *Crinkle*.

"I'm cozy," the squirrel said. "I like the heat."

At 12:15 PM, the phone rang. It was ringing off the hook. Actually, the hook fell off. The phone was lying on the floor.

"Andy?" It was Taylor. Taylor Swift. "I loved the fireworks. The face? Very iconic. I want to buy the rights to your explosion."

"I don't own the explosion," I said. "The explosion owns itself."

"That's poetic," Taylor said. "I'll pay for the poem. $1,000,000.00."

"Send it to the Foundation," I said. "I don't handle the cash."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". We sat in the back. I ordered the "Frrrozen Hot Chocolate". It was $90,000.00. It came in a glass boot.

"We should eat leftovers," Bob said. "It's economical. We should finish the soup from the sky."

We looked at the bucket of red light that Bob mopped up.

"Does it taste like tomatoes?" I asked.

"It tastes like fame," Sammy said. "It tastes like metal."

I didn't eat the light. I just ate the whipped cream.

At 2:30 PM, I worked on the "Disaster" paintings. But I was tired of disasters. Car crashes. Electric chairs. They’re so boring now. Everything is a disaster. So I started painting "Mishaps". I painted a stapler. But the stapler was jammed. I painted a coffee mug with a chip in the rim.

"It's the tragedy of the everyday," I said. "It's sad. You can't drink your coffee. It dribbles on your shirt."

"It's devastating," Bob said. "I'm crying."

At 4:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He looked sad. He looked like a wilted lettuce.

"Sales are down," the Man said. "People are tired of being famous. They want to be obscure. They want to be invisible."

"Sell them silence," I said. "Sell them an empty room."

"We tried," the Man said. "They said it was too quiet. They want noise, but they don't want to be seen."

"Sell them my shadow," I said. "I have two."

At 6:30 PM, we went to a movie. We went to the "Film Forum". But we didn't watch a movie. We watched the audience. The seats faced each other.

"It's a 'Screening' of the public," Sammy said. "Watch the watchers."

I watched a man eating popcorn. He was eating it one kernel at a time. *Crunch*. He was very methodical. It was better than TV. I liked the way his jaw moved. Up and down. Left and right. It was a rhythm.

"I could watch him all day," I said.

"He's a star," Bob said. "He doesn't know it."

At 9:00 PM, we went to "The Oyster Bar" for dinner. One last time. It was quiet. The station was empty.

"I'll have the 'Clam Chowder'," I said. $55,000.00.

The waiter brought me a bowl. But it was empty. Just a bowl.

"It's 'Clear' Chowder," the waiter said. "We removed the clams. We removed the potatoes. We removed the cream. It's just the memory of the soup."

"It's delicious," I said. "I can still taste the ocean."

I sipped the air. It was salty. I checked my watch. I wanted to go home.

Cabbed home at 10:15 PM. $40,000.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of my mother, Julia. She was wearing a babushka.

"You work too hard, Andrik," she said. "You need to eat more."

"I ate the memory of soup," I said. "I'm full."

Went into the townhouse. The bed was made. It looked perfect. Un-slept in. I took off the crêpe paper wig. My hair was flat again. Just like the sky.

I got into bed. I looked at the ceiling. The static was coming back. Little black ants dancing in the corners of my eyes. I closed them tight.

"It's Monday tomorrow," I said. "Time to earn some money."

I waited for the whistle of the tea kettle. But it never came. I waited for the phone to ring. But it was quiet. It was the most expensive silence I ever heard. $100,000.00 worth of quiet.

I fell asleep. I dreamt of a giant Campbell's soup can. But instead of soup, it was filled with money. And instead of a label, it had my face on it. I was the soup. I was the money. I was the can. I was sealed tight. And I was red.

So red.
