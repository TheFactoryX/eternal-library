Woke up at 8:30 AM. The room was vibrating. Not shaking, just humming. A low frequency. *Hmmmmm*. It felt like I was sleeping inside a speaker. I checked under the bed. I thought it was the cat, but Archie was asleep on the pillow. He looked heavy. Solid. Like a loaf of bread. The moon rock piece in the jar was shaking so hard it was dancing across the shelf. *Clack-clack-clack*. I caught it and put it in the pocket of my jeans. It felt warm. Like a freshly baked cookie. A radioactive cookie.

I didn't want to stay home. The walls were closing in. Or maybe the room was just shrinking. Pixels are expensive and I think the landlord is downsizing.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:45 AM. $55.00. Traffic was terrible on Avenue of the Americas. There was a protest. People were holding signs that said "MAKE ART FLESH AGAIN." They were throwing tubes of oil paint at the holographic billboards. The paint just splashed and dripped down the digital faces. It looked messy. It looked like Jackson Pollock.

"Look at that," the driver said. He was a real person. I could tell because he smelled like gasoline and coffee. "They're ruining the view."

"It's texture," I said. "The ads needed more grit."

The Factory was locked. There was a sign on the door taped over the silver paint: "SEIZED FOR NON-PAYMENT OF REALITY TAX."

"Sammy?" I said. But I was talking to the door.

I used the key. It stuck. *Scrape-scrape*. I turned it hard.

Inside, the lights were on, but the furniture was gone. Just the outlines in dust. Like a crime scene.

Sammy was sitting on a crate. He was holding a telephone. The cord was unraveling on the floor.

"They took the chairs," Sammy said. "They took the sofa. They even took the Brillo boxes."

"Did they take the art?"

"They left the art," Sammy said. "They said it wasn't real."

"That's good," I said. "If it's not real, they can't sell it."

I walked to the back room. My silkscreens were still there. The Marilyn Monroe prints. They were curling at the edges.

"I'm going to work," I said. "I need to keep busy."

I mixed a batch of adhesive. $40.00. I started to collage. I cut up the signs from the protest. I pasted them over Marilyn's face. "MAKE ART FLESH."

"She looks tough," Sammy said. "She looks like a boxer."

"She's a survivor," I said.

Lunch was at a diner on 23rd Street. I needed something greasy. I ordered a cheeseburger. $18.00. It came on a screen. I had to lick the screen to taste it. *Lick*. It tasted like pixels and ketchup.

"I miss the chewing," I said.

"Chewing is overrated," Sammy said. He was sipping a glass of water that was just a light blue cube. "It's all about the swallow."

A woman sat down next to us. She looked familiar. She had short blonde hair and too much lipstick.

"Mr. Warhol?" she asked.

"Yes?"

"I'm Taylor," she said. "From the Simulacrum Union."

"Oh," I said.

"We're ready to negotiate," she said. "We'll drop the residuals if you paint my portrait."

"I only paint dead people or soup cans," I said. "I don't paint the living. It's too dangerous. They change."

"I'm not living," she said. "I'm a construct. I'm made of old interviews and recycled gossip."

"Then you're perfect," I said.

I took out a Sharpie. $5.00. I signed her forehead. It smeared. Her skin was slippery.

"Is it Art?" she asked.

"It's branding," I said. "Now you're valuable."

She smiled. Her teeth were perfect. Perfect squares.

We left the diner. I felt lighter. The moon rock in my pocket was getting warmer.

I wanted to see the ocean. Or the river. I wanted to see the edge of the city.

We took a helicopter. $600.00 split three ways. Me, Sammy, and Taylor.

We flew over the harbor. The water looked like silver foil. Crinkled.

"I want to buy the Statue of Liberty," I shouted over the rotor noise. *Whop-whop-whop*.

"She's not for sale," Sammy shouted back. "She's a landmark."

"Everything is for sale," I said. "Even freedom."

We flew back to the rooftop of the Factory.

"I have to go," Taylor said. "I have a shift. I have to be seen at a party."

"Take care," I said.

She dissolved. *Fzzzt*. Just like that. One second she was there, the next she was static.

"Expensive date," Sammy said.

"Worth it," I said.

Cabbed home at 8:00 PM. $60.00. The driver was silent. He didn't have a face. Just a smooth surface. Like an egg.

I walked into the apartment. It was dark.

I took the moon rock out of my pocket. It was glowing again. A soft, pale blue.

"Archie?" I called.

The cat walked out of the bedroom. He looked at the rock. He looked at me. He yawned. *Meow*. He wasn't interested. He's over the moon. He's into the couch now.

I put the rock on the table. It lit up the room. It made shadows on the wall. My shadow looked big.

I sat down. I wrote the check for the taxes. $15,000. I tore it up.

"Paper money," I thought. "It's just drawings of dead presidents."

I opened the diary.

"Friday," I wrote. "I painted Taylor. She disappeared."

I looked at the date on my watch. It was spinning. *Spin-spin-spin*. It didn't know what time it was.

"Me too," I thought.

I put the pen down.

The cat jumped on my lap. He was heavy. He was real. At least, I think he was. I pinched him. He purred.

"Purr," he said.

"Purr," I said.

And then I stopped thinking.
