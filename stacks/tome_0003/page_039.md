Wednesday, March 11, 2026

Woke up at 9:15 AM. I slept late. I was tired. The sky was the color of spilled milk. Cloudy with a chance of meatballs, but unfortunately, it was just pigeons. I looked at the wall. The crack was gone. I looked at the safe. The "Moon Rock" was gone. There was just a puddle of yellow yolk on the shelf. It smelled like sulfur and paint. I used a sock to clean it up. It was a gym sock. I don't go to the gym, but I buy the socks. They absorb guilt better. $15.00 a pair. I threw the sock in the trash. *Plop*.

I put on the "Flesh-Colored Turtleneck" and the "Silver Gor-Tex" parka. Layering is key. The air conditioning in this century is aggressive. It’s freezing outside, but then you walk into a building and it’s a volcano.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. It was $45.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Llama. It had a long neck and kept looking back at me with big, wet eyes. It chewed on the steering wheel. *Munch-munch*. I said, "Do you take credit cards?" It spat on the floor.

When I got to the Factory, the door was huge. It was ten feet tall. I walked in without ducking. I felt small.

The floor was covered in snow. Real snow. It was inside. Sammy was making snow angels in the lobby. He was wearing shorts. He looked up. He said, "Andy, the temperature server crashed. We're stuck in Winter." I walked carefully. My boots made a crunching sound. *Crunch-crunch*. It was very atmospheric. I felt like I was in a globe. A very expensive globe.

I went to the back room. The "Car Crash" paintings were frozen in blocks of ice. It was perfect preservation. The "Blue Screen of Death" series looked like icebergs. I touched one. It was slippery. I liked it. It felt clean.

Sammy came in. He was shivering. He said, "Andy, we had a visitor last night." He pointed to the corner. There was a nest made of shredded money. Real money. Old bills. And wires. Sammy said, "It hatched."

I looked around. "What hatched?"

"The Moon Rock," Sammy said. "It was an egg. It was a crypto-egg."

I felt dizzy. I sat on a block of ice. "Is it dangerous?"

"No," Sammy said. "It's valuable. It pooped Bitcoin." He handed me a small, golden nugget. It was warm. I bit it. It was soft. Like butter. I put it in my pocket.

Around 12:30 PM, I went to lunch at "The automat." But it’s a high-end place now. You don't put coins in. You scan your retina. I ordered a "Slice of Pie." It was Macaroni and Cheese Pie. It was $100.00. It was delicious. It was hot and sticky. I sat next to a "Simulacrum" of Elvis. He was thin. He was wearing a white jumpsuit with sequins. He was eating a fried peanut butter sandwich. He said, "Andy, do you think I'm still relevant?" I said, "You're everywhere. You're pixels." He smiled. He had grease on his chin.

I went to the "Printer" at 2:00 PM. I needed to make more money. The printer was out of black ink. I used red. I printed a series of "Accidents." I printed a picture of the yolk. I called it "Hatching." I made fifty prints. I signed them all. My hand was tired.

At 4:00 PM, a "Simulacrum" of the Mayor called. He was on the video screen. He was floating in a tank. He said, "Mr. Warhol, we need to talk about the zoning." He said, "Your factory is creating weather patterns." He said, "It’s snowing in SoHo." I said, "It's art. It's immersive." He said, "It's a nuisance. I'm fining you." He sent a bill. $50,000.00 for "precipitation violations." I told Sammy to pay it with the golden poop.

We went to a party at "The Morgan Library." But it’s a disco now. They moved the books. They are in storage in New Jersey. The dancefloor was where the rare books used to be. It was tragic. It was wonderful. I danced with a "Simulacrum" of Marie Antoinette. She had a tall wig. She said, "Let them eat cake." She handed me a cupcake. It was $500.00. It was red velvet. I ate it. I got crumbs on my turtleneck. I brushed them off.

I saw "The Kid." He was wearing a suit made of mirrors. He reflected everything. He said, "Andy, did you hear? The gravity failed in Chicago." He said, "Everyone floated away. They’re in the stratosphere now. They're looking down at us." I looked up at the ceiling. "Can they see me?" I asked. He said, "Everyone sees you, Andy."

I felt exposed. I pulled my parka tighter.

Cabbed home at 10:00 PM. It was $55.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of me. He looked better than me. He looked younger. His skin was smooth. No pores. He said, "Rough day, Andy?" I said, "I'm tired of being solid." He said, "Get used to it. It's the only way to stay grounded."

I came home at 10:45. The apartment was warm. Too warm. The radiator was hissing. I took off the parka. I looked at the safe. It was open again. The yellow yolk was gone. But there was a feather in its place. A white feather.

I picked it up. It was light. Lighter than air.

I looked in the mirror. I looked old. I looked tired. I brushed the wig. The hair is falling out. I need a new one. Maybe I should get a feather one. Or a wig made of data. Just a cloud of numbers.

I put the feather on the table. It spun around. It’s windy in here.

I got into bed. The sheets were hot. I turned on the TV. It was static.

I watched the snow fall on the screen.

I thought about the egg. I thought about the Mayor.

I hope it snows tomorrow. It covers up the dirt.
