Woke up at 9:15 AM. The townhouse was shaking. Not from an earthquake, but from the vibration. Everyone in New York was vibrating. It was the hum of the holiday. The air outside was thick with anticipation and nitrogen. I looked out the window. The sky was a bruised purple. It looked like a dye-run on a silk dress.

Put on the "Glitter" wig. It’s made of ground glass. Very hazardous. If I move my head too fast, I could slit someone’s throat. It’s my dangerous look. I put on my sunglasses even though it was cloudy. I didn’t want to see the purple sky directly. It’s too much information.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:00 AM. $30,000.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Benjamin Franklin. He was wearing bifocals and a knit cap.

"Have a good Fourth, Andy," Ben said. "Did you invent electricity yet?"

"I invented the wig," I said.

The streets were quiet. The asphalt had stopped scrolling ticker tape. Instead, the pavement was wet. It looked like rain, but when I stepped out of the cab, my shoe didn't make a splash sound. It made a *click* sound. The ground was hard. Very hard.

When we got to the Factory, the doors were locked. There was a guard dog. But it was a mechanical dog. It was made of old vacuum cleaners. A Hoover terrier. *Whirr*. It sniffed my pant leg.

"It's me," I said. "I'm the owner."

The dog sat down and puffed up a bag of dust.

Inside, the room was filled with Stars. Not real stars. Sea stars. Starfish. Thousands of them. They were stuck to the walls. They were stuck to the ceiling. They were pulsing. *Thump-thump*.

"It's the 'Bursting' installation," Bob said. He was wearing a life vest. "We're celebrating the sea. The stars fell into the water."

"It smells like low tide," I said. "It smells like Atlantic City."

"It's the smell of freedom," Sammy said. He was peeling a starfish off the wall. It left a mark. A wet, salty star.

At 11:30 AM, the rat walked in. He was wearing a tricorn hat. He looked like a Minuteman. He was carrying a musket, but it was made of a pretzel stick.

"The British are coming!" the rat shouted. "Or maybe it's the critics. I can't tell the difference."

The squirrel was burying nuts in the pile of starfish. He was confused.

"I'm saving for winter," the squirrel said. "Winter is in 10 minutes."

At 12:45 PM, the phone rang. It was a red phone. A direct line to the White House. Or maybe just a hotline to a diner in New Jersey.

"Andy?" It was Jean-Michel. "I'm painting the fire. I'm painting the sparks before they fade. It's ephemeral. It's gone before you pay for it."

"Put it on my tab," I said. "Bill me for the heat."

At 1:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Four Seasons." But the pool was empty. The fountain was dry. They had drained the water to fill the champagne glasses.

"I'll have the 'Hamburger'," I said. $85,000.00.

The waiter brought me a plate with a single bun. No meat. Just a bun.

"Where's the beef?" I asked.

"It's conceptual," the waiter said. "You have to imagine the cow. It's better for digestion."

I ate the bun. It was dry. It tasted like air. I washed it down with a glass of "Sparkling Caffeine." *Fizz*.

"I'm hungry," I said. "I want something solid."

"Solid is old-fashioned," Bob said. "Liquid is modern. Gas is future."

At 3:00 PM, I worked on the "Reversal" paintings. I took the old pictures of Jackie and Marilyn and I painted the colors backwards. I painted the skin black and the shadows white. I painted the eyes red. It looked like a negative. It looked like an x-ray of a ghost.

"It's the other side of the coin," Sammy said. "It's the face that doesn't exist."

At 5:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a sparkler. It was sizzling. The smoke smelled like sulfur.

"Good news, Andy," the Man said. "The fireworks display has been sponsored. They're launching 'Explosions' in the shape of your face."

"Really?" I asked. "In the sky?"

"Over Central Park," the Man said. "Ten thousand Andys. Blowing up. It's going to be beautiful."

"I hope I look good," I said. "I hope I'm smiling."

At 8:00 PM, we went to the roof of a skyscraper. We wanted a good view. The wind was strong. My glass wig chimed. *Tinkle-tinkle*. It sounded like a thousand wine glasses breaking.

"It's a glass act," Sammy said.

The city went dark. The lights went out. Then, *Whooooosh*. The first rocket went up. It was red. It exploded.

BOOM.

There I was. A giant green face in the sky. My wig was floating. My glasses were huge. It looked like me, but pixelated.

"Look at that," I said. "I'm being erased."

Another boom. More faces. Silver faces. Gold faces. They were popping. They were disappearing. The smoke drifted over Manhattan. It covered the buildings in a grey haze.

"It's beautiful destruction," Bob said. "It's very American."

At 10:30 PM, the finale was a giant soup can. A massive Campbell's Tomato Soup can made of light. It exploded and rained down red tomato soup onto the crowd. Not real soup. Just light. But people opened their mouths. They wanted to taste the fame.

"I'm full," I said.

Cabbed home at 11:00 PM. $65,000.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Betsy Ross. She was sewing the seatbelt as I buckled it.

"We're making a new flag," Betsy said. "Red, white, and you."

Went into the townhouse. The bed was waiting. The dent in the pillow was gone. Someone had plumped it. Or maybe I just slept on the other side. I took off the glass wig. My hair was flat. My head felt heavy.

I got into bed. The room was dark. But I could still see the green afterimage of my face in the sky. It was burned into my retinas.
