Woke up at 8:15 AM. The room was quiet. No spinning, no vibrating. Just the sound of the cat purring. She was sleeping on the moon rock. It must be warm. Radioactive warm. I poked her. She didn't move. She was stuck to it. Static electricity.

"You're part of the installation now," I said.

I peeled her off gently. *Peel*. She looked annoyed. Her fur was standing straight up. She looked like a sea urchin. A very cute sea urchin.

I went to the kitchen. I poured the last of the tomato juice. $0.00 (already paid for). It was thick. Kind of like sludge. I think the can was rusty. I drank it anyway. It tasted like metal. Good for the iron. I need to be strong.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $45.00. The traffic was bad. Gridlock on 5th Avenue. All the cars were driverless. They were arguing with each other. *Honk-honk*. Beep-boop. My driver was a "Simulacrum" of Grace Jones. She was very tall. Her head hit the roof.

"Move it," she yelled at a Toyota. "I have a portrait sitting at 10."

"You're sitting," I said. "I'm the sitting."

"I know," she said. "I'm the ego."

We got there late. The Factory was full of balloons again. But these weren't helium balloons. They were water balloons. Blue ones.

"Careful," Sammy said. He was wearing a raincoat. "It's the 'Indoor Rain' installation. Very melancholy."

A balloon dropped on my head. *Splat*. Cold water. My hair went flat.

"Great," I said. "Now I look like a wet poodle."

"Very chic," Sammy said. "Poodles are very in this season. The 'Standard Poodle' look."

I went to the back to dry off. I turned on the heater. It blew hot air and confetti. *Whirrr*. Red paper dots.

I looked at the canvas. I started to paint the moon rock. It was easy. Just grey dots. I used a stencil. I made fifty of them. They looked like peas. A mountain of grey peas.

"It's a commentary on consumption," I said.

"It's dinner," Sammy said. "Can we eat them?"

"If you're a robot," I said.

Lunch was at The Oyster Bar again. I wanted to see if the water level had changed. It had risen. Now the boat had a motor.

I ordered the clam chowder. $22.00. It was served by a "Simulacrum" of Calvin Klein. He was wearing underwear made of denim. Very tight.

"Is it comfortable?" I asked.

"It's constraining," Calvin said. "That's the point. It holds everything in. Even the secrets."

"I don't have any secrets," I said.

"Then you're not wearing the right jeans," Calvin said.

I ate the chowder. It was full of rubber bands. I thought they were clams.

"Chewy," I said.

"It's elastic," Calvin said. "It stretches your stomach. So you can eat more later."

I paid the bill. I left a tip. 20%. $4.40. Calvin put it in his pocket. It disappeared.

Went to a matinee at the Museum of Modern Art. They were screening my old film *Sleep*. But they had sped it up. It lasted 3 seconds. *Zip*. The guy slept. It was amazing. The best cut yet.

"I loved the pacing," I told the curator. She was a hologram.

"It was efficient," she said. "Time is money. We saved everyone 5 hours and 57 minutes."

"I should have charged more," I said.

"Afterwards, there was a panel discussion. "The Art of Snoozing." The panel was asleep. They just snored into microphones. *Zzzzz*. It was very ambient. I fell asleep in the front row.

I woke up at 5:00 PM. Drool on my chin.

"Beautiful," someone said.

I wiped my face. "Thank you."

Went to a dinner party at 7:00 PM. It was in a bubble. Floating over Central Park. We had to take a ladder to get in. The host was a "Simulacrum" of Diana Vreeland. She was floating upside down.

"Andy!" she shouted. "You're right side up. How boring."

"I'm heavy," I said. "Gravity loves me."

"Well, un-love it," she said. "Here, have some 'Anti-Gravitation' Champagne. $150 a flute."

I took a glass. The bubbles went down. *Glug-glug*. I felt light. My feet lifted off the floor. *Float*. I hovered over the carpet.

"Now you're decorative," Diana said.

I drifted around the room. I saw Truman Capote. He was sitting on the chandelier.

"Found your wig," Truman said. He pointed to the corner.

The wig was sitting on a bust of Beethoven. It looked bigger. It had eaten the bust.

"It's consuming culture," I said.

"It's hungry," Truman said. "Feed it some money."

I took out a hundred dollar bill. I fed it to the wig. *Chomp*. It ate the money.

"Expensive habit," I said.

"It's an investment," Truman said. "It's compounding interest."

I floated over to the buffet. They were serving pill-shaped hors d'oeuvres. "One bite makes you thin. One bite makes you fat." I took two. I didn't feel any different. Maybe I'm neutral.

I checked my watch. The smiley face was green. Green means "Go."

"I'm leaving," I shouted. But no one heard me. They were all on the ceiling.

I climbed down the ladder. I felt heavy again. *Thud*. My feet hurt.

Cabbed home at 9:30 PM. $50.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum of me." The same one as Saturday.

"Rough day?" he asked.

"I floated," I said. "It was tiring."

"I know," he said. "Flying is exhausting. That's why I drive."

He dropped me off. I walked up the stairs. The elevator was broken. Or maybe it just went to the moon.

I opened the door. The cat was still on the rock. The rock was glowing. A soft blue light.

"New lamp," I said.

I sat on the couch. I turned on the TV. It was a commercial for the "Afterlife."

"Don't wait," the TV said. "Book your plot now. Premium spots available near the celebrities."
