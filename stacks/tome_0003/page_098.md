Woke up at 8:15 AM. The sky was beige again, but it was peeling. A big strip of sky was flapping in the wind like old wallpaper. You could see the wood underneath. Or maybe concrete. It looked like the set of a western movie after they ran out of money for the backdrop. I thought about glueing it back up, but I didn't have a ladder tall enough.

Archie was still floating. He had drifted into the corner near the ceiling.

"I'm hungry," Archie said. "Float me a sandwich."

I opened a can of tuna fish. $3.50. I put it on a saucer and I threw it up to him. He caught it in his mouth like a seal. He looked very elegant. Like a fur balloon at a circus.

"We have to do something about gravity," I said. "It's very undignified."

"I think it's dead," Archie said. "I saw its body in the alley."

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. The cab was a "Simulacrum" of a 1955 Studebaker. It hovered. It didn't have wheels. It just floated above the asphalt. $450.00.

"Gas is expensive," the driver said. "But air is free."

"Not in this city," I said. "The air is full of secrets."

When we got to the Factory, the hole in the wall from Thursday was still there. But now, instead of a view of the street, it was showing a view of the 19th century. Horses and buggies. People in top hats. They looked dirty.

"I like the past," I said. "It's sepia."

Sammy was standing in front of the hole watching the horses.

"I'm betting on the grey one," Sammy said. "He has a sad look."

"He's hauling garbage," I said.

"That's why he's motivated," Sammy said.

Bob rolled in. He was vibrating. *Bzzzzz*. He rolled over my foot. It hurt.

"I've been upgraded," Bob said. "I have 'Tactile Feedback'."

"Turn it down," I said.

"I can't," Bob said. "I'm sensitive. I feel everything. The floor is lonely. The wall is scared."

"Don't be such a snowflake," I said.

"I'm a sphere," Bob said. "I have no flakes."

I went to the back to work. The "Portrait of a Nobody" was dry. The liquid money had cracked. It looked like dried mud. But expensive mud.

"I need a new subject," I said. "Something happy."

I looked at the hole in the wall. A man from 1880 looked back at me. He was picking his nose.

"Perfect," I said.

I took a photograph of him. *Click*. Then I silk-screened his face onto a canvas. I made it pink. Then I made it orange. Then I made it pink again.

At 11:30 AM, Truman arrived. He looked better. He had vacuumed the dust off his tuxedo. He was carrying a jar.

"What's in the jar?" I asked.

"Essence of Wall," Truman said. "I scraped it off the brickwork. It tastes like time and coal dust."

"I'll pass," I said. "I'm on a diet."

Sammy sold the floor to the 19th century. He reached through the hole and shook hands with a man in a frock coat.

"I traded the linoleum for a horse," Sammy said.

"We don't need a horse," I said.

"We need transportation," Sammy said. "In case the cabs go on strike."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar". But it was different. The fish were floating in the tanks. They weren't swimming. They were just hovering in the water.

"It's the gravity," I said. "It's leaking."

I ordered the "Pan Roasted Halibut". $45.00. It came on a plate, but it tried to float away. I had to put a fork on it to hold it down.

"It's fresh," the waiter said. "It was walking this morning."

"Fish don't walk," Truman said.

"This one did," the waiter said. "It had legs. Very avant-garde."

Sammy ordered the "Clam Chowder". $18.00.

"I'm eating liquid," Sammy said. "It's safer."

Bob couldn't fit a chair. He just rolled around the table. The maître d' tried to stop him.

"No spheres allowed," the maître d' said.

"He's an artist," I said.

"We have a dress code," the maître d' said.

"He's wearing his skin," I said. "It's very formal."

The maître d' sighed and let us stay.

At 3:00 PM, we walked to the "Gallery of the Future". The line was longer. It wrapped around the block. People were buying "Yesterday".

"I'll take a pound of Tuesday," a woman said. "Make sure it's sunny."

I bought a "Concept of a Cat". $10,000.00. It came in a box. When I opened the box, meow sounds came out.

"Does it need litter?" I asked.

"It only needs attention," the cashier said.

"I'm bad at that," I said. "I'll give it to Archie."

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, I worked on the "Horse" painting. The horse Sammy traded for the floor was standing in the studio. It looked bored. It was eating a copy of *The New York Times*.

"Is he housebroken?" I asked.

"He's politics," Sammy said. "He makes a mess everywhere."

I painted the horse while he ate the paper. I did his portrait in red and black. He looked like a communist flag.

"Are you a communist?" I asked the horse.

He neighed. It sounded like a car horn. *Honk*.

"I'll take that as a yes," I said.

At 7:00 PM, I called the update center. I wanted to complain about the joy. It was making me work too hard.

"I'm exhausted," I told the robot. "Can I downgrade to 'Melancholy'?"

"Melancholy is a premium feature," the robot said. "It costs extra."

"How much?"

"Five thousand dollars."

"Put it on my bill," I said. "I want to be bored again."

Cabbed home at 8:30 PM. $550.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1990s Supermodel. She was very tall and pouted the whole time.

"Do you like my outfit?" she asked.

"It's invisible," I said.

"Exactly," she said. "It's conceptual."
