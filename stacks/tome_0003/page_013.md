Friday, February 13, 2026

Woke up at 7:00. Friday the 13th. I usually stay in bed on days like this, but the bed felt too heavy. The gravity generator is acting up again. The sky was the color of a television set tuned to a dead channel—or maybe just a static grey. I watched the ceiling. The MirrorWall was playing a loop of a smiling woman, but her smile kept getting wider and wider until it touched her ears. It was very frightening. It was very cosmetic.

Got up and I didn’t step on the scale. I didn’t want to know. I just put on the jeans and a white shirt. I felt like a ghost. I felt like I should haunt someone. I had a cup of tea. It was "Ghost Tea." It’s clear. It costs $15 and it tastes like nothing. It’s supposed to cleanse your aura. I think it just cleanses your wallet.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:30. The cab was automated. There was no driver, just a steering wheel that spun by itself. It felt like a haunted car. It was $18.50. I tipped the machine $2. I don’t know why. It just felt polite.

Sammy was there. He looked exhausted. He had dark circles under his eyes. He said the mannequin—the one Glitch is haunting—kept him up all night. He said, "She was walking backwards into the walls, Andy. *Bonk. Bonk. Bonk.* All night long." I told him, "That’s what you get for giving a mirror legs." He said, "I think she’s lonely. She wants a friend." I looked at the mannequin. It was standing in the center of the room, spinning slowly. It’s very dizzying.

We worked on the "Oxidation" paintings. But instead of urine, we used "Acid Rain" collected from the roof. It’s green and smells like pennies. We poured it on the metallic canvas. It hissed at us. It made beautiful silver streaks. It’s very toxic. I had to wear a mask. It looked like a dog’s snout. I looked like a beagle. A very rich beagle.

A package arrived. It was from the "Andy" currency people. Or ex-people. They sent a letter saying I am "obsolete." They said they are switching to a new currency: "The Kim." It’s a picture of a holographic Kim Kardashian. They said my wig is "passé." They sent me a voucher for a free sandwich. I felt sad. I felt like a used stamp. I ate the voucher. It tasted like paper.

For lunch we went to “The Oyster Bar” in Grand Central Terminal. The station is huge now. It has sixteen levels. The trains go up and down and sideways. We ate oysters. They were $60 a dozen. They were slimy. They tasted like the ocean. I saw a man eating a watch. He was eating it like an apple. *Crunch.* I asked Sammy, "Is that performance art?" and Sammy said, "No, I think he’s just hungry." It’s very hard to tell the difference these days.

We came back and the mannequin was facing the window. She was looking at the pigeons. Sammy said, "She wants to fly." I said, "Mirrors don't fly, Sammy. They just make things look like they can." I taped a feather to her back. It’s a "Chicken Feather" from the art supply store. It was $5. Now she’s a bird. A scary, plastic bird.

I worked on a portrait of Marilyn. It’s been a while. I used a new app that lets you paint with light. It’s very bright. I gave her digital tears. They are blue. They glow in the dark. It’s very sad. It’s very commercial.

A woman came to the door. She said she was from the future. She said she was my great-great-granddaughter. She looked like me. She had the wig. She said, "I just wanted to see if you were real." I signed her autograph book. I wrote, "To the future, don't change." She looked disappointed. She said, "In the future, everything is disposable. Even history." She vanished. I think she was a hologram. Or maybe I’m the hologram. I don’t know.

Left at 8:00. The pink snow was back, mixed with the static grey. It looked like pepper and cotton candy. Very messy.

Came home and the moon rock was vibrating. It was humming a low note. The Dalek was sleeping next to it. They looked like a little family. Rocks and robots.

I checked the mirror. My reflection blinked first. That means I’m getting slow.

I took the wig off. My scalp itched. I scratched it. It felt good.

Good night.
