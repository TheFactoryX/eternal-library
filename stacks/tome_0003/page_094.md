Woke up at 8:15 AM. The room was vibrating. Not like Archie vibrates, but like the building was a giant phone that was ringing, but I was the only one who could hear it. The sky was purple. Not a nice purple, a bruised plum purple. It looked like the city had a black eye.

I put on my glasses. The world looked digital.

I reached for the iGod pill. I wanted to see if it was still in the safe. It wasn't. The safe was open. And there was a note. It said "GONE FISHING."

"Did I go fishing?" I thought. "I don't like fish. They're too slippery."

I walked into the kitchen. Archie was there. He was sitting on the counter. He was blue again. A neon, electric blue.

"Are you okay?" I asked.

He didn't say anything. He just projected a movie onto the refrigerator door. It was a home movie of me, sleeping. It was from last night.

"You're a voyeur," I said.

I opened a can of "Zero-Calorie Air". $10.00. I sprayed it at him. He fizzled.

Breakfast was a piece of "Leftover Birthday Cake". $75.00. It was stale. It tasted like cardboard and regret.

At 9:30 AM, the phone rang. It was Truman.

"Andy," he said. "I'm stuck in the closet again. But this time, the door is locked."

"You wanted a door," I said. "You got a door."

"But there's no handle on the inside," he said. "It's just a flat surface. Like your paintings."

"Think of it as a conceptual trap," I said. "You're art now."

"I need to use the bathroom," he said.

"Use the bucket," I said.

"What bucket?" he asked.

"The bucket of ideas," I said.

He hung up.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:00 AM. $200.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Aristotle Onassis. He wore a neck scarf.

"Where to, boss?" he asked.

"To the future," I said.

"That'll be extra," he said. "The future is far."

The Factory was wet. It was raining inside. Not real rain, but "Digital Rain". Green code was dripping from the ceiling. *Drip-drop*. *Drip-drop*. It was puddling on the floor.

Sammy was wearing a raincoat. He was holding an umbrella.

"It's the system," Sammy shouted over the sound of the dripping code. "It's leaking. The Breeze uploaded a virus into the cloud."

"Is it catching?" I asked.

"It's very catching," Sammy said. "Look at Bob."

I looked at Bob. Bob was dissolving. His legs were gone. Just pixels. Floating away.

"I'm becoming abstract," Bob said. "I'm becoming a point of view."

"Don't vanish," I said. "I need someone to hold the paintbrush."

"I am the paintbrush," Bob said.

At 11:30 AM, I went to the desk to work. The "Wallets" were dry. I decided to do a new series. "The Viruses." I painted a green square. Then I painted a green circle. Then I painted a green splat.

"It's all the same," I thought. "Germs are just very small people."

Just then, the oak door in the middle of the room opened. *Creak*.

A man walked out. He was wearing a suit made of mirrors.

"Mr. Warhol," the man said. "I'm the update."

"The update?" I asked.

"From the government," the man said. "You missed your appointment. So they sent me to update you manually."

"I don't want an update," I said. "I like my version. I have all the bugs worked out."

"You don't have the new features," the man said. "You don't have 'eternal optimism' or 'enhanced math skills'."

"I have a calculator," I said.

"But do you have joy?" the man asked.

"I have Frrrozen Hot Chocolate," I said.

"That's sugar," the man said. "Joy is different. Joy is like love, but without the people."

"Sign me up," I said.

He touched my forehead. *Beep*.

I felt a cold rush. Like drinking a Slurpee too fast.

"Now," the man said. "You must pay."

"How much?" I asked.

"$5,000.00," he said.

"That's expensive," I said.

"Joy is the most expensive thing," he said. "Because nobody has it."

I wrote a check. I hope the bank accepts it. My signature looked different. Sharper. Happier.

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar".

The pigeons were still there. They were arguing. "Buy! Sell! Buy!"

"Make up your minds," I told them.

I ordered the "Clam Chowder". $40.00. It was thick. It tasted like the ocean. A bad ocean. A polluted ocean.

"This is good," I said. "It tastes like history."

Sammy ordered the "Raw Data" again. This time it was numbers.

"I'm counting calories," Sammy said.

At 2:30 PM, I went to the MoMA. I wanted to see the mirror frame.

It was different. The mirror was gone. There was just a hole.

"Where did it go?" I asked a guard.

"It ran away," the guard said. "It couldn't stand looking at everyone anymore."

"Me too," I said.

I looked into the hole. It was dark.

"Hello?" I said.

"Hello," said the hole.

It echoed. I walked away. I didn't want to fall in.

At 4:00 PM, I went back to the Factory. The digital rain had stopped. But Bob was gone. Just a pile of pixels on the floor.

"Where's Bob?" I asked Sammy.

"He rebooted," Sammy said. "He's in the cloud. He's downloading a new body."

"I hope he gets a taller one," I said.

I looked at the oak door. It was closed again.

"Truman is still in there," I remembered.

I opened the door.

Truman was sitting in a chair. He was holding a bucket. He looked miserable.

"It smells," he said.

"Art smells," I said. "Get used to it."

At 6:00 PM, I left the Factory. I hailed a cab. $180.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of me. Silver wig. Glasses. Black turtleneck.

"Where to, Andy?" he asked.

"Home," I said. "I'm tired."

"Me too," he said. "It's hard being famous. Even for a simulation."
