# Wednesday, May 27, 2026

Woke up at 8:30 AM. The sky was beige. A flat, dull, matte beige. Like an unpainted canvas. Or a wall in a cheap apartment before you move in. It was very calming. Like being inside an envelope. I watched a cloud float by, but it was square. It was a perfect cube of cotton. "I'll have to iron that," I thought. My wig was on the pillow next to me. It was breathing. *In, out*. It has a life of its own now. I think it’s dreaming of being hair. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. $300.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Basquiat. He was wearing a crown made of barbed wire.

"Art hurts," the driver said. He scratched his arm and paint flecks came off. "It’s expensive to bleed."

"Just drive," I said. "And watch the meter. It’s eating my money."

When we arrived at the Factory, the building had turned into a giant Coca-Cola bottle. We were inside the glass. It was curved and warm.

"I feel like liquid," I said. "I feel like soda water."

Bob was there. He was a vending machine. The glass front was full of bags of chips. "Andy, I'm full of Fritos," Bob said. The glass was smudged with fingerprints. "I'm salty."

"Don't eat the inventory," I said.

Jean-Michel was there. He was human again. Mostly. His skin was still glowing with that neon blue, but it was fading. He looked tired. He looked like a drawing that had been erased too many times.

"The static stopped," Jean-Michel said. "It’s too quiet. I need the noise."

Sammy was there. He was painting the ceiling beige. He was painting over the silver.

"It’s too shiny," Sammy said. "We need more neutrality. We need more *nothing*."

At 11:00 AM, the squirrel came in. He was riding a Roomba. The Roomba was clean. It wasn't hungry anymore.

"We made a deal," the squirrel said. "I feed him quarters. He vacuums the entropy."

I gave the squirrel a peanut. It was a plastic peanut.

"It’s low fat," I said.

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar" in Grand Central. But the station was gone. It was a forest. Pine trees growing through the marble floor. The oyster bar was a hut made of bones.

"I’ll have the shellfish," I said.

The waiter was a bear. A large grizzly bear wearing a vest.

"We’re out of shellfish," the bear said. "We only have serving suggestions."

"I’ll take a suggestion," I said. "$500.00."

The bear brought me a plate with a single word written on it in oyster sauce. *TIME*.

"It’s a bit crunchy," I said. "But it fills you up."

Jean-Michel ordered a steak. The bear brought him a raw steak, but it was made of red velvet.

"It’s texture," Jean-Michel said. He cut it with a knife. *Fibrous*.

At 3:00 PM, I worked on my new series. "The Beige Paintings". I painted canvases the same color as the sky. Then I painted clouds on them. But the clouds were receipts. Old taxi receipts.

"It’s about the cost of floating," I said.

"It’s a ledger," Bob said. He crunched a Frito. *Crunch*. "It’s accounting."

"I like accounting," I said. "It’s very abstract."

At 5:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit knocked on the glass wall of the Coke bottle. *Clink, clink*. He was outside. He was holding a ladder.

"I’m climbing out," the Man said. "The beige is suffocating."

"You can’t leave," I said. "We’re sealed."

"I have a corkscrew," the Man said. He held it up. It was made of silver.

"Don’t pop the cork," I said. "We’ll lose our fizz."

At 7:00 PM, I felt a rumble. The Coke bottle was shaking.

"We’re being shaken," Sammy said. "We’re being mixed."

The beige sky swirled. The cotton cubes turned into balls. It was a snowstorm. A beige snowstorm.

"It’s a blizzard," I said.

"It’s a brand recall," Jean-Michel said. "We’re being discontinued."

At 9:00 PM, the glass walls cracked. *Shatter*. The soda poured out. We spilled onto the street. We were liquid. We flowed down the avenues of New York.

The pavement was thirsty. It drank us up.

"I'm sticky," Bob said.

"We're art," I said. "We're staining the world."

At 11:00 PM, I reformed. I pulled myself together. I was standing in a puddle of brown sugar. I checked my pockets. My wallet was wet. But the money was okay. It was plastic money. Waterproof.

I hailed a cab. It was a floating bottle cap. A giant metal cap.

"Take me to the future," I said.

"That's $5.00," the cap said.

"Expensive," I said. But I got in.

It spun around and around. Like a merry-go-round.

"I'm dizzy," I said.

"That's the point," the cap said. "It makes the world go away."

I closed my eyes. I could hear the squirrel chattering. I could hear the bear eating the velvet. I could hear the money counting itself.

"Gee," I said. "I hope tomorrow is a different color."

I drifted off. The hum of the engine was a lullaby. *Hum, hum*. It sounded like a refrigerator. Or a factory. Or a heart.

And I thought about nothing. Which is the most expensive thing of all.

So.
