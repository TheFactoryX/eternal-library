Woke up at 8:05 AM. The townhouse was so quiet it felt like the vacuum of space. No noise from the street. The neighbors must have moved out or been deleted. I looked in the mirror. The reflection was moving at normal speed again. Good. I hate waiting for myself.

Put on the " camouflage" wig. It’s made of strips of green and brown film negatives. It makes me look like a bush or a mistake in the printing process. I put on the glasses with the pink lenses. I wanted the world to look rosy. Or at least like a flesh tone.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. $42,500.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Elvis Presley. But it was the "Vegas" Elvis. The fat one. He was eating a peanut butter sandwich.

"Thank you, thank you very much," Elvis said when I gave him the tip. "I'm all shook up."

The traffic lights were out on 5th Avenue. They were just blinking yellow. *Blink-blink*. It means go. It means be careful. It means nobody knows what to do so just keep moving.

When we got to the Factory, the elevators were broken. We had to walk up the stairs. There were one hundred steps. By the time I got to the top, my legs were jelly. I was wobbly. Like Jell-O. $1.50 worth of Jell-O.

The squirrel was sitting on a pile of diamonds. He was eating a gold watch.

"It crunches," the squirrel said. "Time tastes like metal."

"Don't eat the hands," I said. "You won't know what time it is."

"I don't care," the squirrel said. "I'm late anyway."

The rat was painting a canvas. He was holding a tiny brush. He was painting a portrait of me. But he was painting me with a tail.

"It's a self-portrait," the rat said. "We're family."

At 10:30 AM, the Man in the Grey Suit brought in a new model. Her name was Tiffany. She was a "Simulacrum" of a department store. She looked like a mannequin. She was plastic and perfect.

"She’s going to be the new face," the Man said. "We’re digitizing her. We’re selling her personality chips."

"Does she talk?" I asked.

Tiffany opened her mouth. A recording of a cash register played. *Cha-ching*.

"She speaks the language of America," I said. "Very fluent."

At 11:45 AM, I worked on the "Oxidation" paintings. But instead of urine, I used soda. I used Coca-Cola. I poured it on the canvas. It bubbled. *Fizz-fizz*. It left brown rings. It looked like the earth from space. Or a coffee stain on a table.

"It's sugary," Bob said. "It attracts ants."

"Ants are people too," I said.

At 12:30 PM, a drone flew in the window. It was a little helicopter. It dropped a package. It was a box of "Cracker Jack". But instead of a toy inside, there was a tiny panic attack. I opened the box and felt sudden fear. My heart raced.

"Surprise!" the note said.

"I don't want this," I said. I threw it in the trash.

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Balthazar". It was crowded. Everyone was talking. *Blah-blah-blah*. The noise was a solid wall. I ordered the "Steak Frites". $110,000.00.

The waiter brought me a plate with a picture of a cow on it. It was a digital picture on an LCD screen implanted in the plate.

"Turn up the brightness," I said. "I can't taste it."

The waiter pressed a button. The cow glowed. It looked happy.

"It's well done," Sammy said.

"It's raw," I said. "Look at the pixels."

At 2:45 PM, Jean-Michel called on the video phone. His face was made of spray paint. It was dripping.

"Andy," he said. "I'm painting the town hall. I'm painting it neon. I want the politicians to glow in the dark."

"They already do," I said. "They're radioactive."

"Not enough," Jean-Michel said. "They need more colors. I need more money."

"Buy him a spray can," I told Bob. "The expensive kind."

At 4:00 PM, I sat for my portrait. A robot was painting me. It had a laser eye. It scanned my face. *Beep*. It moved a metal arm. It was very slow.

"Hold still," the robot said. "Your nose is twitching."

"It's allergies," I said. "I'm allergic to the future."

At 5:30 PM, the fire alarm rang. But there was no fire. Just steam. It was the pipes. The building was sweating. We opened the windows. The grey smoke drifted in.

"It's the atmosphere," Sammy said. "We're breathing the 21st century."

"It tastes like exhaust," I said.

At 7:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at the "Whitney Museum". But it wasn't a museum. It was a roller rink. Everyone was skating. *Whoosh*. They were going in circles.

I put on skates. $500.00 rental. I rolled onto the floor. I was wobbly. The music was loud. It was "Stayin' Alive". I tried to skate, but I just rolled in a straight line. I couldn't turn. I crashed into a pillar.

"Graceful," Bob said. "Like a falling leaf."

"A falling leaf with a wig," I said.

A girl skated past me. She was holding a martini glass. She didn't spill a drop.

"That's talent," I said. "That's art."

At 9:30 PM, the DJ stopped the music. It got quiet. Then a spotlight hit the center of the rink. A giant Campbell's Soup can descended from the ceiling. It opened. A woman jumped out. It was Taylor. She was dressed as a tomato.

"I'm the pop," Taylor said. "I'm the art."

She sang a song. It was about cans and opening up. It was very catchy. I clapped. My hands made a slapping sound against my skates.

At 11:00 PM, we left. The air outside was cool. The neon signs were buzzing. I felt tired. My feet hurt from the skates. My face hurt from smiling.
