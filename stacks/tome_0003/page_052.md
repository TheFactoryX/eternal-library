# Tuesday, March 24, 2026

Woke up at 8:30 AM. The alarm didn't go off. It just sighed. A deep, electronic sigh. *Ughhhhh*. I pressed the snooze button, but it just said, "What's the point?" and turned off. I think my appliances are depressed. I should buy them flowers. Or maybe just a nice surge protector.

I got out of bed. I didn't float away, which was good. The gravity seems to be back to normal settings. I looked in the mirror. I looked solid. Not 3D, exactly. Maybe 2.5D. Like a lenticular print. If I moved my head left and right, my expression changed from "Sad" to "Sadder." So I stopped moving.

The cat was sleeping on my pillow. She had a little caption bubble floating above her head. It said: **ZZZZ**. I didn't want to wake her. She has a big day of shedding planned.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:45 AM. $42.50. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Grace Jones. She was driving a hearse. It was very shiny.

"Hop in, star," she said. "We're going to the burial."

"Whose burial?" I asked.

"Yours," she said. "It's a metaphor. Or a rehearsal."

I sat in the back. There was no seatbelt. Just a velvet strap. The ride was smooth. We floated over the potholes.

The Factory was buzzing. Not with noise, but with electricity. The air smelled like ozone and hair spray. The neon sign outside was flickering. *ANDY* it said. Then *W*... *ARH*... *OL*. It was spelling things backwards.

Sammy was at the door. He was wearing a suit made of Ziploc bags. It was very transparent.

"We have a problem," Sammy said.

"Money?" I asked. "Time?"

"Pineapples," he said.

"Pineapples?"

"The delivery truck overturned," Sammy said. "The street is covered in them. It's a sticky situation. We can't get the silkscreens in."

"Call the police," I said.

"We did," Sammy said. "They're busy. They're filming a reality show about the traffic. 'Real Housewives of the BQE.'"

"So we're stuck?"

"We have to go around," Sammy said. "Through the sewers. Or we wait."

I didn't want to wait. Waiting is boring. And dangerous. You might think of something.

I walked inside. The studio was full of helium balloons. Thousands of them. Red and silver. They were tied to the furniture.

"What's this?" I asked.

"It's an installation," Sammy said. " 'Up, Up, and Away.' It's about the economy."

"Very nice," I said. "Can I pop one?"

"If you pay the inflation tax," he said. "$5.00 per pop."

I popped a red one. *Bang*. $5.00 gone. It felt satisfying. Like throwing a penny in a well, but louder.

Lunch was at "The Russian Tea Room." But it’s underwater now. They flooded the basement. You have to take a submarine elevator. It was very quiet. Muffled.

I ordered the Beef Stroganoff. $150.00. It was served by a "Simulacrum" of Anna Wintour. She was wearing scuba gear. She handed me the plate through a porthole.

"Chew," she signaled. "Chew fast."

I ate the noodles. They were slippery. The sauce was red. Like a lipstick stain.

I looked out the window. There were fish swimming. Goldfish. But they were wearing crowns.

"Royalty," I said.

"Bottom feeders," Anna signaled back.

She handed me the bill. It was laminated. Waterproof. I left the tip on the floor. A $50 bill. It floated away. A fish ate it. *Gulp*.

"Rich fish," I said.

I took the elevator back up. My ears popped. *Pop*. Now I have two balloons in my head.

Went to a museum at 2:30 PM. The "Museum of Forgotten Futures."

They had an exhibit of my old time capsules. The boxes I buried in the 70s. They were dug up by robots.

One box was labeled "1980."

I looked inside. It was empty.

"Where's the stuff?" I asked the curator. He was a hologram of a guard. He flickered.

"The past ate it," the guard said. "Entropy. It's very hungry."

"I put a cookie in there," I said. "And a wig."

"Gone," the guard said. "Digested."

"By who?"

"By the now."

I felt sad. I wanted to leave a review. *Bad service. No crumbs.*

I walked out. I bought a postcard in the gift shop. It was a picture of nothing. Just white cardstock. $10.00. I wrote "Dear You, Wish you were nowhere." I mailed it to myself. Stamps were $0.65. I put two on. Just to be sure.

Went back to the Factory. The balloons were gone. Sammy had sold them.

"Who bought them?"

"A daycare center," Sammy said. "Or a hedge fund. I can't remember. They all look the same."

I sat down to paint. I was working on a portrait of a generic celebrity. A face that looks like everyone and no one. A blur.

I used a sponge. *Dab-dab-dab*.

The phone rang. *Ring-ring*.

I answered it.

"Hello?"

"Andy?"

"Yes."

"It's Bob."

"Bob who?"

"Bob Colacello."

"Oh. Hi Bob."

"I'm in the computer," Bob said. "I'm an email now."

"That sounds efficient," I said.

"It's terrible," Bob said. "I keep ending up in spam. I'm lonely. Open me."

"I don't know how," I said. "Just write something good."

"Like what?"

"Like 'The party was divine.'"

"The party was divine," Bob said.

"See?" I said. "That's art."

I hung up. The receiver felt warm. Like a hand.

I looked at my watch. The smiley face was yellow. Yellow means "Wait."

So I waited.

I sat on a box of soup cans. The label said "Cream of Mushroom." But I think it was "Cream of Now."

I watched the sun go down through the skylight. It was a purple sun. It looked like a bruise.

I took out my wallet. I had $100 bill.

I folded it. Into a paper airplane.

I threw it.

It didn't fly. It just dropped. Straight down. *Thud*.

"Gravity," I said.
