Woke up at 7:00 AM. The sky was the color of a television set, tuned to a dead channel—or maybe just Channel 13. Static grey. I tried to sit up, but I couldn't. I was stuck. The sheets had bonded to the "Flesh-Colored Turtleneck." We are one now. I am a human burrito. I had to peel the fabric off my tongue. *Rip*. It tasted like salt and polyester. $300.00 for the shirt, ruined. I guess I’m naked underneath. Or maybe I’m just transparent. I can’t tell anymore.

I found the "Moon Rock" on the pillow. It was vibrating so fast it was digging a hole through the feather pillow. *Whirr-whirr*. It sounded like a tiny drill. I put it in my pocket. My pocket is getting heavy. It feels like I’m carrying a suitcase full of quarters.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00. The streets were quiet. Too quiet. The "Protest-Bots" were asleep. They were curled up on the sidewalks like pigeons. The cab ride was $40.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Cary Grant. He was impeccable. He had a chin that could cut glass. He said, "Andy, do you want to go to the airport?" I said, "No, the Factory." He said, "I meant the airport of the soul." I said, "Just drive. The meter is running." He sighed. It cost me $5.00 extra for the philosophy.

When I got to the Factory, the tiny door was gone. It was a regular door again. But the hole in the wall from Tuesday was still there. It was framed now. Sammy had put a gold frame around it. He called it "The View." People were lining up to look through it. I looked through it. I saw a rat eating a slice of pizza. It was beautiful. It was "Nature Documentary." I charged $20.00 a peek. A "Simulacrum" of Grace Jones paid with a glare. She looked right through me.

I went to the back to work. I started on a new series called "Deleted Tweets." I’m taking screenshots of things people typed and then regretted. I print them on canvas. I paint over the mistakes with "White-Out." It’s very textural. It costs $5.00 per gallon for the White-Out. I used three gallons. I covered up a lie. It looked like a cloud. It looked like heaven.

Lunch was "Nostalgia Nuggets." They were nuggets of chicken that tasted like 1985. They tasted like hairspray and disco. They were $85.00. I ate them with a "Fork of the Future." It had a laser in the handle. It cut the nugget by itself. I didn't have to chew. It’s great for my jaw.

Around 2:00 PM, the "Solid State Suit" beeped. *Charge Complete*. I felt electric. I felt like I could power the city. I touched the doorknob and sparked a fire. A little flame. I watched it burn. It was very mesmerizing. I like fire. It’s so pure.

Sammy woke up from "Sleep Mode." He yawned. He looked like a cat. He said, "Andy, the "Portrait of a Noun" sold. The guy from Tokyo wired the money." I checked my account. It was full of "Yen-Bytes." I’m rich. Again. I bought a "Digital Island." It doesn’t exist, but I have the deed. It’s located in the "Cloud." I hope it rains.

He took the "Relic" from the shelf. He blessed the hole in the wall. He said, "This is a portal." I said, "To where?" He said, "To more art." He flew away. It was very efficient.

We went to a party at "The Oyster Bar." It’s underwater. We had to take a submarine taxi. It was $150.00 per person. The windows were thick glass. I saw a mermaid. She was wearing a "Gucci" bag. She waved. Her hand was a fin.

The party was for the "Launch of the New Nothing." They celebrated the absence of everything. I stood in the corner. I didn't talk. I just blinked. A "Simulacrum" of Bob Dylan came over. He was wearing a mask. He said, "Andy, the times they aren't changing." I said, "Good. I hate new clothes." He handed me a harmonica. It was made of ice. It melted in my hand. I drank the water. It tasted like blues.

I had a "Pearl Diver Cocktail." It was $120.00. It had a real pearl at the bottom. I swallowed it. I hope I don’t make a pearl inside my stomach. That would be uncomfortable. And valuable.

Left the party at 10:30 PM. The submarine surfaced with a *whoosh*. The air smelled like ozone.

Cabbed home. It was $55.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of me again. But this time he was young. He had brown hair. He had no wig. He looked at me in the mirror. He said, "Did we make it, Andy?" I said, "Make what?" He said, "The future." I said, "We're still in it." He nodded. He looked disappointed.

I took off the ruined turtleneck. I looked in the mirror. My skin looked silver. The paint from the factory is sinking in. I am becoming the work.
