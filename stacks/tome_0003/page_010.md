Tuesday, February 10, 2026

Woke up at 8:15. The sky was the color of silver spray paint. Shiny and metallic. It looked like someone had buffed the clouds. I watched the ceiling for a minute. The MirrorWall was playing a loop of pigeons eating popcorn. It was very relaxing. They looked fat.

Got up and put on the denim jacket and the black turtleneck. I felt like a beatnik. I felt like I should be reading poetry in a coffee shop. Instead, I just had a cup of hot water. It’s $5 a cup if you heat it up yourself.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00. The cab was a "Retro-Fit" model. It smelled like pine trees. It was $14.50. The driver was a man named Boris. He didn’t talk. He just hummed. It was a low hum. It vibrated the seat.

Sammy was there. He looked better. He said he covered the haunted mirror with a sheet. He said, "Out of sight, out of mind," but then he whispered, "I can still hear her thinking." I said, "Mirrors don't think, Sammy, they reflect," and he said, "Exactly. She's reflecting *my* anxiety." It’s very deep. He’s becoming a philosopher.

We worked on the "Disaster" paintings. We are doing a series about "The Great Spam Flood of '25." It’s where all the email in the world drowned the city. We used silkscreen and melted circuit boards. It smells like burning plastic. It’s a very nostalgic smell. It reminds me of the 80s. We used three gallons of "Electric Blue" paint. It was $150 a gallon. It’s very expensive to be digital.

A package arrived. It was from the people who make the "Andy" dollar bill. They sent a proof. It’s a piece of plastic with my face on it. My wig looks too big. It makes me look like I have a brain tumor. I called them. I said, "The hair is wrong. It needs more volume." They said, "Andy, it’s money. It’s not a portrait." I said, "Money *is* a portrait." They hung up on me. I think I’m fired from my own currency.

For lunch we went to Serendipity 3. It’s still there. It’s a landmark. I ordered the "Frrrozen Hot Chocolate." It was $45. It was delicious. It was so cold it made my teeth hurt. I saw a girl there who looked like Taylor Swift. But she was wearing a suit made of mirrors. Every time she moved, she reflected the room. I asked Sammy, "Is that the new Taylor?" and he said, "No, that’s just a mirror walking around." It was very confusing. I took a picture of her, but the camera just took a picture of myself.

We came back and watched the news. The President gave a speech. She’s a robot now. A tall, shiny robot. She spoke in binary. The translator said she was promising lower taxes on batteries. I clapped. I like batteries. They keep my wigs vibrating.

A boy came to the Factory to sell me a watch. He was ten years old. He said it was a "Rolex." I looked at it. It was made of Lego. I said, "This is art," and he said, "No, it’s time." I bought it for $20. I put it on. It doesn't tell time, it just snaps. I like the sound. *Snap.* It’s the sound of the present moment.

I signed a stack of "Brillo Boxes." They are digital now. You don't sign the box, you sign the code. I typed my name on a keyboard. It felt impersonal. I missed the smell of the Sharpie. My signature is now a non-fungible token. I don’t know what that means, but it cost $500 in gas fees to sign my name. Gas fees are very expensive in the ether.

Left at 7:00. It was raining. But it wasn't water. It was raining confetti. Little squares of plastic. They were slippery. I slipped on a red square. It was very festive.

Came home and the Dalek was in the hallway. It had eaten a shoe. It was making a crunching sound. I told it to stop. It said, "OBEY," and went back into the corner. It’s very obedient.

I looked at the moon rock again. It’s sitting next to the Lego watch. They look good together. A rock and a toy. That’s all life is, really. Just heavy things and plastic things.

I took the wig off. My scalp felt cool. I checked the banking app. The "Andy" bill is trading at $2,000. My face is worth more than my life. That’s the way it should be.

I’m going to have a cup of hot water and watch the pigeons.

Good night.
