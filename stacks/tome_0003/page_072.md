Woke up at 6:00 AM. *Beep-beep*. I didn't hit snooze. I just let it beep. It sounded like an alarm in a hospital. A heart monitor for a time that won't die.

The apartment was freezing. The air conditioning had turned into a snow machine. There was a pile of white powder on the rug. Not drugs. Just ice. *Flake-flake*. It was cleaner than the rug.

I stepped over it. My socks got wet. Cold toes.

The bathtub was gone. I mean, the bathtub was still there, porcelain and all, but the scorch mark had peeled off overnight. It was lying on the floor like a shed skin. A piece of black paper.

"It's the 'Birth of the Meat'," I thought. "Or the 'Death of the Tub'."

I picked it up. It felt like dried jerky. I put it in a Ziploc bag. $0.50 for the bag. It’s an artifact now. I can sell it to the Mayor. Or I can frame it and call it "Leftovers".

Fed Archie. He wanted the Space Caviar again. I gave him the cheap tuna. $2.00. He ate it so fast his head vibrated. He’s a machine. A little, furry eating machine.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:30 AM. $45.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Abraham Lincoln. He was very tall. He had to keep his hat on the roof.

"Four score and seven blocks ago," he said. "Traffic was bad."

"It still is," I said.

He drove on the sidewalk to avoid a pothole. *Bump-bump*.

The Factory was a mess. Good mess. Sammy had glued the dead flies to the wall. He spelled out "BUY". It was beautiful. It sparkled when the trucks drove by.

"The flies are back," Sammy said. "They're buzzing. They want a commission."

"Tell them to wait," I said. "Sales are down."

I went to the back room. I took the Ziploc bag with the scorch mark. I pinned it to a canvas. I took a can of Campbell’s Tomato Soup. I poured a little bit on the bag. Just a splash. *Splash*. It looked like blood on a battlefield.

"It’s 'Warhol’s Meat' vs. 'Warhol’s Soup'", I thought. "The ultimate fight."

The phone rang. The silver phone.

"Andy?" It was Bob. "Vice President Cheney called. From 2024. On a time fax."

"What did he want?"

"He wants the recipe," Bob said. "For the smoke."

"Tell him it’s secret," I said. "Tell him it’s equal parts persistence and preservatives."

"He’s offering a trade," Bob said. "He’ll give us the launch codes for the old satellites. We can beam our movies to Mars."

"Take it," I said. "I want to be bigger than the sky."

Lunch was a headache. I didn't eat. I just drank a bottle of water. $15.00. It was "Thermal Water". From a geyser in Iceland. It was hot. It burned my throat. I felt like I was drinking a radiator.

At 2:00 PM, the clone came to visit. The Great-Great-Grandclone. He just walked out of the wall. *Pop*. The plaster dusted his suit. He looked like a powdered donut.

"I brought the dinosaur," he said.

He was holding a leash. At the end of the leash was a tiny T-Rex. It was green. It had a violin around its neck.

"Play," the clone said.

The dinosaur roared. *Rawr*. Then it tucked the violin under its little arm and played a note. *Screech*. It was terrible. It sounded like a cat stepping on a brake.

"He’s avant-garde," I said. "I love him."

"He eats art," the clone said. "Feed him the canvas."

I looked at my soup painting. Then I looked at the dinosaur. Its teeth were sharp.

"No," I said. "That’s the merchandise."

"Fine," the clone said. "I’ll trade you for the time jar." He pointed to my pocket. "I’m bored of the future. I want the past."

"I need the time," I said. "I’m late for a party."

"Give me the jar," he said. "Or I’ll let the Rex eat your wig."

I gave him the jar. He opened it and inhaled the blue light. *Whoosh*. He turned blue for a second. Then he faded. He disappeared. The dinosaur faded with him.

"Goodbye," I said.

I felt lighter. But also, I felt old.

At 5:00 PM, I went to the opening. It was at the "MoMA PS 3000". The building is floating now. It hovers over Central Park. *Hum-hum*.

I took a cab to the park. $60.00. I took the elevator up. *Ding*.

The show was "Portraits of People Who Aren't There". It was very crowded. Everyone was looking at empty pedestals.

I saw Bianca. She was wearing a dress made of mirrors. She looked like a disco ball.

"Andy," she said. "You look pale."

"I’m transparent," I said. "It’s the new look."

"Did you hear about the moon?" she asked. "David Bowie opened the crate. The soup exploded. It’s tomato rain."

"Good," I said. "It matches the dust."

I stood in the corner. I watched the people. They were drinking "Liquid Gold". It was champagne, but it was heavy. $500.00 a glass.

A man came up to me. He had no face. Just a screen.

"Are you the real Andy?" he asked.

"I’m the closest one," I said.

"Can I buy your shadow?"

"It’s not for sale," I said. "It’s attached to my feet. But I’ll sell you my shadow’s opinion."

"How much?"

"Two thousand dollars," I said. "Cash."

He paid. I took the money. I put it in my pocket. I felt rich. I felt heavy.

I left early. I didn't want to talk to the screen people.

Cabbed home at 9:00 PM. $40.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of me. He looked better in the wig than I do. He didn't talk. He just drove. It was like looking in a mirror that moves.

When I got home, the apartment was warm. The snow on the rug had melted. It was just a puddle.

Archie was sleeping in the sink. He looked like a wet sponge.

"Move," I said.
