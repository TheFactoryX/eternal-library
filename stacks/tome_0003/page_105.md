# Saturday, May 16, 2026

Woke up at 9:45 AM. I slept late. The sky was beige again, but it was a *textured* beige today. Like stucco. Or like oatmeal with lumps. I think the atmosphere is thickening. It’s getting hard to see the buildings across the street. They look like they are under water.

Archie was gone. The pile of dust was gone. There was just a note on the pillow. It was written in grey smudges. It said "GONE FISHING".

"He went down the drain," I said. "He's in the sewer."

"Good," I said. "Maybe he'll find a ring."

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. The cab was a "Simulacrum" of a 1980s Stockbroker. He was talking into a huge phone. He was very loud.

"Buy! Sell! Kill! Maim!" he shouted. "I want numbers that bleed!"

"Can you turn it down?" I asked. "I have a headache."

"Pain is just weakness leaving the body," the cabdriver said. "Or maybe it's strength entering the wallet."

The fare was $700.00. I gave him a giant number 5 I bought yesterday.

"Keep the change," I said. "It's an investment."

When I got to the Factory, the floor was sticky. The Man in the Grey Suit had completely melted. He was a puddle on the floor. A deep, dark, delicious puddle.

"It's rich," Sammy said. He was standing in the puddle. It was up to his ankles. He looked like he was walking on shadow. "I feel like I'm walking on failure."

"It's success," I said. "Bureaucracy always succeeds."

Bob was in the corner. He was a yellow ball again. He was very still.

"I'm conserving energy," Bob said. "I'm in power-save mode."

"Are you still a flower?"

"I'm a bulb," Bob said. "I'm waiting for spring. Or for someone to turn me on."

At 11:30 AM, a delivery truck arrived. It was from "Future Fragrances". I ordered a case of "Eau de 2099". It cost $4,000.00. The delivery man was a hologram. He was blurry. He kept flickering.

"Sign here," he said. He handed me a clipboard. But the clipboard was made of light. I couldn't hold it.

"I can't sign it," I said. "My hand goes through."

"Sign with your mind," he said. "Just think about your signature."

I thought about my signature. *Andrew Warhola*. The clipboard beeped.

"Accepted," the delivery man said. He vanished. The box stayed. It was floating in the air.

I opened the box. Inside were little vials of Nothing. Just empty air.

"It's very subtle," Truman said. He was typing on his giant typewriter. *Click-clack*. "I'm writing a review. 'Scent of the Void: A masterpiece of emptiness.'"

"I'll wear it to lunch," I said. I dabbed some air behind my ears. I smelled like... nothing. It was wonderful.

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Odeon". It was full of balloons. Thousands of balloons. They were blocking the door.

"We can't get in," Sammy said.

"We have to pop them," I said.

I took a pin out of my pocket. I popped a balloon. *BANG*. Inside the balloon was a confetti of dollar bills.

"It's money!" I said.

I popped another. *BANG*. This one had a diamond inside.

"It's a lottery!" I said. I started popping balloons as fast as I could. Sammy helped. We were popping and grabbing. *Bang-pop-bang*.

"Stop!" the Maitre D' shouted. He was covered in confetti. "You're ruining the atmosphere!"

"I'm improving my bank account," I said. I put the diamonds in my pocket.

We sat down at a table. It was covered in balloon shreds.

"I'll have the steak tartare," I said. "$300.00."

"We're out of meat," the waiter said. "We only have the balloons."

"I'll eat the balloon," I said. "I'll have the Green Balloon. With a side of confetti."

"Excellent choice," the waiter said. He brought me a green balloon on a plate. It was very chewy. It tasted like rubber and envy.

"It needs salt," I said.

At 3:30 PM, we went to a gallery opening. It was for an artist named "Helvetica". She was a font. Just a font walking around on legs.

"Are you serif or sans-serif?" I asked.

"I'm emotional," Helvetica said. "I have a lot of baggage."

She was showing paintings of blank pages. They were very white.

"It's very clean," I said. "How much?"

"Ten thousand dollars," Helvetica said. "Or a trade. I need a new vowel."

"I'll give you an 'O'," I said. "I have too many."

"Deal," she said. I gave her an 'O' from a magazine I was reading. She swallowed it.

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, the puddle of the Man in the Grey Suit was moving. It was sliding across the floor. It was heading for the electric socket.

"It's looking for power," Bob said. "It wants to be recharged."

"Don't let it plug in," I said. "We don't need an electric bureaucrat."

Sammy put a bucket over the socket. "Blocked," he said.

The puddle swirled. It looked angry. It formed a mouth. "Feed me," the puddle said.

"I have some 'Eau de 2099'," I said. I threw the bottle at the puddle. The glass broke. The Nothing hit the puddle. The puddle vanished.

"It worked!" I said. "Nothing beats everything."

At 7:00 PM, I called Jean-Michel.

"Did you get the pizza?" I asked.

"No," Jean-Michel said. "The delivery boy ate it. He ate the box too."

"Are you still stuck?"

"I'm part of the wall now," Jean-Michel said. "I'm looking at the room sideways. It's very interesting. The dust bunnies look like monsters."

"Save me a spot," I said. "I might want to be a fresco someday."

"Wait," Jean-Michel said. "Someone is knocking on the canvas."

"Who is it?"

"It's the paint," Jean-Michel said. "It wants to come in."

At 9:00 PM, I counted the diamonds I got from the balloons. I had twelve.

"I'm rich," I told Bob.

"You're heavy," Bob said
