Woke up at 7:30 AM. The sky is turquoise today. Not the blue kind of turquoise, but the green kind. Like the inside of a jewelry box. Or toothpaste. It’s very minty. It hurts my eyes. I put on my sunglasses, but they made it darker green. I feel like I’m living in an emerald. Or a salad. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:45 AM. $350.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Grace Jones. She was wearing a hood made of electricity. Little sparks were flying off her shoulders.

"Hold on," Grace said. "We're going to warp."

She drove right up the side of a skyscraper. Vertical. The gravity pushed me into the seat. My wig stayed flat, though. Good wig. It obeys the laws of physics.

When we got off at the top, the Factory was a cloud. A fluffy, white cumulus floating where the studio used to be. There was a velvet rope leading into the mist.

"Do we have a reservation?" I asked.

"We're on the list," Sammy said. He was the rope. He was the velvet. He was soft.

I stepped onto the cloud. It was damp. Like a sponge. *Squelch*.

Bob was there. He was a weather vane. He was spinning around and pointing at the turbulence.

"I'm predicting the future," Bob said. *Whirrr*. "It looks windy."

Jean-Michel was there. He was painting the clouds. But the paint was heavy. It was made of lead. The clouds were sagging under the weight of the art.

"It's too heavy," Jean-Michel said. "The art is dragging the atmosphere down."

"That's culture," I said. "It weighs on you."

At 10:00 AM, the squirrel arrived. He was wearing a tiny parachute.

"I'm jumping," the squirrel said. "I'm bailing out."

"Don't go," I said. "It's just a little turbulence."

"It's raining cats," the squirrel said. He looked up.

A cat fell out of the turquoise sky. *Splat*. It landed on the cloud and bounced. It was a porcelain cat. A white china cat with a crack in its head.

"See?" the squirrel said. "It's figurative weather."

At 12:00 PM, we went to lunch. We took the "Stairway to Heaven" down to "21". But the restaurant was upside down. We were walking on the ceiling. The chandeliers were on the floor.

"The gravity is inverted," the Maitre d' said. He was standing on his head. His shoes were polished to a mirror shine. "Watch the soup."

I ordered the "Potage Germiny". $1,250.00.

It arrived in a bowl that was floating above the table. I had to pull it down to eat it.

"It's pea soup," I said. "It matches the sky."

"It's made of anticipation," Jean-Michel said. "And peas."

It was thick. I ate it with a silver spoon. The spoon bent. Everything is soft here. The metal is tired.

At 2:30 PM, I did some portraits. The subjects were clouds. I took their pictures with a camera that printed in vapor.

*Click*. A puff of white smoke.

"That's a good likeness," I said. "Very ephemeral."

"It's gone," Bob said. "It dissipated."

"That's the point," I said. "If it stays, it's just dirt."

At 4:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit appeared. He was flying. He had wings made of ticker tape. The tape was streaming behind him, printing stock prices.

"I'm up," the Man said. "I'm flying high."

"Is it the market?" I asked.

"No," the Man said. "It's the helium. I'm full of hot air."

At 6:00 PM, the wind picked up. The cloud factory started to drift. We were moving over the city.

"We're losing our lease," Sammy said. "The atmosphere is unstable."

I looked over the edge of the cloud. The city was a circuit board. Tiny lights blinking on and off. Cars moving like data packets.

"I see a pixel out," I said. "On 5th Avenue."

At 8:00 PM, we ordered dinner. "Ambrosia". $2,000.00.

The delivery was a ray of light. A golden beam that came down from the turquoise sky.

We opened our mouths and drank the light. It tasted like honey and electricity. It buzzed on my tongue. *Zap*.

"It's filling," I said. "But I need something crunchy."

I ate a piece of the cloud. It tasted like nothing. Which is everything.

At 10:00 PM, I got tired of floating. My feet felt heavy. I missed the pavement. I missed the grime. I missed the friction.

"I want to go down," I said.

"It's a long drop," Jean-Michel said. He was painting a lead anchor.

"Just drop me," I said. "I'll land."

I stepped off the edge of the cloud. *Step*.

I fell. *Whoosh*. The turquoise wind rushed past my ears. My wig flew off. It stayed up there, a silver halo in the green.

I fell through the smog. I fell through the neon signs. I fell through the memories.

*Splat*.

I landed on a pile of garbage bags in an alleyway. It was soft.

I checked my watch. It was broken. The hands were spinning.

I found my wig. It had landed on a rat. The rat looked very stylish.

"Nice hair," the rat said. He scurried away, wearing my silver. He looked like me.

"Good," I thought. "Now I don't have to do anything."

I walked out of the alley. The streetlights were humming. *Hum, hum*.

I hailed a cab. It was a hearse. Black and shiny.

"Where to?" the driver asked. He was a skeleton.

"Home," I said. "To the silver room."

"That'll be your soul," the driver said.

"Expensive," I said. "But I have a check."

So I wrote it out. And the drive was very smooth. And I didn't look back. Because looking back is just more work.
