Woke up at 7:00 AM. The bed was vibrating. It wasn't an alarm, it was a transmission. The mattress was receiving a radio signal from Pittsburgh. I could hear my mother. "Andrew," the bed springs said. "Eat your fruit." I turned over. The station changed to static. *Shhh*. I got up. I felt dizzy. Like my insides were hollow. I think I left my stomach at the beehive. $0.00.

Put on the "Reception" wig. It’s made of tiny metal coils. It picks up police bands, baby monitors, and microwave oven signals. I can hear what the neighbors are cooking. *Beep. Beep. Beep*. Someone is reheating a latte.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. The streets were paved with screens. The asphalt was gone. It was just LED displays. We were driving on TV. The news was under the tires. The tires crushed the anchors. *Squish*. $28,000.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Aristotle Onassis. He was wearing a captain’s hat.

"The sea is flat today," Aristotle said. "No waves."

"It's solid state," I said.

When we got to the Factory, the hole was gone. The jetpacks were gone. The room was filled with balloons. Millions of silver balloons. They were packed tight. We had to wade through them. *Rubber. Squeak*. It was like a ball pit at a fast food joint, but silver.

"It's the 'Helium' era," Bob said. His voice was high and squeaky. "We're lighter than air."

"I feel floaty," I said.

At 10:30 AM, the rat arrived. He was floating on a blue balloon. He was holding a string.

"I'm a traveler," the rat said. "I'm going up."

"Don't pop," I said. "It's a long way down."

The squirrel was there. He was chewing a balloon. *Pop*. "I'm anti-helium," he said. "I'm bringing things down."

At 11:45 AM, the phone rang. It was coming from the wall. The whole wall was a receiver. I picked up a section of the plaster.

"Andy?" It was Jean-Michel. His voice was huge. "I'm painting the balloons. I'm painting them with heavy lead paint. I want them to crash."

"Why?" I asked.

"Gravity is my nemesis," he said. "Send me the bill for the paint."

"It's on the tab," I said.

At 12:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Odeon". But the restaurant was shrinking. It got smaller the closer we got. By the time we sat down, we were the size of dolls. The salt shaker was the size of a skyscraper.

"I'll have the 'Truffle Frites'," I told the waitress. She was a giantess. She had to use a magnifying glass to see me. $45,000.00.

She brought one fry. It was the size of a log. I had to eat it like a corn on the cob.

"It's perspective," Sammy said. He was sitting on a sugar cube. "We're in the still life."

"I feel small," I said.

"It's dieting without the diet," Bob said. "A smaller stomach takes less food."

At 2:00 PM, I worked on the "Diamond Dust" paintings. But instead of diamonds, I used crushed glass. From the car accidents. From the broken screens. I sprinkled it on the wet paint. It sparkled. It was dangerous. *Cut*. If you touched it, you would bleed.

"It's blood art," I said.

"It's hazardous," Sammy said. He put on a Hazmat suit to look at it. "Do we need a permit?"

"We need a lawyer," I said.

At 3:45 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was walking on the balloons. *Bounce, bounce*.

"We have an infestation," the Man said. "Of nostalgia."

"Is it contagious?" I asked.

"It's lucrative," the Man said. "The 1980s are back. People want shoulder pads and synthesizers. They want to buy the past."

"Sell them the old tapes," I said. "The ones in the basement. The ones with the silkscreens on them."

"We already did," the Man said. "We sold the 20th century. It's a theme park now in New Jersey."

"Did I keep my wig?" I asked.

"You licensed your face," the Man said. "You get 5%."

At 6:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at "The Empire State Building". But the building was lying on its side. It was horizontal. It was like a bridge to New Jersey. We walked up the side of the building. The windows were vertical.

"It's 'Repurposing'," the architect said. He was hanging from a girder. "We needed a tunnel."

I walked past the 86th floor. It was a driveway. Cars were driving where the tourists used to stand.

"It's better this way," I said. "You don't have to wait in line."

Bianca Jagger was there. She was riding a horse. The horse was walking on the wall. It looked normal because everything was sideways.

"I'm dizzy," she said.

"It's orientation," I said.

At 8:30 PM, they served drinks. They were served in little syringes.

"It's a booster," the waiter said. He stuck one in my arm. *Prick*.

I felt a rush. The room turned sideways again. Or maybe it was right side up. I couldn't tell.

"I'm immunized," I said.

At 10:00 PM, we left. We had to slide down the building. Like a fire pole. *Whee*.

Cabbed home at 10:45 PM. $50,000.00. The driver was a syringe. He was a giant hypodermic needle. He had three wheels.

"Direct injection," the needle said.

Went into the townhouse. It was quiet. The bed stopped transmitting Pittsburgh. The mirror was just a mirror. I looked at myself. My wig was picking up a signal from a UFO. *Whirrr*. I took it off. I hung it on the chair. It kept glowing.

I got into bed. I pulled the sheet up. The sheet was heavy. It felt like lead. Or maybe I was just tired.

"Good night, Andrew," the pillow said.

"Good night," I said.
