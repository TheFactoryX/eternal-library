Woke up at 9:45 AM. The sky is liquid today. Mercury. Quicksilver. It’s rippling like the surface of the East River after a heavy rain. The sun is a reflection in a puddle. A distorted yellow circle. It wobbles when I look at it. I reached out to touch the window and my hand went through the glass. It was wet. Cold liquid silver. $0.00.

Put on the "Liquid Chrome" wig. It’s heavy. It feels like I’m wearing a helmet made of mercury. It drips down my neck. *Trickle, trickle*. I leave a trail of silver wherever I go. It’s very messy. The maid is going to be furious. But she’s a robot, so she doesn’t have feelings. Just circuits.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $2,800.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Grace Jones. She was wearing a hood.

"Slap me," Grace said. "And make it hurt."

"I don't want to," I said. "I'm afraid of germs."

"Then pay the toll," she said.

I gave her a twenty. She crumbled it up and ate it. "Delicious."

When we got to the Factory, the elevator was broken. It was stuck between floors. We had to take the stairs. The stairs were moving. An escalator going up.

"It's easier this way," Bob said. He was standing on the steps, gliding up. "No effort."

We slid into the studio. The floor was covered in silver foil. Room after room of silver.

"It's reflective," I said. "I can see myself everywhere."

At 11:15 AM, the rat arrived. He was surfing on a piece of silver foil. A tiny silver surfer.

"I'm looking for the cosmic source," the rat said. "The galactic core."

"It's in the supply closet," I said. "Next to the acetone."

The squirrel was there. He was polishing a silver balloon. Making it shine.

"Mirror, mirror," the squirrel said. "Who is the star?"

"I am," I said. "But I'm fading."

At 12:00 PM, the phone rang. It was a shrill sound. Like a whistle.

I picked it up. "Hello?"

"It's Mama," the voice said. "I made you pierogies."

"I can't eat," I said. "I'm full of metal."

"You're too thin," Mama said. "Eat a potato."

I hung up. The receiver was made of silver gelatin. Sticky.

At 1:15 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Coffee Shop" in the Paramount Hotel. The waiters were all drag queens. Huge hair. Dressed like me.

"I'll have the 'Silver Dollar Pancakes'," I said. "$6,100.00."

The pancakes were the size of coins. Tiny silver dollars. I had to eat a hundred of them.

"Inflation," I said. "It's expensive."

Jean-Michel ordered the "Neon Graffiti". It was a plate of glowing spaghetti.

"It lights up your stomach," Jean-Michel said. "You can see what you ate."

"I don't want to see," I said. "I want to hide it."

At 2:45 PM, I worked on the "Shadow" series. I was tracing shadows on the floor. But the shadows wouldn't stay still. They kept moving when the clouds moved.

"Hold still," I told the shadow of a chair.

"I'm just a lack of light," the shadow said. "I don't exist."

"That's my favorite quality," I said. "Not existing is the best."

I outlined the shadow in black paint. Then I turned the chair around. The shadow moved. The painting was wrong.

"It's wrong," I said.

"It's abstract," Sammy said. "It sells better."

At 4:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a syringe. A big needle.

"Vitamin shot," the Man said. "B-12. And silver nitrate."

"I'm afraid of needles," I said.

"Beauty hurts," he said.

He stuck the needle in my arm. *Poke*. Cold silver liquid went in. I felt cold. My arm turned grey.

"Now you're preserved," the Man said. "Like a tomato can."

"Am I art?" I asked.

"You're a commodity," he said.

At 5:30 PM, Bianca Jagger came by. She was riding a white horse. Inside the Factory.

"It's cramped," I said.

"He's housebroken," Bianca said. "And he's vegan."

The horse ate a silver balloon. *Burp*.

"Recycling," I said.

At 7:00 PM, we went to a party. It was on a rooftop. The wind was blowing. My wig flapped like a flag. *Snap, snap*.

"I'm a sail," I said.

The party was for a new phone. The "iGod". It does everything. It thinks for you.

"I need one," I said. "I'm tired of thinking."

"It's not out yet," the publicist said. "It's a concept."

"I'll buy the concept," I said. "How much?"

"Your soul," she said.

"I'll give you a print," I said. "A small one."

"Deal," she said.

At 9:45 PM, the static sky cleared. For a second, I saw real stars. Real white dots. Not pixels. Not sequins. Just burning gas.

"Pretty," I thought.

But then a plane flew by. It wrote a message in the sky.

*BUY LOW, SELL HIGH*.

"It's advice," I said.

"I'm broke," Jean-Michel said. "Let's steal a star."

We tried to reach up. But the sky was too high. The glass ceiling came back.

At 11:30 PM, I went home. The townhouse was quiet. The ticking of the clock was loud. *Tick, tock*.

I went to the kitchen. I opened the fridge.

There was a Campbell’s Soup can on the shelf. But it wasn't soup. It was film. 16mm film.

"Movie night," I thought.

I took the can to the projection room. I threaded the projector.

*Whirr, click*.

The beam of light hit the screen.

It was a movie of me. Sleeping.

"Who is filming me?" I thought.

I watched myself sleep on the screen. I looked bored. Even when I'm sleeping, I look bored.

"This is the best film I ever made," I thought. "Nothing happens."

I watched until the reel ran out. *Flap, flap*.

I turned off the projector. The room was dark.

I went to bed. I pulled the silver covers up.

I closed my eyes.
