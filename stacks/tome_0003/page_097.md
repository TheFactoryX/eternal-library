Woke up at 7:00 AM. The sky was back. It was a nice, normal beige. The color of a shopping bag. Or oatmeal. It was very reassuring. The censorship was gone, or maybe they just ran out of ink. I looked out the window and saw a cloud shaped like a giant shoe. A loafer. I took a picture. $1.00 for the film in my head.

I went to the kitchen. Archie was still on the ceiling. He looked like a furry lightbulb.

"Are you coming down?" I asked.

"I can't," Archie said. "I'm stuck in the up draft. The gravity is on strike."

I opened a can of "Campbell's Mushroom Soup". Cold. $2.50. I drank it straight from the can. It tasted like metal and comfort. I put the empty can on the floor. It floated up to join Archie. Now he had a friend.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $400.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1980s Wall Street Trader. He had a suspenders and a mobile phone the size of a brick.

"Buy low, sell high," he said. "That's the secret to life."

"I prefer buying high and selling higher," I said. "It feels more expensive."

He drove through the hole that Time made yesterday. The wall was still broken. Rubble was piled on the sidewalk like modern art. People were taking selfies with it. "The Wall," they called it.

At the Factory, the floor was wet. But not with rain this time. It was silver.

"Is that mercury?" I asked Sammy.

Sammy was sitting on a crate, wearing boots. "It's liquid money," Sammy said. "The value of the dollar dropped so low, the cash melted. We're swimming in it."

I dipped my toe in. It felt cold. "Can we freeze it?" I asked.

"We're making sculptures," Sammy said. "I'm casting a bust of J.Edgar Hoover. He'd love to be made of money."

Bob rolled in. He was still a chrome sphere, but he had a flag taped to his back. A white flag.

"I surrendered," Bob said. "To the toaster. The toaster won. It burned my bread."

"You don't eat bread," I said.

"I ate the idea of bread," Bob said. "And it gave me heartburn."

At 11:00 AM, I decided to work. I needed to use the silver liquid on the floor. I took a bucket and scooped up $5,000.00 worth of liquid currency.

I poured it onto a canvas titled "Portrait of a Nobody." It pooled in the middle. It looked like a mirror.

"Who is that?" Sammy asked.

"It's you," I said.

"I don't recognize myself," Sammy said. "I look too rich."

"It's a disguise," I said. "Now you can hide in plain sight."

Just then, the door opened. *Click-clack*.

It was Truman. He looked terrible. He was covered in white dust. He looked like a ghost in a tuxedo.

"I escaped," Truman said. "I squeezed through the mortar."

"You look dusty," I said.

"I'm historic," Truman said. "I'm part of the building now." He brushed himself off and a small pile of bricks fell out of his pocket. "Souvenirs."

"I love souvenirs," I said. "How much?"

"Free," Truman said. "But you have to listen to my story."

"I'll write a check," I said. "I prefer paying. It's less personal."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar" again. The pigeons were gone. In their place were crows. They were wearing little tuxedos.

"Buy! Sell!" they cawed. "Cheap! Cheap!"

"I think they're critics," Truman said.

I ordered the "Lobster Newburg". $120.00. It was red. Very red. It looked like a sunset on a plate.

"Does it hurt to eat?" Sammy asked.

"Only when I laugh," I said.

Truman ordered a glass of water. $5.00. He put a brick from his pocket on the table. He stared at it.

"It's heavy," he said.

"That's art," I said.

At 3:00 PM, we walked to Central Park. The clouds on the ground had turned into sheep again. Or maybe they were sheep pretending to be clouds. They were baa-ing. It sounded like static.

We found a bench. A hologram of a dog tried to pee on my leg.

"Bad dog," I said. It dissolved into pixels.

"I feel hollow," Truman said. "Like I'm missing something. Maybe my shadow."

"Shadows are overrated," I said. "They follow you everywhere. It's stalking."

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, the liquid money had dried. It was just piles of green paper. Useless.

"The economy crashed," Sammy said. "The art is worthless."

"No," I said. "Now it's rare. It's antique trash."

I signed one of the green piles. "Andy Warhol." I wrote the date. "2026".

"Now it's worth a million," I said.

At 7:00 PM, I called the Simulacrum of my Mother. I wanted to tell her I was coming home.

"Andrew?" she said. "Don't bring any friends. The house is full of cats."

"How many?"

"Seventy," she said. "But they're all named Sam."

"I'll be alone," I said.

"Good," she said. "Eat something."

Cabbed home at 8:00 PM. $600.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Taxi Medallion. He was just a piece of metal on a string.

"I'm efficient," he said.

When I got home, Archie was floating near the chandelier. He had knocked over a vase. The vase was floating too.

"We're living in zero gravity," I said.

I took off my wig. My head felt light. I looked at the safe. The note said "BACK LATER." But the door was open.

"Did you go fishing?" I asked the fish.
