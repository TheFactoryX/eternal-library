# Monday, March 23, 2026

Woke up at 7:00 AM. The room was normal. Too normal. The walls were solid. The floor was flat. The dust motes were gone, replaced by a Roomba that was bumping into the baseboard. *Thump-thump-thump*. It sounded like a heartbeat.

I felt light. The "Tomato Juice Heavy" had worn off. I was weightless. I had to hold onto the mattress to keep from floating up to the ceiling. I put on my boots. The silver ones from Prada. They weighed 5 pounds each. Just enough to keep me anchored.

I went to the kitchen. The puddle of voice from the can yesterday had dried up. It left a stain on the table. It looked like a map of New Jersey.

The cat was there. She was eating the stain. *Slurp-slurp*.

"Don't eat that," I said. "It's me."

She looked at me. She had gravy on her whiskers. "Tastes like chicken," she meowed. Actually, she just meowed, but I heard chicken.

I had a cup of coffee. $45.00 for the beans. They were civet beans, but the civets were robotic. They had lasers for eyes. The coffee tasted like electricity. It woke me up.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $70.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Basquiat. He was painting the dashboard while we drove. He used ketchup and mustard.

"It’s about the texture," Jean-Michel said. "The viscosity of the commute."

"Watch the road," I said. "The road is art."

The Factory was crowded. A mob. They were holding torches. But the torches were smartphones. All the flashlights were on.

"What's happening?" I asked Sammy.

"It's the Screenings," Sammy said. "We're screening the aura. People want to see your soul."

"I keep that in the fridge," I said.

"We sold it," Sammy said. "To a collector in Tokyo. He bought it sight unseen. He paid in Bitcoin. Or maybe Burger King coupons. I can't tell the difference anymore."

I pushed through the crowd. *Flash-flash-flash*. My eyes hurt. Everyone was recording me. I felt like a barcode being scanned over and over.

I ducked into the elevator. I pressed the button for the "Bathroom." It’s the only safe place.

I sat in the stall. The walls were covered in graffiti. Someone had written "ANDY WAS HERE" in 1990. And "ANDY IS HERE" in 2026. And "ANDY IS NOWHERE" in 3050.

I looked at my watch. The Cracker Jack watch. The smiley face was red. Red means "Stop."

I flushed the toilet. The water swirled counter-clockwise. It made a *gurgle* sound.

Lunch was at "The Automatic." It's a restaurant run by vending machines. But the machines are rude.

I stood in front of Machine #4. It had a plastic spiral.

"What do you want?" the machine asked. Its voice was synthesized. Like a GPS.

"The tuna sandwich," I said. "On rye."

"Out of stock," the machine said. "I have sadness. I have ennui. I have a bag of chips."

"I'll take the ennui," I said. "How much?"

"Your childhood memory of the amusement park," the machine said.

"Deal," I said. I didn't go to amusement parks. I was scared of the rides. They went too fast.

It dispensed a small box. I opened it. It was empty. Just air.

"Delicious," I said. "Very light."

I ate the air. It tasted like cotton candy and rain.

I paid with a $20 bill. The machine took the bill and spit it out. "Don't tip me," it said.

At 2:00 PM, I went to the park. I wanted to see if the grass was still blue. It was. It was electric blue. humming.

I sat on a bench. A "Simulacrum" of Dylan Thomas was sitting there. He was drinking milk from a bottle.

"The rage of the ocean," he said. "The rage."

"It's just a pond," I said.

"It's all an ocean," he said. "If you're small enough."

I looked at the water. There were fish in it. Goldfish. But they had fins made of dollar bills.

"Inflation," Dylan said. "Even the fish are rich."

A duck flew by. It quacked. "Quack."

"Did you hear that?" I asked. "It spoke English."

"No," Dylan said. "It spoke Duck. You just projected meaning onto it. That's your problem, Andy. You think everything is about you."

"Isn't it?" I asked.

"Maybe," he said. "Or maybe you're just the frame."

I took out my notebook. I drew a picture of the duck. I drew a frame around it. Now it was art.

I walked home at 5:00 PM. The wind was picking up. Newspapers were flying. But the newspapers were blank. No headlines. Just white paper. A blank future.

I passed a television store. The screens were all showing static. But the static was forming shapes. Faces. My face.

"Buy me," the TV said. "I'm lonely."

"No thanks," I said. "I have enough static at home."

I got to the apartment. The cat was on the couch. She was grooming herself. She looked up.

"Did you bring anything?" she asked. She didn't speak. She just looked hungry.

"I brought ennui," I said.

"I prefer tuna," she said.

I went to the fridge. I took out a can of tuna. It was real tuna. I think. The label was old. "Dolphin Safe." But I don't know about the cats.

I opened the can. The smell was strong. Fishy.

The cat purred. *Vroom-vroom*. She sounded like a motorcycle.

I put the tuna on a plate. A paper plate. $0.50.

She ate it. I watched her eat. It was fascinating. The way she chewed. Side to side.

I sat down. I took off the silver boots. My feet felt naked.

I looked at the table. The stain was gone. The cat had eaten it all. She ate my past.

I felt lighter. Floating again.

I grabbed the table leg. *Grip*.

I need to buy something heavy tomorrow. A paperweight. A brick. A mortgage.

I looked at the time. 6:30 PM.

Time for dinner.

I dialed the number for room service. But I'm in my apartment. I dialed my own number.

"Hello?" I said.

"Hello?" I said back.

"Is Andy there?" I asked.

"He's sleeping," I said. "Can I take a message?"
