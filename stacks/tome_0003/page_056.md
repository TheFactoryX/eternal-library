# Saturday, March 28, 2026

> Written at 2026-03-28 23:45:10

---

Woke up and the floor had stopped spinning. It was a relief. I lay in bed for a long time just enjoying the stillness. The ceiling wasn't dripping or vibrating. It was just white. Boring white. I almost missed the shoe stain.

I got up at 8:30 AM. My body felt solid again. Not like gelatin and not like a sponge. Just heavy, sagging flesh. Gravity is back. I looked in the mirror. My hair is growing back. It’s silver fuzz. Like a peach. A very expensive peach.

The cat was sitting on the windowsill. She was staring at a bird. The bird was a drone. It was hovering and tweeting. *Tweet-tweet*. It was selling insurance.

" Ignore it," I told the cat.

She ignored me.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:00 AM. $42.00. The driver was a human. A real one. I think. He had a face, but he didn't say much. He just listened to talk radio. People arguing about the weather on Mars. "It's too dusty," they said. "We need a dome."

The Factory was buzzing. Not with people, but with electricity. The air smelled like ozone and popcorn.

Sammy was standing by the silkscreen machine. He was wearing a gas mask. He looked like a giant insect.

"Don't breathe the air," Sammy said. His voice was muffled. *Muffle-muffle*.

"Why?" I asked.

"We're printing money," Sammy said. "Literally. We're running out of cash for the helium."

He pulled a sheet off the table. It was a portrait of Benjamin Franklin. But he was wearing a wig. My wig.

"It's the 'Franklin/Warhol' series," Sammy said. "It's worth a trillion."

"How much did the ink cost?"

"About $600," Sammy said.

"Put it on my tab," I said.

I walked to the back room. I wanted to work on my Oxidation paintings. The Piss Paintings. But I didn't have to pee. I was dehydrated. The pills from yesterday dried me out. I felt like a raisin.

I tried anyway. I stared at the canvas. I concentrated. Nothing happened. Just a drop. *Plip*. A tiny copper dot on a sea of silver.

"It's minimalist," I said.

"It's a medical condition," Sammy said. He took off the mask. He looked sweaty. "Drink some water. We need more fluid."

I drank from the water cooler. The water was blue. Electric Blue Kool-Aid. $2.00 a cup. It tasted like sugar and batteries. I felt a jolt. My heart did a *flip-flop*.

"Lunch was at the Boom Boom Room," I said. "But it's in the sewer now."

"Chic," Sammy said. "Very subterranean."

We went down. The stairs were slippery. Moss grew on the walls. It was glowing green. Very radioactive. Very pretty.

The tables were made of old sewer grates. The water rushed underneath. *Gurgle-gurgle*.

I ordered the spaghetti. $40.00. It was served by a rat. A giant rat. He was wearing a tuxedo.

"Here you go, Mr. Warhol," the rat said. He had a squeaky voice. *Squeak*.

"Thank you," I said. "Are you a Simulacrum?"

"I'm a descendant," the rat said. "From the sewers of '86. My grandfather knew Keith."

"Fascinating," I said.

I ate the spaghetti. It was al dente. The sauce was red. Very red. Like a car crash.

I looked at the menu. They were serving "Sushi" made of colored soap. "Don't eat it," the menu said. "Just look."

I paid the bill with a "Franklin/Warhol" bill. The rat sniffed it.

"Feels like linen," the rat said. "Smells like ambition."

"Keep the change," I said.

We went back to the Factory. There was a package at the door. It was wrapped in brown paper. It was vibrating.

"What is it?" Sammy asked.

"I think it's my wig," I said. "From the moon."

I opened it. *Riiiip*.

Inside was a rock. A grey moon rock. And a note.

*Gone native. -The Wig*

"It didn't come back," I said.

"It evolved," Sammy said. "It's a rock now. Very grounded."

I put the rock on my desk. It made a *thud*. A heavy sound.

"I'll paint it," I said. "Fifty times. Grey on Grey."

"Very depressing," Sammy said.

"Very expensive," I said.

I sat down. The phone rang. *Ring-ring*.

I picked it up. It was Jean-Michel. Or maybe it was just a recording of Jean-Michel. He sounded far away.

"Andy," he said. "I'm floating."

"Are you a balloon?" I asked.

"I'm a cloud," he said. "I'm drifting over the Factory. Can you see me?"

I looked up. The skylight was grey. I couldn't see anything.

"I see the pollution," I said. "Is that you?"

"That's my aura," he said. "It's toxic today."

"Nice," I said. "Can you sign it?"

"I already did," he said. "It's on the wind. Just breathe it in."

I breathed in. *Inhale*. I smelled spray paint and ozone.

"I got it," I said.

"Great," he said. "Send the check to the sky."

He hung up. *Click*.

I looked at Sammy. "Did you get that?"

"I'm filing it," Sammy said. "Under 'Atmospheric Assets'."

I looked at my watch. It was 6:00 PM. The smiley face was orange. Orange means "Closing Time."

"I'm going home," I said.

"Do you want the rock?"

"No," I said. "Let it sit. It needs to acclimate."

I walked outside. The street was quiet. The rain was starting again. This time it was horizontal. *Slash-slash*. It hit the buildings like little knives.

I hailed a cab. A yellow one. The driver was a Simulacrum of me. He had the wig. The fake wig.

"Where to?" he asked. He sounded tired.

"Home," I said. "Just drive."

"You look familiar," he said.

"I look like everyone," I said. "I look like a newspaper."

He drove. The wipers went *swish-swish*. They smeared the water. Like my paintings.

I took out my diary. I wrote the date. March 28.

"What are you writing?" the driver asked.

"Nothing," I said. "Just numbers."
