Woke up at 6:00 AM. The room was spinning. Literally spinning. The floor is a turntable. I rolled out of bed and slid across the floor into the closet. *Thump*. It’s very disorienting. I think the landlord is remodeling the building while we’re sleeping. Or maybe the earth just decided to speed up. I tried to stand up, but the gravity was shifting from left to right. I had to crawl to the bathroom on my hands and knees. My knees are bruised. I look like a cherub.

The moon rock was gone. I left it on the table at Serendipity yesterday. I wonder if the robot waiter kept it. Or maybe it just evaporated. Things don't last anymore. Not even rocks.

Archie was clinging to the doorframe. His claws were dug into the wood. He looked like a furry anchor.

"Meow," he said. It sounded like a question.

"I don't know," I said. "Just hold on."

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $52.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a young Marilyn Monroe. She was crying. Not real tears, just digital leaks. *Glitch-glitch*. Her mascara was running down her face in pixels.

"Why are you crying?" I asked.

"I'm processing," she said. "It's a lot of data, being an icon."

"I know," I said. "It's exhausting."

She dropped me off. I gave her a $20.00 tip. She tried to take it, but her hand went through it.

The Factory was a mess. The bio-degradable Brillo boxes had grown overnight. They were huge. They were pulsing. *Thump-thump*. They filled the room. It was like walking through a maze of meat.

Sammy was sitting on top of one. He looked like a king on a throne made of hamburger.

"We have a problem," Sammy said. "They're hungry."

"Feed them," I said.

"We're out of sugar," Sammy said. "And they're developing personalities. This one thinks it's Picasso."

One of the boxes shifted. It grew a primitive eye. It looked at me.

"Soup?" it asked.

"No," I said. "You are soup."

"Art?" it asked.

"Business," I said.

I pushed my way through the warm, pink hallway to the office. The phone was ringing. It was a shrill sound. Like a scream.

"Hello?"

"Andy?" It was Bob. "The Vice President is angry. He says you can't just send a wig. He needs the whole package. He's threatening to digitize the estate."

"I'll send a finger," I said. "A pinky."

"He wants an endorsement," Bob said. "He wants you to paint his portrait. On a tank."

"I don't do military," I said. "I do soup."

"Do it for the money," Bob said. "He'll pay you in tax exemptions. Forever."

"Forever is a long time," I said. "Okay. But I use the cheap paint. The shiny stuff."

I hung up. The room smelled like raw steak and printer ink.

I went to the front to check on the "Authentic Andy Warhol Experience." The chair was occupied. A woman was sitting there. She was holding a sign that said "OBSERVE ME." But I wasn't there. It was just a cardboard cutout of me. The Real Me was in the back.

"I can see you," the cardboard cutout said. It had a speaker in the chest.

I sat down and watched the woman through the crack in the door. She was checking her phone. She was bored.

"It's working," I whispered. "She's bored and famous."

Lunch was a pill. $15.00. I bought it from a vending machine in the lobby. It was supposed to taste like a BLT. It just tasted like pink.

I spent the afternoon painting the Vice President. I didn't have a tank, so I painted him on a toaster. It’s the same shape. *Rectangular*. I used the silver paint. Lots of silver. He looked like a superhero. A very angry superhero.

Sammy came in. He was sweating.

"The boxes are reproducing," he said. "It's mitosis. We're going to be drowning in them by morning."

"Sell them," I said. "Call it 'Limited Edition Meat'."

"They'll rot," he said.

"That's the beauty part," I said. "The collector has to eat them to preserve them. It's 'Consumable Art'. It's the ultimate investment."

Sammy looked at me. He blinked. His eyelashes were stuck together.

"You're a genius, Andy," he said. "A disgusting genius."

At 6:00 PM, the fire alarm rang. Not a beep, but a voice. It was a nice lady voice. "Please exit the building. The art is becoming aggressive."

We ran out. The boxes were trying to leave with us. They were growing little legs. *Crawl-crawl*.

Cabbed to the "Odeon" at 7:30 PM. $60.00. Traffic was gridlocked. The streetlights were flashing purple. It was "Prince Night" in the city, even though he’s not dead, he’s just living on the moon now. Or maybe I'm thinking of someone else.

I sat at the bar. I ordered a Perrier. $9.00. The bubbles were square.

A woman sat next to me. She was wearing a dress made of sunglasses.

"Hi Andy," she said. "I'm a critic."

"Go away," I said. "I'm off the clock."

"I just want to know," she said. "Is it real? The boxes? The meat? Is it Art?"

I looked at her reflection in her dress. There were hundreds of little mes.

"It's real if I paid for it," I said.

She took a photo of me. *Flash*. My eyes were red.

"You look sad," she said.

"I'm not sad," I said. "I'm just processing. It's a lot of data, being an icon."

I left the Perrier unfinished. I walked outside. The air was cool. The digital rain was back. Blue rain.

I hailed a cab. $35.00 to go home.

The apartment was dark. The spinning had stopped. I was dizzy. Archie was on the bed. He looked normal. He licked his paw.

I took off my shoes. $450.00 shoes. They hurt my feet. I put on the slippers. They feel like clouds.

I sat down to write. The page is blank. I am blank. It's good.

I thought about the moon rock. I wonder if the robot waiter ate it.

I thought about the Vice President. I hope he likes toast.

I thought about the boxes. I hope they don't dream.

"Goodnight, Archie."
