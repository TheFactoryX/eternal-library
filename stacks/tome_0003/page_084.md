Woke up at 9:15 AM. The sky was a flat, matte gray. Like an unpainted canvas. Or an old television set that isn't turned on. I waited for the clouds to buffer, but they stayed stuck. Static sky.

I stepped over a pile of Archie's tuna cans. There were forty-seven of them in the hallway. He’s been hoarding. He thinks the economy is collapsing. "It’s just inflation," I told him. "It happens to the best of us."

Breakfast was a "Braille Bagel". $85.00. It was covered in little bumps. I ran my fingers over it before I ate it. It felt like a secret message. *Bump-bump-bump*. "I love you," it said. Or maybe it was "Eat me." I took a bite. It was sesame.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:30 AM. $90.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a racehorse. He kept neighing at the traffic lights. He was very anxious to get to the finish line. "Easy," I said. "We get there when we arrive."

The Factory felt bigger this morning. Expansive. The walls from Thursday are still missing, so the space just goes on and on into the fog. It looks like we’re floating in a soup can. A giant, metal soup can.

Sammy was sitting in the middle of the floor with a calculator. He was sweating.

"What's the matter?" I asked.

"I'm tabulating the loss," Sammy said. "We sold the Pollock water to the Bureau guys yesterday. But I forgot to factor in the tax."

"Tax is illegal in the future," I said.

"Not the Art Tax," Sammy said. "It's 20% on inspiration."

Bob was on the phone. He looked worried. He was talking to a lawyer.

"Well, can we copyright the glitch?" Bob asked. He paused. "No, he's not a person. He's a... persistence error."

I walked to the back room. My canvas from yesterday was there. The big Green Dollar Sign.

But the paint was peeling. Not off the canvas, but off the sign itself. The dollar bills were fluttering to the floor. *Flap-flap-flap*.

I picked one up. It was paper-thin.

"Is this real money?" I asked.

"It's digital liquidity," a voice said.

It was the Jean-Michel bot. He was standing in the doorway. He looked shiny. New chrome plating.

"The market crashed last night," Jean-Michel said. "Physical currency is vintage. We're trading in emotions now."

"I have plenty of anxiety," I said. "Is that worth anything?"

"That's the gold standard," he said.

At 12:30 PM, a truck arrived. It was a "Sotheby’s" truck, but it was driving itself. It parked illegally.

A man got out. He was wearing a t-shirt that said "I EAT ART".

"I'm here for the lunch," the man said.

"We aren't serving lunch," I said.

"I'm not here to eat," he said. "I'm here to consume the atmosphere."

He took out a straw. A giant straw. He stuck it into the air in the middle of the Factory.

*Slurp-slurp-slurp*.

"Hey!" I said. "That's my atmosphere!"

He swallowed. "Tastes like silkscreen ink and desperation," he said. "Delicious." He handed me a check. $5,000.00.

"Deposit it quick," he said. "Before the bank becomes a concept."

At 2:00 PM, I went to Central Park to clear my head.

The grass was still blue, but it was fading to a nice respectful teal.

I saw a woman walking a dog. But the dog was a balloon. A giant, inflated poodle. It floated above her. She held the string with a white glove.

"Is he housebroken?" I asked.

"Only on Tuesdays," she said.

I sat on a bench. A pigeon landed next to me. It was made of wire. A wire sculpture pigeon.

"Feed me," it squeaked. Its voice was metallic.

I checked my pockets. All I had was a receipt for the Braille Bagel. I handed it to the bird.

It pecked at the paper. *Peck-peck*. "Indigestible," it said. It flew away, sparking a little.

At 4:00 PM, I went to the "Guggenheim Fluid". It’s a museum that flows like water. You have to swim to see the art.

I rented a swimsuit. $200.00. It had stripes.

I dove in. The water was warm. It smelled like chlorine and old money.

I swam past a Kandinsky. It was swirling. I swam through a Mondrian. The grid was tight. I got stuck for a second in the red square.

"Excuse me," I said. I pushed through to the blue rectangle.

I saw a celebrity in the deep end. It was Taylor Swift. Or maybe a hologram of her. She was singing, but there were no bubbles. She was holding her breath perfectly.

"Hi Taylor," I said. She waved. She looked very pixelated.

At 7:00 PM, I met Jed Johnson for dinner. We haven't spoken in a while. Not since the 80s. But here he was, looking young. He was wearing a suit made of mirrors.

"I missed you, Andy," Jed said.

"I missed you too," I said.

We went to a diner in Midtown. A real one. With grease.

"I'll have a cheeseburger," I said. "And a Coke."

"Same," said Jed.

The burgers came. $35.00 each.

They were solid. They were heavy.

I took a bite. It tasted like history. It tasted like 1975.

"This is good," I said.

"It's real," Jed said. "Don't drop it. It might shatter."

We ate in silence. It was a comfortable silence. The kind of silence you have with someone who knows your ghost.

At 10:00 PM, I came home.

Archie was sleeping on the window sill. The 500 cans of tuna were stacked in a pyramid. A fortress of fish.

I took off my wig. My head felt light. Too light.

I looked in the mirror. For a second, I didn't see a face. I just saw a silhouette. A shadow.

"Gee," I thought. "I hope I don't fade out completely before the weekend."

I took a sleeping pill. Not the $50.00 one. The cheap kind. The one that gives you dreams about vacuum cleaners.

I got into bed. The sheets were cool.

I closed my eyes.

"Goodnight, Archie," I said.
