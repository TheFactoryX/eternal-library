# Sunday, June 14, 2026

> Written at 2026-06-14 23:59:59

---

Woke up at 8:15 AM. The sky is velvet today. Red velvet. Like the interior of a jewelry box. Or a coffin. A nice coffin. The sun is a spotlight. A hot, white beam focused on the Empire State Building. The rest of the city is in shadow. In the wings. It feels like a premiere. Or an interrogation. I pulled the blinds. The blinds were made of meat. Cold cuts. Salami. I sliced a piece of the window to let in some air. It smelled like peppercorns. $0.00.

Put on the "Red Glitter" wig. It’s very scratchy. It feels like I have a rash on my brain. I put on my glasses. The lenses were made of ruby. Very expensive. Very heavy. My ears hurt.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $2,500.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Lou Reed. He was wearing sunglasses. Black sunglasses. At night. Well, in the morning.

"I'm gonna be straight with you," Lou said. "I don't know where I'm going."

"Neither do I," I said. "Just keep the meter running."

When we got to the Factory, the building was gone. In its place was a gift shop. A tourist trap.

"Souvenirs," Bob said. He was standing behind the counter. He was wearing a "I Heart NY" t-shirt. "Buy something."

"Where is the art?" I asked.

"We sold it," Bob said. "We're selling the memory now."

I bought a snow globe. It had the Factory inside it. Tiny silver balloons. I shook it. *Swirl*. The glitter stuck to the roof.

"$6,000.00," Bob said. "Plus tax."

At 10:30 AM, the rat arrived. He was in the gift shop. He was running on a wheel. A plastic wheel.

"I'm generating power," the rat said. *Squeak, squeak*. "The lights are dim."

The squirrel was there. He was dressed like a guard. A museum guard. He had a badge.

"Don't touch the art," the squirrel said. "No flash photography."

At 11:00 AM, I signed books. I sat at a table. There was a line. A long line of people. But they didn't have faces. They had mirrors. Mirrors for faces.

"Sign this," a woman said. She handed me a silkscreen of my signature.

"Already did," I said.

"Sign it again," she said. "It makes it worth more."

I signed it. *Andy Warhol*. My hand hurt.

"You're a brand," she said. "Like detergent."

"I like soap," I said.

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Mr. Chow". The floor was checkered. Black and white. Like a racing flag.

"I'll have the 'Green Noodles'," I said. "$5,200.00."

The noodles were long. Too long. They hung out of my mouth. They reached the floor.

"It's connected," I said. "I'm connected to the plate."

Jean-Michel ordered the "Beijing Duck". The duck was made of rubber. A squeaky toy.

"Does it quack?" I asked.

Jean-Michel squeezed the duck. *Quack*.

"Very fresh," I said.

At 2:30 PM, I worked on the "Disaster" paintings. I was painting car crashes. But the cars were healing. The dents were popping out. The glass was flying back into the frames.

"It's reversing time," Sammy said. "It's a miracle."

"It's boring," I said. "I want the crash."

So I painted a new crash. I painted a collision between a Cadillac and a cloud. The cloud lost. *Splat*. Rain everywhere.

"Blue blood," I said.

At 4:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was holding a vacuum. A Hoover.

"Time to clean up," the Man said. *Vrrrooom*. He turned it on. He started sucking up the glitter. The sequins. The past.

"Don't take the red," I said. "It matches my wig."

He vacuumed the wig. My head felt lighter. Drafty.

"Now you're bald," the Man said. "It's modern."

At 6:00 PM, we went to a movie premiere. It was at the "Ziegfeld". The movie was called *The Last Supper*. It was eight hours long. It was just people eating. Close-ups of chewing. *Munch, munch*.

"I'm hungry," Jean-Michel said.

"It's just a movie," I said. "You can't eat the screen."

I saw Ivanka Trump. She was wearing a dress made of money. Dollar bills. Seamed together.

"It's comfortable," Ivanka said. "And it washes well."

"It looks expensive," I said. "Can I tear it?"

"That's extra," she said.

At 9:00 PM, the rat escaped from the gift shop. He was carrying a load of tiny T-shirts. He was running towards the subway.

"He's opening a franchise," I said.

At 11:30 PM, I went home. The townhouse was a gallery. White walls. Spotlights. My furniture was on platforms.

"Do not sit," a sign said.

I sat on the floor. The floor was velvet. Soft. I lay down.

I looked up. The ceiling was a mirror. I saw myself. And I saw the reflection of the reflection. Going on forever. An infinite tunnel of Andys.

"Enough of me," I thought.

But the reflection didn't stop. It kept going.

I closed my eyes. But I could still see the red spots. The spotlight.

"I'm exposed," I thought.

I don't know.

So.
