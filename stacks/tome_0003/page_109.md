Woke up at 7:15 AM. The sky was plaid. Red and black checks. It looked like a lumberjack shirt wrapped around the world. I put on my glasses to check, but that just made the checks bigger. I felt like I was inside a flannel box. It was very cozy. Too cozy. I felt like I needed to chop down a tree, or maybe just drink some coffee. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $2,000.00. The traffic was terrible because the clouds were low. They were resting on the rooftops. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Marcel Duchamp. He was wearing a glass cube on his head.

"It's a readymade helmet," he said. "It protects my thoughts from the air."

"Can you see the road?" I asked.

"I don't look at the road," he said. "I look at the concept of transit."

We drove on the sidewalk for three blocks. I paid him with a urinal cake I found in my pocket.

"It's a fountain," he said. "Bravo."

When I got to the Factory, Bob was mountain. The numbers had stopped dripping, and now he was a solid pile of integers. He looked like a pyramid made of calculators.

"I've stabilized," Bob said. His voice sounded like a dial-up modem. "I am the Dow Jones Industrial Average."

"Are you up or down?" I asked.

"I'm volatile," Bob said. "Tickle me and the market crashes."

The squirrel was sitting on top of the Bob-mountain. He was using the golden hanger as a fishing rod. He was fishing in the air.

"I caught a boot," the squirrel said. He pulled up a shoe. "And a tire."

"There's an ocean above us," I said. "I knew it."

Sammy was sweeping the floor again, but the broom was arguing with him.

"Not there," the broom said. "I like that corner. It's dusty. It's vintage."

"You're fired," Sammy said.

"I'm union," the broom said.

At 11:00 AM, the doorbell rang. It wasn't a sound. It was a smell. Fried chicken. *Crispy. Greasy.*

I opened the door. It was a giant bucket of chicken. But the bucket had legs. Little red plastic legs.

"I'm the Colonel," the bucket said. "I'm here to franchise the Factory."

"We're not open," I said. "Unless you have mashed potatoes."

"I have slaw," the bucket said.

"Come in," I said.

The bucket waddled in. It left a trail of grease. The shadow I sold yesterday was gone, but the grease looked like a shadow. It was greasier though.

At 12:30 PM, Jean-Michel came out of the wall. He was still a sketch, but he was in color now. Blue lines.

"I found some crayons," Jean-Michel said. "I'm vibrant."

"Smear-proof?" I asked.

"Only if you frame me," he said.

We went to lunch at "The Odeon". It was crowded. But everyone was silent. They were eating with their mouths closed.

"It's mimes," the host said. "It's Mime Lunch."

We sat at a table. I ordered the "Steak Frites". $1,500.00.

The waiter brought out a picture of a steak on a plate.

"It's mime food," the waiter said. He put a finger to his lips. *Shhh*.

I pretended to cut the steak. I pretended to chew.

"It's imaginary," I said. "Does it have calories?"

"If you believe it does," the waiter said. "Then you're fat."

"I'm full," the squirrel said. He had eaten the centerpiece. A plastic daffodil.

At 3:00 PM, I went to the bank. I needed to cash the glass check Mr. Cash gave me. The bank was a vault. No tellers. Just a machine.

"Insert check," the machine said.

I slid the glass check into the slot. *Crinkle. Smash.*

"Insufficient funds," the machine said. "But excellent transparency."

"Can I get a receipt?" I asked.

The machine spat out a confetti cannon. *Pop*. I was covered in colorful paper dots.

"It's a party," I said. "I love a party."

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory, the sky turned plaid again, but now it was purple. Purple plaid. It looked like a bruise wearing a kilt.

Sammy was finally cleaning the grease trail the bucket of chicken left. He was using a baguette.

"It's a sponge," Sammy said. "A french sponge."

"Don't eat it," I said. "It's not baguette time."

I decided to work on my portraits. I sat the Bucket of Chicken in the "Bama" chair.

"Give me glamour," the Bucket said. "Make me a star."

I took a Polaroid. The flash was bright. *Flash*.

The photo came out. It wasn't the bucket. It was a photo of me taking a photo.

"It's recursive," I said. "It's deep."

"I look good," the Bucket said. "Do I have thighs?"

"You have drums," I said.

At 7:30 PM, Truman Capote called. He was still tiny.

"Andy," he said. "I'm stuck in a sentence. It's a very long sentence. It has too many clauses."

"Use a period," I said.

"I can't find one," Truman said. "I think the grammar police took them."

"I'll send a search party," I said.

At 9:00 PM, we ordered dinner. "Chinese food". $600.00.

The delivery arrived by catapult. A container of Moo Shu Pork smashed through the window. *Crash*.

"Fast food," I said.

We ate on the floor. The Moo Shu Pork was wrapped in pancakes that looked like the sky. Plaid pancakes.

"It tastes like geometry," the squirrel said.

I looked at the glass check shards on the floor. They were sparkling.

"Money is beautiful when it's broken," I said. "It has more edges."

Bob hummed. *Beep-boop*. The lights flickered.

"It's a surge," Bob said. "Buy low. Sell high."

"I already sold my shadow," I said. "I'm all out of assets."

I put a pancake in my pocket. For later. Or for never.

I looked out the window. The plaid sky was fading. It was turning into a solid sheet of white. Like an unpainted canvas.

"I hope they don't paint us black," I said.

"I prefer gold," the Bucket said.
