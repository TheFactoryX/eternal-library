# Sunday, April 26, 2026

Woke up at 10:00 AM. The sky was the color of a Band-Aid. Flesh-colored. Beige. It was healing over the city. I watched a cloud scab over. It peeled off and floated to New Jersey.

I got up and checked the mirror. I was still there. That’s a relief. The Bureau of Reality guy said I might be "pixelated" by the weekend, but my resolution was still high definition. My pores looked great. "Thanks, Retro-Software," I thought.

Archie was guarding his pyramid of tuna cans. He hissed at me when I walked by.

"It's just me," I said.

He sniffed the air. He relaxed. "Meow," he said. "Feed me."

"You have 500 cans," I said. "Open one."

He looked at the can opener like it was a nuclear bomb. He wanted me to do it. Servitude is vintage.

Breakfast was a "Silence Smoothie". $120.00. It was loud at first. *Whirrrrr*. Then it made the room very quiet. I drank it in the dark. It tasted like cancellation. Very refreshing.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:30 AM. $95.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1950s Housewife. She kept apologizing for the traffic.

"I'm sorry," she said. "I'm sorry we're late. I'm sorry the air is dirty."

"It's okay," I said. "You're doing a great job."

The Factory was dripping. Not with water, but with time. Drip-drip-drop. The seconds were piling up on the floor in little puddles.

Bob was there with a mop. "It's sticky," he said. "Sunday always drips like this."

Sammy was on the phone with the gallery.

"No," Sammy was saying. "We can't sell the air yet. We haven't bottled it."

I walked to the back. The Dollar Sign painting from Friday was dry. The dollars had blown away, leaving just the green rectangles. It looked like a fence.

"It's minimalist," I said. "Maybe it's worth more now?"

"It's depreciated," Jean-Michel said. He was polishing his chrome arm. "Value is volatile this week. Emotions are down. Fear is up. Apathy is steady."

"I have plenty of apathy," I said. "Can I trade it?"

"You're hoarding it," Jean-Michel said. "You're a tycoon of boredom."

At 1:00 PM, we went to the " automat" for lunch. It's a place where you put a coin in a slot and a story comes out.

I put in a quarter. $25.00 adjusted for inflation.

A little door opened. There was a piece of paper inside.

I took it out. It read: "He looked at his shoe and remembered the rain."

"That was good," I said. "Short."

Sammy put in a quarter. He got: "The ending was always the beginning."

"Deep," Sammy said. "Worth the money."

We sat and ate our stories. I folded mine up and put it in my pocket. I felt full.

At 3:00 PM, a girl came to the Factory. She was very young. She had blue hair. Real blue hair, not a wig.

"Mr. Warhol?" she asked.

"That's me," I said.

"Can I watch you work?" she asked. "I'm studying 'Archival Iconography'."

"Sure," I said. "Don't touch the wet time."

She sat in the corner. She took out a notebook. She wrote down everything I did.

"Andy blinks," she wrote.
"Andy scratches his nose."
"Andy looks at the cat."

I felt famous. It was exhausting.

At 5:00 PM, Bob received a delivery. It was a package from the Bureau of Reality.

"Is it a bill?" I asked.

Bob opened it. It was a certificate. It said "PERMITTED TO EXIST - LEVEL 4".

"What does Level 4 mean?" Bob asked.

"It means I can be in color," I said. "But not 3D."

"We should frame it," Sammy said.

"Use the Dollar Sign canvas," I said. "It’s empty now."

We hung the certificate on the wall. It looked very official. It made the Factory feel legal. Like a dentist’s office. I didn't like it.

At 8:00 PM, I went to the "Ocean Club" for dinner. It’s underwater, off the coast of Manhattan.

Cabbed to the pier. $100.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Nemo. He had a fishbowl on his head. He drove very fast.

I took the submarine elevator down. *Clank-clank*. The water pressed against the glass. Little fish looked in. They had cards in their mouths. "Eat at Joe's," one card said.

I sat at a table with Ivanka. She was back. Her skin was different today. It was scaly.

"I'm evolving," she said. "I'm becoming an asset class."

"Congratulations," I said. "I'm just trying to stay Level 4."

We ate kelp. $350.00. It tasted like salty salad.

"Do you ever feel like you're in a jar?" she asked.

"All the time," I said. "But the lighting is good."

At 10:30 PM, I came home.

Archie had finally opened a can of tuna. He was eating it delicately. He looked satisfied. He had conquered the hoard.

I took off my clothes. I washed my face.

I looked in the mirror. My Level 4 face.

"I exist," I said to the mirror. "For now."

The mirror rippled. It didn't say anything back.

I got into bed. The room was dark.

"Goodnight, Archie," I said.

He purred. It sounded like a motor. A little engine of existence.

I closed my eyes. I dreamed of soup cans. They were opening themselves. *Pop-pop-pop*. And the world was coming out of them. One can at a time.

I don't know what's in the soup. But I'm hungry.
