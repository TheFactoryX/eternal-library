Woke up at 7:15 AM. The alarm didn't go off. My phone just vibrated off the nightstand. *Buzz-buzz*. It fell into the half-eaten bowl of cereal from last night. *Splosh*. Now the phone smells like milk and sugar. It’s very sweet. I didn't wash it. I just licked the screen. It tasted like blueberries.

Put on the "Business" wig. It’s a short, grey wig. Very sensible. It makes me look like a banker. Or a grandmother. I put on the glasses with the clear lenses. I wanted to see everything clearly today. Or at least see the dust.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:30 AM. $45,000.00. The traffic was terrible. It was stopped dead. Just rows of cars honking. *Honk-honk*. It sounded like a symphony of geese. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Henry Ford. He was shaking his head.

"Nobody moves," Henry said. "They just buy things."

"We're moving," I said. "We're just staying in the same place."

When we got to the Factory, the doors were wide open. Too wide. It looked like a mouth waiting for a dentist. The floor was covered in paper. Holes. Paper holes. Donut holes. The squirrels were throwing them at each other. *Splat*.

"It's the 'Circle' series," Bob said. He was sweeping up the mess. "We're emphasizing the void. The part that isn't there."

"It's economical," I said. "You get nothing for free."

At 10:00 AM, I sat down to do the "Interviews." I have to interview people every day. It’s how I find out what’s happening. I sit behind the desk and they sit on the sofa.

First up was a girl named Chloe. She was a "Simulacrum" of TikTok.

"How are you famous?" I asked.

"I hold things up," Chloe said. "I held up a can of soup. Then I held up a shoe. Then I held up my hand."

"Do you say anything?" I asked.

"No," she said. "Just silence. Or maybe I hum. *Hummmmm*."

"It’s performance art," I said. "Very deep."

I gave her a signed banana. $10,000.00 value. She took a bite. *Munch*.

At 11:30 AM, the rat was organizing the clutter. He was stacking the film canisters.

"I'm making a tower," the rat said. "To the moon."

"The moon is full of garbage," I said. "We've been dumping there for years."

"That's why I want to go," the rat said. "It's a treasure chest."

At 12:45 PM, the phone rang. It was Bianca. Bianca Jagger. Or maybe it was a horse. I couldn't tell.

"Andy?" she said. "I'm at Studio 54. But it's a gym now. We're dancing on the treadmills."

"Is it sweaty?" I asked.

"It's glitter," she said. "Sweat is just glitter from the inside."

At 1:15 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Coffee Shop" on Union Square. But they brought me a plate with a photograph of a sandwich.

"I'll have the 'Turkey Club'," I said. $95,000.00.

The waiter put a Polaroid on the table. It showed a sandwich with three layers of meat.

"It’s high-def," the waiter said. "Look at the mayonnaise. It glistens."

I picked up the photo. I put it in my mouth. It was chewy. The paper tasted like chemicals.

"It needs more salt," I said.

"That’s the nostalgia," Sammy said. "It tastes like the past."

At 3:00 PM, I worked on the "Diamond Dust" paintings. But I was using real diamonds. Broken diamonds. Dust from the pawn shop. I sneezed. *Achoo*. A cloud of sparkle filled the air.

"Bless you," Bob said. "That’s a million-dollar sneeze."

"I feel expensive," I said.

I rubbed my nose. It felt sharp.

At 4:30 PM, a UPS truck arrived. But it was filled with Time. The driver brought in a crate marked "Monday Afternoon."

"Sign here," the driver said.

I signed the pad. He opened the crate. Nothing came out. But the room got slower. The clock ticked louder. *Tick*. *Tock*. The light stretched long across the floor. We sat there for hours. Or maybe it was minutes.

"This is boring," the squirrel said. "I want to chase a nut."

"Boredom is the best part," I said. "It means you're not doing anything."

At 6:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was wearing two ties. One was red. One was blue.

"Good news," the Man said. "The government approved the camouflage. They're going to cover the White House in your flowers."

"Marigolds?" I asked.

"No," the Man said. "Flowers from the 'Flowers' series. The bright ones. The scary ones."

"They won't scare anybody," I said. "They’re just flowers."

"That’s the point," the Man said. "They’ll think it’s a garden. They’ll walk right in."

At 7:30 PM, we went to a premiere. It was for a movie called "The Life of Andy." It was four hours long. It was just me sleeping. I sat in the back row and watched myself sleeping on the screen.

"He looks good," I said.

"He’s tired," Sammy said.

"He’s working," I said.

After the movie, people lined up to ask for my autograph. But they wanted me to sign their screens. Their phones. Their faces.

"Sign my forehead," a boy said.

I wrote "Andy" in black marker. $0.00.

"Now you’re a masterpiece," I said.

Cabbed home at 11:00 PM. $55,000.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of myself. He had the wig. He had the glasses.

"Goodnight, Andy," the driver said.

"Goodnight, Andy," I said.

It was like talking to a mirror. A mirror that charges by the mile.

Went into the townhouse. The air smelled like blueberries and chemicals. I took off the grey wig. My scalp itched. I scratched it. *Scratch-scratch*. flakes of diamond dust fell on my shoulders.

I looked in the mirror. My reflection was delayed. I moved my hand, and the mirror moved a second later. It’s lag. Reality lag.
