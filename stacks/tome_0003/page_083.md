Woke up at 7:15 AM. The sky was "Coca-Cola" brown. Not the classic bottle, the plastic kind that's been left in the sun. It looked toxic. I liked it. It matched my curtains.

I got out of bed and checked the floor. It was solid again. Mercury is terrible for your back. Too hard.

Archie was in the kitchen. He was sitting on the counter. He was typing. He had his paws on my phone. *Tap-tap-tap*.

"Who are you calling?" I asked.

He looked up. He looked guilty. "Meow."

I looked at the screen. He had ordered 500 cases of tuna. "Cancel," I said. "We don't have room."

He typed again. *Enter*. "Confirmed."

"You're fired," I said. "But you can keep the tuna."

Breakfast was a "Breakfast of Champions" pill. $500.00. It was shaped like a little baseball. I swallowed it without water. I could feel the calories hitting my stomach. It felt like a home run.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $80.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was tiny.

"It's a vile day," Truman said. "Everyone is ugly."

"Even me?" I asked.

"Especially you," he said. "In a beautiful way."

The Factory was quiet. But it was a "heavy" quiet. Like the air was made of lead.

Sammy was there. He was sweeping up the pieces of the wall that Bob cut yesterday.

"We have to put it back," Sammy said. "The wall is leaking."

"Leaking what?"

"Nothing," Sammy said. "That's the problem. It's leaking the void."

Bob came out of the office. He was holding a phone. He looked pale.

"Andy," Bob said. "That guy from the Bureau. He called back. About you being dead."

"I'm not dead," I said. "I'm right here."

"You're a glitch," Bob said. "He said you're a 'persistence error'. He's coming to patch you."

"I don't want a patch," I said. "Patches itch."

"He's bringing a deletion crew," Bob said. "They're going to format us."

"Can we bribe them?" I asked.

"We can try," Bob said. "Do you have any leftover art?"

"I have a sketch of Archie," I said.

"That's worth a million," Bob said. "Cats are the new currency. The 'Doge' revival."

At 11:00 AM, I went to the back to paint. I wanted to make something real. Something they couldn't delete.

I took out a canvas. I took out a brush. Real bristles. Real wood.

I painted a dollar sign. Big green dollars.

"It’s timeless," I thought. "Money never goes out of style."

While I was painting, the hole in the wall opened again. *Pop*.

It was Vincent. But his head was on a robot body.

"Hey Vincent," I said. "Nice body."

"It's a rental," he said. "My biological one is in the shop. They're upgrading my liver."

"Good luck," I said.

"Andy," Vincent said. "You have to come to my party tonight. It's a 'Fade to Black' party. We're celebrating the end of the world."

"Is it ending?" I asked.

"It's buffering," Vincent said. "But it might not load."

"I'll be there," I said. "If I'm not deleted."

At 1:00 PM, I went to Serendipity 3 for lunch.

I sat in the corner booth.

The waitress was a "Simulacrum" of Marilyn Monroe. The white dress version.

"What can I get you, Andy?" she asked. Her voice sounded like static.

"I'll have the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate," I said. "But make it cold."

"Coming up," she said.

She brought it. $45.00. It was cold. It had whipped cream. I ate it slowly.

I watched the people outside. They were walking fast. They were looking at their phones. They were ignoring the sky.

"Ignorance is beautiful," I thought. "It's the best abstract art."

At 3:00 PM, the Bureau of Reality arrived. Three black vans pulled up outside the Factory. They were silent. No engines. Just gliding.

"They're here," Bob said. "The cleaners."

Three men got out. They were wearing gray suits. They were wearing sunglasses.

They walked in. *Thud-thud-thud*. Their boots were heavy.

"Andrew Warhol," the leader said. "You are in violation of Code 404. You do not exist."

"I exist a lot," I said. "I have a diary."

"That's just data," he said. He pulled out a device. It looked like a giant magnet. "We're here to wipe the drive."

Sammy stepped forward. He was holding the "Abstract Expressionist Water" bottle.

"Don't delete him," Sammy said. "Drink this."

"What is it?" the man asked.

"It's liquid art," Sammy said. "It's Jackson Pollock in a bottle. It's worth millions."

The man took the bottle. He opened it. He sniffed it.

"It smells like turpentine and mop water," he said.

"It's an acquired taste," I said. "Like fame."

He took a sip. He gagged. Then he smiled. His teeth turned gray.

"I feel... creative," he said. He looked at his partner. "Let's go paint a wall."

"Are you sure?" the partner asked.

"Yes," the leader said. "Let's leave the glitch alone. He's inspiring."

They got back in the vans and drove away. *Whoosh*.

"That was close," Bob said. "We lost the water though."

"It was a good trade," I said. "I prefer existence to mop water."

At 8:00 PM, I went to Vincent's party. It was on the roof of the Chelsea Hotel.

Cabbed there. $110.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a race car driver. He drove on the sidewalk. "Faster," he said.

The roof was crowded. But it was dark. Very dark. Everyone was wearing black.

"My dress is black," a girl said. "And I'm in mourning."

"For who?" I asked.

"For the future," she said. "It's cancelled."

I found Vincent. He was back in his biological body. He looked weak.

"Did you get your liver?" I asked.

"I got a used one," he said. "It belonged to a poet. It processes metaphors instead of alcohol."

"Fascinating," I said.
