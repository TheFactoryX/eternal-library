Woke up at 8:30 AM. I didn’t know where I was. The townhouse is still split in half. There is a three-foot gap between the bedroom and the kitchen. The wind is blowing through the hallway. It’s like a drafty loft. A very dangerous loft. I had to jump over the abyss to get to the bathroom. *Leap*. I missed and I grabbed the doorframe. My manicure is ruined. $0.00.

Put on the "Zipper" wig. It’s made of interlocking metal teeth. I can unzip my head and fold it flat if I want to take a nap. Very convenient for travel.

I jumped the gap. *Thud*. Landed in the kitchen. It was cold. The view from the gap was interesting. I could see into the neighbor’s apartment. They were blue. Smurfs. A whole family of Smurfs. They were eating giant mushrooms.

"Good morning," I said.

"La la la," the neighbor said. He was wearing a Phrygian cap.

"It's a cartoon," I thought.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:00 AM. I had to take a special "Gaps" cab. It had a suspension system that could bridge separations. $45,000.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote again. He was inflatable. He squeaked when he hit a bump. *Squeak*.

"It's a disjointed city, Andy," Truman said. "Everything is coming apart at the seams."

"Just don't deflate," I said. "I hate paying the surcharge for flat passengers."

When we got to the Factory, the grid floor was gone. The floor was transparent. Glass. And underneath the glass was... the 1970s. I could see the old Factory. I could see me. I was wearing a leather jacket. I was talking to Candy Darling.

"Look," I said. I pointed down. "That's me."

Bob looked down. "You look thin," he said.

"I look tired," I said.

It was a loop. A time loop underneath our feet. Every five minutes, the me on the other side looked up and waved. I waved back. It’s like being buried, but with a better view.

At 11:30 AM, the rat arrived. He was wearing a space suit. A tiny white suit with a bubble helmet.

"I'm colonizing," the rat said. His voice was tinny. *Crackle*. "I'm moving to the basement. The 1970s basement."

"Is the rent cheaper?" I asked.

"It's 1974 prices," the rat said. "A slice of pizza is a dollar. But the music is loud. Disco."

The squirrel was there. He was glued to the ceiling. He was hanging upside down. He was eating a nut.

"I'm batty," the squirrel said. "Gravity is optional today."

At 12:45 PM, Jean-Michel called. He was on the mirror. The mirror in the hallway turned into a screen.

"Andy?" he said. "I'm painting with bricks. I'm building a wall."

"Is it political?" I asked.

"It's heavy," he said. "I'm creating an obstruction."

"Will it sell?" I asked.

"It's a barrier," he said. "Millionaires love barriers."

At 1:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "Serendipity 3". But the tables were seesaws. We sat on one end, the waiter sat on the other.

"I'll have the 'Frrrozen Hot Chocolate'," I said. $50,000.00.

The waiter slid a glass down the table. It was perfectly balanced. I caught it. It was warm. Hot hot chocolate.

"It's not frozen," I said.

"It's a contradiction," the waiter said. "It's an oxymoron sundae."

I drank it. It burned my tongue. *Ouch*.

"It hurts," I said.

"Pain is real," Bob said. He was eating a burger. The burger was eating him back. The lettuce was wrapping around his fingers. "It's aggressive food."

At 3:00 PM, I worked on the "Disaster" paintings. I painted a car crash. Then I crashed a real toy car into the canvas. *Smash*.

"It's 3D," I said.

"It's debris," Sammy said. He swept up the broken plastic. "Do you want me to frame the parts?"

"Yes," I said. "Frame the wreckage. Charge extra for the cleanup."

At 5:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was wearing two ties. One red, one blue.

"We have a problem with the neighbor," the Man said. "The Smurfs. They are suing for visual harassment."

"What for?" I asked.

"They say you stared," the Man said. "They say you looked into their apartment without paying the viewing fee."

"I was just looking at history," I said.

"Looking is expensive," the Man said. "We settled. We gave them a crate of Campbell's Soup."

"Did they take the green kind?" I asked.

"Green is their favorite color," the Man said.

At 7:00 PM, we went to a party. It was at the "Whitney Museum". But the museum had turned into a giant washing machine. We were inside the drum. The art was spinning around. *Swish, swish*.

"It's a cycle," Bianca Jagger said. She was holding onto a Henry Moore sculpture. "It's the rinse cycle."

"I'm getting dizzy," I said.

"It's refreshing," Bob said. Foam was rising. Bubbles. Giant soap bubbles. *Pop*.

I floated in a bubble. I rose to the top of the museum. I looked down. Everyone was tumbling. Like socks and shoes.

"I'm art," I said. "I'm laundry."

At 10:00 PM, the machine stopped. *Ding*.

We fell out. Wet. Clean.

"I need a cab," I said. "A dry cab."

Cabbed home at 10:45 PM. $55,000.00. The driver was a washing machine. He spun me all the way home.

Went into the townhouse. The gap was still there. The Smurfs were asleep. I could hear them snoring. *Snore*.

I walked to the edge of the bedroom. I looked at the missing floor.

"I need a bridge," I thought.

I took my wig off. I unzipped it. *Zip*.

I laid it over the gap. It stretched. It made a fuzzy metal bridge.

I walked across. *Creak*.

I got into bed.

The ceiling was dripping. But it wasn't water. It was silver paint. *Drip, drip*.

I closed my eyes.
