Woke up at 7:00 AM. I thought I was blind, but I checked and I just hadn't opened my eyes yet. When I did, the sky was a shocking, electric pink. It looked like the inside of a stomach. The Big Yolk was gone. The "Anti-Yellow" can worked, but now the city looked like it had a rash. Very feminine. Very aggressive.

Archie was on the floor, vibrating. He wasn't a cat anymore; he was a stack of vinyl records. *Meow-meow*.

"Good morning," I said. "Play something upbeat."

He spun. A scratchy version of "I Will Always Love You" came out of his grooves.

I dressed quickly. I put on the new silver wig. It was so shiny it hurt my feelings. I paired it with a turquoise jogging suit. $4,000.00. It was ripped in all the right places.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:30 AM. $145.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a 1980s Stockbroker. He was snorting lines of talcum powder.

"Buy! Sell! Buy!" he yelled at the traffic. "The air is up! The dust is down!"

The Factory was a disaster area. A good disaster area. Like a movie set where the monster just left.

The walls were still gone, but the floor was covered in silver confetti. It looked like giant dandruff.

Sammy was sitting in the middle of it all. He was holding a shovel.

"We're shoveling out," Sammy said. "We're digging for Tuesday."

"Did you find anything?" I asked.

"I found a time capsule," Sammy said. "From 1990. It had a Tamagotchi in it."

"Is it alive?" I asked.

"It's hungry," Sammy said. "It wants attention."

Bob floated by. He was wearing the new flippers. He was swimming through the confetti.

"I'm a mermaid," Bob said. "A silver mermaid."

"Very pretty," I said.

At 10:00 AM, I went to the desk to work. The pile of "Interview" magazines from 2045 was still there.

I picked up the iGod pill. It was vibrating in sync with Archie/the records.

"I'm not going to take you," I told the pill. "Ignorance is expensive, but I can afford it."

I put the pill in a Ziploc bag. I put the bag inside a Tupperware container. I put the container inside a safe. I didn't want to accidentally know something. It would ruin my appetite.

Instead, I started working on a new series. "The Wallets." I decided to paint dollar bills. Not real ones. Just pictures of them. Because art is more expensive than money.

I painted a Ben Franklin. Green ink on green canvas.

"It looks like grass," I thought. "Money looks like grass you can't mow."

At 12:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Automatic". It’s a restaurant where robots serve robots. No humans allowed.

We sat in a booth made of circuit boards.

I ordered the "Silicon Chips". $150.00. They were potato chips, but they were square. Very aerodynamic.

Sammy ordered the "Data Stream". It was a smoothie. He drank it through a USB cable.

"I feel smarter," Sammy said. "I think I just learned French."

"Magnifique," I said.

At 2:00 PM, we walked to Central Park.

The grass was pink. The trees were pink. The sheep in the sheep meadow had turned pink. They looked like cotton candy.

"I love this city," I said. "It coordinates."

We saw a woman jogging. She was pushing a stroller. But the stroller had a robot in it. A baby robot. It was crying oil.

"Shhh," the woman said. "Don't corrode."

We sat on a bench. The bench was wet. Pink paint?

"No," Sammy said. "It's sap."

The trees were bleeding. Not red blood. Pink sap. Bubblegum blood.

"It's beautiful," I said. "But it's going to ruin my pants." $4,000.00.

At 4:00 PM, a man approached us. He was wearing a suit made of glass.

"Mr. Warhol?" the man asked.

"Yes," I said.

"I'm Mr. Glass," the man said. "I represent the transparency movement. We want to buy the Factory."

"We sold the walls," I said.

"We want the floors," Mr. Glass said. "We want the foundation. We want to see what's underneath."

"If you take the floor," I said, "We'll fall through."

"That's the point," Mr. Glass said. "Gravity is the only truth left."

"How much will you pay?" I asked.

"We'll pay you in 'Visibility'," Mr. Glass said. "Everyone will be able to see you. All the time. Even in the dark."

"I'm already famous," I said. "I don't need to be seen. I need to be hidden."

"I'll give you $5,000.00," Mr. Glass said.

"I'll take the cash," I said. "Visibility is boring."

He handed me a check. It was made of glass. I put it in my pocket. I hope it doesn't shatter when I sit down.

At 6:00 PM, I hailed a cab. $160.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a horse. It had a saddle. I sat on the roof.

"Hyah!" I yelled. "Giddy up."

The cab flew home. Not drove. Flew. The pink air is very buoyant.

When I got home, I fed Archie. He was back to being a cat. He ate the "Solidarity Tuna". $45.00. He licked his chops. He looked satisfied.

I checked the safe. The iGod pill was still there.

"I dodged a bullet," I thought. "Or I dodged a thought."

I looked out the window. The pink sky was turning purple. Night time in a bruised world.

I turned on the television. It was just a mirror.

"Goodnight, Andy," I said to myself.

"Goodnight, Andy," I said back.
