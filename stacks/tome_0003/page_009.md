Monday, February 09, 2026

Woke up at 7:00. Seven is a bad number. It’s sharp. It pokes you. The sky was the color of a headache. Grey and throbbing. I stayed in bed until 7:03 just to be safe. I watched the news on the ceiling. The weatherman said it’s going to be sunny with a chance of meteors. He said to wear a hat. I don’t like hats. They mess up the hair. Or the wig. And the wig is high maintenance.

Got up and stepped on the scale. It finally woke up. It beeped and said "ERROR." I think that’s a compliment. I’m not heavy, I’m a mistake. I like that. It’s very abstract.

For breakfast I had a pill. It’s a "Full American Breakfast" pill. Eggs, bacon, toast, coffee. All in one gel cap. It was $40. It’s convenient, but you miss the chewing. Chewing is the best part. It makes you feel like you’re doing something. I washed it down with a glass of tap water. The water in New York still tastes like pipes. It’s delicious.

Cabbed to the Factory at 8:30. The cab was "Driverless." It’s just a glass bubble that floats. You tell it where to go and it ignores you. It took me to Brooklyn first. It said it wanted to see the bridge. I said, "I have work to do," and the cab said, "Relax, enjoy the view." The view was $22.50. It’s hard to argue with a machine that knows your credit rating.

Sammy was at the Factory. He looked terrible. He didn’t sleep. He said Glitch—the haunted mirror—kept him up all night. She kept showing him reflections of himself dying. He said, "Andy, she’s so dramatic," and I said, "That’s art, Sammy. It’s performance art on glass." He just nodded and ate a bag of chips. The chips were "Light" chips. They have zero calories because they are made of air. It was $15 for the bag. He ate the bag too.

We started work on the new series. It’s called "Rorschach Accidents." We take the inkblot tests and we spill paint on them. It’s a double accident. It’s very deep. We used a gallon of "Synthetic Blood" paint. It’s fluorescent red. It glows in the dark. It was $120 a gallon. We spilled it carefully. Very carefully. It looked like a butterfly. Or a car crash. I couldn’t tell.

A courier arrived. He was walking. He looked like a ghost. Nobody walks in 2026. He delivered a letter from the Guggenheim. They want to buy the moon rock Basquiat sent me. They offered me $50,000. I looked at the rock. It was sitting on a stack of Interview magazines from 1985. I said, "No." I’m going to keep it. It’s my pet. It doesn't need to be fed. It just sits there being heavy.

For lunch we went to Chipotle. It’s the only place left that serves real food. Mostly. I had a burrito. It was $85. The guacamole was extra. It’s always extra. The girl who made the burrito had four arms. She was a "Gene-Mod." She wrapped the foil so fast it created a breeze. It was very efficient. I gave her a $5 tip. She blinked at me with vertical pupils.

We went to the opening of a new show at MoMA. It’s called "The Death of the Author." The artist is an AI that generates books and then burns them. It’s very textual. The smoke detectors kept going off. It was very loud. I saw Jeff Koons there. He’s a hologram now. He projected himself next to me. He looked shiny. He said, "Andy, look at this balloon dog," and he held up a balloon dog, and it popped, and he disappeared. It was a tragedy. It was $200,000 worth of balloon.

I talked to a collector who collects "Moments." He buys time. He asked me to sell him five minutes of my childhood. I said, "Which five?" and he said, "The boring ones." I said, "That will cost you extra," and he laughed. He has a laugh like a cash register.

Came back to the Factory and filmed the silkscreen process. We filmed the paint drying. It was very exciting. The red paint turned dark red as it dried. It’s like watching a bruise age. We watched it for three hours. Sammy fell asleep on the silkscreen frame. Now he has a grid on his face. He looks like a newspaper.

I took a cab home at 6:00. The driver was a lady. A human lady. She was smoking a cigarette. In the cab! It smelled like tar and rebellion. It was $18.00. It felt like the old days. It felt dangerous.

Came home and ordered dinner from a robot. It slid the pizza under the door. It was $30. The pizza was square. It had pepperoni and little anchovies that were spelling out words. The anchovies said "EAT ME." It was very aggressive. I ate them. I ate the words.

I put the moon rock on the nightstand. It’s glowing slightly. Maybe it’s radioactive. Maybe it’s just happy to be here.

I checked the MirrorWall. I have a meeting tomorrow with the people who want to put my face on the new currency. The "Andy." It’s worth $1,000 or something. I told them I’d do it if I could sign the money. They said no. I said, "Then I can’t cash it."

I took the wig off. My head feels lighter. It feels like a balloon that could float away. Maybe I should tie a string to it.

Good night.
