# Saturday, April 11, 2026

Woke up at 8:00 AM. The apartment smelled like a butcher shop in July. *Smell*. It was the Meat Art. It had finally stopped steaming, but now it was weeping. A clear, salty liquid was leaking out of the tub and creeping across the tiles. Archie was lapping it up.

"Don't drink the art," I said. "It's not vintage yet."

He ignored me. He thinks he's a critic. I put on my flip-flops. I didn't want to get my toes wet. It felt like walking on a cold tongue.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $50.00. The driver was a "Reagan-bot". It kept smiling and waving at buildings.

"Morning in America," it said. "Or whatever this is."

"It's Saturday," I said. "America is closed."

The Factory was wet. Sammy was mopping up the ceiling. It was raining inside. Again.

"The clouds are back," Sammy said. "I think they nested in the rafters."

"I want a dry studio," I said. "Is that too much to ask?"

"Dry is dead," Sammy said. "Wet is alive."

I went to the back room. The phone was ringing. It was the red phone. The one that only rings for money or trouble.

"Hello?"

"Andy?" It was Bob. His voice sounded distant. Like he was calling from a submarine. "The Vice President ate the toaster."

"I told him not to," I said. "I hope he didn't break a tooth."

"He broke three," Bob said. "He's suing us. For dental damages and emotional distress."

"I'm an artist," I said. "I don't make warranties. I make fame."

"He wants a settlement," Bob said. "He wants a portrait of his teeth."

"Paint them on a shoe," I said. "A high heel."

"I'll negotiate," Bob said. "Maybe we can pay him in exposure."

"Exposure doesn't pay the rent," I said. "Unless it's X-ray exposure."

I hung up. I felt tired. Litigation is so boring. It’s just talking in rooms with no windows.

Lunch was a slice of pizza. $250.00. It was delivered by a kid on a hoverboard. He knocked over a can of paint. *Splash*. Red paint everywhere. It looked like a crime scene.

"Great composition," I said. "Keep the change."

I gave him a twenty. He looked at it like it was Monopoly money. "I need crypto," he said. "Or credits."

"Buy yourself a wig," I said.

In the afternoon, the fire alarm went off. *Whoop-whoop*. It wasn't a drill. The Meat Art had overheated in the bathtub. The smoke detectors were screaming.

I ran home. Cab fare. $40.00. I was scared for the cat.

When I got there, the door was open. The smoke was pink. It smelled like barbecue.

A fireman was there. He was made of asbestos.

"The meat is gone," he said.

"Gone?"

"It cooked itself," he said. "It self-immolated. It’s very profound."

I looked in the bathroom. The tub was empty. Just a black scorch mark. The Meat Art had become a ghost. It was performance art.

"Is the cat okay?"

"The cat is in the kitchen," the fireman said. "He's eating a fish."

Archie was sitting on the counter. He looked fine. He looked satisfied.

"You cooked the art," I told him. "You're a collaborator."

"Meow," he said. "Feed me."

I opened a can of tuna. $5.00. I shared it with him. We ate on the floor. It felt like a picnic in a ruin.

At 7:00 PM, I got a call from the Great-Great-Grandclone. The jar of time in my pocket was glowing. It was vibrating. *Buzz-buzz*.

"How's the future?" I asked.

"Dusty," he said. "We found your glasses. The ones from '86."

"Do I look smart in them?"

"Very," he said. "We're putting them in a museum. Next to the toast."

"Toast?"

"The Vice President's toaster," he said. "It's an artifact now."

"Good," I said. "Everything is art eventually. Even lawsuits."

I decided to go out. I needed to see something that wasn't pink smoke.

Cabbed to the "Odeon" at 8:30 PM. $60.00. It was raining money. Not real money, but coupons. They were sticking to the windshield.

Inside, it was dark. The Simulacrum of me was there. He was wearing the silver wig. He looked tired.

"Rough day?" I asked.

"I signed 500 autographs," he said. "My hand hurts. I wish I was a real human so I could feel pain."

"Pain is overrated," I said. "Try being invisible."

I ordered a coffee. $15.00. It was decaf. I need the sleep.

A girl came over. She had a TV in her sunglasses. She was watching *The Andy Warhol Story*.

"Are you him?" she asked.

"I'm the uncle," I said. "The quiet one."

"Can I buy you a drink?"

"I only drink milk," I said. "And soup."

She bought me a glass of milk. $8.00.

"You look just like the picture," she said.

"I hope so," I said. "I spent a lot of time on it."

I finished the milk. It was cold and chalky.

Walking home. The streets were slick with rain and coupons. I slipped on a "Buy One Get One Free" for pizza. *Whoosh*.

I landed on my hands. My palms were black with soot from the railing.

"Great," I thought. "Now I'm an installation."

Got home at 11:00 PM. The apartment still smelled like smoke. But it was a clean smell. A sterile smell.

I took off my clothes. I washed my face. The water ran grey.

I looked at the empty tub. It felt lonely. I missed the Meat. It was so alive. It was so hungry.

I put on my pajamas. The ones with the Campbell's labels.

"Goodnight, Archie," I said.

He was asleep in the sink. He was purring. A low, rumbly sound. Like a distant subway train.

I got into bed. The sheets were cool. I closed my eyes.

I thought about the Meat Art. It didn't die. It just changed state. Solid to smoke to memory. That's the best kind of art. The kind you can't hang on a wall. The kind that gets in your hair.

I wonder if the Vice President will like the scorch mark.
