Saturday, March 14, 2026

Woke up at 8:30 AM. The room was flat. Very flat. I touched the wall. It felt like paper. Like the glossy paper from a magazine. I scratched it. *Crick-crack*. A flake of white paint came off. Underneath, it was grey. Cardboard grey. I’m living in a diorama. Or a notebook.

I looked for the mirror. It was just a silver rectangle hanging on the wall. It didn't show me. It showed what was behind me. The bed. The table. It was very honest. I hated it. I put on my sunglasses. $200.00. Ray-Ban. They help me hide.

Put on the "Flesh-Colored Turtleneck." It felt stiff. Like it had been starched. And the "Silver Gor-Tex" parka. I felt like a spaceman landing on a planet made of sketchpads.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. It was $35.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of a Google Maps pin. He was teardrop-shaped and yellow. He didn't speak. He just dropped me off. *Ping*.

The Factory was different. The ceiling was missing. It was open to the sky. Blue sky. Fake blue. Like the blue in a printer cartridge when it’s running low. Cyan. The snow was gone. The moss was gone. The floor was... pixelated. If I looked closely, I could see little squares. *RGB*. I tried to step on a black pixel, but my foot went right through. Like it was a hole. I stepped back quickly.

Sammy was sitting on a crate. He was blurry. Low-resolution. I said, "Sammy, you’re buffering." He nodded. *Lag*. He nodded again. "Andy," he said. "The rendering engine is down." He said, "The Crypto-Bird ate the graphics card."

The bird was back. It was sitting on a rafter. It looked like a wireframe. Just lines. No feathers. It looked like a drawing come to life. It pecked at the air. *Beep-boop*.

I went to the back. The paintings were gone. The "Electric Chair" was just a label on the wall. "Electric Chair (Placeholder)." I was annoyed. I can't sell a label. Well, maybe I could. If I signed it. "Conceptual Art is very cheap," I said.

Around 11:00 AM, I decided to go shopping. I needed real things. Things I could touch. I went to "The Palm Court" at the Plaza Hotel. But it’s a petting zoo now. Very chic. They serve tea and you feed the goats.

I ordered the "Queen’s Tea." It was $250.00. It came in a pot that looked like the Queen’s head. Very creepy. I drank it. It tasted like berets and rain. I sat with a "Simulacrum" of Truman Capote. He was tiny. Like a gnome. He was eating a sugar cube. He said, "Andy, the party is over." He said, "We’re just footnotes now." I said, "Footnotes are important. They explain the joke."

A goat ate my scarf. The red one my mother gave me. It chewed it up. *Munch-munch*. I tried to pull it out, but the goat bit me. It didn't hurt. It felt like a pinch. I didn't pay for the tea. I left the pot.

I walked to Central Park. It was flat. The trees were cutouts. Like in a school play. I touched a tree. It felt like plastic. I sat on a bench. It was painted on the ground. I had to hover. It was tiring.

I saw a "Simulacrum" of Bianca Jagger. She was riding a horse. But the horse was stuck in a loop. It kept rearing up. *Neigh-whinny*. It couldn't move forward. She looked bored. She checked her watch. She said, "Andy, this reality is buggy." I said, "It's just a feature."

At 2:00 PM, I went to the "Printer." I wanted to print something real. I took a picture of the painted bench. I printed it on canvas. It came out 3D. The paint was wet. It cost $5.00. I called it "Park Bench (Real)." It was the most real thing in New York.

I went to a party at "The Whitney." But the building is melting. Literally dripping onto the High Line. It looks like a candle. It was very warm. The art was sliding down the walls. I saw a "Simulacrum" of Jeff Koons. He was holding a balloon dog. The balloon dog was popping. *Pop-pop-pop*. He said, "It's the sound of money."

I sold my "Park Bench" to a collector. He was a robot. He had a slot for a mouth. He paid me in coins. Gold coins. He dropped them into my hand. *Clank-clank*. $20,000.00.

I came home at 7:00 PM. The apartment was loading. *Loading... 99%*.

I sat on the bed. I took off the sunglasses. I looked at the wall. The crack was back. But this time, it was leaking light. White light.

I put my hand in the crack. It was warm.

The phone rang. It was a landline. I didn't know I had a landline.

I picked it up. "Hello?"

A voice said, "Andrew. It's mother. Did you wear the scarf?"

I looked at the empty space around my neck. "I lost it," I said.

"That's okay," she said. "It keeps you warm."

"How did you call?" I asked.

"I dialed the number," she said.

"Which number?"

"The number of the beast," she said. She giggled.

I hung up.

The room was fully loaded now. It looked solid. The texture was back. The "Car Crash" painting was on the wall. It looked wet. Like it was still drying.

I went to the safe. I opened it.

Inside was a single perfect tomato.

I took it out. It was heavy. Heavy as a heart.

I put it on the table.

I turned off the light.

I ate the tomato in the dark.
