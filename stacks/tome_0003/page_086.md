Woke up at 6:45 AM. I couldn't sleep. The sky was bright green. Like the inside of a pickle jar. Or a computer screen right before it crashes. I checked the window. It wasn't the sky. It was a projection. The city was glitching again. I pulled the shade down. $0.00. It’s good to own the shade.

I stepped out of the bedroom and slipped on something. It was a tuna fish. A whole, unopened can. Archie has been building barricades. He thinks the "Economy" is coming to get him. He’s probably right.

"Archie," I said. "This is a safety hazard."

He looked at me from the top of the refrigerator. He was wearing a tiny helmet. "Meow," he said. "Defcon 1."

"Get down," I said.

He jumped. *Cling-clang*. He landed on a stack of magazines.

Breakfast was a "Packet of Data". $200.00. It was just a small envelope you pour into your ear. It tasted like electricity and strawberries. It gave me all the news I needed for the day. The President is a cloud. The stock market is based on how many people sneeze.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:00 AM. $75.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Carrie Fisher. She was wearing the cinnamon buns on her head.

"It's a hyper-space morning," she said. "Hang on."

We drove through a wall of fog. It smelled like ozone.

The Factory was quiet when I arrived. Too quiet.

I walked in. The space looked... stretched. Like a painting that’s been in the sun too long. The colors were faded.

Bob was sitting on a crate. He looked thin. Transparent. I could see the wall through his shoulder.

"Bob," I said. "You're disappearing."

"I know," Bob said. "The Bureau cut our funding. They said we're 'low resolution'."

"Can we upgrade you?" I asked.

"It costs $5 million," Bob said. "For the 8K plugin."

"I'll write a check," I said. "Do they take checks?"

"They take memories," Bob said.

Sammy was sweeping up pixels. *Sweep-sweep*. They looked like silver dust.

"We have a problem," Sammy said. "The Diaries are leaking."

"What do you mean?"

"People are reading them," Sammy said. "And they're falling out. If they read about the lunch, the lunch disappears. It’s quantum observation."

I looked at my hand. It looked solid. But when I moved it, it left a trail. Like a snake in the grass.

At 11:00 AM, I decided to work. I needed to make something heavy. Something to anchor us.

I took out a box of prints. The "Diamond Dust Shoes" series.

I started silk-screening. I used real diamond dust this time. Not glass. Real diamonds from a pawn shop on 47th Street. $4,000.00 a carat.

*Squeak-squeak*. The squeegee felt good.

I printed a shoe. Then another. Red. Blue. Purple.

"Shoes ground you," I thought. "You can't float away if you're wearing shoes."

As I worked, a girl walked in. She was wearing a dress made of holographic tape. She shimmered.

"Mr. Warhol?" she asked.

"Yes," I said.

"I'm from the 'Future Foundation'," she said. "We want to buy your time."

"I'm all out of time," I said. "I used it up in the 70s."

"We have more," she said. She opened a bag. She poured time onto the floor. It looked like glitter. It pooled around her ankles.

"Where did you get that?" I asked.

"We mine it from old movies," she said. "We extract it from the pauses between dialogue."

I looked at the glitter. It was tempting. Time is the most expensive thing. Even more than diamonds.

"I'll trade you," I said. "One shoe for an hour."

She took the red shoe. "Deal," she said.

She handed me a jar of glitter. I put it in my pocket. It felt warm. It felt like a heartbeat.

At 2:00 PM, we went to "The Void" for lunch. It’s a restaurant that serves nothing. You sit there and they don't bring you food.

We sat at a table. The waiter came over.

"Are you ready to not order?" he asked.

"Yes," I said. "I'll have the Nothing."

"Excellent choice," he said.

We sat there for an hour. It was wonderful. We talked about the weather. We talked about how expensive chairs are getting.

The bill came. $600.00.

"For nothing?" Sammy asked.

"Inflation," the waiter said. "Nothing is in high demand."

I paid with the diamond dust I had on my hands.

At 4:00 PM, I went to the "Guggenheim Fluid" again. I wanted to see if the Mondrian grid had loosened up.

I swam to the deep end. It was dark. The water was cold.

I saw a figure in the corner. It was a man. He was wearing a suit. He was painting a canvas underwater.

He turned around. It was me.

"Hello," I said.

"Hello," he said.

"Are you me?" I asked.

"I'm a backup," he said. "I live here in the museum. The lighting is better."

"Are you happy?" I asked.

"I'm damp," he said. "But I'm timeless."

I swam away. It was too meta. I prefer things I can eat.

At 7:00 PM, I went to a party at the "Chelsea Hotel". It was a "Save the Glitch" party.

The elevator was broken. We had to climb the stairs.

My legs were tired. The jar of time in my pocket was heavy.

The party was in Room 100.

There were so many people. Young people. Old people. People who looked like cats. People who looked like televisions.

A girl came up to me. She had antennas.

"I love your work," she said. "The Soup Cans. Very iconic."

"Thank you," I said.

"Do you think soup is real?" she asked.

"Soup is the most real thing there is," I said. "It starts as a can and it ends as lunch. There's no in-between."

She looked confused. "Can I buy you a drink?"

"I'll have a 'Campbell’s Tomato'," I said. "On the rocks."

She brought it to me. $75.00. It was cold and salty. It tasted like metal and nostalgia.

I drank it slowly. I watched the room spin. Everyone was dancing. They were dancing like they wanted to be scanned. Like they wanted to be downloaded.
