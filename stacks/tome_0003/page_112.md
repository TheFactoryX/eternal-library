Woke up at 8:45 AM. The sky was beige. A nice, soft, sandy beige. It looked like the walls of a very expensive mental hospital. Or a linen suit that hadn't been pressed in a while. I felt textured. I felt like a grain of rice in a big bowl of tapioca. I put on my glasses and the beige turned into little dots. Pointillism. I was seeing like Georges Seurat. Everything was dots. Even the bed sheets were dots. I tried to smooth them out, but I just made more dots. It was overwhelming. $0.00.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $85.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Rudolf Nureyev. He was driving the cab with his feet. He was doing pirouettes while steering.

"It's the only way to turn," Rudolf said. "En dehors."

"I get motion sickness if I spin," I said.

"You just have to spot," he said. "Focus on the horizon."

The horizon was beige, so I focused on the meter. It was spinning like a roulette wheel.

When I got to the Factory, the space was vertical. The floor had become the west wall. Gravity had shifted. I was walking on the wall. The real floor was above me, dripping chocolate syrup onto the ceiling.

"We're installing a new perspective," Sammy said. He was stapled to the ceiling by his suspenders. "It's conceptual."

"I prefer the floor," I said. "It's easier to walk on."

I walked along the wall. My art supplies were floating in the middle of the room. The silkscreens were drifting like jellyfish.

Bob was there. He wasn't a barcode anymore. He was a sphere. A disco ball. He was spinning and shooting light beams everywhere. *Vwoom, vwoom*.

"I'm reflecting," Bob said. "I'm sending signals."

"Who are you signaling?" I asked.

"The disco in the sky," Bob said.

Jean-Michel was stuck to the ceiling. He was solid gold now. Heavy gold.

"I'm melting," Jean-Michel said. "I'm dripping." A drop of gold hit the wall-floor with a *splat*. "I'm worth a fortune."

"Don't slip on the paint," I said.

The squirrel was floating in a zero-gravity bubble. He was wearing a tiny astronaut helmet made of a walnut shell.

"I'm an astro-nut," the squirrel said. "I'm going to the beige moon."

At 11:00 AM, the doorbell rang. It was a wolf whistle.

A woman walked in. She was wearing a dress made entirely of Campbell's Soup cans. Not the labels. Actual cans. Metal cylinders. She clanked when she walked. *Clank, clank, clank*.

"I'm the Soup Lady," she said. "I'm ready for my close-up."

"You're very industrial," I said.

"I'm pop," she said. "I'm nutritious."

She sat on a floating canvas. "Do you have a can opener?" she asked. "I'm hungry."

"I have a glass check," I said. "But it's broken."

At 1:00 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Oyster Bar" in Grand Central Terminal. But the terminal was a clock. A giant Swiss clock.

We sat inside the gears. Every time the minute hand moved, the table shifted.

"It's temporal dining," the waiter said. He was a pendulum. "We serve time."

I ordered the "Clams Casino". $700.00.

He brought me a plate of empty shells.

"Where's the clam?" I asked.

"It was here a second ago," the waiter said. "You missed it. You're always late, Andy."

"I'm prompt," I said. "I'm just time-shifted."

I ate a shell. It tasted like sand and time. "It's crunchy," I said.

At 3:00 PM, I went to the bathroom. But the bathroom was a painting. A Whistler's Mother. She was sitting in a rocking chair, guarding the toilet.

"You have to pay respect," Whistler's Mother said. "Or no pee."

"I respect you," I said. "I love grey."

"Then you may pass," she said.

It was very efficient.

At 5:00 PM, back at the Factory (on the wall), I decided to work. I took the silkscreens that were floating. I grabbed one of "Marilyn". I screenprinted her onto Bob the Disco Ball.

"Smile," I said.

*Mnnn-ahh*. Marilyn's face spun around the room. It was dizzying. Her smile was everywhere. On the ceiling. On the walls. On me.

"She's ubiquitous," I said. "She's a virus."

"A beautiful virus," Bob said.

At 7:30 PM, the Bucket of Chicken woke up. He was floating near the ceiling.

"I'm upside down," the Bucket said. "My gravy is leaking."

"It's zero-gravity gravy," I said. "It's abstract."

The squirrel opened his walnut helmet. He stuck a straw out and drank the floating gravy. "It's space food," he said. "Tangy."

At 9:00 PM, we ordered dinner. "Pizza". $1,200.00.

The delivery boy was a robot on a unicycle. He threw the box like a frisbee. It spun through the zero-G room. *Whoosh*.

We chased the pizza. It was like catching a butterfly. Finally, I caught the box with my wig.

"It's pepperoni," I said. "But the pepperoni is moving."

The pepperoni slices were little ladybugs.

"Eat fast," the Soup Lady said. "They're lucky."

I ate a slice. It tasted like metal and luck. "Good," I said.

At 11:00 PM, I looked out the window. The sky was beige. But the beige was cracking. Behind the beige was... silver. Shiny, metallic silver.

"I think we're in a can," I said. "The world is a soup can."

"We're the label," Bob said.

I took my wig off to scratch my head. My hair was silver. I was silver.

"Gee," I said. "I'm valuable."

I put my hand on the wall. It felt cold. Like tin.

"I hope the opener comes soon," I said. "I'm ready to be eaten."
