Friday, March 20, 2026

Woke up at 8:00 AM. The alarm didn't go off. It just texted my brain. *New Message*. I opened my eyes. The room was bright. Too bright. The sun was screaming. I pulled the covers up. The sheets were $400.00 Egyptian cotton, but they felt like plastic wrap.

I sat up. I looked at the nightstand. The square egg was still there. It hadn't hatched. It was just sitting. Being square. Being expensive. I poked it. It was warm. Like a fresh loaf of bread.

Bridget was on the windowsill. She was pecking at the glass. *Tap-tap-tap*. She wanted out. Or maybe she wanted in. I opened the window. The air smelled like burnt rubber and strawberries. She flew out. She didn't flap her wings. She just hovered up. Like a helium balloon. I watched her until she was a speck. A glitch in the sky.

I went to the bathroom. I brushed my teeth with toothpaste that costs $120.00 a tube. It’s made of diamond dust. It’s supposed to make your smile sparkle. I looked in the mirror. My teeth looked the same. Gray. Sort of transparent. I can see my tongue through them.

I put on the wig. Today it felt heavy. Like a wet cat. I pinned it on. *Ouch*. I scratched my ear. It felt plastic.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:30 AM. $60.00. Traffic was terrible on the digital highway. We were stuck behind a hologram of a truck accident. It looked so real. Smoke. Fire. But it was just pixels.

The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Elvis. He was wearing the white jumpsuit. He was sweating. But it wasn't sweat. It was olive oil.

"Thank you. Thank you very much," he said when I paid him.

The Factory was buzzing. Not like bees. Like electricity. Sammy was there. He was holding a blowtorch.

"We're having a sale," Sammy said. "Everything must go. Even the walls."

"Are we moving?" I asked.

"No," he said. "We're becoming dematerialized. We're going pure concept."

"Concept is cheap," I said. "You can't frame a concept."

"We can try," he said.

I walked to the back. The silk screen machines were running by themselves. *Shoosh-shoosh*. They were printing blank canvases. Pure white. Nothingness. It was beautiful. Very Zen.

I sat down to watch. I like watching machines work. It’s better than TV.

Around 11:00 AM, I got a call. It was a "Simulacrum" of Bob Dylan.

"I'm writing a song about you," he said. His voice was raspy. Like sandpaper on silk.

"Is it nice?" I asked.

"It's about the can," he said. "The soup can. How you trapped the tomato. How you imprisoned the Campbell's."

"I didn't trap it," I said. "I liberated it. I gave it a frame."

"Whatever," he said. "I’m calling it 'Tin Fool'. Do you mind?"

"As long as I get royalties," I said. "10%."

"Deal," he said. He hung up.

I felt a song coming on. I hummed a few bars. It sounded like a cash register opening. *Cha-ching*.

Lunch was at "The Gingerbread House." But it’s not made of gingerbread. It’s made of ginger. Just the root. It smells spicy. It makes your nose tickle.

I ordered the "Campbell’s Soup Dumplings." $150.00. They were served in a can. A gold can.

I opened it. Steam came out. I ate the dumplings with a silver spoon. They tasted like tomato. And nostalgia. And preservatives.

"Delicious," I said. "Very 1962."

The waiter brought the bill. It was written on a napkin. A silk-screened napkin.

I paid with a card. I have so many cards now. I don't know which ones are money and which ones are maps.

At 2:00 PM, I went shopping. I needed new shoes. My feet are starting to look flat. Like 2D drawings.

I went to "Prada." Or maybe it was "Payless." The logos are blurry this season.

I found a pair of silver boots. They were made of Mylar. They reflected everything. I put them on. I looked down and saw my own chin.

"How much?" I asked.

"Your autograph," the salesgirl said. "On the receipt."

So I signed. "Andy Warhol." I wrote the date. "2026."

She gave me the boots. A fair trade.

I walked out. *Clop-clop-clop*. The sound of money hitting the pavement.

I passed a gallery. A "Pop-Up" gallery. It was popping up and down. Like a whack-a-mole game.

I saw a painting in the window. It was a portrait of me. Sleeping.

"Hey," I said. "That's me."

A man walked by. He looked at the painting. He looked at me. He looked at the painting again.

"Is it the original?" he asked me.

"I hope so," I said.

"It's better," he said. "The painting is more alive."

I felt sad. I felt like the copy. I am the Simulacrum of myself.

I bought the painting. $5,000.00. I carried it under my arm. It was heavy. It was breathing.

I came home at 5:00 PM. The apartment was dark. The square egg was still on the table.

I sat down. I took off the silver boots. My feet were relieved.

I looked at the egg. A crack appeared. A jagged line.

I leaned in close. *Peek*.

Something was pushing out. A beak? A nose? A wig?

Suddenly, the shell shattered. *Crash*.

But it wasn't a bird inside.

It was a watch. A tiny gold watch.

It was ticking. *Tick-tock*.

I picked it up. It was set to 6:00 PM.

I put it on my wrist. It fit perfectly.

I looked at the time.

6:00 PM.

Right on time.

But then I realized. The watch wasn't ticking forward.

It was ticking backward.

I watched the hands spin. *Whirrrrr*.

I felt dizzy. The room started to melt. The walls became liquid. Dripping with color.

I reached for the phone to call Sammy. To tell him about the time.

But the receiver was a banana.

"Hello?" I said into the fruit.

"Slip on it," the banana said.

I don't know
