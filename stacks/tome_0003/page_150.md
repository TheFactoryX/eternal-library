Woke up at 9:15 AM. The bed was vibrating again, but this time it was a number. A very low frequency. I checked the pillow. It was counting. *3, 4, 5*. I think the bed is doing my taxes. It’s calculating the cost of sleeping. It’s expensive. I owed the mattress $40.00 for the hours. I put a quarter under the pillow. I hope that covers the interest. $0.40.

Put on the "Transparency" wig. It’s made of clear plastic wrap. You can see my thoughts. Or at least, you can see my scalp. It’s very aerodynamic. The air goes right through my hair.

Cabbed to the Factory at 10:00 AM. $15,000.00. The streets were wet, but it wasn't rain. It was Diet Coke. The gutters were bubbling with brown foam. The city is sweet today. Sticky. The tires of the cab made a sticky sound. *Schlopp*. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of Liza Minnelli. She was wearing sequins. Even her eyelids were sequined.

"It’s a fizzy morning, Andy," Liza said. "The sewers are backed up with soda."

"I hope it’s caffeine-free," I said. "I’m jittery."

When we got to the Factory, the silver balloons were gone. The room was filled with telephones. Just phones. Thousands of them. Black rotary phones. Pink princess phones. They were ringing. *Ring, ring*. It was deafening. It was like a symphony of bells.

"It’s the 'Connection' series," Bob said. He was holding two receivers, one to each ear. "We’re selling conversations."

"I don’t want to talk to anyone," I said.

"You don’t have to," Bob said. "Just listen."

At 11:30 AM, the rat was there. He was sitting on a phone book. He was dialing a number with his tiny paws.

"Who are you calling?" I asked.

"Information," the rat said. "I want to know where the cheese is. They keep moving it."

"It’s in the fridge," I said.

"Which fridge?" the rat asked. "The 1970s fridge or the 2026 fridge?"

"The one that works," I said.

The squirrel was on top of a phone pole. He was chewing on the wires. *Crunch*. He was disconnecting the calls.

"I’m the operator," the squirrel said. "I’m cutting you off."

At 12:45 PM, I walked over the phone lines. They were like tightropes. I had to balance. *Wobble*. I almost fell into a pile of receivers. I picked one up.

"Hello?" I said.

"Andy?" It was Jean-Michel. He was on the line. "I’m painting a phone booth. I’m trapping people inside."

"Is it a portrait?" I asked.

"It’s a box," he said. "It’s rectangular confinement. I’m going to put it in a museum and charge people to get out."

"Genius," I said. "Send me the key."

At 1:30 PM, we went to lunch. We went to "The Russian Tea Room". But the waiters were robots. Chrome robots with tray arms. They rolled on tracks.

"I’ll have the Borscht," I said. $85,000.00.

The robot poured soup into my mouth. It used a funnel. *Glug*. No spoon required. It’s efficient.

"It’s beet red," Bob said. "It looks like blood."

"It’s beets," I said. "It’s vegetables."

"It’s industrial," the robot said. Its voice was metallic. *Bzzt*.

At 3:00 PM, I worked on the "Ads" paintings. I painted a perfume bottle. But the bottle had a smell. I mixed the paint with gasoline and roses. It smelled like a gas station. Like high-class exhaust.

"It’s intoxicating," Sammy said. He sniffed the canvas and passed out. *Thud*.

"Good," I said. "He’s part of the art now. Can we frame him?"

"It’s a performance," Bob said. "Sleeping Beauty."

At 5:00 PM, the Man in the Grey Suit came in. He was wearing a suit made of telephones. He was ringing. *Ring*.

"We have a merchandising deal," the Man said. "They want to put your face on a stamp. A 'Forever' stamp."

"Is it forever?" I asked.

"It’s until the post office collapses," the Man said. "Which is next Tuesday."

"Do I get free postage?" I asked.

"You get canceled," the Man said. "That’s the point."

At 7:30 PM, we went to a party. It was at "Studio 54". But the disco ball was the sun. It was a miniature sun, hanging from the ceiling. It was very hot. We had to wear sunglasses. It was nuclear disco.

"It’s the ‘Thermonuclear’ night," the doorman said. He was wearing a lead suit.

I danced under the sun. My shadow was long. It stretched across the dance floor. My shadow was dancing with a girl. I was just standing there.

"I’m tanning," I said.

"You’re burning," Bob said.

Bianca Jagger was there. She was wearing a dress made of mirrors. She reflected the sun. She started a fire on the rug. *Crackle*.

"It’s a hot track," she said.

At 10:00 PM, the sun exploded. *Pop*. Confetti. Just shiny paper. It was fake. It was just a giant balloon filled with glitter.

"I’m disappointed," I said. "I wanted a tan."

Cabbed home at 11:15 PM. $65,000.00. The driver was a shadow. He didn't say anything.

Went into the townhouse. The floor was made of circuits. The house is a giant computer. I stepped on a "Return" key and the day restarted. I felt like I went back to morning.

"I’m tired of looping," I said.

I took off the wig. My scalp was sweating sparks. *Zzzzt*.

I got into bed. The bed was done calculating. It printed out a receipt.

*Balance Due: 42 years.*

I’ll pay tomorrow. Or maybe the next day.

"Good night, mother," I said to the mattress.

It didn't answer. It just rebooted.
