Woke up at 7:00 AM. The silence was loud. *Beep-beep-beep*. It was the alarm, finally catching up to yesterday. I hit the snooze button, but it just beeped faster. It’s aggressive. Machines are so aggressive lately.

I went to the bathroom. The bathtub was full. Not with water, but with Meat. The "Meat Art" box had expanded completely. It was filling the porcelain. It was undulating. Like a pink ocean.

"Soup?" it asked. The voice was deeper now. Resonant.

"You are tub-sized," I said. "You're blocking the drain."

"Art?" it asked.

"Blockage," I said.

Archie was sitting on the toilet lid, staring at it. His tail was twitching. He looked like a captain watching a ship go down. I didn't shower. I didn't want to get wet meat on my clean clothes. I just washed my face in the kitchen sink. The water tasted like pennies.

Cabbed to the Factory at 9:15 AM. $47.50. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of James Dean. He drove very fast. Reckless. He kept looking in the mirror and brooding.

"You're angsting," I said. "Watch the road."

"I only live fast," he said. He ran a red light. It was purple.

The Factory was buzzing. Literally buzzing. There were flies everywhere. Not real flies, but tiny mechanical ones. *Bzzt-bzzt*. They were painting the walls.

"It's a new installation," Sammy said. He was wearing a beekeeper’s hat. "They're spray-painting pixels. They’re creating a mosaic of dirt."

"It’s filthy," I said.

"It's 'Grime 2.0'," Sammy said. "Very tactile."

I went to the back. The "Invisible" painting was still blank. I stared at it. It stared back, in a way. It was heavy. The absence of color is heavier than the presence of it. It’s like carrying a secret.

Bob called. "The Vice President loves the toaster portrait," he said. "But he says it’s too crunchy. He wants a soft portrait. A pillow."

"I'll paint a pillow," I said. "I'll paint it on a ham."

"He's also upset about the tip," Bob said. "The diamond dust scratched his throat."

"He should have dissolved it," I said. "Like a celebrity."

Lunch was a salad. $200.00. I bought it from a street vendor who sells "organic air". It was just a ziplock bag of wind. He said it was harvested from the Hollywood Hills. It smelled like car exhaust and desperation. I ate it with a fork. It felt like eating nothing, which is very chic.

After lunch, I sat for an interview. A journalist from *Time* magazine. She was floating. Anti-gravity boots. She kept bumping her head on the ceiling tiles.

"Mr. Warhol," she asked. "Is the Meat Art supposed to represent the body? Or is it supposed to represent the *market*?"

"Yes," I said.

"But which one?"

"It represents the price," I said. "If it sells, it's the market. If it rots, it's the body. Right now, it's just lunch."

She took a hologram of my head. *Flash*. My head spun around on her screen.

"I love your wig," she said. "Is it detachable?"

"Hopefully," I said.

At 4:00 PM, the Fire Marshal robot returned. The one with the hose nose. He sniffed the air.

"The meat is sweating," he said. "It’s raising the humidity."

"It's emoting," I said.

"It needs to be vented," he said. He opened a valve on his chest and sucked all the air out of the room. My ears popped. *Pop*.

I felt light-headed. The flies fell to the floor, stunned.

"That's better," the robot said.

At 6:30 PM, I got a call from the Moon.

"Andy?" It was a static voice. Crackly. "It's David."

"David who?"

"Bowie. I'm still here. The gravity is low. My hair is huge."

"I thought you were singing," I said.

"I'm curating," he said. "We need the soup cans. The Space Museum is empty. We need things to look at."

"Send a rocket," I said. "I’ll pack a lunch."

"I'm sending a drone," he said. "It will be there in five minutes."

I waited on the corner of 33rd Street. A drone landed. It looked like a giant spider. It grabbed a bag of silkscreen prints I had left by the dumpster. It buzzed and shot up into the sky. *Whoosh*. Gone.

"I hope they don't freeze," I said.

Cabbed to Serendipity at 8:00 PM. $55.00. I wanted the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. I need the sugar to stop the shaking.

The robot host sat me at the "VIP Table". It was in the window. I felt like a mannequin.

A man sat opposite me. He was wearing a mask. A clear plastic mask.

"I'm a fan," he said. "I collect your toenail clippings."

"I don't have any," I said. "I'm stainless."

"I have a replica," he said. "It cost me $5,000."

"Good," I said. "Invest in nails. Build a house."

I ordered the Frrrozen Hot Chocolate. $22.00. It came with a sparkler. The sparkler spelled out "BUY". I watched it burn until it was just a wire.

Walking home. The streetlights were turning into faces. Lincoln. Washington. The guy on the dime. They were all looking at me.

"Fame is heavy," I thought.

When I got home, the apartment was hot. The Meat Art in the tub was steaming. It was hot to the touch. Like a fresh loaf of bread.

Archie was sleeping on top of it. He looked like a king on a heated throne. He looked very comfortable.

"Good boy," I whispered.

I took off my shoes. $450.00 shoes. My feet were swollen. I looked like a cherub. A tired cherub.

I sat down to write this.

I thought about the Vice President. I hope he chokes on a feather.

I thought about the Moon. I hope David likes the soup.

I thought about the jar of time my great-great-grandclone gave me. It’s still in my pocket. I didn't use it. I saved it. Saving time is better than spending it. It’s the only thing that goes up in value.

"Goodnight, Meat."

It didn't answer. It just steamed.
