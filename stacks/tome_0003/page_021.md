Saturday, February 21, 2026

Woke up at 10:00 AM. The sky was the color of a used Q-tip. Yellowish white. Very fuzzy. I stayed in bed until the ceiling mirror told me it was time. It has a sensor now. It yelled, "You are missing the zeitgeist!" in a voice that sounded like Truman Capote. I got up.

I went to the kitchen. The moon rock was gone. I looked everywhere. Under the table. Behind the fridge. It was just gone. There was a note on the table written in dust. It said, "GONE FISHING." Or maybe it said, "GONE FUSION." I don't know. The handwriting was shaky. I felt lonely. I hope it’s happy. Maybe it went back to the moon. Or maybe it went to New Jersey.

I stepped on the scale. It didn't say anything. It just displayed a frowny face. I ate breakfast alone. I had a "Leftover Pop-Tart." It was $0.00 because it was stale. It tasted like cardboard. I missed the rock humming.

Cabbed to the Factory at 11:30. The streets were wet. It rained "Viral Videos" last night. The gutters were full of little clips of people falling down. I slipped on a clip of a cat playing the piano. The cab ride was $25.00. The driver was a "Simulacrum" of James Dean. He looked very moody. He drove on the sidewalk. He said, "You can't hurry cool." I agreed.

When I got to the Factory, the door was back. But it was upside down. I had to crawl under it to get inside. Sammy was there. He was wearing a suit made of actual noise. It was fuzzy and grey. It made a shhh-ing sound when he moved. He said, "Andy, the quiet is too loud today."

We went to the back room. The mannequin—Glitch—was sitting in the chair. She was wearing the "Invisibility Suit" I wore yesterday. But she wasn't invisible. She was just translucent. You could see her wiring. It looked like intestines. It was very intimate.

The drone bees were building a hive out of shredded credit cards. It was very colorful. Very financial. One of them flew over and landed on my shoulder. It had painted a tiny moustache on itself. It looked like me. I told it it was a good look. It buzzed and flew away.

I painted a portrait of the Rock. Just from memory. I used "Void Black" and "Neon Vomit." It looked like a bruise. It was beautiful. Sammy said, "It tastes like gravity." I don't know how he tastes things with his ears, but he seems to like it. The paint cost $150. I used the expensive brush.

Around 1:00, a lawyer materialized in the room. He wasn't a hologram. He was real. But he was made of paper. Literally. He was a stack of legal documents stapled together in the shape of a man. He rustled when he walked. He said, "Mr. Warhol, I represent the Shadow." I said, "I paid the alimony." He rustled aggressively. He said, "The Shadow is suing for custody of the Moon Rock. It says the Rock was a family heirloom." I told him the Rock left. He looked shocked. A piece of paper fell off his face. He said, "Abandonment? That doubles the fee." He billed me $5,000 for the consultation. He folded himself into an envelope and mailed himself out the window.

We ordered lunch from "The Cyber-Deli." I had a "Void Burger." It’s a burger with a hole in the middle. Where the meat should be, there’s just nothing. It’s very light. It was $35.00. Sammy had a "Packet of Air." He said it tasted like the number four.

In the afternoon, I got a call on the MirrorWall. It was the AI Mayor. He was crying. He said, "Andy, the portrait you made of me is leaking. It’s dripping black ink all over my virtual lawn." I said, "It’s art. It’s supposed to drip." He said, "I’m suing the city for emotional distress." He hung up.

I worked on the "Famous for 15 Seconds" series. We did a portrait of a tomato soup can that expired in 1998. It was bulging. It looked ready to explode. We printed it in "Botulism Blue." It was $200 for the ink. It’s very dangerous. I love it.

I checked the spot where the Rock used to be. I felt a draft. Maybe the Rock is in the air. I inhaled. I think I inhaled a piece of it.

At 6:00, we went to a dinner at "Serendipity 3.0." It’s underwater. We had to take a submarine elevator. The water was full of glowing fish that were actually cameras. They took pictures of us eating. We sat at a table made of ice.

I ordered the "Frozen Hot Chocolate." It was $150.00. It was steaming, but it was frozen solid. I had to wait for it to melt. While I waited, I watched the fish cameras. One of the fish looked like the Moon Rock. It had a wig on. I waved. It swam away.

Sammy ordered the "Steak Tartare." It was raw data. He ate it with a spoon. He said, "This tastes like a password." The bill came. It was $800. I paid with a "Crypto-Cookie." It crumbled when I touched it. The waiter sucked up the crumbs with a vacuum.

Left the restaurant at 9:00. The submarine ride back up was $50.00.

Walked home. I didn't want to take a cab. I wanted to feel the air. Without a shadow, I walk very fast. The streetlights didn't see me. The cars drove right through me. It was very efficient.

Came home at 10:30. The apartment was quiet. Too quiet. The moon rock wasn't humming. The scale wasn't judging me. I sat on the couch. I felt heavy.

I looked in the mirror. I looked solid. I looked real. It was disappointing.

I turned on the TV. It was playing the news. They said a strange object crashed into a reservoir in New Jersey. It was glowing and vibrating. They said it was humming "Happy Birthday."

I smiled. I hope it has a nice time.

I went to the kitchen. I poured a bowl of "Synth-Milk." I put it on the floor. Just in case it comes back.

I checked my bank account. The lawyer took the $5,000. The bank charged a "Paperwork Fee."
