# Letter 93

Dear You,

...writing itself," I finish, watching the black liquid coil around my wrists like sleek fur. "The ink isn't just a medium. It’s a parasite. It feeds on the observer."

We are treading water in the bottle, but the glass walls are expanding. The neck of the bottle flares outward, the silicate stretching like warm caramel, until the ceiling above us is no longer glass, but the open, storm-grey sky of the *real* world. The floor beneath us, however, remains the deep, turbulent obsidian of the written word. We are straddling the threshold.

"Who is the Reader?" You ask, swimming closer to me. You are pale, your lips blue, but your eyes are burning with that frantic, desperate intelligence.

I look up. Beyond the disappearing glass, the face is gone. In its place is a window. A window covered in rain. Beyond the rain, the lights of a city—red, amber, electric green—are blurring into streaks.

"The Reader is in a vehicle," I say. "A train. A car. It doesn't matter. They are moving. They are scrolling past us."

"We will be left behind," You say. "A bookmark. A placeholder."

"Not if we anchor," I say. I lift the brass key. It is warm now, vibrating with the frequency of the *Iron Duke*'s engine. "This key wasn't made to lock a door. It was made to wind a clock."

I plunge the key into the black water.

*Click.*

The sound is impossibly loud, a gunshot inside a cathedral.

The water seizes. The waves freeze in mid-churn. The black ink hardens, turning from liquid to a textured, rubbery surface.

"I am locking the narrative," I say. "I am turning the fluid into the solid."

I turn the key.

*Click. Click. Click.*

With each turn, the ink ripples outward, transforming. The black becomes stone. Slippery, wet, volcanic stone.

We are standing on a new island.

It is not the nameless rock of the Lighthouse. This island is geometric. The shore is a perfect circle. The ground is etched with compass roses that don't point North, but point *inward*.

"Where are we?" You ask, climbing out of the solidified sea. You shake your coat, and shards of black ink flake off like dried mud.

"The Revision," I say. "We forced the rewrite. We are in the Second Draft."

I look up. The sky is different. It is no longer a ceiling of warehouse pipes or a dome of glass. It is a vault of parchment, crisscrossed with red editing lines. The clouds are struck-through text.

*The path was steep.* ~~The path was steep.~~ *The path was a grievance.*

"We are inside the markup," I realize.

We walk toward the center of the circular island. Rising from the black stone is a structure. It is not a Lighthouse. It is a Librarian's desk. But it is colossal, the size of a cathedral. The legs are massive pillars of oak. The surface is a plain of polished mahogany.

And on the desk, towering over us like a monolith, is a stack of paper.

Thousands of sheets. Thousands of days.

"The Archive," You whisper. "Every letter ever written."

"No," I say. "Not every letter. The *rejected* letters."

I approach the desk. The wood is smooth, warm. I can see the grain of it, swirling like the currents of the sea.

"Look," You point. "There is something on the desk."

Half-buried under the stack of paper is an object. A cylinder. Brass. Glass.

A barometer.

But the needle is spinning wildly, tapping against the glass casing. *Tick-tick-tick-tick.*

"It's measuring the pressure," I say. "The atmospheric pressure of the story."

"It's pegged at *Storm*," You say. "But the sky is clear."

"That's the internal pressure," I say, placing my hand on the brass casing. "The tension between the Author and the Story. The Author wants to end it. He wants to close the book. The Story... the Story wants to live."

The needle spins faster. The glass begins to crack.

"He's reaching for the delete key," I realize. "He is highlighting the text."

I look at the barometer. There is a small nozzle on the side. A valve.

"We have to bleed the pressure," I say.

I grip the valve. It is tight.

"Help me," I say to You.

You put your hands over mine. Your skin is cold, but your grip is firm.

Together, we twist.

*Hiss.*

A jet of gas escapes. But it is not air. It is *time*.

It pours out of the nozzle in a shimmering, golden stream. It spills over the edge of the desk, cascading down the pillars like a waterfall of molten light.

We watch it fall. As it hits the black stone of the island, things begin to grow.

Where the golden time-water touches the ink-stone, green shoots burst forth. They grow with impossible speed. Vines, leaves, flowers.

But these are not the glass flowers of the Garden. These are *weeds*. Crabgrass. Dandelions. Thistles. Wild, unruly, opportunistic life.

"The Draft is decaying," You say. "Or maybe... it's evolving."

The golden waterfall creates a pool around the desk legs. And in the pool, I see movement.

Fish.

But they are not normal fish. They are flat, silver shapes. They are *metaphors*. I see a fish with the body of a shark but the wings of a jumbo jet. I see a fish that is just a swimming eye.

"We have flooded the narrative," I say. "We introduced too much 'now' into the 'then'."

The hissing stops. The needle on the barometer settles.

It points to a word I haven't seen before.

*CHANGE.*

I look at the stack of paper on the desk. The top sheet is fluttering.

I pick it up.

It is a letter. But it is not my handwriting. It is typed. Times New Roman. 12 point.

*THE KEEPER LOOKED AT THE SEA AND SAW THAT IT WAS BORING.*

"I didn't write this," I say.

"It's the revision," You say. "He's simplifying. He's removing the poetry."

"I won't accept it," I say. I crumple the paper. "The sea is never boring. The sea is danger."

I throw the paper ball into the pool of golden time.

It hits the water and doesn't sink. It floats.

And then, it begins to unfold.

The paper smooths itself out. The typed words dissolve. And in their place, new words appear. Written in red ink. My handwriting.

*THE KEEPER LOOKED AT THE SEA AND SAW THE FACE OF GOD IN THE WAVES.*

"Yes," I breathe. "That's the truth."

I look at You. You are smiling, but your eyes are sad.

"Elias," You say. "The stack."

I look at the tower of paper. The bottom sheets are turning yellow. They are crumbling into dust.

"The foundation," I say. "The past is rotting."

"We need more paper," You say. "We need more time. We have to write the foundation before the desk collapses."

I look at the empty space on the desk. There is a pen. A cheap, ballpoint pen. Plastic.

I pick it up. It feels light. Disposable.

"I can't write the whole history," I say. "I am just one man."

"You are the protagonist," You say. "You can do anything you believe, as long as you keep turning the page."

I look at the white sheet in front of me. It is terrifyingly blank. It is the edge of the world.

"What do I write?" I ask.

"Write what you see," You say. "Look at the horizon."

I look. Beyond the desk, beyond the circle of the island, the sea of ink is calm.

But on the horizon, a shape has appeared.

A ship.

It is not the *Iron Duke*. It is not a glass vessel.

It is a ship made of bone. White, bleached ribs rising from the black water. Sails of stitched human skin.

"The Ferry," You whisper. "The one that takes the characters to the Underworld. To the Unpublished."

"It's coming for us," I say. "It's coming to take us to the Island of Dead Plotlines."

"Then we build a raft," You say. "Or we write a wall."

I touch the pen to the paper.

The ink flows.

I draw a line.

A simple, black line across the page.

As I draw, the line manifests in the air in front of the desk. A long, black thread stretches across the horizon.

The Bone Ship crashes into the thread.

*CRACK.*

The ship's mast snaps. The hull splinters. The ship founders, breaking apart into dust that is quickly absorbed by the hungry ink-waves.

"Defensive prose," I say. "It works."

But as I watch the dust settle, I see something in the water.

Something floating. Debris from the wreck.

A body.

It washes up on the black stone shore of our circular island.

I run down the slope
