We jumped.

We leaped off the edge of the Paper World, the vast, white plain of the "I", and fell into the Margins.

I expected nothingness. I expected the silence of the unprinted void.

Instead, we landed on a floor of creaking, polished oak.

The impact knocked the air from my charcoal lungs. I rolled, coming to a stop against a wall of mahogany wainscoting. The air here didn't smell of ozone or salt water. It smelled of coffee, lemon polish, and the sweet, cloying scent of drying glue.

"Keeper?"

I looked up. You were standing a few feet away, your yellow raincoat dripping—not with water, but with ink. Black droplets fell to the floor, staining the polished wood.

"Where are we?" You asked. The echo was strange. It didn't bounce; it was absorbed by the thousands of books lining the walls.

We were in a hallway. A corridor of shelves that stretched infinitely in both directions, lit by flickering gas lamps. It felt familiar. The "Lighthouse has too many rooms," I remembered.

"We're in the architecture," I said, standing up. My legs felt wobbly, like fresh ink not yet set. "We're in the space between the paragraphs."

I walked to a shelf and pulled out a book. It was heavy, bound in leather the color of dried blood. I opened it.

The pages were blank. Completely, utterly white.

"It's an unread book," I whispered.

"Or an unwritten one," You said, peering over my shoulder. "Is this us?"

"No," I said, snapping the book shut. "This is the Potential."

I looked down the hallway. The perspective was skewed. The shelves seemed to converge toward a single point in the distance, where a warm, golden light was burning.

"That light," You said. "It looks like the sun we just left."

"No," I said. "That’s a fireplace."

We walked toward the light. The floor was uneven, sloping upward. As we walked, I noticed things tucked between the books on the shelves.

A rusted key.
A music box cylinder.
A locket with a photograph of a face I didn't recognize, yet felt a profound, aching nostalgia for.

"The lost objects," I said. "The things the Author cut from the text."

We reached the end of the hallway.

The room opened up into a vast study. It looked like the Author's room, the blue room, but... warmer. More solid.

There was a fireplace, roaring with a fire that popped and hissed. Before the fire sat two high-backed armchairs.

And in one of the chairs, a figure.

They were asleep. Their head was tilted back, their mouth slightly open. A book lay open on their chest, rising and falling with their breath.

I stepped closer. The carpet was thick, swallowing the sound of my boots.

"Is that the Author?" You whispered.

"I don't think so," I said. "The Author was terrified of the blank page. This one... this one is dreaming *on* it."

The figure shifted. They murmured something. A word that sounded like my name, but twisted, as if spoken underwater.

Suddenly, the room changed.

The shelves groaned. The books began to rearrange themselves. They slid across the shelves, spinning, flapping their covers like wings. They were flying off the shelves, swirling around the room in a tornado of leather and paper.

"They're waking up!" I shouted over the rustling roar. "The stories are waking up!"

A heavy encyclopedia flew past my head, grazing my ear.

"Who are you talking to?" the figure in the chair shouted.

They weren't asleep anymore. They were sitting upright, the book falling to the floor. They looked at me with eyes that were entirely white—no pupils, no irises. Just the white of the page.

"I am the Keeper!" I yelled back. "I am the one who tends the light!"

"There is no light here!" the figure roared. Their voice sounded like a cracking spine. "Here, there is only the reading!"

The figure stood up. They were immense, towering over us, their shadow stretching across the room to swallow the fireplace.

"You have brought the draft into the library!" the figure boomed. "You have brought the mess into the order! You are smudging the margins!"

"I am not a smudge!" I yelled. I felt the anger rising, hot and sharp. The anger of the character who refuses to die. "I am the Ink!"

I reached into my pocket. My hand closed around the silver pen. It was cracked, the stone jammed in the barrel, but as I gripped it, I felt a pulse.

I pulled it out.

"Stop!" the Librarian commanded. "You will disturb the canon!"

"This canon has been cracked for a long time!" I screamed.

I didn't throw the pen this time. I didn't write on the wall.

I pointed the pen at the floor.

At the carpet. At the beige, safe, predictable carpet.

"Give me the sea," I growled.

I pulled the trigger.

There was no trigger. But the intention was enough. The pen vomited ink.

It didn't drip. It exploded.

A torrent of black, salt-smelling liquid erupted from the cracked barrel, hitting the floor with the force of a fire hose.

The ink spread instantly. It ate the carpet. It dissolved the wood.

"What are you doing?" You screamed, grabbing my arm. "You're flooding the world!"

"I'm making it real!" I shouted back. "Paper can burn! Paper can tear! But ink... ink stains! Ink is forever!"

The black pool spread, climbing the legs of the furniture, soaking into the Librarian's robes.

The Librarian shrieked—a sound like a razor blade tearing a page.

"The Unreliable!" they cried. "The Unreliable is loose!"

The ink reached the fireplace. It hit the fire.

*HISSSSSS.*

The fire didn't go out. It turned blue.

Then green.

Then purple.

The fire was burning colors that didn't exist.

The floor under us gave way. The ink had eaten through the floorboards, through the foundation, through the reality of the study.

We fell.

We fell through the burning ink, through the dissolving books, through the screaming Librarian.

We fell into a new room.

A circular room.

A stone room.

We landed, hard and wet, on the cold stone floor of the Lighthouse.

I scrambled to my knees. The air was cold. The smell was of ozone and rotting kelp.

We were back.

I looked up.

The spiral stairs were there.

The door to the outside was there.

But something was wrong.

The dimensions were off.

The stairs were too steep. The door was too small. It looked like a set piece built by a clumsy carpenter.

"It's a set," I whispered. "We're back on the set."

You stood up. You walked to the heavy iron door.

"Is the sea out there?" You asked.

I didn't know. I was afraid to look.

You pushed the door open.

I squeezed my eyes shut. I waited for the static. I waited for the parchment sky.

"I hear it," You said.

I opened one eye.

You were standing in the doorway, looking out. The wind was whipping your yellow raincoat.

I walked over. I stood beside you.

It was the sea.

But it wasn't the black water. It wasn't the static sea.

It was a ocean of glass.

Solid, jagged, waves of translucent green glass, frozen in mid-crash. The sun was above us—a real, burning sun—and it refracted through the glass waves, casting rainbows across the stone floor of the tower.

"It's beautiful," You said.

"But is it true?" I asked.

I reached out and touched the doorframe. It felt solid. Realer than the library. Realer than the paper world.

I looked down at my hand.

The ink was fading from my skin. The charcoal lines of my knuckles were solidifying. I could see pores. I could see the faint blue of veins beneath the skin.

"I'm becoming flesh," I said. I touched my face. I had stubble. I had warmth.

"So am I," You said. You looked at your hands. They were pink and scarred.

"The cost of the Genre," I said. "To leave the book, you have to become the reader. And to become the reader, you have to leave the dream."

You looked at the sea of glass.

"Can we walk on it?" You asked.

"We can try," I said.

We stepped out of the Lighthouse.

Our boots crunched on the glass waves. It sounded like breaking windows.

We walked.

We walked until the Lighthouse was just a speck behind us.

And there, in the middle of the frozen, glass ocean, we found it.

A boat.

Not a skiff. Not a steamship.

A small, wooden rowboat, sitting perfectly still on a crest of glass.

And in the boat, there was a box.

A wooden box. Sealed with wax.

We climbed into the boat. The glass held us.

"What is in the box?" You asked.
