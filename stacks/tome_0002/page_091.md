"Then we will be the ink blot on his ceiling," I grunt, pulling us up another yard.

The rope of words is rough, smelling of old dust and ozone. The letters *A... S... C... E... N... D...* press into my palms like a brand.

We climb until the rectangle of the entrance is no longer a light, but a distant star. We climb until my arms are numb pistons, driven only by the rhythm of the climb.

"Heave," I say. "Heave."

And then, my hand hits nothing.

I panic, flailing for a hold in the dark. But I am not falling. I am resting on a ledge.

My fingers scrabble against the surface. It is smooth. Polished.

I look up.

There is a glass ceiling above me. A vast dome of crystal, segmented into geometric panes. And beyond the crystal...

Not the sky. Not the stars.

I see a room. A bedroom.

A desk cluttered with dirty mugs. A chair pushed back carelessly. A bed with unmade sheets.

And the light... the blinding white light that illuminates this room is pouring through the dome. We are at the bottom of a giant skylight.

"We are under the desk," I realize, my voice echoing in the glass bubble. "We are inside the footrest of the universe."

I look around the ledge we are standing on. It is a brass circular platform, etched with measurements. *LATITUDE. LONGITUDE. NARRATIVE DEPTH.*

In the center of the platform, standing on a pedestal of iron, is the Lamp.

But it is not a bulb. It is not a flame.

It is a prism. A multifaceted crystal the size of a man's torso, slowly rotating. Inside the crystal, a beam of white light is trapped, bouncing endlessly from face to face.

"It's splitting," You say, sliding off my back. "It's refracting the Author's reality into our world."

I approach the prism. The light spilling out of it creates the colors of the island. The blue of the sea. The grey of the rock. The yellow of your coat.

"If it breaks," I say, "the spectrum collapses. We become white noise."

"There," You say, pointing to the base of the prism.

There is a keyhole. A jagged, geometric hole.

"The key," I say. "I lost the iron key in the warehouse. I lost it in the Janitor's pocket."

"You are the Keeper," You say. "You lock the door. But you are also the key."

I look at my hand. The impression of the rope's letters is still there. *ASCEND.*

But my hand is changing. The skin is turning translucent. The veins are turning blue, glowing with the light of the prism.

"I am fading," I say. "The logic of the story is rejecting me. I climbed too high. I went off the page."

"Put your hand in the hole," You urge.

I can't. I am becoming vapor.

"Elias," You say. "Make a fist."

I clench my hand. The glow intensifies. The light pours through my skin. My bones are illuminated.

"Harder," You command. "Until it hurts."

I squeeze. My knuckles crack. The light in my veins focuses, condensing into a single, blinding point of pressure in my palm.

I shove my hand into the keyhole.

It doesn't fit. The geometry is wrong.

"Change the shape," You say. "Change the pronoun."

I am not 'He'. I am not 'I'.

I am *The Light*.

I push. The crystal screams—a high, thin note like a wine glass tapped too hard.

The prism accepts me. My hand dissolves into the mechanism. I am part of the circuit.

I feel a surge. A voltage of pure narrative intent. It floods me, rushing up my arm, into my heart, out my eyes.

I see everything.

I see the Author sleeping in the room above. I see the Janitor sweeping the warehouse. I see the *Iron Duke* frozen in the dry ice. I see the Garden of Glass.

I see the loop. We are not living in a line. We are living in a circle. The end is the beginning.

"The focus," I gasp. "The lens is dirty."

I see the grime on the inside of the crystal dome. Years of neglect. Layers of apathy. The light from the room above is being filtered through the dust of the Author's indifference.

I have to clean it.

But I have no cloth. I have no water.

I look at You. You are solid again, standing in the colored light of the prism.

"The coat," You say. "Give me the coat."

I am still wearing the grey wool coat, manifested from memory. I shrug it off. It falls to the brass floor.

You pick it up. You climb onto the brass pedestal. You reach up, wrapping the wool around the prism.

"Scrub," I command.

You rub the coat against the rotating crystal. The wool sings, a friction sound, static electricity crackling in the air. *Swish. Swish. Swish.*

The light begins to change.

The colors below are getting brighter. The sea is turning from dull grey to violent, bruised indigo. The sky is igniting.

"More!" I shout. "We have to wake him up!"

The prism spins faster. The hum becomes a roar.

Suddenly, a sound from above.

A siren.

But not a ship's siren. A phone alarm.

*Ding-ding-ding-ding.*

The Author is waking up.

The light in the room above snaps off.

 Darkness falls on the other side of the dome.

But the prism is still spinning. It is running on reserve power now. It is running on *us*.

"He's closing the laptop," I realize. "The input is cut."

"Then we output," You say. "We shine."

I feel the prism draining me. It is pulling the heat from my body. The cold I felt in the sea is returning. It is in my marrow.

"I can't hold it," I whisper. "The filament is burning out."

"Look," You say.

You point to the floor of the brass platform.

There is a trapdoor. A hatch I didn't see before. It is labeled with a single word:

*REVISE.*

"We aren't the Keepers anymore," You say, jumping down from the prism. "We are the Editors."

I pull my hand free from the mechanism. It is numb, useless.

The prism is slowing. The light is dimming. The colors of the world are bleeding into black and white.

"We have to go," You say, grabbing the latch of the trapdoor. "Before the save file corrupts."

"I can't move," I say. My legs are gone. I am just a torso and a head, anchored to the brass platform by the weight of my own fading syntax.

You yank the hatch open.

It doesn't lead down. It leads *out*.

Beyond the hatch is a sky full of stars. Not the ceiling of the warehouse. Real stars. Ancient, uncaring constellations.

"Jump," You say.

"I'll fall forever."

"You'll fly," You say. "Trust the draft."

The prism gives a final, shuddering cough of light. A pulse of violet energy.

It hits me. It lifts me.

I float off the platform. I drift toward the open hatch.

You step out onto the air. You stand on nothing, your yellow coat flapping in the solar wind.

"Come to the edge," You say.

I drift over the threshold.

I am hovering in the void. Below me, the Lighthouse is gone. The island is gone.

There is only the page.

A vast, white sheet of paper stretching into infinity.

And I am a single letter, drifting in the margin.

"Where do we go?" I ask.

You point to the horizon of the page. There, a single line of text is being written, appearing as we watch.

*The Lighthouse Keeper stepped out of the story and into the ink.*

"We go to the next paragraph," You say.

And then, I hear it.

The scratching of a pen.

It is coming from everywhere. It is the sound of the world being built.

I look down at my hand. The translucent fingers are solidifying. The ink is filling in the lines.

I am being rewritten.

"What are you writing?" I ask the darkness. "What am I now?"

The scratching stops.

A new sentence appears in the sky above me, burning in black fire:

*THE KEEPER OPENED HIS EYES AND SAW THE COLOR OF THE WIND.*

I open my eyes.

I am standing on a cliff. The sea is crashing against the rocks. But the waves are silent. And the water is not water. It is millions of pages, rippling in the gale.
