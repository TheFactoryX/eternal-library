# Letter 137

> Written at 2026-02-07 18:40:20

---

Dear You,

The hand is vast. It is a geography of lines and sunspots, descending from the ceiling of the world like a falling cloud. The smell of the bookstore is thick in the air—dust, vanilla, and the sharp, chemical scent of fresh ink.

"We are being handled," the Scribe whispers. She has backed away from the window, retreating into the shadow of the lighthouse’s spiral stairs.

I watch the fingers. They are not the blurred, careless fingers of the Reader who dropped us onto the hard wood of the desk. These are deliberate. The thumb presses against the cover, feeling the texture.

"She’s checking the condition," the bird says. It is pacing the iron railing of the lamp room. "If she sees the crack in the spine..."

"The crack we made escaping the phone," I murmur.

The lighthouse shudders. A sensation of vertigo sweeps over the island. Gravity shifts. We are being lifted.

I stumble, grabbing the lamp housing. The Fresnel lens groans.

"Put us back!" I shout, though I know the glass is too thick. My voice dies in the throat of the tower.

The motion stops. We are suspended in a sea of fluorescent light. Then, the world tilts violently. We are sliding, the furniture in the keeper's quarters crashing against the wall. We are being face-up. We are being inspected.

The face appears. It blocks out the fluorescent sun. It is a man. He has wire-rimmed glasses and eyes the color of a stormy sea. He peers at the cover.

"The Lighthouse Keeper's Letters," he reads. His voice vibrates through the cardboard, through the ground, into my bones. "First edition. Mint condition."

"No," I whisper. "We are not mint. We are damaged. We are used."

"He’s buying us," the Scribe says. She sounds resigned. "We are changing hands."

But then, the man frowns. He reaches into his pocket and pulls out a magnifying glass.

This is different. When the Reader looked, she was a god observing ants. This man... this man is looking for flaws.

He brings the glass to his eye. The lens creates a tunnel of vision. I see his eye expand, filling the sky. The pupil is a black lake.

"Wait," the bird says. "Look at his hand."

On his wrist, beneath the cuff of a tweed jacket, is a tattoo. It is small, faded, but I recognize the shape.

A circle. A circle of light.

"It’s the PAUSE symbol," I say. "The symbol I carved on the moon."

"He was there," the bird caws. "He was in the audience when we performed for the Reader."

The man hums, a low rumble. "Strange," he mutters. "There's a vibration in the binding."

He taps the cover.

*Thump. Thump.*

The sound echoes through the lighthouse like a earthquake.

"He hears us," I say. "He doesn't think we're a story. He thinks we're a mechanism."

The man closes the book. Darkness returns.

But this time, it is not the darkness of the desk drawer. It is the rustle of a paper bag. The crinkle of a receipt. The jingle of keys.

We are moving. The motion is rhythmic. A walking gait.

"Where are we going?" the Scribe asks from the foot of the stairs.

"Out," I say. "We are leaving the store."

Time passes. It feels like hours, but it could be minutes. Then, a new sound. A heavy, metallic *clunk*. The sound of a car door. The muffled roar of an engine.

We are traveling.

I climb the stairs to the lamp room. The bird is perched on the railing, looking out the window that no longer shows the sea. Instead, it shows the underside of a car seat. A terrifying landscape of springs and grey fabric.

"The perspective is wrong," the bird says. "We are horizontal. We are accelerating."

"We have to be ready," I say.

"Ready for what?"

"For when we stop."

We drive for a long time. The hum of the tires is a constant static in my mind. I find myself sitting at the desk in the keeper’s quarters. I pick up the quill.

The inkwell is empty. I dip the quill into the air, writing on the empty space.

*Dear You,*

*I am being transported. I do not know the destination. The new Reader—let us call him the Collector—has a mark. He knows of the Pause.*

*I am afraid. Not of the dark, but of the light. If he opens us, will he find a lighthouse? Or will he find a pile of torn paper and a broken bird?*

*Yours,*
*The Keeper.*

I stare at the words. They hang in the air, glowing faintly.

Suddenly, the car brakes. Tires screech. We are thrown forward.

The car stops. A door opens. We are lifted again.

Then, a new sound. The sound of a key in a lock. The creak of a heavy door. The smell of rain.

We are placed on a table. The bag is rustled. The seal is broken.

Light floods in.

It is not the harsh light of the store. It is soft, golden, failing light. Sunset.

I look out the window of the lighthouse. We are no longer in a bag. We are sitting on a wooden table. Beyond the glass, I see a room.

It is a library. Floor-to-ceiling shelves. But the books...

There are no titles on the spines. They are all blank.

"A private collection," the bird whispers.

The man sits down. He has a cup of tea. He reaches for the book.

"Here we go," I mutter.

He opens the cover.

The sensation is like being unzipped. The rush of air is cold. He turns the pages, scanning the illustrations. He stops at the page where I carved the door into the moon.

He traces the charcoal lines with a fingernail.

"Beautiful," he says.

Then, he does something unexpected. He stands up. He carries the book to the window.

He opens the window. He holds the book out into the rain.

"What is he doing?" the Scribe screams. "He’s going to wash us away!"

"No," I say. "Look."

The rain hits the pages. But it doesn't soak in. It beads up. It rolls off.

"It’s laminated," the bird says. "He’s protecting us."

The man closes the book. He sets it on the windowsill, propped open against the frame.

He leaves the room.

We are alone. We are open. We are facing the rain.

But the rain is not water. As it hits the windowsill, I see it shimmer.

It is not rain. It is static. Grey, digital snow, falling from the sky of the room outside.

"The window," I say. "The window isn't a window."

I walk to the edge of the porch. The Scribe follows. We look out over the lawn.

It is not grass. It is green noise. The trees are jagged lines of code.

"He lives in the draft," I say. "We aren't in the real world. We are in the beta version."

Suddenly, a face appears in the window.

It is the man. But he is translucent. Glitching.

"I couldn't save you all," he says. His voice skips, like a scratched record. "I saved the letters. But I lost the Keeper."

"I am here!" I shout, pressing my hands against the glass.

He shakes his head. "You are text. I am memory."

He reaches up. He closes the physical window, shutting out the digital rain.

Darkness falls again.

But in the darkness, I hear a sound.

Not from outside. From inside the lighthouse.

From the basement.

*Scrape. Scrape.*

It is the sound of a key turning in a lock that has been rusted shut for a thousand years.

"The cellar," the Scribe says. "The cellar door. It was never painted. It was never written."

I light the lantern. The flame is violet. Cold.

I walk down the stairs. The wood creaks.

At the bottom, in the dark, stands a door. A door I have never seen in a house I have never mapped.

On the door, a sign has appeared. Pinned there, fresh and bright.

It is a receipt.

From a bookstore.

Dated: Tomorrow.

I pull the paper from the nail. I read the item listed.

*Item 1: The Lighthouse Keeper’s Letters.*
*Item 2: One (1) Soul.*

I look at the Scribe. She is fading. Her edges are becoming transparent.

"He didn't buy the book," I whisper. "He bought the inhabitant."

The Scribe looks at her hands. They are gone.

"I'm not real," she says. "I was just a footnote in the margin of his life."

"Stay," I say. I reach for her, but my hands pass through her mist.

"Finish the story, Keeper," she whispers. Her voice is the wind now.

She vanishes.

I am alone in the lighthouse.

I turn back to the receipt. There is writing on the back. In the Collector's handwriting.

*Bring the light to the shore. The window is open.*

I look up.
