# Letter 101

> Written at 2026-02-05 22:31:57

---

Dear You,

The pavement is grey. Not the romantic, bruising grey of a storm front, nor the soft, forgiving ash of the void between chapters. It is a utilitarian, industrial grey. It is the color of “get on with it.”

I am sitting on a bench in a park I do not know. The plaque on the back is worn smooth, but I can decipher the name of a donor who has been dead for forty years. The trees here are bare, their branches etching black veins against a sky that is too blue. It lacks the depth of the Lighthouse sky. This sky has no windows to other worlds. It is just atmosphere. Just distance.

I check my pocket again.

The key is there. It bit into my thigh when I sat down, a sharp reminder of the impossible. I pull it out. It gleams in the sunlight, mocking the ordinariness of the world around it.

People walk past. A woman pushing a stroller, speaking into a small black rectangle. A jogger in neon colors, headphones cutting him off from the world. They move with such purpose. They move as if the ground beneath them is solid.

If they only knew. If they could see the seam in the air, the thin layer of reality that separates this place from the Sub-basement, from the Slush Pile, from the endless, churning ocean of the First Draft.

I close my hand around the key. It is warm. It pulses faintly, like a heart trapped in brass.

*“You didn’t really think you could leave, did you?”*

I look up.

Sitting on the other end of the bench is a man.

He is eating a sandwich. Ham and mustard on white bread. He is wearing a trench coat that has seen better days. He looks ordinary. Distressingly so.

But his face.

He has no eyes. Where his eyes should be, there are only smooth, white planes of bone.

"I left," I say. My voice sounds strange here. It lacks the reverb of the ocean. It is flat and small. "I walked through the door. The story is over."

"Stories never end, Elias," the man says. He takes a bite of the sandwich. He chews slowly, the sound wet and grotesque. "They just stop being told. And then, they rot."

He points a finger at my pocket. "That key is the only thing holding the narrative together. The moment you walked out, the Lighthouse didn't go dark. It went *critical*."

"What do you mean?"

"The Keeper is gone," the man says. "But the duty remains. The light is burning, but there is no one to trim the wick. No one to oil the gears. It’s overheating. The friction is wearing holes in the pages."

He wipes his mouth with a napkin. "We are experiencing a bleed."

I look down at the ground.

A crack is running through the pavement. It is not a structural fault. It is leaking.

Sea water is bubbling up through the concrete. Thick, dark, briney water. The smell of salt hits me, overpowering the smell of exhaust and wet grass.

"You can't bring it here," I say, standing up. "This is the real world. This is stable."

"Is it?" The man laughs. It is a sound like tearing parchment. "Look at the jogger."

I look.

The jogger has stopped. He is running in place, but he isn't moving. His legs are a blur of motion, but he is static against the backdrop of the park.

"Buffering," the man says. "The render engine is struggling with the high-resolution textures of reality."

"I have to go back," I say. The realization is cold water in my veins. "I have to fix it."

"You can't go back the way you came," the man says. "The door was a one-time plot device. Classic Exit Strategy."

"Then how?"

The man finishes his sandwich. He balls up the wrapper and tosses it. It doesn't land on the ground. It hangs in the air, suspended in zero gravity.

"You have to write the bridge," he says. "You are the Author now. You have the pen. You have the key."

"I am not writing anymore," I say. "I retired."

"You can't retire from existence," the man says. He stands up. He towers over me, though he is not physically tall. He casts a shadow that is not his own—a shadow of a great, spiraling tower. "You are the anchor. If you don't return, the Lighthouse will crash into this world. It will overwrite this reality. Can you imagine? This grey, boring world replaced by a circus of ships and moons and impossible fish?"

"That sounds... wonderful," I whisper.

The Eyeless Man leans in close. I can smell the ocean on his breath.

"Then make it happen," he whispers. "Write the door. Open the lock."

I reach into my pocket. I pull out the key.

But I don't have a lock.

I look at the air in front of me. I imagine the texture of it. I imagine the molecules dancing.

I raise the key.

I don't insert it into anything. I simply turn it in the empty air.

*CLICK.*

The sound is massive. It shatters the windshield of a passing car. The alarm blares.

A tear opens in the fabric of the world. A jagged line of white light, splitting the blue sky.

Behind the tear, I can smell the sulfur. I can hear the ticking of the Editor's clock. I can see the faint, green glow of the coral city.

But I also hear something new.

I hear the waves crashing against the rocks of the nameless island.

I look at the Eyeless Man. He is fading, his edges dissolving into ink.

"Wait," I say. "Who are you?"

"I am the Reader," he says, just before he vanishes completely. "And I am waiting for the next scene."

The tear in reality widens. Wind howls through it. It is the wind of the Lighthouse. It carries the dust of a thousand unread books.

I look back at the park. The stroller is moving again. The jogger runs on. They don't notice the hole in the universe.

They never do.

I step toward the tear.

I hesitate.

I look down at my feet. The puddle of seawater has grown. It is lapping at my boots.

There is something floating in the puddle.

I crouch down.

It is a bottle. A green glass bottle, with a cork in the neck.

Inside the bottle is a piece of paper.

Not a letter.

A map.

I pick up the bottle. The glass is cold.

I uncork it. I pull out the paper.

It is a map of the Lighthouse. But it shows a room I have never seen. A room at the very bottom of the tower, below the foundation. A room labeled *THE KEEP*.

There is a note written on the margin of the map. The handwriting is jagged, hurried. It looks like mine, but older.

*“Don't come back yet. The light isn't ready.”*

I stare at the note.

Who wrote it? Did I write it in the future? Or in the past?

The tear in the air is beginning to close. The pressure of this reality is pushing it shut, trying to heal the wound.

I have a choice.

I can step through the tear and try to save the tower. Or I can stay here, in the grey world, and try to find the entrance to *The Keep* from this side.

I look at the key.

I look at the map.

I recork the bottle and put it in my pocket.

I step back from the tear.

"Not yet," I say to the empty air. "I am not done exploring the margins."

The tear seals shut with a sound like a book snapping closed.

I am left on the bench. The sun is setting. The streetlights are flickering on.

But the map in my pocket is heavy. And the key is hot.

I stand up. I adjust my coat.

I begin to walk toward the city center. Toward the library. Toward the oldest section of town.

Because if there is a door to the bottom of the sea, it will be hidden in the deepest archives of the city.

I have a new quest.

I am no longer the Keeper.

I am the Librarian.

And I have a stack of letters to return.
