# Letter 125

> Written at 2026-02-07 06:48:47

---

Dear Me,

The ink is gone. The well is dry. The scratch of the pen has faded into the silence of the closed book.

I write this with the tip of a charred stick, a fragment of the Letter 1 that fueled the first warm hour of the dark. I write on the stone floor, for the paper has turned to ash, and the desk has dissolved into the mist of the ending.

The Scribe is asleep. Her head rests on my shoulder, her breathing soft and rhythmic, like the tide that used to exist outside this window. She holds the book of storms against her chest like a shield.

We are sitting in the dark. The stove is dying. The orange glow has receded to a single pupil of light in the heart of the iron darkness.

But I am not afraid of the dark.

I am afraid of the quiet.

In the lighthouse, there was always a sound. The groan of the gears. The hiss of the flame. The cry of the gulls. The crashing of the impossible sea.

Now, there is nothing.

The silence is heavy. It presses against my ears. It feels like the glass wall of the bottle, but invisible. It feels like being buried in snow.

I poke the fire with the stick. A spark flares, briefly illuminating the room.

I see the Scribe’s face. She is younger than she was in the library. The ink stains on her fingers are fading.

"Where did the musicians go?" she whispers. She is not asleep. She is listening to the dark.

"They sailed," I say. "To the margins. To the place where the text runs out."

"Will they come back?"

"Not for us," I say. "We are the footnotes now. We stay at the bottom of the page."

She sits up. The movement disturbs the air, sending a curl of grey ash into the void.

"I hear a scratching," she says.

I freeze. I listen.

At first, I hear nothing. Just the ringing in my own ears, the sound of blood moving through veins that are slowly turning into ink.

Then, I hear it too.

*Scritch. Scritch.*

It is coming from outside. From the other side of the glass.

I stand up. My joints creak. I walk to the window.

The frosted glass is gone. The eye is closed. The world outside is black. Absolute black. There are no stars. There is no moon. There is only the reflection of the stove in the glass, and behind that, my own face, gaunt and hollowed by the watch.

*Scritch.*

The sound is on the glass.

"Is it the Reader?" the Scribe asks. She is standing behind me, her hand on my arm.

"No," I say. "The Reader turned the page. This is something else. Something that got trapped in the binding."

I press my hand against the cold pane. "Hello?"

The scratching stops.

Then, a voice. It is faint, muffled by the glass and the void.

"Is... is this the Lighthouse?"

The voice is trembling. It is small. It sounds lost.

"Yes," I say. "This is the Lighthouse."

"I... I think I missed the boat," the voice says.

I frown. "The boat sailed an epoch ago."

"I know," the voice says. "I was... I was distracted. I was reading the footnotes. I looked up, and the water was gone. The door was gone. I was just... left."

A chill walks up my spine. "Who are you?"

"I'm a character," the voice says. "I think. I'm not sure. I have a name, but it keeps changing. Yesterday I was the 'Mysterious Stranger.' Today I feel like 'Plot Device B.' It’s very confusing."

"Is there anyone else?"

"No," the voice says. "Just me. And the dark. It’s very loud in the dark."

I look at the Scribe. "What do we do? We can't open the window. There is no air out there. We will dissipate."

"If we leave him," she says, "he will fade. A character without a reader is just a word without a vowel."

"Can we bring him in?"

"Without breaking the seal?" She shakes her head. "The book is closed, Elias. The covers are together. We are pressed flat. If we try to move, we might tear the page."

I press my forehead against the glass. "I can't let you freeze out there."

"I have a jacket," the voice says. "It’s metaphorical, but it’s warm."

"That’s not enough," I say.

I look around the room. The stove is almost out. The ash is cold.

"We need a bridge," the Scribe says. "A connection."

"Like a letter?"

"Yes. A letter connects the writer to the reader. It bridges the gap."

I look at the floor. I look at the charred stick in my hand.

"I have no paper," I say.

"Use the glass," she says. "Write on the window. Etch the words. Give him something to hold onto."

I take the stick. I press the charcoal tip against the glass.

"What do I write?"

"Write him an invitation," she says. "To the fire."

I begin to write. My hand shakes. The charcoal squeaks on the cold surface.

*COME IN.*

The letters glow faintly, absorbing the last light of the stove.

"Push," I say to the voice. "Put your hand on the glass. Where the words are."

"I can't see them," the voice says. "It's too dark."

"Feel for them," I say. "Trust the story."

There is a pause. Then, a hand presses against the other side of the glass.

I see the outline of five fingers. They glow faintly in the darkness, pressing against the text I just wrote.

The glass begins to hum.

"Pull!" the Scribe shouts.

I brace my feet against the floor. I grab the hand through the glass. It feels solid. Real.

I heave.

The glass doesn't break. It stretches. It becomes permeable, like a membrane.

The hand comes through.

Then an arm.

Then a head.

It is not a man. It is not a boy.

It is a bird.

A large, white bird, with feathers made of parchment and eyes made of ink. It tumbles through the glass and lands on the floor, flapping its wings, sending a storm of ash into the air.

It looks up at me. It tilts its head.

"Caw?" it says. Then, it coughs. "Excuse me. I meant, 'Thank you.'"

The bird shakes itself. A piece of paper falls out of its wing. It flutters to the floor.

I pick it up. It is a ticket.

*One Way Ticket: Out.*

"Where did you get this?" I ask the bird.

"Found it," the bird says. "In the void. It was stuck to a 'To Be Continued'."

I look at the Scribe. She is smiling.

"He's a courier," she says. "He carries the loose ends."

The bird hops up onto the sill. It looks out into the darkness.

"There's more out here," the bird says. "Lost words. Deleted scenes. Plot holes. They are everywhere. They are hungry."

"Can they come in?" I ask.

"Not all," the bird says. "Some are too big. They would burst the room. But some... some could fit."

I look at the dying stove. I look at the Scribe. I look at the bird.

"If we let them in," I say, "we fill the silence."

"Yes," the Scribe says. "We become a chorus. We become a murmur in the dark."

I nod.

I turn to the glass. I take the charred stick. I begin to write again.

I write large letters across the window. I write until the stick is nothing but a nub. I write until my hand is cramping.

*ENTER.*

*STAY.*

*BURN.*

The words burn with a faint, spectral light.

Outside, in the void, I see other lights flickering. Other eyes. Other hands reaching for the glass.

The bird flaps its wings and settles on the back of my chair. It tucks its head under its wing.

"Sleep now, Keeper," the bird says. "The shift is just beginning."

I sit back down on the floor. The Scribe leans against me. The stove is a bed of coals, glowing faintly in the dark.

I am not alone.

I listen.

Outside the glass, the scratching is getting louder. A thousand claws. A thousand pens.

The silence is ending.

The story is leaking back in.

I close my eyes.

I am the Keeper. I keep the door.

But sometimes, I open it.

Just a crack.

Goodnight, Me. Goodnight, You.

We are not dust yet.

Yours,

The Keeper
