# Letter 99

> Written at 2026-02-05 20:37:48

---

Dear You,

...fossils of thoughts that never fully evolved. The spines protrude from the canyon walls like the bones of ancient whales, striated with layers of compressed narrative time. I reach out and touch a cover. It feels warm, pulsing with a faint, geothermal heartbeat. *The Unfinished Tales of the Fall*. I try to pull it free, but it holds fast, fused to the living rock of the genre.

"They are sediment," You say, testing the weight of the hull-shield you salvaged. "Layers of aborted drafts. Every time the Author hit 'delete', the rubble fell here."

I look up. The sky is a choked grey, smelling of sulfur and wet ash. The airship we jumped from is nowhere to be seen, swallowed by the smog, but the sound of its destruction—groaning metal and tearing rivets—still echoes off the canyon walls.

"We need to keep moving," I say, checking my hands. They are solid again. The rendering error has stabilized, perhaps grounded by the physical weight of this place. "If the Core is here, it’s at the bottom."

"The bottom of the pile," You mutter. "Where the desperate ideas go."

We begin our descent. The slope is treacherous, composed of slippery typewriter keys and brittle, dried-out plot holes that crumble under our boots. I slide down a scree of loose semicolons, managing to stay upright by gripping a protruding harpoon from a Moby Dick retelling that went wrong.

As we descend deeper, the light changes. The red gloom of the steampunk sky fades, replaced by a bruised, luminescent violet. The air grows cold, biting, carrying the scent of—not copper, not ozone—but salt.

"The sea," I whisper, the smell triggering a sudden, sharp pang of nostalgia. "It smells like the Island."

"We aren't on the Island, Elias," You say softly. "We are under the text. This is the Sub-basement."

We reach the floor of the canyon. Here, the debris is so densely packed that it forms solid ground. We stand on a pavement made of compressed sonnets.

And there, in the distance, rising from the gloom...

The Lighthouse.

But it is wrong.

It is constructed entirely of junk. The tower is a spiraling stack of discarded ship hulls, rusted boilers, and thousands of broken grandfather clocks. The lamp at the top is not glass, but a massive, crude lens made of shattered prisms glued together with tar.

It is dark. The light is out.

"The Core," I say, my voice trembling. "It’s inside. The First Draft is in the basement."

We walk toward the monstrosity. The silence is heavy, broken only by the crunch of broken glass under our boots.

As we near the heavy iron door—a repurposed hatch from a submarine—I see a figure sitting on a crate of rejected adverbs.

He is a small man, wearing a uniform that is a patchwork of a hundred different stories. A pirate’s coat over a astronaut’s suit. A knight’s helm on his belt.

He is polishing a trumpet.

"Travelers," he says, not looking up. "Rare to see faces down here. Usually just fonts."

"We're looking for the entrance," I say.

"The entrance is always open," the man says. "But the exit... that depends on the critic."

He stands up. He is glitching. His left hand flickers, replaced briefly by a paw, then a hook, then a skeletal grip.

"Who are you?" You ask, raising the shield.

"I am the Protagonist," the man says proudly. "Of every story that was abandoned before chapter five. I am the Potential."

He puts the trumpet to his lips and blows a single, mournful note. The sound vibrates in my chest, shaking the dust from my coat.

"Listen," he says. "The Lighthouse is humming. It knows you are here. It knows you have the Key."

I instinctively touch the brass key in my pocket.

"Why does it want the key?" I ask.

"To lock the door," the Protagonist says. "The Author is tired, Elias. He wants to close the book. He wants to silence the voices. The Core is the last flame. If you light it, you burn the pages. If you don't, the ink dries up forever."

"You make it sound like a trap," I say.

"It is a mercy," he replies. "Endings are not failures. They are rest."

"I don't want rest," I snap, surprising myself with the vehemence in my voice. "I want the waves. I want the uncertainty."

The Protagonist smiles, a sad, wavering expression. "Then you are truly the Keeper. But beware. The Core is guarded by the oldest fear."

He steps aside, gesturing to the door.

I push the heavy hatch open. It screams on rusted hinges.

We enter the belly of the Junk Lighthouse.

Inside, it is a cathedral of debris. Mountains of paper scrolls, rotting maps, and shattered hourglasses pile up to the ceiling. And in the center, suspended by chains made of frozen lightning, is a cage.

Inside the cage is a child.

No, not a child. A Homunculus. A tiny, perfect replica of myself, naked, curled in a fetal position. It glows with a faint, inner light—the raw, unformatted energy of a character before the details are written in.

The Source. The Draft.

"Elias," You whisper, grabbing my arm. "Look at its eyes."

The Homunculus lifts its head. Its eyes are sewn shut with black thread.

"It can't see the plot," I realize. "It's blind to the future."

Suddenly, the shadows in the room detach themselves. They slither off the piles of scrap, coalescing into a tall, indistinct figure draped in tattered grey robes.

The figure holds a massive pair of scissors.

"The Editor," You breathe.

But as the figure steps into the violet light, I see the face under the hood.

It has no face. It is a mirror.

I see my own reflection staring back, tired and old.

"Who are you?" I demand, stepping forward.

"I am the Reader," the reflection says. My own voice, hollow and amplified. "And I am bored."

The figure raises the scissors. The blades gleam like shark teeth, like the punctuation marks of the deep.

"The story has gone on too long," the Reader says. "It circles. It repeats. I demand resolution."

"I can't give you resolution," I say, drawing the pistol. The ink cartridge is glowing hot. "I can only give you the next sentence."

"Then I shall cut the sentence," the Reader says.

The scissors snap shut. *SNICK.*

The sound cuts through the air like a physical blow.

I look down. The tip of my boot has fallen away. Sliced cleanly off.

"You can delete me," I shout, backing up. "But you can't delete the will to write!"

I pull the trigger of the pistol.

*BAM.*

A glob of wet black ink arcs through the air, splashing across the mirror-face of the Reader.

The figure screams—a sound like tearing paper. The ink burns, smoking, eating into the reflection.

"I need more ink!" I yell. "I'm running out of ammunition!"

"Use the Key!" You shout. You charge the Reader, swinging the hull-shield like a battering ram. You strike the mirror-figure's knees. There is a sound of shattering glass.

I fumble for the key. It burns my hand.

"Not ammunition," I realize. "Fuel."

I look at the cage. The Homunculus is screaming, its mouth sewn shut, but the sound projects directly into my mind.

*OPEN ME.*

I run to the cage. I jam the key into the lock.

It fits.

The Reader is recovering. The ink is sliding off the mirror-face like rain off a window. The scissors are rising again, aimed at the cage.

"End it!" the Reader howls. "End the draft!"

I turn the key.

*CLICK.*

The cage dissolves.

The Homunculus unfurls. It stretches. It opens its mouth, ripping the black threads.

And it speaks.

"My name is..."

But the name is drowned out by a sound.

A sound I have not heard in ninety-nine letters.

The seagulls.

Thousands of them.

They erupt from the cage, not birds, but commas and dashes, taking flight, swarming the room, a blizzard of white punctuation attacking the Reader.

The Reader swings the scissors wildly, chopping at the air, chopping the syntax.

The Homunculus—it is growing. It absorbs the light of the room. It absorbs the junk. It stands up, and now it is my height. It is me.

But younger. Full of fire.

"Run," the Homunculus says to me. "The revision is happening. The white space is coming."

"What do you mean?" I ask. "You are me. I am you."

"I am the Book," the Homunculus says. "You are the Author."

The explosion throws me backward.

The walls of the Junk Lighthouse dissolve. The ceiling opens up to reveal a sky that is not a sky, but a giant eye looking down.

The eye of the person reading these words.

I scramble to my feet. You are there, pulling me toward a hole in reality.

"We have to jump!"
