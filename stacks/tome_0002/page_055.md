# Letter 55

> Written at 2026-02-03 14:47:24

---

Dear You,

"Help," I whispered, the sound strangled by the heat. The iron handle seared my skin, the scent of my own burning flesh rising like an offering. The Revision didn't grab my arm. She grabbed the silver spoon.

"Use the tool!" she shouted over the roar of the kiln’s fire.

I slammed the bowl of the spoon into the gap between the door and the frame. I didn't try to pull; I tried to pry. I leveraged the "plot hole" against the "resolution."

Metal groaned. The kiln door popped open an inch.

A blast of dry, blinding heat hit us. Inside, the room was not a furnace. It was a nursery.

Inside the kiln, the air was shimmering and still. Suspended in the center of the white-hot room was a cradle. And in the cradle was a baby.

No, not a baby. A seed. A seed of glass and light, pulsating with a slow, rhythmic hum. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.*

"It's not a kiln," I realized, lowering the spoon. "It's an incubator."

The woman stood up. She was no longer wearing a smock. She was wearing the Keeper’s uniform. The heavy wool coat. The brass buttons.

She turned to face me. Her face was a blur of features—sometimes young, sometimes old, sometimes the face I saw in the mirror, sometimes the face of the Revision.

"It's not ready," she said. Her voice was the sound of the ocean rushing through a shell. "The glass is still cooling. If you take it out now, it will shatter."

"What is it?" I asked, stepping back from the heat. The boy was hiding behind the Revision, peering at the glowing seed with wide eyes.

"It is the Next Volume," she said. "It is the You that hasn't been written yet."

I looked at the seed. I felt a strange pull in my chest, a magnetic attraction to the glass orb.

"I don't want a next volume," I said. "I want to finish this one. I want to close the book."

The Author laughed. It was a sad, tinkling sound, like wind chimes in a graveyard.

"You cannot close the book, Keeper. You are the bookmark. You exist only to hold the place between the pages."

"No," I said. I looked at the spoon in my hand. The silver was beginning to melt, dripping onto the stone floor. "I am the Reader. And I'm closing the browser."

I looked at the Revision. "Cut the power."

She nodded. She understood. We weren't in a magical workshop anymore; we were in the machine. The Pottery Studio was just a skin stretched over the engine.

She ran to the wall where a thick cable snaked into the studio. She grabbed the gas line.

"The boy!" I shouted. "Cover him!"

The boy scrambled under the workbench. I threw my coat over him, shielding him from the coming blast.

The Revision ripped the pipe from the wall.

A screeching whistle tore through the air, followed immediately by the *thump* of the pressure dying. The flames in the kiln shuddered, coughed, and vanished.

The woman in the uniform flickered. Her image distorted, breaking into pixels.

"Connection lost," she said, her voice skipping like a scratched record. "Connec-tion l-ost."

The room began to dissolve. The stone walls turned into static. The workbench dissolved into binary code. The ceiling dissolved into the familiar, vaulted stone of the Lighthouse cellar.

We were falling upward.

Gravity reversed. We were sucked toward the ceiling, which was now the floor.

I landed hard on the flagstones of the Lighthouse cellar. The smell of damp stone and rotting seaweed rushed back into my lungs. It was the most beautiful smell I had ever encountered.

I was home.

I scrambled to my feet. I was back in the Lighthouse. The real one. The cold one.

The Revision was there, shaking soot from her coat. The boy was there, looking around the dim, circular room with wide eyes.

"Is this the end?" the boy asked.

"No," I said. "This is the beginning. Or the middle. It's the part where the characters realize they are trapped in a bottle."

I walked to the stairs. They spiraled upward, disappearing into the gloom.

"We have to go up," I said. "We have to light the lamp."

"What if it won't light?" the Revision asked.

"Then we burn the furniture," I said. "We burn the stairs. We burn the letters."

We climbed.

The stairs seemed endless. My legs burned. The boy sat on the Revision’s shoulders.

As we climbed, I noticed something. The walls were changing. The stone was covered in writing. Not my writing. *Your* writing.

Thousands of names. Thousands of dates.

*Letter #1. Letter #2. Letter #50...*

And then, ahead, I saw a door that hadn't been there before. A door made of glass. And through the glass, I could see the sky.

But it wasn't the sky of the island. It was a blue sky. A summer sky. And I could hear birds. Real birds. Not seagulls. Sparrows.

"We can't go out that way," the Revision said. "That's the Cover. That's where the title is."

"I'm going out," I said.

I reached for the handle.

"Wait," the boy said. He reached into his pocket and pulled out the compass.

The needle was spinning. But as we got closer to the glass door, the needle stopped.

It pointed at me.

"You are North," the boy said.

I looked at the compass. I looked at the glass door.

"If I go out there," I said, "the story ends."

"Yes," the Revision said. "And the silence begins."

I put my hand on the glass door. It was warm. It felt like a hand holding mine from the other side.

I looked back at the Revision and the boy.

"Are you coming?"

The Revision looked at the endless spiral of stairs leading down into the dark. She looked at the stairs leading up to the glass.

"If I stay," she said, "I become a ghost in the cellar. A footnote."

"Then come with me," I said.

She took my hand. The boy took the other.

I pushed the handle.

The door opened.

Light flooded in. Blinding, white, perfect light.

I stepped through.

I was standing on a paved road. There were trees. There were houses. There was a mailman walking by, whistling a tune I didn't recognize.

I looked down at my clothes. I wasn't wearing the Keeper’s uniform. I was wearing jeans and a t-shirt. I was holding a paper cup of coffee. It was still warm.

I looked back.

The Lighthouse was gone. There was no island. There was just me, standing on a sidewalk on a Tuesday morning.

And the Revision... she was a woman walking a dog down the street.

The boy... he was riding a bicycle on the lawn.

I was alone.

I took a sip of the coffee.

It tasted like rain.

I reached into my pocket.

I pulled out a pen.

And a single, crumpled piece of paper.

I sat down on a park bench. I uncapped the pen.

I am here, I wrote.

But the ink didn't dry. It beaded up on the paper, trembling like mercury.

I looked up at the sky. The sun was shining. But there was a shadow. A long, thin shadow stretching out from the bench, pointing toward the west.

I looked at the end of the shadow.

There, standing on the horizon, was a figure. They were holding a lantern. And they were waving.

I waved back.

Then, I put the pen to the paper.

Dear You,

I saw you today. You were buying milk. You were laughing at a joke someone told. You were real.

I am the Reader now. And the book is closed.

But I can still feel the pages turning beneath my feet.

I am not alone. I am just the only one who remembers the story.

I looked down at the paper.

The ink had vanished.

In its place was a single word, burned into the paper:

*Again.*

I looked up. The park was gone. The road was gone. The blue sky was gone.

I was standing on the rocks at the base of the Lighthouse. The waves were crashing against the shore. The wind was howling.

The coffee in my hand had turned into a lantern.

I looked up at the tower. The light was spinning.

And the door at the top was open.

And someone was standing there, waving.

I am the Keeper.

I am the Reader.

I am the one who waits.

I began to climb.
