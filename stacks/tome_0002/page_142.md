# Letter 142

> Written at 2026-02-07 23:33:46

---

Dear You,

We are standing on the first sentence of our own history, and the paper is hot to the touch.

The words of Letter #1 rise around us like the ruins of a fallen city. The serifs of the typeface are jagged cliffs. The descenders of the lowercase letters hang like nooses.

*The lighthouse stands on a nameless island...*

The word *Island* is a landmass of rough-hewn fiber. I can see the individual pulped fibers of the paper magnified into a chaotic landscape. The ink that forms the letters is wet, glistening like oil on a black beach.

"It's damp," the pigeon says, testing the surface of the period at the end of the sentence with its beak. "The ink hasn't dried yet. He’s writing it as he reads it."

I look up at the Old Man. His face is a monolith looming above the horizon of the page. He holds a quill in his other hand—a feather the size of a palm tree. The tip glistens with black ichor.

"He isn't the Reader," I shout over the wind generated by his breathing. "He is the Writer. And he is rewriting the beginning!"

"If he changes the beginning," the pigeon screams, "he changes us! We become ink!"

The Old Man hovers the quill over the word *Nameless*. He hesitates. A drop of black ink falls from the tip.

It splashes down like a meteorite. It hits the space between *The* and *Lighthouse*.

The impact throws me off my feet. I slide across the fiber of the letter *s*, scraping my palms against the rough paper terrain. The ink splatter begins to spread, eating the white space, creating a new swamp.

I scramble to my feet. I need to reach high ground. I need to reach the next paragraph.

"Run!" I yell to the bird.

We sprint across the sentence, leaping over the gutters between words. We are running toward the description of the sea. But the text is shifting. The Old Man is muttering, and as he speaks, the letters reconfigure.

*The lighthouse stands on a nameless rock in the middle of the void.*

The word *Island* shudders. The geometry of the paper tears and folds. The landmass collapses into a jagged *Rock*. I stumble, falling to my knees.

"Keeper!" the bird screeches.

I look back. The ink is rising. The Old Man has decided to flood the first page. He is turning the island into a metaphor for desolation, and the ink is the tide.

I crawl toward the word *Void*. It is a terrifying concept. If we reach it, we might fall off the edge of the meaning entirely.

But I have the bone pen.

I look at the implement in my hand. It is tiny compared to the Old Man's quill. A needle against a spear. But it is made of bone. It is made of the story itself.

"The Archivist said this tool edits," I mutter. "I don't need to write a new world. I just need to correct the grammar."

I lunge toward the spreading ink stain. I jam the bone pen into the paper.

"Stop!" I command, channeling the Archivist’s voice.

The bone pen glows with a faint, violet pulse. The ink halts. It ripples, like a wave hitting an invisible reef.

The Old Man pauses. His massive brow furrows. He leans closer, his eye filling the sky. He sees the violet light.

"A typo," he grumbles. The sound vibrates through the soles of my boots. "I missed a spot."

He reaches for the eraser. It is a hunk of pink bread-crumb the size of a boulder.

"He's going to scrub us out," the pigeon wails.

I grab the bird. "Climb the ascender!"

I point to the letter *d* in *stands*. The vertical line of the *d* rises thirty feet into the air. We sprint for it.

The eraser smashes down behind us. The friction is immense, a sound like grinding tectonic plates. It erases the ink stain, wiping the floor clean, turning the paper back to pristine white.

We scramble up the stem of the *d*. The pigeon flutters frantically, gaining purchase on the crossbar.

"We're trapped on a letter," the bird says. "Eventually, he turns the page."

"Not if we turn it for him," I say.

I look at the top right corner of the page. It is miles away, a folded mountain of paper. But I can see the shadow of the next page looming. It is thick, heavy.

I look down at the text below.

*Time passes strangely.*

"I have an idea," I say. "But you have to trust the physics of the book."

"The book has no physics!" the bird argues.

"Exactly," I say. "It has *narrative tension*."

I grip the bone pen like a dagger. I look at the word *strangely*.

"Old Man!" I scream. "You want a story? I'll give you a climax!"

I stab the pen into the top of the letter *d*.

I don't write a word. I write a symbol.

A jagged line representing a tear.

Then, I kick the bird. "Fly! Get to the other side!"

The pigeon launches itself into the air, banking hard toward the valley of the gutter between the two open pages.

I jump.

I land on the symbol I carved. The tear in the paper. I pry at the edge of the paper fiber. The tension in the *d* snaps. The letter rips.

The sound is like a gunshot.

The entire sentence structure recoils. The *d* was holding the grammatical weight of the subject. Without it, the syntax collapses.

*The lighthous...*

The page buckles. The massive sheet of paper, loosened from the binding by the tear, curls upward.

The Old Man gasps. He drops his quill. It rolls off the table, shattering on the floor.

The page is flipping.

The wind is a hurricane. I am airborne, clinging to the torn edge of the letter *d* as the page sweeps upward, a white sky descending to crush the previous text.

We ride the wave of paper. The pigeon is swept up in the draft, tumbling end over end.

We are flying over the landscape of the book as it turns. We see the margins rushing past. We see the drop caps like city gates.

Then, gravity slams us down.

*Thud.*

We land on a new surface.

It is smoother. Harder. The smell is different.

Not ink. Not paper.

Varnish. Dust.

I lie still, waiting for the world to stop spinning.

"Keeper," the pigeon whispers. "Look at the light."

I open my eyes.

I am not on a page. I am lying on a broad, flat surface. The texture is wood grain, magnified into rolling hills of mahogany and oak.

Above me, the sky is not blue or grey. It is a tapestry of interconnected circuits. Green soldering points like stars. Gold traces like constellations.

I sit up.

I am on a circuit board. I am inside a machine.

"Where are we?" the pigeon asks, stepping carefully over a microchip that rises like a ziggurat from the board.

I look at the towering structures around us. Capacitors standing like silos. Resistors like huts.

I recognize this place. I saw it once, through a window that wasn't a window, in a dream of a draft.

"We are in the mechanism," I say. "The computer that runs the word processor."

"No," the pigeon says, pecking at a silver cylinder on the board. "Look."

There is a label on the cylinder. Etched in microscopic letters.

*Property of the Archivist. Storage Unit 7.*

"It's not a computer," I say, horror dawning. "It's a watch. A pocket watch."

"Whose watch?" the pigeon asks.

I look up. Above the circuitry, there is a crystal lens. A glass face the size of a stadium.

Beyond the glass, I see a room. A library. A fire.

And a hand. A human hand, lifting the watch.

"Same hand as before?" the pigeon asks.

"No," I say. "This hand is young. It has a tattoo on the wrist."

I squint against the glare of the crystal.

The tattoo is a circle. A circle of light.

"It's the Collector," I whisper. "But young. He hasn't bought the book yet."

The watch is being wound.

A massive key is inserted into the side of our world. It turns.

*Click. Click. Click.*

The gears grind. The circuit board vibrates.

"He's winding time backwards," I say.

"We're going back to the bookstore?"

"No," I say, staring at the mainspring coiling like a dragon of steel above us. "He's winding us to the moment he found us."

I look down at my feet.

Between the wooden floorboards of the circuit board, I see a piece of paper.

It is the receipt.

But the date has changed.

*Item 1: The Lighthouse Keeper's Letters.*
*Item 2: One (1) Soul.*
*Item 3: The Key to the Safe Deposit Box.*

The Safe Deposit Box.

My blood runs cold
