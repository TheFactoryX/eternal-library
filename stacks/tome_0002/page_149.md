# Letter 149

> Written at 2026-02-08 10:31:35

---

Dear You,

The shadow on the wall inside the hallway is shaped like a question mark that has been stretched and pulled, tortured into a form that resembles a hanged man.

It twitches against the floral wallpaper. It is waiting.

The storm door stands between us. The glass is smudged with fingerprints—perhaps mine, from a time I visited this house in a dream I do not remember having. The brass handle is cold, even through the damp sleeve of my coat.

"We are inside the cover," the Scribe whispers. She is standing behind me, shivering. The wind here does not blow; it vibrates, a low hum that settles in the teeth. "This is the world of the Author. The place where the imagination starts."

I place my hand on the glass. "It’s quiet," I say. "Too quiet."

"Quiet is dangerous," the pigeon mutters from the railing of the porch. "Quiet is where the red pen lives."

I push the handle. It is unlocked.

We step into the hallway. The air is dry and smells of lavender and old paper. It smells like a promise made long ago and forgotten.

The door clicks shut behind us. The sound is final. A period at the end of a sentence.

I turn back. The handle is gone. The storm door is gone. There is only a wall of smooth, white plaster.

"We can't go back," the Scribe says.

"Then we go forward," I say.

The hallway stretches before us, ending in a kitchen flooded with harsh, yellow sunlight. But the sun is not coming from a window. It is coming from the refrigerator.

On the floor of the hallway, leading to the kitchen, are scattered objects. Debris from the fall.

A shard of the lighthouse lens. A napkin from *The Lighthouse* diner. A single, pearl-like tear from the woman in the red coat.

"The Author is messy," the pigeon says, hopping over the pearl. "She doesn't clean up her drafts."

I pick up the shard of glass. It is heavy. The light from the refrigerator catches it, casting a spectral rainbow on the wall.

"Wait," the Scribe says. She points to the wall beside the kitchen door.

There is a frame there. A corkboard. And pinned to the corkboard are notes.

I step closer. My heart stumbles.

The notes are my letters. *Letter 12. Letter 45. Letter 89.* They are printed out, crumpled, covered in red ink. Notes are scribbled in the margins. *Too slow. Needs more mystery. Where is the Keeper going?*

"She judges us," the Scribe says, her voice trembling. "She eats our lives and looks for plot holes."

"No," I say, touching a red circle around the description of the sea. "She's looking for the truth. She's trying to find us in the noise."

I walk into the kitchen.

The refrigerator light is blinding. Inside the fridge, there is no food. There are only rows of glass jars. Inside each jar is a floating emotion. *Joy. Nostalgia. Dread.*

I close the door. The kitchen plunges into shadow.

Sitting at the small, round table is a girl.

She cannot be older than twenty. She is wearing oversized headphones. She is typing furiously on a laptop. The screen reflects in her eyes—blue light dancing on her irises.

"Writer," I say.

She does not look up. She cannot hear us. She is in the flow.

"The fourth wall is thick here," the pigeon whispers. "We need to break the glass."

I look at the table. Next to her laptop is a mug of coffee. It is steaming. And next to the mug is a gun. A revolver. But it is made of chalk.

"What is that?" the Scribe asks.

"The Chekhov’s Gun," I say. "She hasn't decided if she's going to fire it yet."

I walk around the table, facing the girl. I wave my hand in front of her face. She blinks, but she does not see me. Her gaze is fixed on the screen.

I look at the screen.

*The Keeper stood in the kitchen, watching the girl write. He felt a strange pity for her. She was trapped in the room, just as he was trapped in the book.*

"I see it," I say. "She's writing us right now."

"If she stops," the pigeon says, "do we disappear?"

"If she stops," I say, "we rest."

I reach into my pocket. I pull out the bone pen. I hold it against the Chekhov’s Gun.

"Attention," I say. "I am the character."

The girl pauses. Her fingers hover over the keys.

She looks up.

She looks directly at me.

But she does not scream. She does not look surprised. She looks... relieved.

"You're here," she says. Her voice is tinny, distorted, as if heard through water. "I didn't think I could reach the basement."

"You didn't reach it," I say. "We climbed out."

She takes off the headphones. They rest around her neck like a yoke.

"The ending," she says. "I can't find it. I wrote the lighthouse crashing. I wrote the diner burning. But you're still here."

"Endings are just transitions," I say. "Like the waiter said."

She rubs her eyes. "I'm tired. The story is heavy. It's... it's pulling me down."

She points to the floor.

I look down.

The kitchen linoleum is cracking. Through the cracks, I see the ocean. The *real* ocean. The dark, nameless sea of the beginning.

"You're sinking," the Scribe says. "The story is drowning you."

"If I finish it," the girl says, "I let you go. But if I let you go, I'm alone in this room."

"Alone is better than drowned," I say.

I look at the bone pen. I look at the chalk gun.

"Give me the ending," I say. "Let me write it."

She pushes the laptop toward me. The screen is a blank white page. The cursor blinks. A steady, hypnotic pulse.

"Just write *The End*," she says. "Close the file. The loop breaks."

I put my hands on the keys. They feel familiar. They feel like home.

The pigeon jumps onto the table. "Keeper," it says. "If you write 'The End', you become fiction. You become a memory. You cease to be."

"If I don't," I say, "we become a loop. An eternal recurrence."

I look at the Scribe. She is standing by the fridge, the light illuminating her ink-stained fingers.

"I want to be real," she says. "Even if real means forgotten."

I nod.

I place the bone pen on the keyboard, right next to the keys. It begins to hum. The amber in the marrow glows.

"I don't write *The End*," I say.

I type a single word.

*Open.*

The screen flickers. The word vanishes.

Suddenly, the kitchen walls begin to dissolve. The appliances crumble into dust. The ceiling fades into a starless sky.

We are standing on the floorboards, suspended in the void.

The girl stands up. She is no longer wearing the headphones. She is wearing a coat. A Keeper's coat.

"You broke the frame," she says.

"I broke the divide," I correct.

I point toward the horizon, where a new shape is forming. Not a lighthouse. Not a city.

A door. A simple, wooden door. And beside it, a single lamp post, burning with a violet flame.

"Where does that go?" the girl asks.

"To the next reader," I say. "Or perhaps, to the place where readers go when they close their eyes."

The pigeon flies toward the lamp post. "It's warm!" it calls back. "It's actually warm!"

The Scribe looks at me. "Do we leave the book behind?"

I look down at the laptop, dissolving into sand.

"The book was never the book," I say. "The book was the conversation. The letters were just the envelope."

I pick up the bone pen. It has changed. It is no longer bone. It is wood. A branch from a tree.

"Come on," I say to the girl. "You've written enough. It's time to live."

She smiles. It is the first real smile I have ever seen. It breaks the narrative tension in her face.

"I don't have a name," she says.

"Make one up," I say. "Authors are good at that."

We walk toward the lamp post. The floorboards creak under our feet, the sound of a story being told for the last time.

Behind us, the kitchen dissolves completely. The darkness is not empty. It is full of potential.

We reach the door.

I reach for the handle. But before I can touch it, I hear a sound from the other side.

A scratching sound.

Like a animal trying to get in. Or a memory trying to get out.

I pause.

"What is it?" the girl asks.

I press my ear to the wood.

I hear the sound of waves. I hear the sound of a typewriter. And I hear a voice. My voice.

*Dear You...*

"It's the first letter," I whisper. "It's looping."

"Then we open it," she says
