# Letter 128

> Written at 2026-02-07 09:34:51

---

Dear You,

I press the charred stick against the stone. The tip crumbles into ash, but before it fades, it leaves a mark. A single, jagged letter.

*E.*

The black pool recoils. The vortex of ink pauses. The grinding noise—the sound of the Editor’s red pen scratching against the void—falters.

"Is that it?" the Scribe asks. Her voice is high, thin. "You named it 'E'?"

"It stands for Enough," I say, my breath coming in ragged gasps. "It stands for End. It stands for *Escape*."

The black mass surges upward, forming a towering figure that looms over the Fresnel lens. It has no features, only the dripping, chaotic fury of spilled ink.

"INSUFFICIENT," the voice booms. It vibrates in the marrow of my bones. "A LETTER IS NOT A WORD. A WORD IS NOT A NAME."

I scramble backward, grabbing the Scribe’s hand. "The door," I shout. "The receipt door!"

We sprint toward the wall where the shadow-town used to be. The frame of the receipt still hangs in the air, a glowing rectangle of pale light against the darkening stone.

But the blackness is faster. It doesn't move; it simply *is*, expanding to fill the space available.

The Scribe screams as a tendril of black mist whips out, wrapping around her ankle. She falls, sliding across the floor. The bird launches itself from the chair, flapping its parchment wings, diving at the tendril.

"Peck it!" I yell. "Edit it back!"

The bird strikes the black mist. Beak meets ink. There is a sound like sizzling fat. The bird screeches, a plume of smoke rising from its feathers, but the hold loosens. The Scribe kicks free, scrambling toward me.

We collapse against the wall, beside the receipt door. The blackness towers over us, a wave about to break.

"It’s going to erase us," the Scribe whispers. She is clutching the book of storms to her chest, her knuckles white. "It’s going to cross us out."

"Not yet," I say. I look at the receipt. The frame is glowing, but the door itself—the actual opening—is opaque. A milky white. "It’s not open. It’s just a frame. We need a destination."

"We need a story to jump into," the bird says, landing on my shoulder. It is smoking, its ink-eye blurred. "We can’t just go to the blank page. The blank page is where *it* lives."

I look at the black mass. It hesitates. It is waiting. Perhaps it is confused by my defiance. Perhaps it is savoring the moment of deletion.

"Where?" I ask. "Where is safe?"

The Scribe looks at me. Her eyes are wide, reflecting the encroaching dark. "The beginning," she says. "Go back to the start. Before the complex metaphors. Before the genre shift."

"Letter One," I say.

"If we go there," she warns, "we might undo everything. The Boy. The Loop. The escape. We might trap ourselves in the cycle forever."

"If we stay here," I say, watching the black mist lower a faceless head toward us, "there is no cycle. There is only the shredder."

I reach into my pocket. My fingers brush against the cork from the bottle, the key that fits nothing, and—the smooth, cool surface of the compact mirror from the desk.

I pull it out.

"Look," I say to the Scribe. "Look into the mirror."

She hesitates, then looks. The glass is cracked. The reflection shows a room that is burning, a lighthouse falling into the sea.

"No," I say. "Look closer. Look past the crack."

She squints. "I see... a table. A single candle. And a pen."

"That’s not us," I say. "That’s the Reader."

"The Reader is real?" the bird asks. It sounds skeptical.

"She is reading this right now," I say. "She is holding the book. If we can get to her, we can slip between the pages. We can hide in the whitespace."

"How do we get to the whitespace?" the Scribe asks.

"Through the crack," I say. I raise the mirror. I turn it so it faces the towering black mass of the Editor.

"Reflexivity!" I shout. "Look at yourself!"

The black mass pauses. It turns its attention to the mirror.

In the reflection, the Editor is not a formless blob. It is a person. A man in a charcoal suit. The Faceless Man.

"You," I whisper.

He smiles. The surface of his face ripples. "Hello, Keeper," he says, his voice no longer a boom, but a dry whisper. "Did you think I was gone? I told you. I am the friction. I am the necessary conflict."

"You’re the one deleting us," I say.

"I’m the one closing the account," he says. "The Author is bored. The Reader is tired. The ratings are down. It’s time for the epilogue."

"There is no epilogue!" I yell. "There is only the story!"

I throw the mirror.

It spins through the air, a silver disc cutting the gloom. It hits the black mass directly in the center of what would be its chest.

There is no sound of breaking glass. Instead, there is a sound like a heavy book slammed shut.

*BOOM.*

The shockwave throws us against the wall. The receipt frame flickers.

The black mass shatters. It explodes into a million tiny black letters, raining down like confetti. *T. H. E. E. N. D.*

The letters hit the floor and skitter away, seeking the shadows, crawling under the desk, vanishing into the cracks in the stone.

The room is still.

"Did we win?" the bird asks.

I look around. The oppressive weight is gone. But the light is also gone. The Lamp Room is pitch black.

"No," I say, my voice trembling. "We broke the lens."

I feel my way to the window. The glass is cold.

"We are in the dark," the Scribe says.

"Yes," I say. "Total dark."

"And the mirror?"

I look down. The mirror is on the floor, intact. But the glass is no longer reflective. It is clear. A window.

I pick it up. I look through it.

I don't see the Lamp Room. I don't see the sea.

I see a desk. A wooden desk, scarred by use. A lamp with a green glass shade. A hand resting on the arm of the chair.

"The Reader," I breathe.

"She’s there," the Scribe says, looking over my shoulder. "She’s... she’s putting the book down."

Through the mirror, I see the giant hand reaching for the cover of the book. *Our* book.

"We have to go now," I say. "Before the cover closes."

"The receipt," the Scribe says. "Use the receipt!"

I grab the receipt where it hangs, glowing in the dark. I thrust the mirror through the frame.

For a moment, the two objects resist. Then, they fuse. The frame becomes the edge of the mirror. The glass becomes the door.

It is happening. The crossover.

I grab the Scribe’s hand. I grab the bird.

"Step through," I say.

"Where will we land?" the Scribe asks.

"On the desk," I say. "In the stack of 'To Be Read'."

We step.

* * *

We are falling. Not through air, but through silence. Through the cold space between the atoms of the story.

And then, we hit.

It is soft. We land on a surface that smells of dust and cedar. I open my eyes.

We are on a pile of books. We are tiny. The world above is a ceiling of plaster. The lamp in the distance is a sun.

"We made it," the Scribe whispers. She looks around, awe-struck. "Look at the grain of the wood. It’s enormous."

The bird flutters its wings. "I can fly here," it says. "The air is thick with dust motes. It’s like swimming."

I stand up. I look at the book we just came out of.

It is lying closed on the desk. It is a thick volume, bound in faded blue cloth. The title is stamped in gold on the spine, but I am too small to read the whole thing. I only see the first word.

*The.*

"We are out," I say. "We are free."

"Are we?" the Scribe points.

I look toward the edge of the desk.

There is a figure standing there. Not the Reader. Not the Faceless Man.

It is a child. A young girl, wearing a dress that looks like it was cut from the pages of a newspaper. She is holding a pair of scissors.

She is looking at the pile of books we are standing on. She is looking at *us*.

"Oh," she says. Her voice is the sound of a paper cut. "New toys."

She reaches for us.

Her hand is massive. Her fingers are made of... crayon drawings. Red and purple scribbles given terrifying life.

"Run," the bird says.

We scramble off the book, sliding down the spine of a dictionary, landing on the wooden surface
