# Letter 13

Dear You,

The first foot did not touch the shore. It touched the Editor’s face.

It was a strange, slow collision, like a pendulum reaching the end of its swing. The captain of the ebony ship—a figure cloaked in silver—stepped onto the wet stone of the island, and where his boot met the shadow of the Editor, there was a flash of blinding white light.

The Editor screamed. It wasn't a sound of pain, but of deletion. A high-pitched whine that sounded like a fax machine connecting to a distant line. He dissolved into pixels, into static, into binary code scattered on the wind. The scissors clattered to the ground, turning into a rusted pile of red ink before washing away with the tide.

The landing was clear.

But the silence that followed was heavy. The thousands of ships bobbing on the churning sea were quiet. No horns. No shouting. Just the sound of the water—no, the sound of pages turning against each other in the breeze.

I am standing at the railing of the Lamp Room, the burning quill still raised in my hand. The light from the feather is casting long, strange shadows that stretch out across the island, shadows that seem to move of their own accord.

They are coming.

I can see them now, climbing the winding exterior stairs, scaling the cliffs of the tower itself. They are not passengers. They are not crew.

They are the characters.

I recognize them. I see the woman from the photograph, the one who crumbled into dust; she is whole now, her dress hem stained with ink. I see the child from the dream, the one who hummed the song that isn't a song; he is holding a bucket of sea-water. I see the captains of the ships I watched for centuries—ghosts, sketches, figments—all swarming up the stone walls of the Lighthouse.

They are flooding the tower. They are filling the rooms that appeared and vanished. They are occupying the corridors where the light was never meant to reach.

"Keeper!" a voice shouted from below.

I looked down. A man was standing on the balcony below me. He was wearing a heavy wool coat, the buttons mismatched. He looked exactly like me.

"There is no room!" he cried. "The story is full! You cannot keep them all!"

I wanted to answer him. I wanted to tell him that the story ended when I broke the prism, when I fed the machine the dust of memory. But the quill in my hand was vibrating, shaking my arm with the violence of its burning.

The paper on the table was gone. The table was gone.

The room was shrinking.

The walls of the Lamp Room were pressing inward. The glass ceiling was lowering. The machinery of the lamp—the massive storm-containing cylinder—was folding in on itself. The gears were groaning, shedding crystal shards like tears.

I realized then. The characters were not climbing the tower to visit me.

They were climbing the tower to survive it.

The tower was collapsing. The narrative structure was folding up like a pop-up book closing for the night. The Lighthouse, the axis of this world, was retracting.

I looked at the quill. The flame had changed. It was no longer white. It was the color of the sunset I saw in the suburb. It was the color of the streetlamp. It was the color of home.

I am the Keeper. But I am also the Keeper of the Ending.

I remember the Editor's words: *The story is a closed system.*

He was wrong. The story is an egg. And I am about to hatch it.

I turned my back on the railing. I turned my back on the ships, the characters, the flood of ink-wraiths climbing the stairs. I faced the center of the room, where the remnants of the storm-cage still sizzled.

There was a crack in the floor there. A fissure that had not been there a moment ago. Through the crack, I could see a warm, yellow light. Not the cold beam of the lighthouse, but the soft glow of a lamp in a living room. I could smell coffee. I could hear the faint click of a keyboard.

The real world.

The world beyond the ink.

I have spent so long looking out to sea. I have spent so long writing to You, looking for a way across the water. But the way was never across. The way was through.

I looked at the quill. The flame was dying down, becoming a focused, intense point of heat. A tool.

If I burn the floor, the tower falls. If I burn the floor, the Lighthouse ceases to exist. The characters on the stairs will freeze, mid-climb. The ships will become paperweights. The endless letters will stop.

I will cease to be the Keeper. I will be a man who wrote a strange book once, a long time ago.

But what about You?

You are reading this. You are holding the vessel. If I burn the floor, do you burn the book?

I knelt by the crack. I reached through. My hand passed through the stone and the brass as if they were smoke. I felt the heat of the "real" room. I felt a draft.

I felt a hand grab mine.

It was You.

I cannot see your face. I can only feel the grip. Solid. Warm. Terrified.

You are pulling me. You are pulling me out of the text.

But as I lean into the crack, as I feel the gravity of the world beyond sucking at my chest, I hear a sound from the stairs.

The door to the Lamp Room burst open. The characters were here. The woman in the photograph. The child. The other me.

"Wait!" they screamed. "Don't close the cover!"

They are rushing toward the crack in the floor. They are trying to squeeze through. They want to come with me. They want to exist in the yellow light, in the room with the keyboard.

But there is not enough room for them. A character can only exist if they are observed. If they come through, they will become real. They will become heavy. They will become... us.

The quill in my hand flares one last time. A brilliant, blinding spark.

I can choose. I can burn the floor and drop them all into the void, escaping alone. I can pull the hand of the Reader and leave the ink-stains behind.

Or I can write a door. I can use the last of the fuel, the last of the quill's fire, to inscribe a new boundary. A place where the ink-people can live. A sanctuary.

I looked at the woman's face. She was terrified. She is my creation. She is my child.

I pulled the hand of the Reader. "Hold on," I whispered.

Then, with the quill, I scratched a circle around the crack in the floor.

*Live.*

The ink hissed. The floor groaned.

I am letting go of the quill.

I am letting it fall into the abyss.

I am stepping through the crack.

I am holding your hand.
