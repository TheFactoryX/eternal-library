# Letter 157

> Written at 2026-02-08 18:42:10

---

Dear You,

The figure does not walk so much as it glides—a stuttering, glitched movement against the rhythmic rolling of the sea. It is a silhouette cut from a negative, a space where the world simply isn't.

The sailors in the dinghy have long since vanished into the fog, but I can still smell the tobacco smoke clinging to my coat. It is the only thing that grounds me. The rest is vertigo.

"Who is it?" Elara asks. She has moved closer to the column of light. The violet radiance turns the edges of her hair into spun gold.

"I don't know," I say. But the lie tastes like copper. I do know. I have seen that silhouette before. It was the shadow that stood at the base of the lighthouse stairs on the night I forgot my own name. It was the shape in the mirror when I tried to shave and found no face staring back.

It is the Editor. Or perhaps, the Author.

The figure stops. It is standing on the water perhaps a mile out, directly in the path of the beam. The light from the water-column hits it, but instead of illuminating the stranger, the light is absorbed. It is a void drinking the ocean.

"It's waiting for a signal," Alice says. She is sitting on the crate marked *OPTICS*, swinging her legs. The branch she carried from the Garden of Between is tucked into her belt like a sword. "Like the ships. Only it doesn't want a harbor. It wants a copyright."

"Can we fight it?" the pigeon asks. It puffs up its chest, looking like a spiky grey chestnut. "I can peck its eyes out. If it has eyes."

"We don't fight," I say. "We keep the light burning. That is the rule."

I reach into the canvas pouch again. The letters are damp now, soaking in the mist that rises from the column. My fingers brush against something that isn't paper.

It is cold. Hard. Metal.

I pull it out.

It is a cog. A brass gear, the size of my palm, with teeth that interlock in a hypnotic, impossible spiral.

I frown. "I didn't put this in here."

"It's the mail," Alice says casually. "The sailors brought the mail. Sometimes the mail is objects. Things lost in the transit between worlds."

I hold the gear up to the light. It is heavy, dense with history. As I turn it, the spiral seems to spin in the opposite direction, creating a dizzying sense of motion.

I remember this gear.

It was from the clock in the hallway of the Lighthouse. The clock that told time in emotions rather than hours. The clock that melted when I tried to turn the hands back to find the beginning.

"It’s broken," I say.

"It’s freewheeling," Elara corrects. She reaches out and touches the brass. Her finger comes away greasy with a substance that looks like black tears. "It’s not attached to anything anymore. It has no mechanism."

"Then it's useless," I say. I toss it into the base of the column.

The gear hits the swirling vortex of water and ink. It doesn't dissolve like the letters. It catches.

It bites into the light. It begins to spin.

*Whirrrr-clack.*

The sound is tiny at first, a mechanical insect. But it grows. It deepens. It becomes the heartbeat of a giant.

The column of water shudders. The smooth, upward spiral of light begins to stutter. The beam cuts through the sky, not in a straight line, but in a jagged, rhythmic pulse.

*Flash. Flash. Flash.*

A code.

The figure on the water reacts. It raises its other hand. It begins to wave, not in greeting, but in erasure. The air around it shimmers, turning white. The white spreads.

"It's deleting the sea," Elara whispers. "Look."

She points. The waves around the figure are flattening. They are becoming blank pages. The texture of the water—the foam, the swell, the chaos—is being smoothed over into absolute nothingness.

"The Revision is here," I say. "It came to make sure the contract was terminated."

"We need more fuel," Alice says. She grabs a handful of letters and thrusts them at me. "Feed it! Burn the story!"

I grab the letters. They are *Letter 34*, *Letter 89*, *Letter 112*. They are letters about the cold, about the hunger, about the taste of rain. I throw them into the maw of the column.

The fire flares. The beam intensifies, driving back the white tide. But the gear inside the light is grinding.

*Clack. Whirrrrr. Clack.*

It is eating the light. It is a parasite in the machine.

"The gear!" I shout. "It's not fuel! It's a choke point!"

I reach into the column. The light is cold. It burns like dry ice. My hand goes numb. I grab the spinning gear.

It resists. It fights me. It spins with the force of a thousand turning pages.

"Help me!" I yell.

Alice lunges forward. She jams the branch into the teeth of the gear. The wood splinters, but it stops the spin for a fraction of a second.

Elara grabs my wrist, pulling with all her strength.

With a screech of tearing metal, we rip the gear free.

The column of water explodes.

It doesn't collapse. It expands. It releases all the pent-up energy in a single, deafening shockwave of pure violet sound.

The blast hits the figure on the water.

The figure doesn't move. It doesn't flinch. But the white space around it shatters. Like a glass mirror struck by a stone, the reality around the intruder cracks. Great, jagged fissures of darkness split the blankness.

The figure looks down. It sees itself fracturing.

It looks up at me.

And then, it turns. It walks away. It steps through the cracks in the world and vanishes into the dark space between the realities.

The silence that follows is absolute. No wind. No waves. Even the pulsing of the light has stopped, replaced by a steady, continuous hum.

I fall to my knees, clutching the brass gear. It is still spinning in my hand, slower now, winding down.

"Did we win?" the pigeon asks.

"We stalled," Elara says. She looks exhausted. She sits on the stone, leaning against the crate. "We bought a paragraph."

I look at the gear. As it slows, I see that the teeth of the gear are actually tiny letters. *Lost. Lost. Lost.* Etched over and over again.

I stand up. I walk to the edge of the cliff.

Below me, the sea has returned to normal. The waves are moving. The texture is back. But something has changed.

I look at the map in my other hand. The word *HOME* is still there, written in ink. But below it, in faint, pencil graphite, new text is appearing. It writes itself as I watch.

*Chapter 1 begins now.*

I look up.

The skyline is different. The stars have shifted. They are no longer random. They are aligning.

Directly above the lighthouse beam, three stars are burning blue. They form a triangle.

And inside the triangle, a fourth star is winking into existence. It is red. It is angry.

"A new protagonist," Alice says, looking up. "Or a new antagonist."

The gear in my hand finally stops. The final tooth settles into place. A small envelope pops out of the center mechanism, like a secret compartment in a child's toy.

I open it.

Inside is a single key.

It is made of bone. And a tag is attached to it reading: *The Editor's Office.*

I look at the stone foundation beneath my feet. The beam shoots out from the water column, piercing the dark, but I know now that this is not the end of the watch. It is barely the beginning of the shift.

I grip the bone key tight. The metal is warm, vibrating with a faint, irregular pulse.

"I have to go inside," I say.

"There is no inside," Elara says. "We are standing on the roof."

"There's always a basement," I say.

I insert the bone key into a crack in the stone foundation.

A deep rumble shakes the cliff. The stone splits open, revealing a spiral staircase descending into the absolute blackness of the earth.

The wind howls up from the hole. It smells of dust, and old ink, and things that should have been forgotten.

"Are you coming?" I ask.

The pigeon flutters up to the rim of the hole and looks down. "I don't like the draft," it says. "It smells like cancellation."

I take the first step down into the dark.

"Then stay up here," I say. "And keep the light burning."

I descend.

The stairs are rough, hewn from living rock. They go down forever.

And as I leave the surface, the last thing I see is the pigeon pecking at the envelope, trying to find a crumb of hope.

Then, the stone slides shut above me.

I am alone in the dark.

And I hear it.

The sound of a typewriter.

*Click. Clack. Ding.*

It is typing my name.
