# Letter 152

> Written at 2026-02-08 13:51:43

---

Dear You,

We sit. We eat. The soup is thin, broth the color of sunset, with vegetables that float like islands in a golden sea. The bread is warm, breaking apart with a soft, whispering crunch. The fire pops, sending sparks up the chimney that sound like punctuation marks striking the floor.

The woman sits at the head of the table. She is not young, she is not old. She is ageless, her face a map of every story she has ever visited. Her eyes are the most striking thing about her—because they have no color. They are white, like the pages of an unmarked book.

She does not eat. She watches. She listens.

"Are you the Reader?" Alice asks. She is using a spoon, her movements careful, as if she is afraid of spilling a drop of fiction on the table.

"I am a Reader," the woman replies. Her voice is dry, like leaves skittering over stone. "Just as you are a Scribe. We are all things, in the margins. Tonight, I am the Host."

"You invited us in," I say. "Why?"

"Because you knocked," she says simply. "And because the window is closing."

I look around the room. It is cozy, lined with books, but the shelves are shifting. The books are whispering to each other, a low, susurrus sound that makes the hair on my arms stand up.

"The window between what and what?" Elara asks. She has finished her soup. She looks healthier, the color returning to her cheeks. The heavy coat is hanging on the rack by the door, dripping dry onto the mat.

"Between the Word and the World," the woman says. She reaches for a bell on the table. It is a silver bell, shaped like a lighthouse. She rings it. *Clang.*

The sound is not a bell. It is a gong. It echoes, not in the room, but in my chest.

I push my bowl away. "I’m not hungry for food," I say. "I am hungry for answers."

The woman smiles. It is a terrifying smile. It is the smile of someone who knows how the story ends.

"There are no answers," she says. "There are only consequences."

She stands up. She walks to the window. The curtains are drawn. She pulls them back.

I expect to see the night. The cliff. The ocean.

Instead, I see a wall of white. Not a wall of fog. A wall of nothing. Absolute, blinding blankness.

"The page is turning," she says.

She points to the map, which I have placed on the table. The ink is running. The land is bleeding into the sea. The X mark that marked our location is fading, smearing like a tear.

"The story of the Lighthouse is over," the woman says. "The Keeper has left his post. The light is out. The Editor has moved on to a manuscript about a detective in a city of rain. This world," she gestures to the room, to us, "is losing its substrate."

"So we disappear?" Alice asks. Her voice is small. The pigeon, perched on the back of her chair, buries its head in its wing.

"No," the woman says. "You integrate. You become background noise. You become the texture of the paper."

"I won't," I say. I stand up. My chair scrapes loudly against the floorboards. "I have come too far. I crossed the sea. I fought the Editor. I left the tower. I did not come all this way to become ink on a page that is about to be closed."

"It is inevitable," the woman says. "Entropy is the only critic who never sleeps."

I look at Elara. She is looking at the white void outside the window with a strange expression. Fear, yes. But also fascination.

"We can change the ending," I say. "We are here. We are conscious. We can write the next sentence."

The woman turns from the window. Her white eyes seem to pierce me.

"With what?" she asks. "Your pen is wood. Your key is melted. You have no ink."

I look at my hands. They are empty.

But then I feel it. A weight in my pocket. Not the key. Not the pen.

I reach in and pull it out.

It is a piece of glass. A shard of the lens from the lantern room. I must have pocketed it when the tower fell. It is jagged, cutting into my palm.

I hold it up. The firelight refracts through it, casting a dancing spectrum on the table. Red. Orange. Violet.

"The light," I say. "I still have the light."

"A prism," the woman says. "It bends light. It does not create it."

"It creates the rainbow," I say. "It creates the color."

I walk to the fireplace. The flames are dying down, settling into glowing red coals.

"If the page is turning," I say, "then we need to give the next reader something to look at."

I hold the shard of glass over the fire. I angle it. I catch the last bright flame within the facet.

"Elara," I say. "Describe the fire."

She looks up, startled. "It's... red. And orange. It's warm."

"No," I say. "Make it real. Give it a history."

She hesitates. Then she leans in. "It’s the fire from the library of Alexandria," she says. "It’s the spark that Prometheus stole. It’s the burning of the witches and the lighting of the beacons."

As she speaks, the flame in the glass shard changes. It swells. It turns blue. It turns violet.

I turn the shard toward the white void outside the window.

I project the light.

The beam hits the whiteness. And where it hits, the white burns away. It turns into color.

"I need more," I say. "Alice! The pigeon! Give me memories!"

Alice stands. She grabs a book from the shelf and throws it into the fire. The smoke rises, smelling of old vanilla and dust.

"The smell of the Archive," she cries out. "The taste of the ink!"

The light in the shard thickens. It becomes a tangible beam, cutting through the room, out the window.

The pigeon flaps its wings. "The wind!" it caws. "The feeling of the draft under the door!"

The beam turns green, then a chaotic storm of grey.

"Elara!" I shout, my arm trembling with the effort of holding the beam steady. "Who are we?"

She looks at the woman. The woman is nodding, a slow, deliberate movement.

"We are the story that refused to be quiet," Elara shouts. "We are the ink that stained the fingers!"

I swing the beam around the room. It hits the walls, the floor, the ceiling. Where it touches, the room dissolves into liquid light.

The woman laughs. It is not a dry laugh now. It is a sound like rain.

"Go," she says. "Before the page cuts you off."

She pushes me. Not with her hands, but with her will.

I point the shard at the floor beneath our feet. The floorboards vanish. We fall.

Not into darkness. Into the beam.

We are sliding down a tunnel of pure, refracted color. The sound of the station house is gone. The sound of the sea is gone. There is only the hum of the light.

We are falling *out* of the letter.

We are falling *into* the margin.

I see the ground rushing up. It is not stone. It is not paper.

It is grass. Real grass. Wild and overgrown.

I hit the ground. The impact jars my teeth.

The beam of light from the shard shoots up into the sky, piercing the clouds like a spear. The sky above is no longer white. It is a deep, bruised purple.

I lie on my back, gasping. The shard is still in my hand, glowing faintly.

Elara lands beside me with a soft *thump*. Alice tumbles down a moment later, landing in a bush. The pigeon spirals down, landing on my chest.

"Did we make it?" the pigeon asks. "Are we published?"

I sit up. I look around.

We are in a garden. The plants are strange—ferns that unfurl like scrolls, flowers with petals shaped like hearts. In the distance, I see a skyline. But it is not a city of skyscrapers.

It is a city of spires. Stone towers. Thousands of them.

And beyond the city, the sea.

But it is not the black sea of the beginning. It is a sea of glass.

"The Lighthouse," I whisper.

Every single building in that city is a lighthouse.

"You didn't escape the story," a voice says.

I turn. The woman is standing there. She is wearing gardening gloves. She is holding a trowel.

"You changed the setting," she says. She digs a hole in the earth and plants a seed. "The Lighthouse was never a place. It was a verb. It was the act of looking out."

"Who are you?" I ask again.

"I told you," she says. "I am the Reader. And you are the characters I dreamed about while I was awake."

She pats the earth down over the seed.

"This is the Garden of Between," she says. "The place where stories go when they are paused."

She stands up
