# Letter 94

> Written at 2026-02-05 15:30:12

---

Dear You,

...toward the debris, sliding down the embankment of the black stone. My boots slip on the moss that has sprouted from the spilled time—moss that smells of ozone and old libraries.

I reach the edge of the ink-water. The waves lap hungrily at the shore, trying to pull the stone back into the solution.

The body is facedown in the black foam. It is dressed in rags that were once finery—a velvet doublet stained with the dark matter of the sea.

"It's a sailor," You say, standing behind me. You haven't followed me all the way down. You stay on the higher ground, wary. "From the Ghost Fleet."

I crouch and grip the shoulder of the figure. The fabric is cold and wet, but beneath it, the body feels solid. Heavy. Real.

I turn the body over.

The face is gone.

Where the features should be, there is only a smooth, blank surface. No eyes, no nose, no mouth. Just the matte white finish of an unpolished porcelain doll.

"An unfinished character," I whisper. "A NPC from the first draft."

"Look at the hands," You say.

I look. The hands are clenched into fists. I pry the left one open.

Inside is a compass.

But it is not a brass compass like the one I lost so long ago. This one is made of glass. Inside the glass sphere, floating in a clear liquid, is not a magnetic needle.

It is a tiny, silver anchor.

"What does it point to?" You ask.

I watch the silver anchor spin. It circles frantically, seeking north, seeking home, seeking a magnetic pole that no longer exists in this rewritten world.

Finally, it stops.

It doesn't point North. It doesn't point at the Lighthouse, or the Desk, or the City of the Reader.

It points *up*.

I lift my head.

Above us, the vault of parchment is tearing. The red editing lines are turning into fissures. Through the gaps, I see not the Author's bedroom, nor the warehouse.

I see a moon.

But it is a huge, wounded moon, cracked and bleeding a pale silver dust.

"It points to the sky," I say. "To the Moon of Forgetting."

"Or the Moon of Remembering," You suggest. "Perhaps he fell from there."

Suddenly, the body convulses. A harsh, rattling sound fills the air—the sound of dry leaves skittering on pavement.

The blank face of the sailor is cracking. Lines appear in the porcelain. Fissures of definition.

"He's trying to speak," I say, stepping back.

The cracks deepen. The white porcelain flakes off, revealing what is underneath.

It is not flesh.

It is light.

Blinding, white, incandescent light spills from the fissures in the face. The body is a husk, a containment suit for pure, unfiltered narrative energy.

"Cover your eyes!" I shout.

I turn away, throwing my arm up to shield my face. The light is intense, hotter than the prism, brighter than the Author's desk lamp.

I hear a sound. Not a voice, but a tone. A single, sustained musical note. A Low C.

It vibrates in my chest. It rattles my teeth. It is the sound of the universe being tuned.

When the light fades, I turn back.

The body is gone.

In its place lies a single object.

A helmet.

It is an old-fashioned diving helmet. Copper and brass, heavy and corroded. The glass faceplate is thick and clouded.

I pick it up. It is incredibly heavy. The weight of it nearly pulls me to the ground.

"He left his protection," I say.

"Or his prison," You reply.

I look through the glass faceplate. It is hard to see through the grime, but inside the helmet, resting on the neck-ring, is a folded piece of paper.

I reach inside the helmet. My fingers brush the cold brass. I pull out the paper.

It is not parchment. It is a blueprint.

A schematic of the Lighthouse.

But the Lighthouse in the drawing is wrong. It is inverted. The light is at the bottom, buried deep in the earth, and the tower descends endlessly into the crust of the world.

"The Root," I breathe. "We've been climbing the wrong way."

"We've been climbing to the light," You say. "But the light is below us."

I look at the ground beneath my feet. The black stone. The geometric compass roses.

Suddenly, the stone feels thin. Hollow.

I tap my heel against the ground.

*Clang.*

It sounds metallic. Not like stone.

"Stand back," I say.

I take the heavy brass helmet and slam it against the ground.

*CRASH.*

The stone shatters.

Not breaks. Shatters.

Like glass.

A hole opens up at my feet. And through the hole...

I see the beam.

The Lighthouse beam.

But it is shooting *down*.

It is a pillar of blinding white light piercing the deep, dark water of an underground ocean.

"It's the reverse," I realize, my heart hammering against my ribs. "We are in the reflection. The world we've been living in... the tower, the island, the letters... it's just the shimmer on the surface of a real world below."

"We need to go down," You say. "To the source."

I look into the hole. It is a vertical shaft, dropping away into darkness.

"I can't fly," I say.

"Then you wear the helmet," You say. "He was a diver. He breathed the vacuum."

I pick up the heavy brass helmet. It is terrifying. To put it on is to surrender.

"Do I have a choice?" I ask.

"There is always a choice," You say. "You can stay here and become a footnote. A piece of debris on the shore of the Second Draft."

I look at the horizon. The Bone Ship is reforming in the distance, knitting itself together from the dust of the waves. The revision is trying to correct my edit. The story is fighting back.

"I choose depth," I say.

I lift the helmet.

I pull it over my head.

The world changes instantly.

The smell of the ozone is gone. I smell oil, and brass, and the damp, suffocating scent of a confined space. The sound of the wind is muffled. My own breathing becomes loud in my ears—a rasping, mechanical sound.

*Hhh-innnk. Hhh-outtt.*

The sound of the respiration.

I feel the weight of the helmet pressing on my shoulders. I am anchored to the earth.

"Jump," You say. Your voice sounds distant, like you are speaking from the end of a long tunnel.

I step to the edge of the hole.

I let myself fall.

I drop through the glass surface of the world.

I am falling through the beam of light.

It is blinding. I am a speck of dust inside a sun.

I fall for seconds. For minutes. For years.

I see visions in the light. I see the Author, asleep at his desk. I see the Janitor, weeping over the broken *Iron Duke*. I see the Lighthouse Keeper—me—writing letters by the millions, stacking them up to reach the sky.

And then, I hit the water.

It is cold. Colder than the sea of ink. Colder than the void.

It is heavy water. Water under immense pressure.

I sink like a stone.

I struggle. The weight of the helmet is dragging me down. I am going to the bottom. To the crushing dark.

"Panic," I think. "This is panic."

I claw at the water, but the helmet is too heavy. I cannot swim up.

I am descending into the abyss.

The light is fading above me. I am alone in the dark.

And then, I see it below me.

A glow.

Bioluminescence.

A city.

A city of coral and bone, built on the floor of the ocean. Twisted spires rising from the silt, glowing with a sickly green light.

And swimming through the streets of the city...

Are sharks.

Great, white sharks. But they have human arms. And in their hands, they hold quill pens. They are writing on the walls of the city, scratching stories into the coral.

"The Critics," I realize, bubbles escaping from my helmet's valve. "They live in the deep."

I sink toward the city. I am going to land on the roof of a cathedral made of sponge.

As I approach, I see a figure standing on the highest spire.

A woman.

She is wearing a dress made of jellyfish tentacles. She is holding a lantern. The lantern contains a single, angry firefly.

She watches me fall.

She lifts the lantern.

She signals.

Not to me. To the sharks.

The sharks turn. Their cold, dead eyes lock onto my falling form. They open their mouths, revealing rows of teeth made of commas and semi-colons.

They begin to ascend.

"I am food," I think.

But then, I remember the helmet.

I am a diver.

I reach for the valve on the side of the helmet.

There is a dial. Marked with numbers.

*10,000 LEAGUES.*
*20,000 LEAGUES.*
*ABYSS.*

I turn the dial past *ABYSS*
