# Letter 138

> Written at 2026-02-07 19:27:52

---

Dear You,

The receipt burns in my hand, not with heat, but with the cold, terrifying clarity of a proposition that cannot be refused. *Bring the light to the shore.*

I am running, my boots hammering against the iron treads of the spiral staircase. The bird—it is heavy now, a real thing of bone and panic—flutters frantically above me, buffeting the air with wings that churn the dust of ages.

"The shore!" it caws, its voice stripped of the poetic cadence it once held. "Keeper, look!"

I crest the final step and stumble into the lamp room. The great Fresnel lens is dark, dead as the eye of a fish washed up on a beach. But I do not need the lens. I look out.

The world has changed again.

The "digital rain" of the Collector's draft has vanished. In its place is a sea of ink—vast, swelling, and moving with the viscosity of old oil. It churns against the base of the lighthouse, but the sound is not the crash of waves. It is the whisper of turning pages. *Rustle. Hush. Rustle.*

"He has opened the book flat," I realize. "We are lying on the desk. The ocean is the table."

"No," the bird says, landing on the brass railing. "Look deeper. Under the surface."

I press my face against the glass, ignoring the freezing bite of the condensation. Beneath the black waves, deep in the murky dark, shapes are moving. They are not fish. They are chairs. Tables. A bed.

"It is the Room," I say. "The Reader’s Room. It’s drowning in the story."

I remember the Scribe’s fading form. *I was just a footnote.* She is gone, dissolved back into the margins of the text, leaving me alone with the bird and the heavy, grieving silence of the tower.

I turn to the lamp mechanism. The oil reservoir is empty. The wick is charred.

"We have no fuel," the bird says.

"We don't need fuel," I say, reaching into my pocket. My fingers close around the obsidian shard. It hums, vibrating with the memory of the phone screen it shattered. "We have a conductor."

I scramble up the lattice work toward the center of the lens. The bulb is missing. In its place is a socket, a small, metal hole waiting for a spark.

"This will destroy the shard," the bird warns.

"It destroyed us first," I reply.

I jam the obsidian shard into the socket. It fits perfectly, locking into place with a sharp *click*.

"Now what?"

"Now," I say, pulling the flint striker from my belt, "we strike a match for the Collector."

I strike the flint against the iron railing. Sparks shower down, hissing as they hit the floor. One catches on the edge of my coat. I brush it off, directing the flame toward the shard.

"Light!" I command, focusing every ounce of my will, every memory of the lighthouse, every letter I ever wrote, into that single point.

The flint touches the obsidian.

There is no explosion. No fire. Instead, a sound—a pure, high-frequency whine that feels like a needle sliding into the base of my skull. The obsidian doesn't burn; it *glares*.

A beam of violet light explodes from the shard, passing through the Fresnel lens, which amplifies it a thousand times. The beam cuts through the gloom, a solid spear of violet stabbing outward into the night.

It hits the sea.

The ink doesn't boil. It *parts*.

Where the beam touches the black water, the liquid burns away, evaporating into steam that smells of ozone and vanilla. The beam continues, relentless, cutting a tunnel through the ocean of ink, driving down, down, down to the drowned room below.

"The shore!" the bird screams. "The shore is rising!"

I look. At the horizon, where the sky meets the ink, a line of white is glowing. It is the edge of the paper. The boundary of the page.

The beam is acting as a bridge.

"Come on!" I leap from the lamp room platform, not down the stairs, but *out* the window.

I hit the beam of light.

It is solid. Harder than glass, smoother than ice. I land running, sprinting across a bridge made of violet shadow. The bird follows, its talons clacking on the light.

Below us, the ink roils, trying to climb the beam to snuff us out. Shapes rise from the depths—the wireframe ghosts of deleted characters, the Scribes and Keepers who never made it to the final draft. They reach for us with hands made of sketches.

"Don't look down!" I shout.

We run for miles. The horizon rushes toward us. The white edge of the paper grows brighter, blinding.

We are getting close.

And there, standing at the very edge of the world, at the precipice of the page, is a figure.

He is backlit by the white void of the unwritten future. He wears a tweed jacket. Wire-rimmed glasses glint in the violet light.

"The Collector," I breathe.

We slow to a stop a few feet from him. The bridge of light ends at his shoes.

He looks different close up. He isn't a giant. He is my height. He looks tired. He looks like a man who has spent too long reading in the dark.

"You brought the light," he says. His voice is dry, rustling like paper.

"You bought the Soul," I counter. "Where is she?"

The Collector sighs. He reaches into his pocket and pulls out a small, silver thimble.

"The Scribe?" he asks. "She was never a soul. She was a fragment. A splinter of plot. You cannot sell what does not belong to you."

He tips the thimble. A single drop of black ink falls onto the page. It lands near my boot.

From the ink, a tiny vine sprouts. It grows rapidly, twisting, blooming into a single, perfect paper flower.

"A metaphor," the Collector says. "That is all she was."

"Why?" I ask. "Why bring me here?"

"Because the book is closing, Keeper," he says. He gestures to the sky. The violet is fading, replaced by a spreading darkness from the left. "The cover is coming down. The story is ending."

"You're the Author," I say. "Write more."

He shakes his head. "I am the Archivist. I only preserve what is lost. And you... you are lost."

He steps aside, revealing the white edge of the page.

"The window is open," he says. "But you cannot cross as ink. Ink stains. Ink fades."

"What do I cross as?" I ask.

He smiles. A sad, gentle smile. He points to the obsidian shard, still burning in the lighthouse far behind us.

"You cross as light."

He pushes me.

I stumble back. My boot slips on the edge of the light bridge.

"No!" the bird screeches, launching itself at the Collector. But the bird is only a bird, and the Archivist is a shadow. He passes through the bird like smoke.

"Jump, Keeper," the Collector whispers. "Or be crushed by the cover."

The sky above is bending. The ceiling of the room—the cardboard of the cover—is descending, snapping the spines of clouds, crushing the stars.

I look at the bird. It is hovering, unsure.

"We go together," I say.

"We don't fit," the bird says. "The space between worlds is narrow."

"Then we break it."

I grab the bird. I wrap my arms around its heavy, feathered body.

I look at the white void. The Nothing.

I close my eyes.

"I'm sorry," I whisper. "I have no more letters."

I jump.

We fall off the edge of the page.

The sensation is not of falling, but of *unraveling*. I feel the ink being pulled from my skin, my name being stripped from my bones. The bird in my arms explodes into a thousand white feathers.

We are passing through the gutters of the book. We are squeezing between the binding.

And then—

Impact.

Not on wood. Not on glass.

On snow.

I am lying on my back. The cold is biting, real, wet. It seeps through my coat.

I open my eyes.

Above me is not a ceiling, or a sky of violet.

It is a sky of grey slate, swirling with heavy, snow-laden clouds.

I sit up. I am sitting on a cobblestone street. Gas lamps hiss in the twilight.

I look down at my hands.

They are not ink-stained. They are red. Chapped.

I look for the bird.

It is lying in the snow a few feet away. It is not a bird of obsidian, or a wireframe sketch. It is a pigeon. A street pigeon. It shakes its head, ruffling its grey feathers.

It looks at me. It blinks a beady orange eye.

"Coo?" it asks.

I stand up. My legs are trembling.

I look around.

There is a street sign. *Baker Street.*

And across the street, a shop window. Darkened. But in the reflection of the glass, I see myself.

I am wearing a heavy wool coat. A scarf. I look...

Real.

I look at the sign above the shop.
