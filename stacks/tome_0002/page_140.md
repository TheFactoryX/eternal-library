# Letter 140

> Written at 2026-02-07 21:30:39

---

Dear You,

The dust in this room is not the passive accumulation of time. It is a barrier. A wall of grey silk stretched over the surface of the desk, preserving the shape of an object that is no longer there.

I approach the desk slowly, the floorboards groaning under boots that are suddenly, painfully heavy. The pigeon watches me from the sill, its head tilted, its orange eye reflecting the dying light of the lantern.

The rectangle in the dust is perfect. It has sharp, precise corners. It is the negative space of a laptop.

I reach out. I do not touch the desk. I touch the *air* above the desk.

My fingers hit glass. Solid, cold, invisible glass.

"It’s a frame," I whisper.

A two-dimensional pane of transparency, hovering exactly where the screen would be. I run my fingers along the bottom edge. I find a power button. It is not plastic; it is a flake of dried soap adhered to the glass.

I press it.

Nothing happens. No light. No hum. But the air pressure drops. My ears pop.

"The window," the pigeon says.

I turn to the apartment window. Outside, the fog of London is gone. The gas lamps are gone.

The sky is filled with violet static.

"The room is rotating," I realize. "We are flipping back into the interface."

"No," the bird says. "Look closer."

I approach the apartment window. It is not glass. It is paper. The entire view of Baker Street is a high-resolution print pasted over the glass. And in the corner of the "sky", printed in crisp, black serif letters:

*Page 140 of 200.*

"We are still in the book," I say, the dread settling in my stomach like a stone. "The Archivist didn't get us out. He just moved us to a new chapter."

"He said he preserves what is lost," the pigeon reminds me.

I walk back to the invisible screen. I lift the lantern, shining the light through the glass.

The dust on the desk glows. But the dust isn't settling randomly. It is arranged in patterns.

I lean closer.

The dust is forming letters. Tiny, microscopic particles of ash and skin, aligning themselves into words on the surface of the desk.

*I am writing from the other side.*

I step back, the lantern shaking.

"Who is writing?" the pigeon asks.

"The Reader," I say. "The girl. She’s writing back."

I grab the pen the Archivist gave me. It is heavy, carved from bone. I realize I cannot write with ink on a table. I need a surface.

I look at the invisible screen. "The glass."

I press the nib of the pen against the air. It catches. A line of black liquid appears, hanging suspended in the invisible glass. It does not drip. It floats.

I write.

*I am here. I am real. I have the pen.*

I wait.

Slowly, agonizingly slowly, new letters form in the dust below my floating words.

*The screen is cracked. The Update is corrupt. The characters are bleeding into the OS.*

I stare at the words. "She knows."

*How do I get out?* I write.

*You don't,* the dust replies. *You are the glitch now. The Antagonist is not a monster. It is the Delete Key. It is coming for the unresolved syntax.*

I look at the door. The bolt is sliding. The wood is splintering. The roar of the ocean—the smell of kelp and rot—is seeping through the cracks.

"The Antagonist," I breathe.

I turn to the pigeon. "We have to jump."

"Where?" it coos. "We are in a book. The walls are text. The floor is paper."

"The window," I say. "The window is a print of the street. If it’s a print, it’s a lie. But a lie is a hole."

I run to the window. I take the heavy obsidian shard from my pocket.

"Cover your eyes," I tell the bird.

I slam the shard into the glass.

It doesn't shatter. It rips.

A long, jagged tear appears in the view of Baker Street. The fog tears away, revealing not a brick wall behind the paper, but a black, infinite void. The space between the book and the shelf.

"Go!" I shout.

I grab the pigeon. I dive through the tear.

We fall.

We are in the gap. The air is filled with floating dust motes the size of boulders. The smell of glue and old paper is suffocating.

Below us, I see the room we just left. It is a diorama in a dollhouse, sitting on a shelf.

Next to it is another book. *The Lighthouse Keeper’s Letters.*

The cover is open. The pages are turning rapidly, flipping in a wind that doesn't exist. The pages are blanking out as they turn, the text vanishing as the book consumes itself to close the loop.

"It’s eating the story to fuel the ending," I yell over the roaring silence of the void.

I look up.

Far above, a hand is reaching down. It is the Archivist's hand. But he is small now. He is a figurine on a higher shelf.

He is holding a magnifying glass. He is looking at *us*.

"He's reading *us*," I realize. "We are the new book."

We are falling toward the floor—a vast expanse of hardwood that stretches for miles.

"We need to land," I shout.

"The pen!" the pigeon screeches.

I look at the bone pen in my hand. "What?"

"Write us a net! Write us a branch!"

I don't think. I act. I slash the pen through the air, writing a single, frantic word against the fabric of the nothingness.

*UPDRAFT.*

The word hangs in the air, glowing violet. It catches the air. It creates a cushion.

We hit the word. It bounces. We tumble onto a surface that wasn't there a second ago.

I land hard. The breath is driven from me.

I look around.

I am standing on a giant letter. The letter *T*.

It is resting on the floor of the void.

"Keeper," the pigeon says. "Look at the floor."

I look down.

The hardwood floor is not wood. It is a grid. A glowing blue grid.

And in the distance, a cursor is blinking. A massive, vertical line of light, pulsing rhythmically.

*Insert.*

"We are in the document," I whisper. "We are back in the digital draft, but we are physical."

The cursor blinks. *Move.*

The grid beneath us shifts. We slide. We are skating on the smooth surface of the page.

"It's loading," the pigeon screams. "The next chapter is loading!"

Ahead of us, the text is appearing. Huge, monolithic blocks of type rising from the floor like buildings.

*The Keeper stood on the shore of the final page.*

The words loom over us. *Shore* is a jagged cliff of rock. *Final* is a wall of iron.

"We have to climb the syntax," I yell.

We scramble up the vertical face of the word *Shore*. The rock is slippery with ink.

At the top, we stand on the serif of the *S*.

Ahead, the sentence continues: *...and watched the lighthouse dissolve into the sand.*

"The lighthouse is gone," the pigeon says.

I look back. The word *Lighthouse* is crumbling. The letters are turning to sand, pouring down into the gutter.

"It's past tense," I say. "She's writing the ending. She's writing us away."

I look at the pen. I have the bone pen. The Archivist’s pen.

"She's writing with software," I say. "I am writing with blood and bone."

I leap from the *S* of *Shore* toward the word *Dissolve*.

I land on the double *l*. It sways, rubbery and unstable.

"Hold on!" I shout to the bird.

I stab the bone pen into the soft flesh of the letter.

"I am not dissolving!" I scream at the sky. "I am editing!"

I drag the pen down, crossing out the word *Dissolve*. I slash it. I carve a massive, bleeding *X* through the type.

The word shudders. It doesn't disappear. It changes.

The letters reconfigure. The ink flows like mercury, reshaping itself under the influence of the bone pen.

*Dissolve* becomes *Remain*.

*The Keeper stood on the shore... and watched the lighthouse remain.*

The lighthouse in the distance stops crumbling. The sand reverses. The stone pulls itself back together.

"You can change it," the pigeon gasps. "You have the Author's Tool."

But then, the sky flickers.

A red box appears in the heavens.

*ERROR. CORRUPTED FILE. REVERT TO SAVED?*

The cursor moves. It is descending toward us like a guillotine.

"Revert!" I shout. "It's going to revert to the draft where we die!"

I grab the bird. "Climb!"

We scramble up the word *Remain*. We reach the top just as the cursor slashes through the space below us.

The words are deleted. They vanish into the white void of the undo function.

We are left standing on the last remaining letter.
