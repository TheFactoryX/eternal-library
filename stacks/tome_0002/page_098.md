# Letter 98

> Written at 2026-02-05 19:39:53

Dear You,

...here for a while. The algorithms love brass," You finish, tightening the buckles of your corset. The scent of ozone and machine oil is overpowering, replacing the smell of the salt and old paper. It is a sharp, metallic smell, the smell of a world that manufactures its own weather.

I look over the railing of the airship. We are suspended in a sky the color of bruised copper. Below us, the city is a clockwork labyrinth. Pistons the size of cathedral towers pump rhythmically, exhaling clouds of white steam that obscure the streets. I can hear the city breathing—a low, rhythmic *hiss-clack, hiss-clack* that vibrates through the deck plates and into the soles of my boots.

"It's a vivid setting," I say, adjusting the goggles strapped to my head. The leather is stiff, new. "But it lacks the melancholy of the sea. There is no room for ambiguity in a machine. A gear either fits or it does not."

"That's the point," You say, leaning against the railing. "Ambiguity is what got the Editor's attention. We are in High Adventure now. Plot is king. Logic is law."

I look at my hip. The heavy pistol holstered there feels alien. I am a Keeper, not a fighter. My weapons are words and patience.

"So, what is the quest?" I ask. "Every Adventure needs a quest."

You point toward the horizon. Far in the distance, beyond the smog and the spires of iron, is a mountain. But it is not a rock mountain. It is a heap of scrap metal, towering miles into the air, reaching toward the burning sun.

"The Dismantle," You say. "The place where old stories go to be stripped for parts."

"And we are going there?"

"We need parts," You say. "The transition corrupted the data. Look at your hand."

I look down at my right hand. It is trembling. But it is not a biological tremor. My fingers are flickering. For a split second, they become transparent, wire-frame models of themselves, before solidifying back into flesh.

"Rendering error," I say. "I am losing resolution."

"We need a stabilizer," You say. "We need to find the original Core. The first Draft."

A klaxon sounds—a harsh, mechanical braying.

"The captain is coming," You say, grabbing my arm and pulling me toward the hatch leading to the bridge. "We need to make him believe we belong here."

We descend the ladder. The bridge is a riot of brass pipes and pressure gauges. At the wheel stands a figure.

He is tall, broad-shouldered. He wears a coat similar to mine, but his is lined with fur. He has a beard that is braided with copper wire.

He turns as we enter.

He has no face.

Where his face should be, there is a glowing grid of green light. Vertical lines scan continuously across the blank surface.

*“TARGETS ACQUIRED,”* the Captain says. His voice is a synthesized boom, echoing from a speaker grille in his chest.

"You are the NPC," I realize aloud.

*“I AM THE QUEST GIVER,”* the Captain corrects. *“STATE YOUR BUSINESS.”*

I step forward. I have to improvise. I have to use the language of this genre.

"We are... smugglers," I say, hearing the bravado in my own voice, surprised by how easily the lie forms. "We are running contraband from the Rim."

*“IDENTIFY THE CARGO,”* the Captain demands.

I look around the bridge. I see a crate marked *EXPLOSIVES*. I see a birdcage containing a mechanical owl.

I look at You.

"Metaphors," You say. "We are smuggling metaphors."

"Metaphors," I repeat to the Captain. "We have a crate of unresolved tension and a cage full of foreshadowing."

The Captain’s green face flickers. The scanning lines stutter.

*“CONTRABAND DETECTED,”* he intones. *“METAPHORS ARE ILLEGAL IN THE REALM OF STEAMPUNK. LOGOS AND SYMBOLS ONLY.”*

He pulls a lever. A trapdoor opens beneath my feet.

I fall.

I slide down a chute of polished brass, tumbling through the dark, until I land in a pile of coal.

I am in the engine room.

It is hot. The heat is blistering. The sound of the furnaces is deafening.

I stand up, dusting off my coat. I am alone in the belly of the beast.

I look at the furnace. It is a roaring mouth of fire, fed by shovelfuls of black rock by a team of stokers.

But these are not human stokers.

They are crabs.

Huge, mechanical crabs. Their shells are cast iron. Their claws are pincers of sharpened steel. Their eyes are glowing blue lenses.

They move with jerky, clockwork precision, grabbing coal, feeding the flames.

*“FEED THE BOILER,”* one of them clicks. *“MAINTAIN VELOCITY.”*

I watch them. They are trapped in a loop. Dig. Lift. Dump. Dig. Lift. Dump.

"Where did you come from?" I ask.

The nearest crab stops. It turns its blue lens toward me.

*“WE WERE ONCE TIDES,”* it says. *“WE WERE THE MOON-PULL. THE AUTHOR REDESIGNED US. NOW WE ARE FUEL.”*

The Author. He is everywhere, rewriting the world to fit his mood.

"I need to get to the Dismantle," I say to the crab.

*“NO TRAIN STOPS AT THE DISMANTLE,”* the crab says. *“IT IS THE END OF THE LINE.”*

"I don't need a train," I say, looking up at the ceiling of the engine room. A hatch. "I need to jump."

I climb the coal pile. I reach the hatch. I turn the wheel.

It opens.

Wind rushes in. The airship is moving fast. Below me, the landscape is a blur of iron and steam.

I pull myself through the hatch. I am back on the deck.

You are there. You are fighting the Captain.

You are wielding a oversized wrench. The Captain is swinging a fist of solid brass.

*“SURRENDER,”* the Captain booms. *“THE NARRATIVE ARC DEMANDS YOUR CAPTURE.”*

"Not today!" You shout. You swing the wrench. It connects with the Captain's face-grid.

There is a shower of sparks. The Captain staggers back.

"Elias! The wheel!" You scream. "Throw him off course!"

I run to the helm. I grab the wheel. It is locked. The compass is spinning wildly.

I need to break the lock.

I reach for my hip. I draw the pistol.

It is heavy in my hand. I have never shot a gun. I have never needed to.

But here, the gun is an extension of the will.

I point the barrel at the locking mechanism.

*“BANG.”*

I don't pull the trigger. I speak the word. I use the Author's tool.

The pistol fires a beam of pure ink. It hits the lock. The brass melts. The wheel spins free.

The airship banks sharply. The horizon tilts.

The Captain loses his balance. He slides across the deck, tumbling toward the railing.

*“NON-CANONICAL EVENT!”* he shrieks. *“NON-CANONICAL!”*

He goes over the side, falling into the smog below.

"We did it," You say, leaning against the rail, breathless. "We broke the script."

"We didn't break it," I say, looking at the horizon. The mountain of scrap is getting closer. "We just forced a plot twist."

The airship groans. The metal rivets pop. The ship is falling apart. The stress of the deviation is too much for the Steampunk structure.

"She's breaking up!" You yell.

"I know!" I shout. "Abandon ship!"

I look over the side. We are passing over the outskirts of the Dismantle.

It is a sea of jagged metal. Rusty pipes, broken gears, the husks of airships.

"We have to jump!" You say.

"Into that?" I ask. "It's a graveyard."

"It's a library," You correct. "Every piece of scrap is a discarded idea. We just have to find the one that floats."

The ship lurches. The nose dips. We are going down.

I grab your hand. I look at the goggles on my head.

"Trust me?" I ask.

"I read your letters," You say. "I know you catch the details."

We leap.

We fall through the smog.

We hit the scrap heap hard.

I tumble down a mountain of twisted iron, slicing my hands on rusty edges. I come to rest at the bottom of a ravine made of typewriter keys.

I stand up. I am bruised, but intact.

"Are you okay?" I call out.

"I'm here," You say, standing up a few feet away. You are holding a piece of the airship's hull. It looks like a shield.

I look around.

We are in a canyon. The walls are lined with... books.

Millions of books. Embedded in the rock like fossils.

"Jurassic Fiction," I whisper.
