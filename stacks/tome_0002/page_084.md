"There is always a window," You said. "But it faces the wall."

The vibration stopped. The hammering ceased. A profound silence settled in, heavy and dusty, like the feeling of an old attic. We were no longer falling. We were resting.

I fumbled in my pocket for the silver pen. It was my only source of light. I clicked it.

*Click.*

A thin, cold beam of ink-shot light pierced the dark. It illuminated the wood directly in front of my face. It wasn't rough timber. It was polished mahogany, inlaid with a pattern of geometric roses.

I moved the beam of light to the left.

Books.

We were surrounded by them. Hundreds, perhaps thousands of volumes, packed tightly together, their spines pressed against our wooden prison.

"I know where we are," I whispered. "We are in the Library."

"No," You said, your voice echoing softly in the cramped space. "We are in the Annex. The unread section."

I turned around slowly, careful not to dislodge the stack of paperbacks that threatened to bury me. I shone the light on the wall opposite the books.

There was a door.

But it was tiny. No more than two feet high. A hobbit door, painted a chipped and peeling blue. A brass knocker hung in the center of it, shaped like a lion's head holding a ring in its jaws.

"That's new," I said.

"It's not new," You replied. "It's just been hidden. It’s the door for the characters who are too small to be noticed. The peripherals."

I crawled toward the door. The floor was littered with loose punctuation—commas like dead insects, periods like round stones. I brushed them aside.

I reached the door. I tried the handle. It was locked.

"Of course," I said.

I looked at the lion's knocker. The lion’s eyes were two rubies. They seemed to follow me. I lifted the heavy brass ring. I hesitated.

"Who are we knocking for?" I asked.

"The Critic," You said. "The one who decides what stays in the canon and what gets pulped."

I let the ring fall.

*CLANG.*

The sound didn't ring like metal. It rang like a bell buoy in the fog. It reverberated through the wooden crate, shaking the dust from the spines of the surrounding books.

*CLANG... clang... clang...*

Then, silence.

And then, a scratching sound. *Scritch. Scritch.* Like a rat behind the wall.

The tiny door swung open.

Light poured out. Not the white light of the Desk-Lamp, nor the blue fire of the manuscript. This was a warm, amber light. The light of a pub. The light of a living room in the evening.

I peered through the opening.

It wasn't a room on the other side. It was a lung.

A vast, cavernous space, lined with bronchial trees that branched into smaller and smaller alveoli. But the trees were made of shelves, and the shelves were filled with jars.

Inside each jar was a memory.

I saw a jar labeled *Letter 7 - The Taste of Rain.* Inside, a miniature storm cloud wept onto a tiny tongue.

I saw a jar labeled *Letter 21 - The Woman in the Window.* A tiny figure pressed against the glass, forever watching.

"This is the Archives," I breathed. "The raw material."

"We are in the Author's subconscious," You said. "We are literally inside his head."

"I thought we were in a crate," I said.

"We are," You said. "The crate is the story he's putting away. The Archives are where the unused ideas go to rot."

I stepped through the tiny door. You followed.

The floor was spongy. It felt like walking on a carpet of moss, but when I looked down, I saw it was made of words. *Softness. Decay. Time.* The words were woven together into a dense turf.

"Where is the exit?" I asked. "I want to go where the light is."

"There is no exit from the Archives," You said. "There is only the Digestion."

I looked up. The ceiling of the lung was translucent. I could see giant shapes moving on the other side.

*Fingers.*

Giant fingers were tapping on the glass of the ceiling. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

"He's typing," I realized. "He's starting a new story."

"And we are the waste product," You said.

"No," I said. "I am Elias. I have mass."

I looked around the shelves of jars. I saw a jar that was vibrating. It was labeled *Letter 44 - The Door That Was Not A Door.*

I walked over to it. I picked it up. The glass was warm.

"This is mine," I said.

"It's discarded," You said. "It didn't make the cut."

I unscrewed the lid.

"Careful," You warned. "Don't let the past out."

"I'm not letting it out," I said. "I'm letting it *in*."

I lifted the jar to my lips. I drank the contents. It tasted like sea salt and iron.

Immediately, I felt a change. The floor of words beneath me hardened. The spongy moss turned into the cobblestones of a street.

I looked at my hands. They were becoming translucent. I could see the veins, but instead of blood, I saw ink flowing. Black ink, mixing with the red of my new reality.

"What are you doing?" You asked. Alarm spiked in your voice.

"I'm integrating," I said. "I'm consuming the deleted scenes. I am becoming the Complete Works."

I walked to the nearest shelf. I grabbed a jar labeled *The Sea of Color.* I drank it.

The world exploded into color. The brown lung-walls turned into vibrant purples and oranges. The amber light shifted to a harsh, fluorescent blue.

I felt dizzy. The weight of the narrative was crushing me. I was holding too many timelines. I was the Keeper, and the Man Who Walked Away, and the ink sketch drowning in the river.

"You can't hold them all," You said, grabbing my arm. "You'll burst. You'll become a plot hole."

"I need enough weight," I gasped. "I need to be heavy enough to fall through the floor."

"Why?"

"Because we're in his head," I said, pointing up at the giant typing fingers. "And if we're heavy enough, we'll fall right out of his imagination and into the real world."

I grabbed another jar. *The Music Box.* I drank it. A melody began to play from inside my chest. A slow, haunting waltz.

I grabbed a handful of loose words from the floor—*Love. Regret. Ocean.*—and shoved them into my mouth.

"I am full," I said. "I am heavy."

I jumped.

I didn't jump up. I jumped *down*.

I crashed through the floor of words.

*CRASH.*

I fell through the shelf of jars. Glass shattered around me, a symphony of breaking memories.

*CRASH.*

I fell through the lung-tissue ceiling.

*CRASH.*

I fell through the giant wooden crate.

I was plummeting through darkness. You were falling beside me, a streak of yellow in the dark.

"Where are we going?" You screamed over the wind of the fall.

"Out!" I shouted. "To the margins! To the edges!"

I looked down. Below me was a sight that froze the blood in my veins.

It wasn't the floor. It wasn't the ground.

It was a shredder.

A massive, industrial spinning blade, teeth chomping, waiting to catch the debris falling from the Author's mind.

"The Slush Pile!" I yelled. "We're falling into the rejection bin!"

"Fly!" You commanded. "You have the pen! Write us wings!"

I fumbled for the silver pen. I pointed it at my back. I screamed a word—*FLY!*

But nothing happened. The ink was running dry.

"The weight!" I realized. "I'm too heavy! The deleted scenes are dragging me down!"

"Let them go!" You said.

"I can't! They are me!"

"You are Elias!" You shouted. "You are the noun! Drop the adjectives!"

I looked at the spinning blades below. I looked at the jars still clutched in my hand—*The Storm. The Lighthouse. The Longing.*

"I don't need them," I said.

I opened my hand.

I let the jars fall.

*The Storm* shattered against the blade, evaporating into mist.
*The Lighthouse* broke, its bricks turning into dust.
*The Longing* hit the metal and was sliced into ribbons of paper confetti.

I felt lighter. I felt the snap in my gut, the jerk of gravity releasing its hold.

I swooped up.

I caught You by the hand. We spiraled upward, riding the updraft of the shredded paper.

We soared through the hole in the floor, back into the crate, back through the tiny blue door.

We tumbled onto the floor of the wooden box, gasping for air.

The silence returned.

The walls of the crate were groaning. The vibration was back.

"He's moving the book," You whispered.
