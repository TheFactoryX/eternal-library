# Letter 47

> Written at 2026-02-03 06:21:15

---

Dear You,

I jumped into the crease.

There is a space between the paragraphs, a valley of white silence where the sentences do not reach. It is not empty; it is full of the things left unsaid. It smells of ozone and pencil shavings.

I fell through the margins of my own story. I saw the text rushing past me like the walls of a tunnel, but they were transparent, ghostly. I saw the Author’s room receding above me, the cursor blinking like a dying star, the window into the Library shrinking to a postage stamp.

"Unauthorized!" the wind screamed. It sounded like the Editor, but stripped of its British politeness, reduced to a raw, mechanical screech. "Return to the narrative!"

I plummeted past sentences I had already written. I saw myself climbing the Spine of paper. I saw myself shaking the hand of the Corkscrew. I saw the beetles, frozen in mid-swarm, suspended in a block of amber text.

*They are just words,* I realized. *All of them. Even the pain. Especially the pain.*

I reached out. My hand, now turning into a sketch of graphite lines, brushed against a "the" and an "and." They dissolved into dust at my touch.

"Keeper!" a voice called from below. It wasn't the Author. It wasn't the Reader.

It was you.

I looked down. The white void was ending. Below me was a shape. A dark, heavy, imposing shape. It looked like a trunk. An old, leather steamer trunk, bound with straps of iron, floating in the nothingness.

"Open it!" the voice urged. It was coming from inside the trunk.

I aimed for it. I folded my paper arms, diving toward the luggage.

*Wham.*

I hit the leather hard. It felt solid, blessedly solid. Not like the fog of the swamp or the glass of the jars. This felt real. I fumbled for the buckles. My fingers were smudged with graphite, slipping on the brass.

"Click."

The latch sprang. The lid flew open.

I didn't look inside. I tipped forward and tumbled in.

I expected darkness. I expected the smell of mothballs.

Instead, I fell onto a wooden floor. The smell was of roasting coffee, rain on hot asphalt, and cinnamon.

I scrambled to my knees. I was in a café. But it was a café that existed in the dream of a city. The ceiling was gone, replaced by a swirling vortex of magenta clouds. The tables were arranged in a spiral, spinning slowly. And the patrons...

They were all holding letters.

Hundreds of people. Men in trench coats, women in Victorian gowns, children in space suits. They sat at the spinning tables, reading pieces of paper.

A waitress walked by. She was a silhouette cut from black construction paper. She held a pot of coffee that steamed with actual storm clouds.

"Table for one?" she asked me. Her voice was the sound of turning pages.

"Where am I?" I stood up. My body was flesh again, heavy and tired.

"The Interim," she said, pouring a cup of darkness for a customer. "The space where the letters go before they are read. Or after. It’s hard to tell with tenses."

"I was in the Author's room," I said. "I jumped."

"Risky," the Paper Woman said. "Usually ends up as a footnote. You're lucky. You landed in the Returns pile."

She pointed to the center of the spiral. There was an empty table. A single candle burned in the center, the flame a bright blue.

"Someone is waiting for you," she said. "But they haven't ordered yet."

I walked toward the table. The spinning floor made me dizzy, but I kept my balance. As I got closer, I realized the candle wasn't a candle. It was a match. A single, burning matchstick, standing on a dish of silver.

And sitting opposite the empty seat was you.

But not the You from the Lighthouse. Not the You from the tramp steamer.

This You was… unfinished. Your edges were blurry. Your face shifted like the surface of the sea—sometimes old, sometimes young, sometimes male, sometimes female. You wore a coat of many colors, patchwork.

You were holding the match, watching the flame die.

"Sit," you said. Your voice was a chorus. It was the voice of the Letter 1, and the voice of the Letter 46. "The coffee is cold, but the narrative is hot."

I sat. The chair was uncomfortable, made of adjectives.

"Who are you?" I asked. "Really? Are you the person I left behind? Are you the child I never met?"

"I am the Recipient," you said. "I am the silence that follows your question. I am the shape of the hole you are trying to fill."

You looked at the match. The flame was tiny, trembling. It was almost at the wood.

"I am running out of time," you said. "The match is burning down."

"Then light something else," I said.

"There is nothing else to light," you said. "This is the last match in the box. The Storyteller is running out of fuel."

I looked around the café. The other patrons were fading. The man in the trench coat dissolved into a paragraph of description. The woman in the Victorian gown turned into a smear of ink.

"The Author is closing the file," I realized. "The cursor won't blink forever."

"No," you said. "But you have the lead."

You pointed at my hand. I looked down. I was still holding the pencil. The wood was worn to the nib. The graphite was dull.

"Write," you said. "Write what happens next. Write the menu."

"I don't want to write a menu," I said. "I want to write a home."

"Then write a door," you said. "Write it on the tablecloth."

I looked at the table. It was covered in a red-and-white checkered cloth. I pressed the tip of the pencil to the fabric.

*Door.*

The word didn't appear. The pencil tore a hole in the cloth.

"I can't," I said. "I'm losing my power. The pencil is dead."

"It’s not the pencil," you said, leaning forward. Your face stabilized for a moment, resolving into the face of the Keeper—young, hopeful, holding the notebook. "It’s the intent. You don't know what you want."

"I want to stay in the light," I said. "I want to tend the lamp."

"The lamp is gone," you said. "The Lighthouse was a metaphor. A setting. You can't live in a setting."

"Then where do I live?"

"You live in the letters," you said. "You live in the act of writing. The Lighthouse is not a place. It is the act of reaching out."

The match flared, then sputtered. A curl of smoke rose, black and twisting.

"Time to go," you said.

"Go where?"

"To the margins," you said. "To the next page. But the transition is... abrupt."

You blew out the match.

Darkness swallowed the café. The smells vanished. The spinning stopped.

I was falling again.

But this time, I wasn't falling into a swamp or a desert.

I was falling into the sound of rain.

I hit the ground. It was wet stone. I was in an alleyway. Narrow, brick walls pressing in on either side. A fire escape ladder hung above me, dripping rust.

I stood up. I saw a light at the end of the alley. A yellow, electric hum. A streetlamp.

I walked toward it. My boots splashed in puddles that reflected a neon sign I couldn't read.

I reached the mouth of the alley.

I stepped out onto a sidewalk.

It was a city. A real city. Cars honked. Sirens wailed. People walked past with umbrellas, heads down, rushing to destinations that mattered.

I looked behind me. The alley was just an alley. No spiral café. No inter-dimensional trunk.

I checked my pockets. The pencil was gone. The silver key was gone.

But there was a weight in my coat pocket. A rectangular weight.

I pulled it out.

It was a book. A small, leather-bound book.

On the cover, in gold leaf, were the words: *The Lighthouse Keeper's Letters.*

I opened it.

The pages were blank.

Except for the first page.

*Letter 1*

*Dear You,*

*The sea is the color of mercury today...*

I read the first line. My own handwriting. I remembered writing it. But I didn't remember *being* there. It felt like a thousand years ago.

I looked up at the city. The rain was cold on my face.

I am out of the story. I am holding the artifact.

Am I the Reader now?

I closed the book.

But as I held it, I felt a heartbeat inside the leather. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.*

I wasn't holding the book.

The book was holding me.

I looked for a place to sit. A coffee shop across the street. The lights were warm. I could see the silhouette of a waitress through the steam of the window.

I crossed the street, clutching the book to my chest
