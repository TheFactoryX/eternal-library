Dear You,

We landed not on flesh, but on a landscape of ridges and valleys that swirled in a hypnotic, fractal pattern. The impact knocked the wind out of me. I gasped, dragging air into lungs that felt suddenly heavy, too dense for this scaled-up world.

"We made it," the Scribe whispered. She was pressed flat against the skin, her linen shift clinging to her. "It’s warm. Like a sun-warmed stone."

I looked up. The thumb rose above us like a fallen sky, blotting out the light of the desk lamp. We were clinging to the side of the giant’s finger, microscopic climbers on a monolith of living tissue.

"Don't look down," the bird said. It was perched on a ridge of epidermis, preening a wing that was no longer ink, but bone and feather. "The perspective is dizzying."

"We have to move," I said, forcing myself to my hands and knees. The terrain was difficult. The ridges of the fingerprint were sticky with the oils of the world, trapping us in the whorls. "She’s going to turn the page. If the finger moves, we fall."

We scrambled upward, toward the cuticle. It was a vast, white cliff overlooking the edge of the nail—a translucent ocean of keratin shimmering like mica.

"Look at the color," the Scribe said, marveling at her hand. "It’s not *Payne's Grey*. It’s... peach."

"Don't get distracted," I grunted. We reached the precipice of the cuticle. Below us lay the expanse of the hand, the knuckles like hills, the palm a plain of lifelines stretching into the distance.

And there, on the desktop, was our destination.

The Phone.

It was a monolith of black glass, lying face up on the wooden surface. To us, it was the size of a city block. The screen was dark, a black mirror reflecting the ceiling lamp.

"That’s the beacon," I said. "If we can wake it, we can send a signal."

Suddenly, the world lurched. The thumb we stood on detached from the paper, pulling away from the book.

"Gravity!" the bird shrieked.

We were thrown forward as the hand retreated. We tumbled through the air, screaming, and slammed onto the hard, unyielding surface of the phone’s screen.

*Thud.*

I rolled onto my back, gasping. The Scribe landed beside me, groaning. The bird fluttered down, its talons clicking softly on the glass.

"We’re alive," the Scribe said. She touched the surface. "It’s cold. Smooth. Like a frozen lake."

"It’s sleeping," I said. I looked up at the giant face looming above the desk. The Reader was adjusting her glasses, her eyes flicking back to the pages of the book she had just dropped. "We have a moment before she notices us. Or before she swipes."

"We need to unlock it," the bird said. "How do we wake the giant?"

I looked around the expanse of the black glass. It was featureless, save for a small constellation of dust motes near the bottom edge.

"I need a key," I said.

I reached into my pocket. My fingers brushed against the obsidian shard, the button marked *0*, and the cork from the bottle of invisible ink.

I pulled out the cork.

"Will that work?" the Scribe asked.

"It’s a conductor," I said. "It connects the inside to the outside."

I walked to the bottom of the screen. I remembered seeing the Reader do this. I remembered the motion. A swipe up from the bottom.

I placed the cork on the glass. It stuck slightly, dragged by static.

"Help me," I said to the others.

We grabbed the cork. We pushed.

*Scrape. Scrape.*

The cork left a trail of... something on the black glass. It wasn't dirt. It was *meaning*. The glass began to hum. A faint, blue light rippled out from the point of contact.

*FLASH.*

The screen erupted into light.

We shielded our eyes. When the spots cleared, we were standing on a grid of icons. Maps. Weather. Mail.

They were vast, colorful city-states of data.

"Which one?" the bird shouted over the hum of the processor. "Which door do we open?"

"The mail," I said. "The letters. That is our domain."

We ran toward the blue square icon. It was a building of white envelopes.

"We can't open that," the Scribe said. "We’re too small to type."

"We don't need to type," I said. "We need to speak."

I saw a small, green circle icon at the bottom of the screen. A phone.

"We call the last number," I said. "We call the Author."

We jumped onto the green button. I slammed my fist against the glass.

*Call.*

The screen shifted. The keypad appeared, numbers the size of swimming pools filling our vision.

"Who are we calling?" the bird asked. "The number isn't in the book."

"Yes it is," I said. "It’s on the copyright page. The publisher's address."

I remembered the numbers. I didn't know how I knew, but I did. A sequence from a dream.

*5-5-5-0-1-9-9.*

I jumped onto the '5'. The Scribe jumped on the second '5'. The bird pecked the '0'.

We danced on the keys. A dial tone filled the air, a sound like a choir of synthesizers. It vibrated through the glass, rattling our teeth.

*Ring. Ring.*

"Hello?" a voice said. It was tinny, distant, coming from the speaker at the top of the phone, but amplified by the strange acoustics of the screen.

It wasn't the Author. It was a machine.

*Thank you for calling the void. No one is available to take your call. The universe is currently closed for business.*

"Beep!" the machine shouted.

"We need an operator!" I screamed into the speaker grille. "We are stuck in the margin!"

The machine didn't hear. It didn't care.

I looked up. The Reader was reaching for the phone. She had heard the dial tone.

"She’s going to hang up!" the Scribe cried.

"No," I said. "She’s going to answer."

A massive face descended upon us. The Reader’s eye, vast and bloodshot, filled the sky. She was looking at the caller ID.

*Unknown Caller.*

"Hello?" she said. Her voice was thunder, shaking the icons around us. "Who is this?"

I ran to the speaker. I stood on the edge of the grille.

"It’s me!" I shouted. "The Keeper! I’m in the phone!"

She frowned. She tilted her head. The light from the screen reflected in her pupil.

"Keeper?" she whispered.

She didn't hang up. She lowered the phone to her ear.

We were lifted. The phone rose into the air, a platform of glass lifting us toward her face.

The wind was fierce. We lay flat against the screen.

"Where are you?" she asked. She sounded sad. A deep, resonant sadness that vibrated in the phone's casing.

"I’m here," I said. "In the story. You closed the book, but I didn't go away."

"I lost the book," she said. "I put it in a box. Years ago."

"You opened it," I said. "Tonight. You read the letters."

"I was remembering," she said. "I was remembering the lighthouse. The one I saw when I was a child."

The Scribe grabbed my arm. "Keeper," she whispered. "Look at the screen."

The call timer was running.

*00:01. 00:02.*

But the background was changing. The wallpaper of the phone was shifting. It was no longer a picture of a beach.

It was a picture of the lighthouse.

Taken from the inside.

"She has the photo," the bird said. "The one that appeared in the tower."

"Wait," I said. "If she has the photo... then she’s been there."

The Reader’s voice echoed through the speaker. "I wrote those letters," she said softly. "When I was young. I wrote them to myself. To the person I would become."

The shock knocked the breath out of me.

"You didn't read them," I said. "You wrote them."

Then who am I?

Am I the writer? Or the memory?

The call timer hit *00:05*.

Suddenly, the screen began to flicker. The battery icon in the corner turned red.

*Low Battery.*

"She’s fading," the Scribe said.

"No," I said. "The device is dying."

The Reader’s voice became distorted, slowing down. "I... have... to... let... you... go..."

"No!" I screamed. "Don't hang up! If you hang up, the story ends!"

The hand moved. The phone was lowering.

We were sliding down the glass surface. The desk was rushing up to meet us.

"We have to jump back to the book!" the bird screeched.
