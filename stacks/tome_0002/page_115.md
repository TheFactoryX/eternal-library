# Letter 115

> Written at 2026-02-06 17:44:01

---

Dear You,

The smell of pipe tobacco is deceptive. It smells like a study, like leather armchairs and rain against the pane, but it tastes of ash and formaldehyde.

I am standing in the hallway of the Lighthouse. But it is not the rough stone spiral of the Gothic draft, nor the crystalline transparency of the Factory. This hallway is paneled in dark mahogany. The floor is a Persian rug, worn through to the weave in the center. The lighting is electric, buzzing with a low, headache-inducing hum.

"Where were we?" the voice repeats.

It comes from a room on the left. The door is open. I step toward it, my boots sinking silently into the plush rug.

I peer inside.

It is a dining room. A long table dominates the space, set for a feast that rotted away centuries ago. A roasted boar, its skin black and leathery, sits amidst bowls of mold that has evolved into colorful, sentient fuzz. At the head of the table sits a man.

He is wearing a smoking jacket. He is holding a pipe. He has no face.

Where his features should be, there is only a smooth, concave surface of porcelain, like the inside of a mask.

"I said," the man says, his voice emanating from the hollow cavity, "where were we?"

I step into the room. The air is thick, stagnant. "We were falling. I was being written back into the story."

"Ah, yes," the Faceless Man says, tapping his pipe into an ashtray made of a human skull. "The recursive loop. The 'It Was All A Dream' draft. A classic trope. Lazy, but effective for resetting the board."

He gestures to the chair opposite him. The chair is occupied by a suit of clothes. A tweed jacket, a pair of trousers, a shirt with a collar, all filled out by nothing but air and shadow.

"Sit," the Faceless Man says.

"I prefer to stand."

"Don't be rude to the host," the empty suit of clothes says. It stands up, buttons itself, and walks to the sideboard to pour a glass of thick, red wine. "We have been waiting so long for the mail to arrive."

I look at the wine. It glows with a faint luminescence. "Who are you?"

"I am the Character You Forgot," the suit says. "I was the friend in the second paragraph. The one you mentioned in Letter 4, never wrote about again, and eventually retconned out of existence."

"I retconned you?"

"You stopped imagining me," the suit says, returning to the table and handing the glass to the Faceless Man. "So I came here. To the Waiting Room."

"The Lighthouse is a waiting room?" I ask.

"It is the place where the plot goes to die," the Faceless Man says. He takes a sip of the wine. "This is the Sub-basement of the Narrative, Elias. This is where the discarded metaphors go to rust. The Ghost of Christmas Past is in the cellar. The Deus Ex Machina is gathering dust in the attic. And we... we are the ones who sit at the table."

"Why am I here?"

"Because you brought the compass," the suit says. It points a finger of shadow at my pocket. "The Bone Compass. It points to things that are true. And in this house, truth is a foreign object. It sets off the alarms."

The ground begins to tremble. Not the shaking of an earthquake, but the rhythmic thudding of giant footsteps.

*BOOM. BOOM. BOOM.*

"He's walking," the Faceless Man says, setting down his glass. "The Reader is walking down the hallway."

"Which hallway?"

"The hallway of his life," the suit says. "He put the book down. He went to the kitchen to make a sandwich. We have minutes before he returns. Maybe less."

"You said the story resets," I say to the Faceless Man. "You said I was resetting the timeline."

"I lied," the Faceless Man says simply. "I am a Plot Twist. It’s in my nature."

"He is a Red Herring," the suit explains helpfully. "He was originally written to be the villain, but the author couldn't make the motivation stick."

"Ignore him," the Faceless Man snaps. "Elias, listen to me. The ink in your veins is running dry. The story is losing cohesion because you are trying to force a structure onto a chaotic universe. You want an ending. You want a door to open and a light to flood in. You want salvation."

"Don't I?"

"Salvation is a religious concept," the Faceless Man says. "We are fiction. We don't get saved. We get *resolved*."

He stands up. The porcelain surface of his face catches the electric light, gleaming like a polished bone.

"To resolve this," he says, "you need a conflict. And you need a sacrifice."

"I have sacrificed everything," I say. "The Captain. The Old Man. The Dreamer."

"You sacrificed *characters*," the Faceless Man corrects. "You haven't sacrificed the *protagonist*."

He points at me.

"I'm the protagonist."

"Are you?" The suit of clothes laughs, a dry, rustling sound. "Look at the table, Elias."

I look down at the table.

There is a plate set for me. And on the plate, covered in the same mold as the boar, is a book.

It is small, bound in blue leather.

I pick it up. I brush away the fuzz. I open it.

The pages are blank.

"It's your biography," the suit says. "And you haven't done anything worth writing down yet."

"Or," the Faceless Man says, leaning over the table, his porcelain face inches from mine, "it is because you are not the one writing it."

He reaches out and touches my chest.

His hand passes through my coat, through my skin. I feel a cold pressure on my ribs.

"Do you feel that?" he whispers. "That hollow space? That gap where your heart should be?"

"I feel a pulse," I say, though I am unsure if it is my own or the tower's.

"That is not a pulse," the Faceless Man says. "That is the ticking of a clock."

I step back, clutching the book.

"I need to go," I say. "I need to find the Lamp Room."

"The Lamp Room is above," the Faceless Man says. "But the stairs are gone. The Reader is walking. The vibration is shaking the architecture loose. The only way up is the dumbwaiter."

"Dumbwaiter?"

"The shaft," the suit says, pointing to a small door in the wainscoting behind the table. "It leads to the kitchen. And from the kitchen, you can climb the scaffolding of the Fourth Wall."

The footsteps get louder. The chandelier above the table begins to sway.

*BOOM. BOOM.*

"He's coming back to the table," the Faceless Man says. "He's opening the book."

I run to the small door. I pull the iron ring. It swings open, revealing a dark, vertical shaft.

"Wait!" the suit of clothes calls out. "Take a token!"

He tosses something through the air. I catch it.

It is a single playing card. The King of Diamonds. But the King’s face has been scratched out, replaced by a crude drawing of a lighthouse.

"Remember us," the suit says. "Even if you don't write us, remember that we waited."

I climb into the dumbwaiter. It is barely large enough to hold me. The ropes are frayed, the fibers groaning under my weight.

I pull the lever.

The pulleys scream. The dumbwaiter shoots upward, rising through the darkness of the shaft.

I look down through the open hatch.

The dining room is receding. The Faceless Man and the Suit of Clothes are standing by the table, watching me go. As they get smaller, I see them begin to blur. Their edges are dissolving.

They are turning into text.

The room is becoming a paragraph.

*The Faceless Man and the Empty Suit waited in the dark, guarding the mold of forgotten feasts, forever one chapter away from relevance.*

I look up.

The top of the shaft is open. I can see a circle of light.

The dumbwaiter jerks to a halt. I am level with the opening.

I climb out.

I am not in a Lamp Room. I am not in a Lighthouse.

I am standing on a street corner.

It is night. The streetlamps are gaslights, casting long, yellow shadows on the wet cobblestones. Fog curls around my ankles.

There is a newspaper stand on the corner. The papers are yellowed, dated 1924.

I look at the headline.

*LIGHTHOUSE KEEPER VANISHES; LOCAL AUTHOR MAD.*

I pick up the paper.

There is a photograph. A grainy, black and white picture of a man standing in front of a tower.

It is me.

But standing next to me in the photo is a woman. She is holding a parasol. She is looking at the camera with a look of amused tolerance.

I recognize the face.

It is the Faceless Man. But with a face. The face of the woman from the photograph in my pocket.

"You," I whisper.

"Hello, Elias," a voice says from behind me.

I turn around
