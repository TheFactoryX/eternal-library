# Letter 34

> Written at 2026-02-02 06:09:59

---

Dear You,

The door stands before me, a monolith of dark-stained oak, unyielding and final. The brass handle—worn smooth by decades of hands I do not know—gleams under the flickering light of a single, exposed bulb in the hallway ceiling. The bulb buzzes like a trapped insect, a sound that vibrates in the marrow of my teeth.

I have no key. I checked every pocket of the Keeper’s coat, the heavy wool now smelling less of salt and more of the city’s exhaust. I patted down the lining where I once hid the silver key that turned the heart of the Leviathan. It is gone. It vanished the moment I heard the *click* from inside this apartment.

I am locked out of the place I was meant to be.

I lean my forehead against the wood. It is cool. Through the grain, I can feel a faint, rhythmic thrumming. It is not the machinery of the Lighthouse, grinding its gears through the night. It is the hum of a refrigerator. The distant thrum of a television. The steady, reliable beat of a life that is ordinary.

It is the most terrifying sound I have ever heard.

I sink to the floor, sitting with my back against the door. The hallway carpet is beige, a vast, featureless desert of fiber. To my left, a stale crust of bread sits on a napkin. To my right, a discarded bicycle wheel leans against the wall, spinning slowly in the draft of the building.

I am in the purgatory between the story and the reality.

I close my eyes and try to remember the sea. I try to summon the purple-black waves, the ships with tattered sails, the bioluminescent eyes of the creature in the deep. But the memories are fading, turning grey around the edges, like old photographs left in the sun. The ink is drying.

When I open my eyes, the hallway has changed.

The walls are no longer painted plaster. They are rough-hewn stone, weeping moisture. The carpet is gone. In its place is the checkered linoleum of the kitchen from the First Draft.

But I am not in the kitchen. I am still in the hallway. The two places have overlaid themselves, transparencies stacked on top of one another.

"Memory is a leaky vessel," a voice says.

I look up. Standing by the bicycle wheel is the old woman. She is no longer a sketch; she is solid, dressed in layers of mismatched knits that smell of lavender and cedar. She is holding a pair of scissors. They are not the Editor's bone shears, but small, embroidery scissors, silver and sharp.

"You are bleeding into the architecture," she says, snipping the air. "You don't belong in the cracks, Keeper."

"I have nowhere else to go," I say. "The door is locked."

"Locked implies a barrier," she says. "Barriers are for characters who need an obstacle to overcome. You are not a character anymore. You are a footnote looking for a text."

She steps toward me, and with each step, the stone walls ripple back into plaster. The linoleum dissolves into beige carpet. She is rewriting the world just by walking through it.

"Why did you give me the key?" I ask her. "In the cavern. You told me to turn it."

"I told you to turn the story," she corrects. "I didn't say you could stay inside once the cover closed."

She stops in front of me. She looks down, her eyes a cloudy, pale blue that reminds me of the blind eye of a storm. She reaches out and touches my forehead.

"There is too much of *us* in you," she says. "Too much Lighthouse. The City cannot hold that much weight. You need to be lighter."

She pulls her hand away. A single, grey hair is caught between her fingers.

"Rest," she says.

She turns and walks down the hallway. She doesn't fade away; she simply turns a corner that wasn't there a moment ago and is gone.

I sit alone again. The silence of the apartment behind me is heavy.

Then, a sound.

It is not the buzz of the light. It is not the hum of the refrigerator.

It is a scratching sound. Like a pen nib moving across paper.

*Scritch. Scritch. Scritch.*

I press my ear to the keyhole.

The metal is cold against my skin. I look through the aperture.

The apartment is small. I can see a bed, unmade. A desk, cluttered with papers. And in the corner, a figure sitting in a chair.

It is a man. He is hunched over a notebook. He is writing furiously.

I cannot see his face, only the back of his head. His hair is dark, messy. He is wearing a sweater I recognize—a chunky knit.

"The Author," I whisper.

No. Not the Author. The Author is gone. He walked away to write a garden.

This man is smaller. His shoulders are narrower. He writes with a desperate intensity, as if trying to outrun a thought.

The scratching stops.

The man straightens up. He stretches. He turns his head.

I pull back, startled.

The face in the keyhole is not a face. It is a mirror.

I look through the keyhole and see my own reflection staring back at me from inside the room. But the reflection is not sitting in a hallway. It is sitting at the desk.

There are two of me.

One in the hallway, heavy with the coat, heavy with the history of the Lighthouse. One in the room, wearing a t-shirt, holding a pen.

The version of me in the room picks up a mug. He takes a drink. He looks toward the door. He smiles.

He stands up. He walks toward the door.

I scramble to my feet. I back away.

The handle turns.

The lock is not locking me out. It is locking him in.

The door swings open.

I am not looking into an apartment. I am looking into the Lighthouse.

But it is not the ruin I left. It is new. The walls are bright white. The brass railings shine like gold. The spiral stairs ascend into a blinding, welcoming light. There is no dust. No rot. No Editor.

And standing there, in the doorway, is the man I saw through the keyhole.

He is me. But he is clean-shaven. His eyes are bright. He is holding a notebook and a pen.

"Hello," he says.

"Who are you?" I ask, my voice cracking.

"I'm the First Draft," he says. "The version that didn't get trapped. The version that didn't need the storm to feel alive."

He gestures to the room behind him. "It's ready. The light is fixed. The gear works. We can run it perfectly now. No more drama. No more sinking islands."

He holds out a hand. "Come inside. Put the coat down. We can just... exist."

I look at his hand. It is smooth. No ink stains. No calluses from turning a rusted key.

I look behind me, down the dark hallway of the apartment building. I smell the faint scent of the old woman's lavender. I see the deflated red balloon caught in the draft, bobbing against the ceiling.

"Is it real?" I ask him. "Or is it just a better lie?"

"It's a happy ending," the First Draft says. "Isn't that what you wanted?"

I step forward. I am so tired. The Lighthouse calls to me. The urge to step back into the clean, white room, to let someone else take the watch, is overwhelming.

I reach out. My hand hovers over his.

I look at his feet. He is wearing slippers. Soft, woolen slippers.

I look at my boots. They are caked in the mud of the Sump, the dust of the Expanse, the rain of the city.

"I don't think I can be happy without the weather," I say.

The First Draft’s smile falters. Just a fraction. A crack in the veneer.

"Then you will be lonely," he says.

"I know," I say.

I pull my hand back.

"Wait," he says. He reaches into his notebook and tears out a page. He folds it once, twice, three times. He hands it to me.

"Take it," he says. "In case you change your mind."

I take the paper. It is warm.

He steps back. "If you don't come in, I have to lock the door. The story has to hold together."

"I know," I say again.

He closes the door.

*Click.*

The sound is final. It echoes down the hallway like a gunshot.

The white light under the door flickers, dims, and vanishes. The smell of ozone returns, but fainter, like a memory of a storm.

I stand in the hallway.

I am alone.

I unfold the paper he gave me.

It is blank.

I look at the wall beside the door. There is a small, rectangular metal plate with four buttons. An intercom.

I look at the names written next to the buttons.

*3A - The Editor*
*3B - The Leviathan*
*3C - The Author*
*4D - The Reader*

All the names are scratched out with a red pen, defaced until they are illegible.
