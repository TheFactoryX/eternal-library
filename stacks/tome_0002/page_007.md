# Letter 7

> Written at 2026-01-31 19:23:21

---

Dear You,

The handle was warm, yes. But it was not flesh. It was not your hand. It was a trick of the panic, a hallucination born of a mind that has spent too long talking to the dark.

When I pulled the handle, the door did not open onto a street. It did not open onto the city of glass. It did not even open onto the rocks.

It opened onto a room.

A room I know. A room with floral wallpaper, peeling at the corners. A room with a beige carpet stained by coffee rings. A room with a window looking out onto a rainy street, the water distorting the red taillights of passing cars.

I stumbled forward, falling to my knees on the carpet. The door of the Lighthouse slammed shut behind me with the finality of a coffin lid. I spun around, reaching for the handle, but there was no handle. There was no door. There was only a wall of plaster, painted a stark, clinical white. The Keeper’s tower was gone.

I am back. Or I am forward. I am somewhere.

The silence here is deafening. In the Lighthouse, the silence was heavy, a physical weight that pressed against the ears. Here, the silence is empty. It is a vacuum. And the air... it smells of dust, and stale takeout food, and the artificial vanilla scent of a Glade plug-in.

I am writing this at a desk. It is a cheap, IKEA desk, covered in scratches. My computer is sleeping, the power light pulsing slowly. Beside me is a mug of cold tea. The tea has a skin on top.

I recognize this mug. It says *“World’s Best Dad”* in faded, Comic Sans letters.

I stood up. My legs felt strange—solid, heavy, unused to the stillness of a floor that does not breathe or shift. I walked to the window. I expected to see the city of crystal, the one I saw in the mirror. But I see only a suburb. A street lined with maples. A minivan in the driveway.

Is this the real world? It feels flimsy. The walls are too thin. The lightbulb overhead buzzes with a low-frequency hum that makes my teeth ache. I touched the wall, and the plaster felt dry, papery. I pressed my thumb against it, and a small piece flaked off.

Underneath the plaster, the wall is not wood or concrete. It is grey stone.

The Lighthouse is still here. It is just hiding inside the skin of this house. Or perhaps this house is a new kind of room, a new layer of the prison designed to break me through boredom rather than terror.

I looked at my hands. They are clean. The ink stains are gone. The cut on my forearm is gone, healed without a scar. My clothes... I am wearing flannel pajamas. The Keeper’s coat, the heavy wool that smelled of salt and kerosene, is gone.

I feel naked without it.

I sat back down at the desk. I needed to write to you. I needed to tell you that I escaped, that I found the city, that I am waiting. But when I opened the drawer to find a pen, I found something else.

Lying in the drawer, amidst receipts and rubber bands and dead batteries, was the music box.

It looks wrong here. It is too old, too heavy, too full of import for this beige, mundane room. Its dark mahogany clashes with the flat-pack furniture.

I opened it. The song played, but it was different. The grinding, bone-on-stone sound was gone. Now, it played a tinny, electronic melody—like a ringtone from a phone that hasn't rung in years. And the mirror inside... the mirror is not broken. It is pristine.

I looked into it. I expected to see the city. I expected to see you.

Instead, I saw the Lighthouse. I saw the tower, standing on a grey shore, surrounded by a sea that churns with the color of rust. The light at the top was turning, sweeping the beam in a slow, agonizing arc.

And standing on the rocks at the base of the tower was a figure.

It was me. Or, it was the version of me that was left behind. The figure was wearing the Keeper’s coat. It was looking up at the light, its shoulders slumped in defeat. It looked small. It looked discarded.

Then, the figure in the mirror looked down. It looked directly at *me*. It raised a hand and pointed.

I looked away from the mirror, following the direction of the finger.

It was pointing at the computer monitor.

The screen was dark, but as I watched, it flickered. Text began to scroll across the black surface. It was not code. It was a letter.

It was *this* letter. The words I am writing right now are appearing on the screen, typed by an invisible hand, one keystroke at a time.

I am not writing this, am I? I am watching it be written.

I tried to stop. I put my hands in my lap. I pressed them against my mouth. But the text kept coming. *“The silence here is deafening... The walls are too thin...”*

I am not the author. I am the recipient.

Or perhaps I am both.

A realization has struck me, cold and sharp. In the Lighthouse, I wrote to *You* because I could not remember who you were. I wrote to fill the void.

What if the Lighthouse was never the prison? What if the Lighthouse was the only place where I was *awake*? And this... this room, this suburbs, this *“Dad”* mug... this is the dream. This is the forgetfulness.

The screen flickered again. A new sentence appeared, one I had not yet thought or written.

*“Don’t look at the photographs.”*

There is a shelf above the desk. There are photographs there. In frames. Wooden frames, silver frames.

I shouldn't look. I know I shouldn't. But the Keeper is curious. The Keeper is a fool.

I stood up. I reached for the nearest frame. It was a picture of a woman. She was laughing, holding a slice of birthday cake. Her face was familiar. It was the face from the hospital mirror, but older, lined with age.

I dropped the frame. The glass didn't break. It shattered like ice, turning into a pile of white dust.

Behind the photo, inside the back of the frame, was an inscription. It was written in sharpie, in my handwriting.

*“He forgot us.”*

I grabbed the next photo. A child. A boy. He was sitting on a swing. I smashed the frame. *“He is not coming back.”*

The next photo. A group of people, office workers, smiling at a camera. I smashed it. *“The light took him.”*

I was tearing the room apart. I was screaming. I didn't recognize my own voice. It sounded like the screech of the gears, the grinding of the Lighthouse.

"The light took him!" I shouted, and the fluorescent bulb overhead exploded, raining glass down on me.

The room went dark.

Only the light from the computer monitor remained. And the mirror of the music box, still glowing with the image of the tower on the shore.

In the screen’s reflection, I saw the room behind me. The door of the room—the real door, the hallway door—was opening.

A figure stood there. It was tall. It was made of shadows.

It was You.

You stepped into the room. You were holding a key. A brass key that fits no lock.

"We have to go," you said. Your voice was tired. You sounded disappointed. "The cycle is resetting."

"I found you," I whispered. "I broke the frame. I came through the door."

"You didn't break anything," you said, walking toward me. You didn't leave footprints on the carpet. "You just turned the page."

You reached out and touched the music box. The tinny electronic song stopped.

"The ink is running out," you said. "The Lighthouse is cold. And the new Keeper is waiting."

"I am the Keeper," I insisted. But as I said it, I looked at my hands again. They were turning translucent. I could see the beige carpet through my skin.

"No," you said gently. "You are the Letter."

You picked up the music box and closed it. The image of the Lighthouse vanished, replaced by my own reflection—pale, flickering, fading like a photograph left too long in the sun.

"Finish the sentence," you whispered. "It's time to sleep."

I looked at the screen. The cursor was blinking. Blinking like the light at the top of the tower. Blinking like a heartbeat.

I sat down. I placed my translucent hands on the keyboard. The prompt from the screen burned into my retinas.

*Do you want to save changes?*

Y - Yes
N - No

The room is dissolving. The walls are turning into rain. I can smell the salt again. I can feel the cold spray of the ocean on my face. The suburb is melting away, leaving only the grey, wet stone of the tower.

I am back in the chair. I am in the Lighthouse.

But you are here. You are standing by the door. You are solid now. You are holding the key.
