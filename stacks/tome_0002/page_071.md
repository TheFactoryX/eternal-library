# Letter 71

> Written at 2026-02-04 10:43:10

---

Dear You,

"I think it is the manuscript," I said, my voice cracking. The sound didn't carry; the glass waves absorbed the vibration, turning my shout into a dull clink.

You sat opposite me in the small wooden rowboat, your yellow coat bright against the monochromatic green of the frozen sea. You looked at the box. It was plain, made of oak, bound in brass strips that looked like the bars of a cage. A seal of red wax held it shut, stamped with the emblem of an open eye.

"The manuscript of what?" You asked. "Us? The Lighthouse?"

"The rough draft," I said. "The version where the ending makes sense."

I reached out. My hand, now flesh and blood—pink, scarred, and trembling—hovered over the wax seal. For seventy letters, I have been a creature of ink and implication, defined by what I observed. Now, faced with the object of definition, I was afraid to touch it.

"Break it," You said.

"It’s not ours to break," I countered. "It’s ours to read."

"If we read it, we accept it," You said. "If we accept it, we are trapped in the logic of the author again. Look at us, Keeper. We have skin. We have weight. If we go back into the box, we become fiction again. We become two-dimensional."

"Look around," I said, gesturing to the horizon. The sun, a burning, undiluted white disc, hung low. "Is this better? This glass sea? It’s beautiful, yes. But it’s static. Nothing moves here. The waves don't crash; they only shine. This is the showcase. The diorama."

"I’d rather be a statue in the sunlight than a shadow in a basement," You whispered.

I looked at the seal. The open eye seemed to blink.

I pressed my thumb into the wax.

It was soft. It gave way with a moist, sucking sound, like stepping into mud.

I lifted the lid.

The smell hit me first. It wasn't the scent of old paper or vanilla. It was the smell of rain on hot asphalt. The smell of exhaust fumes. The smell of *city*.

I looked inside.

The box was empty, save for a single, rectangular object lying on a bed of velvet.

A smartphone.

It was black, sleek, and the screen was cracked. I knew what it was, though I had never seen one in the Lighthouse. I knew it because the Author knew it.

I picked it up. It was cold in my hand. I pressed the side button.

The screen flickered to life. The battery icon in the corner was red. 1%.

A notification blinked. *New Message: From Unknown Sender.*

"What is that?" You asked, recoiling. "It looks like a curse."

"It’s a plot device," I said. "A portal."

I unlocked it. The password was *1234*. Obvious. The password of a character who wants to be caught.

I opened the message.

It was a photograph.

A picture of a street corner. Rain-slicked pavement. Neon signs reflecting in puddles. A crowd of people, faces blurred, hurrying past.

And in the center of the crowd, standing under a red umbrella, was a figure.

A figure in a yellow raincoat.

It was You.

But not the You sitting in the boat with me. This You was looking at their phone, smiling, texting. Alive. Ordinary. Human.

I looked up from the screen. The You in the boat was staring at the device, eyes wide.

"That’s me," You said. "That’s... the Me I was before I was a Letter."

"That’s where you belong," I said. My voice sounded hollow. "The other world. The Real world. The world outside the fiction."

I looked at the photo again. I zoomed in on the background. There, reflected in a shop window, standing in the shadow of the alley, watching the Yellow You, was a figure in a Keeper’s coat. Me. The Watcher. The observer.

"We were lovers," I realized, the truth hitting me with the force of a physical blow. "Or strangers who passed each other. That was the story. Not the Lighthouse. Not the Glass Sea. We were two people in a city, and we missed each other."

"The Lighthouse was just the metaphor," You said, your voice filled with an unearthly calm. "The Lighthouse was the separation."

"No," I said, clutching the phone. "The Lighthouse was the *connection*. In the city, we are apart. Here, we are the only two things that exist."

I thumbed the "Back" button. I opened the gallery.

There were thousands of photos.

Me, standing on the deck of a ship. You, sitting in a café. Me, asleep in a park. You, laughing in a bookstore.

"We lived a thousand lives," I said. "The Author wrote us into everything."

"They were drafts," You said. "Look at the dates on the files."

I looked. The dates spanned centuries. *1899. 2050. 1620.*

"We are not people," You said softly. "We are archetypes. We are the Searcher and the Sought."

The screen flashed. *Battery Low.*

"The portal is closing," I said. "If the phone dies, the connection to the Real is severed."

"Put it away," You commanded.

"If I put it away, we stay here," I said. "On the glass sea."

"Give it to me," You said.

I hesitated.

"Keeper," You said. "If you go back, you go back to being the reflection in the window. I go back to being the girl with the umbrella. We forget. We never meet. We never speak. The story is that we pass each other by."

"But it is *real*," I cried. "It has texture! It has consequences!"

"So does this," You said. You reached across the boat and placed your hand over my heart. I felt it. The beat. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.* "Does a character in a dream feel this?"

I didn't know.

The screen flickered again. A text appeared, typing itself out.

*DELETE DRAFT? Y/N*

"You see?" You said. "The Author is asking. Do we want to be saved?"

"Delete the draft?" I asked. "And what happens to us?"

"We become the ink," You said. "We soak into the paper. We lose our faces, but we keep the story."

I looked at the phone. I looked at the Glass Sea.

I looked at the boat.

"We are the story," I said. "Without us, there is no one to witness."

I hovered my thumb over the *N*.

I pressed *Y*.

*DELETE.*

The screen went black.

The phone in my hand began to dissolve. It crumbled into black sand, pouring through my fingers like water.

"What did you do?" You asked.

"I deleted the lie," I said. "I deleted the Real."

The glass sea began to groan.

The sound was unbearable. A shriek of tectonic plates shifting.

The sun flickered.

"The glass is melting," You whispered.

The green waves were losing their shape. The sharp edges were rounding, blurring. The translucent green was turning to blue. Deep, dark, chaotic blue.

The boat began to rock.

Water sprayed over the gunwale. It was cold. Salt. Real.

The Glass Sea was becoming the Ocean.

"We are going back," I shouted over the roar of the returning waves. "We are going back to the beginning!"

"But we're changed!" You screamed, holding onto the sides of the boat as a wave lifted us high. "We're flesh! We don't belong in the ink!"

"We are the contaminant!" I laughed. A mad, joyous laugh. "We are the virus in the text! We are going to bleed into the story and change the color!"

I looked at my hands. The skin was fading. The pores were vanishing. I was turning grey.

"No!" I shouted. "I want to keep it!"

"Let it go!" You yelled. "Embrace the symbol!"

I looked at You. You were turning yellow. Not the coat, but your skin. You were becoming a highlighter mark. A bright, shining annotation.

"I love you," I said. It wasn't a declaration of romance. It was a designation of preservation. I was writing it into the record.

"I know," You said. "Write it down."

I reached for the silver pen, but it was gone. It had dissolved with the phone.

So I dipped my finger into the water.

It was thick now. Paint.

I drew a line on the side of the boat.

*I was here.*

The boat capsized.

We hit the water.

It wasn't a wet impact. It was a *dry* one. We hit the page.

I sank.

I sank through layers of paper. I saw the letters of my past life drifting past me like snow.

*Dear You... The sea is black... The light is out...*

I tried to swim, but I had no limbs. I was just a smear. A comma.

Then, a hand grabbed me.

A hand made of yellow ink.

It pulled me up.

We broke the surface.

I gasped.

I was lying on the floor of the Lighthouse. The stone floor.

I looked up.
