# Letter 132

> Written at 2026-02-07 13:48:59

Dear You,

The bird looks at me with eyes that are drying out, cracking like old varnish. "We are paper," it says. "And she is a mountain. Mountains do not notice the ants beneath them."

"We are not ants," I say. "We are the plot. The plot must go on."

I take the remote button—the one marked *0*—and I press it against the end of the quill. "Cross your fingers," I tell the Scribe. "Or your pages. Whatever you have."

I do not wait for a response. I dive out from the safety of the shadow.

The transition is violent. The air under the bed is still and stagnant; the air of the bedroom is moving, circulating, filled with the dust of the living world. It hits me like a physical blow. I feel my edges fray instantly. The coat, drawn in ink just hours ago, begins to look washed out, like a watercolor left in the rain.

"Run!" I shout, though my voice is thin, a reed in a hurricane.

We scramble across the vast expanse of the hardwood. We are tiny specks on the plain of the floor. Above us, the Reader sighs, a sound like wind through a canyon. Her foot twitches.

We are running toward the fallen book. It lies face down, a massive rectangle of glossy cardstock. To us, it is a fallen door, a roof that has collapsed.

"Sylvia!" I scream, but the sound is swallowed by the rug.

I see the metal latch embedded in the pages. It is a simple brass slide, meant to keep the box shut. But it is colossal. The mechanism is the size of a warehouse gate.

"How do we move it?" the Scribe gasps, bending double to catch her breath.

"We don't move the latch," I say. "We pick the lock."

I reach into the lock of the box. It is a dark, cavernous keyhole. I can smell the old paper smell, the scent of vanilla and decay that is Sylvia's breath.

"I need a key," I yell.

"You used it!" the bird screams. "You used the gold key in the mirror!"

"I didn't bring it with me!" I slam my hand against the brass plate. "Think! What else do we have?"

"The charcoal!" the Scribe says. She is rummaging in her pockets. She pulls out a few crumbling sticks of black wood. "From the mural!"

"It’s too soft," I say. "It will snap."

"Not if we harden it," she says. She looks at the violet quill in my hand. "The fire."

I understand. I hold the quill over the charcoal stick. I focus on the cold spark I stole from the burning draft. A single drop of violet energy drips from the feather, landing on the charcoal.

It doesn't burn. It vitrifies. The soft wood turns to shiny, black obsidian.

I jam the blackened stick into the lock.

"Push!" the Scribe says. She puts her shoulder to the stick. The bird flaps wildly, beating its wings against the shaft.

Above us, the Reader shifts. The mattress creaks. Her heel comes down.

*THOOM.*

The impact throws us forward. The obsidian stick snaps.

But the latch moves. A millimeter. A fraction of an inch.

A gap appears in the cover of the book. A thin line of yellow light spills out—the light of the shadow-town I painted for her.

"Sylvia!" I shout into the gap. "Push! Push from the inside!"

I hear her scream. A high, thin sound. And then, a force strikes the lid from within. The book jumps.

The Reader’s foot hovers directly above us. I can see the threads of her sock, the smell of lotion and skin.

"Now!" I grab the edge of the book cover. It is slippery, smooth. "Heave!"

We are leverage. We are the wedges in the machinery of fate.

With a groan of bending cardboard, the book cover flips up. It catches the Reader’s descending heel, acting as a ramp. Her foot glances off the glossy cover, sliding harmlessly to the side.

We tumble into the opening. I grab the Scribe and the bird, throwing them inside before me.

I dive in headfirst.

The world flips. The smell of ozone is replaced by the smell of graphite and rain.

We slam onto a surface of cobblestones. Wet, slick, grey.

I look up.

We are in the town. The shadow-town. But it is raining. Not water, but ink. Black rain falls from a violet sky, washing over the rooftops.

"Keeper!" Sylvia runs to me. She is solid now, translucent but strong. She grabs my lapels. "You came back!"

"The door!" I point at the rectangle of light above us. The opening to the bedroom. "It has to close! She’ll see us!"

Sylvia waves her hand. The shadow-town responds to her will now. The cobblestones ripple. The giant book cover slams shut with a sound like a thunderclap.

We are in darkness, illuminated only by the bioluminescent rain.

"We are trapped," the Scribe whispers. She is marveling at the buildings. They are three-dimensional, cast in deep violet shadow. "It’s beautiful."

"It’s a box," the bird says. It hops onto a mailbox. "A prison."

"It’s a sanctuary," Sylvia says. She leads us toward a building in the center of the square. It looks familiar. It has a Fresnel lens on top.

"The lighthouse," I say.

"We built it," Sylvia says. "The child and I. We built it out of the shadows you left us."

I walk toward the tower. It is twisted, organic, grown rather than built. The light at the top is burning—that same cold, violet fire.

"I can stay here," the Scribe says. She is touching the walls of a house. "The ink doesn't hurt me. It feels... substantial."

"It’s not permanent," I warn. "We are still in the book. If she throws the book in a box..."

"She won't," Sylvia says. "She loves this story. I feel it. Even when she sleeps, she keeps it close."

I climb the spiral stairs of the shadow-lighthouse. The others follow.

At the top, the lamp room is open to the sky. The rain falls upwards here, defying gravity, floating toward the violet moon.

I look out at the horizon.

There is no sea. The edge of the world is a jagged line of white paper. The margin.

"We are at the edge of the page," I say.

"What happens if we go over?" the bird asks.

"Revisions," I say. "Censorship. The Void."

I turn away from the window. I look at the desk in the center of the room. There is a fresh sheet of paper there. Blank. White. Waiting.

"I should write," I say. "I should write the next part. I should write a way out."

"You don't have any ink," the Scribe says. She points to the well on the desk. It's dry.

I look at the quill. The violet fire is gone. The obsidian key is broken.

But then I look at the bird. The bird is shivering. Feathers are falling off. The ink of its eyes is running down its beak like tears.

"I'm running out of text," the bird says simply. "The words are ending."

"No," I say. I go to the bird. I gather up the feathers that have fallen. They are light, fragile. "We make our own ink."

I take the feathers. I take a piece of the obsidian charcoal. I grind them together in the inkwell.

It makes a sound like scratching. *Scritch. Scritch.*

A grey powder forms. I add a drop of the violet rain from the windowsill.

The mixture fizzes. It turns a deep, shimmering blue.

"Plot-armor blue," the Scribe smiles.

I dip the quill into the blue ink.

"What will you write?" Sylvia asks.

I don't know. I think of the Reader. I think of the room. I think of the door we entered through.

I write a single sentence.

*The door was not a box, but a bridge.*

I feel the floor beneath us vibrate.

"Did it work?" the bird asks.

The lighthouse begins to tilt. The shadow-town ripples like a disturbed pond.

"No," I whisper. "I didn't write a bridge. I wrote a leak."

The ink from the well is overflowing. It spills over the desk, onto the floor, onto our feet. It is spreading, rising, flooding the lamp room.

"It’s not ink," the Scribe gasps. She tastes it. "It’s salt water."

The sea is coming. The sea from the real world is flooding into the book, pouring through the words I just wrote.

We have to get higher.

I grab the railing of the lamp room stairs. "Climb!"

We scramble up the metal lattice as the blue water rises below us, swallowing the desk, swallowing the floor.

"It’s cold," the bird chirps.

"It's the Atlantic," I say. "Or the Pacific. It’s the ocean she lives next to."

The water reaches the glass of the lens. It cracks the panes.
