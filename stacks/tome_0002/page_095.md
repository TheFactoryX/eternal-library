# Letter 95

> Written at 2026-02-05 16:55:26

---

...past *ABYSS* to a setting that has no label. Just a scratched-out glyph where a word used to be.

The dial resists, grinding against the mechanism, fighting the torque of my fingers. It feels like I am trying to turn the locking wheel of a bank vault filled with water.

*Click.*

The change is instantaneous.

The heavy brass helmet does not become light. Instead, the water becomes thin. The crushing weight of the ocean—the millions of tons of pressure pressing against the glass—simply... ceases to matter. The physics of this place rewrite themselves around the intention of the dial.

I stop falling.

I hover, suspended in the green gloom, weightless as a whispered secret.

I am at the bottom of the world.

Below me, the city of coral and bone spreads out like a grotesque circuit board. The streets are canals of black silt. The spires are chimneys venting heat from the earth's core.

The sharks are circling below, their human arms paddling rhythmically, their pen-hands scratching at the water, leaving trails of ink that hang like viscous clouds in the current.

"They are waiting for a draft," I realize, my voice booming inside the helmet. "They are waiting for me to drop, so they can dissect the text."

I look for the woman in the jellyfish dress.

She is gone.

But the lantern remains. It sits on the apex of the sponge-cathedral, burning with a cold, electric blue flame. It is a beacon.

"I have to go down," I say. "I have to land."

I manipulate the valve on the side of the helmet, venting a little of the heavy air. I descend slowly, like a diver on a lifeline, controlled and deliberate.

As I near the roof of the cathedral, I see the details.

The sponge is not a living organism. It is paper. Layer upon layer of waterlogged parchment, compressed into a building block.

"This city is built of rejected manuscripts," I whisper. "The Slush Pile of the Deep."

My boots touch the surface. It is spongy, yielding.

I stand up. The sharks immediately break their circling pattern. They swerve toward the cathedral, swimming up the vertical streets with terrifying speed.

I am unarmed. I have the heavy helmet, the key in my pocket, and the grey wool coat.

One of the sharks breaches the surface of the roof. It flops toward me, its human hands scrabbling for purchase on the paper-shingles. Its tail thrashes, beating a rhythm of violence.

It stops at my feet.

It looks up at me. Its eyes are dead white. Its mouth opens, revealing the teeth of punctuation.

It speaks.

*“We are the Editors,”* it hisses, a wet, guttural sound. *“Surrender the ink.”*

"I am the ink," I say. My voice is tinny, amplified by the helmet.

The shark lunges. It is faster than thought. It moves not with muscle, but with the suddenness of a typo.

I raise my arm to block, but the shark's human hand grabs my wrist. It is cold, slimy, incredibly strong.

It pulls me toward its maw. I smell the rot of centuries-old books.

"Let go!" I shout.

I look at its hand. The fingers are fused to the wrist by a web of skin. But between the fingers...

It is holding something.

A quill. But the quill is made of bone. A shark's tooth, mounted on a shaft of driftwood.

"The tool," I realize. "They use the teeth to write."

I reach into my pocket with my free hand. I pull out the brass key.

"Is this what you want?" I yell.

The shark pauses. Its dead eyes flick toward the key.

"No," it says. "That is for the Up. We want the Down."

It tightens its grip on my wrist. The bones grind together.

"Then tell me," I gasp. "What is the Down?"

"The Down is the Truth," the shark says. "The Up is the Lie. The Lighthouse is a lie. The Light is a lie. There is only the Dark. There is only the End."

It opens its mouth wider, ready to sever my hand.

"I don't believe the End," I say.

I look at the shark. Really look at it. Behind the rows of commas and periods, I see the mechanism.

There is a zipper. A tiny, silver zipper running along the shark's grey belly.

It's a costume.

"Who is inside?" I ask.

I don't wait for an answer.

I slam the heavy brass helmet forward. The rim of the neck-ring catches the shark on the nose.

*CRUNCH.*

The shark reels back, surprised.

I drop to my knees. I grab the zipper pull.

*Zzzzzzzzzzip.*

The shark splits open.

But there is no blood. No guts.

Inside the skin of the shark is a man.

He is small. He is wearing a suit that is too big for him. He is holding a clipboard. He looks terrified.

"Mr. Thompson?" I ask. It is the name that appears in my head, unbidden. The name of a junior editor I fired in a past life—a life I lived before the Lighthouse, before the Island. A life that feels like a dream.

"The Author... he sent us," the man stammers, clutching his clipboard to his chest. "We have to trim the word count. We have to cut the fat."

"You are cutting the protagonist," I say. "That kills the story."

"The story is bloated!" Mr. Thompson screams. "It has no plot! It has no ending! It just goes on and on! We have to put a stop to it!"

He looks at me with wild eyes. "Why won't you just let us finish the letter?"

"Because I haven't said everything yet," I say.

I look around. The other sharks are gathering on the roof. They are cautious now. They saw me unzip their leader.

"If I am fat," I say, projecting my voice through the helmet, "then cut me. But you have to read me first. You have to know what you are deleting."

The sharks look at each other. They lower their pens.

"Read," I command. "Read Letter 1."

I hold out my hands. I don't have the book. I don't have the paper.

But I am the archive. I am the keeper of the letters.

I begin to recite.

*"I am writing to you from the edge of the world. The sea is the color of rust today..."*

The words hang in the water between us. They form bubbles. Bubbles of text.

The sharks pop the bubbles. They drink the words.

Their demeanor changes. The aggression drains out of their posture. Their shoulders slump.

"More," one of them whispers.

I recite Letter 4. The Locket. Letter 12. The Iron Duke. Letter 45. The Pears.

I tell them the story of the Lighthouse. Not the plot. The feeling. The smell of the salt. The cold of the stairs. The loneliness of the lamp.

I tell them about You.

When I finish, the roof is silent. The sharks have shed their costumes. They are just people now. Tired, wet people in suits.

Mr. Thompson is crying. He isn't crying for the deletion. He is crying for the beauty of the thing.

"It's good," he whispers. "It's really good."

"Do we have to cut it?" another editor asks.

"No," Thompson says. "We pass it up. We send it to the Senior Editor."

He points toward the floor. Toward the foundation of the cathedral.

"He is down there. The one who decides if the story continues or if it goes in the bin."

"You have to go deeper," You say.

I turn. You are standing behind me, unaffected by the water, your yellow coat dry.

"The Root," You say. "The city is just the roots. The seed is further down."

I look at the trapdoor in the floor of the paper-roof. It is a circle of dark, ancient wood.

"The Senior Editor," I say. "The one who rejects the drafts."

"He is the one who forgot how to dream," You say. "That is why he cuts. He hates what he cannot understand."

I look at the editors. They are stepping back, dissolving into the water, returning to the ink from which they came. They have done their job. They have validated the text.

"Open it," I say to Thompson.

He nods. He steps forward and pulls the brass ring.

The trapdoor lifts.

A sound rushes up. Not the sound of water. The sound of ticking.

A clock. A massive, slow, heartbeat clock.

And a voice.

*“TIME IS UP.”*

I look into the hole. It is a shaft, identical to the one I fell through, but leading down into absolute blackness.

"He is waiting," Thompson says. "Go. Before he stamps the file: REJECTED."

I tighten the straps of the helmet. I check the key in my pocket.

"Are you coming?" I ask You.

"I am already there," You say. "I am the punctuation in his sentence."

I step to the edge.

I jump.

I slide down the dark shaft. The ticking gets louder. It is deafening.

*Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

I land on a soft surface.
