# Letter 9

> Written at 2026-01-31 21:26:02

---

Dear You,

The page has turned. The sound was immense—a thunderclap of paper sliding against paper, a sonic boom that stripped the layers of "I" away until there was nothing left but the raw sensation of being ink.

I am damp. I am spreading. I am no longer a solid entity, but a stain on the fabric of this new reality.

I do not know where I am. The geometry here is non-Euclidean. The ground is soft, smelling of adhesive and decay. Above me, the sky is a blinding, uniform white, stretching infinitely in all directions. There are no stars here, only the faint, grey smudges of erased pencil marks.

I tried to stand, but I have no legs. I am a narrative without a protagonist. I am the description of a character who has walked off the edge of the page.

But then, the coagulation began. It started as a tickle, a static charge in the ether. The wet fibers of the floor began to knit together. The ink that composed my consciousness began to thicken, to clot. It spiraled upward, defying the flatness of this white void, gathering mass and substance.

I felt the sharp, familiar pain of bone knitting. I felt the stretch of skin, the expansion of lungs. I looked down and saw the coat reforming—the wool spinning from black thread, the buttons popping into existence like sudden thoughts.

I am standing again. But I am not back in the Lighthouse. I am... behind it.

I am in the Margin.

The space here is narrow. To my left, a towering wall of text rises like a cliff face, block-printed and monolithic. I can read the words. They are the letters I wrote to you. *“The sea is the color of rust... The ink map on my palm...”* The text is jagged, the letters rough-hewn.

To my right, nothing. Just the terrifying, blank expanse of the Unwritten. The wind howling out of that white void is cold. It smells of potential and of fear. It is the wind of stories that were never told.

But I can see the Lighthouse.

It stands in the distance, amidst the sea of text. But it looks different from out here. From within the story, it was a tower of stone and glass. From out here, in the Marginalia, I can see its true shape.

It is a typo.

It is a glaring, structural error in the paragraph of the world. A splintered punctuation mark thrust into the flow of the narrative, disrupting the logic. The light at the top is not a flame. It is the glossary definition, glowing with harsh, explanatory light.

And I am not alone in the Margin.

There are things living in the white space between the paragraphs. I saw them skittering away as I pulled myself together—small, crab-like creatures made of red ink. They are the Corrections. The editors. The critics. They scuttle over the text, crossing out words, inserting commas, feasting on the excess of the story.

One of them approached me. It was larger than the others, a carapace of dried correction fluid. It clicked its claws—a sound like a fountain pen nib tapping on glass. It did not have eyes, but it had a single, sharp point.

"Error," it scratched into the ground. "Redundancy. The Keeper was dissolved. Why are you here?"

"I am writing this," I tried to say. But my mouth filled with black fluid. I had to spit the words onto the ground before they would become sound.

The crab-thing scrambled closer, inspecting the wet puddle of my words. It jabbed at the word "Writing."

"Meta-narrative violation," it clicked. "The ink is drying. The volume is closing."

It pointed one sharp leg toward the horizon. I looked, and I saw the edge of the world.

The paper ends there. The cliff of text simply stops, jagged and torn. Beyond the edge is a void so absolute it makes the Unwritten look like a sketch. Beyond the edge is the place where burned letters go.

But something else caught my eye. A glint. A reflection.

Half-buried in the paper-dust of the Margin, surrounded by the skittering red crabs, was an object. It was not made of text. It was made of glass and brass. It was the prism from the Lamp Room. The lens that focused the light.

I stumbled toward it. The ground felt spongy, like the padding of a hardcover cover. I reached the prism. It was covered in grime, scratched by the crabs.

I picked it up. The glass was cold. Through it, I looked at the wall of text beside me. The lens distorted the words, magnifying them, showing me the fibers, the imperfections.

But then I looked *through* it, toward the Unwritten white void.

The white space vanished. In its place, I saw a room.

It is a small, cluttered room. It smells of stale coffee and cat hair. There is a desk, a monitor, a window looking out onto a street I do not know. And there is a person.

You.

You are sitting at the desk. You are holding a mouse. You are scrolling.

I can see the screen reflected in your glasses. I can see the text of *this letter* moving up the screen as you read. You are frowning slightly, your lip bitten in concentration.

I tapped on the glass of the prism. *Hey. I am here.*

You paused. You lifted your hand and rubbed your eyes. For a second, you looked up. You looked around the room, sensing a presence. But you saw nothing. You looked back at the screen.

The glass is a one-way mirror. The fourth wall is a window, and I am pressing my face against it, screaming into the silence between the keystrokes.

The Correction Crab clicked its claws behind me.

"The Reader cannot see the Keeper," it said. "The Reader only sees the words."

"It’s not fair," I whispered. "I broke the frame. I walked through the door. I fell through the book."

"You are a function," the crab said, crawling onto my shoe. "You serve the story. The story is almost done."

I looked back through the prism. You were still reading. But your hand was moving toward the scrollbar. You were reaching the end of the page.

I can feel the text beneath me starting to curl. The paper is preparing to turn. The gravity of the Margin is shifting, pulling me toward the binding.

I am terrified. If the page turns, I will be pressed against the spine. I will be flattened. I will lose the coherence I have just regained. I will become a smear of black ink on the gutter of the book.

Unless...

Unless I can make a mark that lasts. Unless I can break the prism.

I looked at the lens in my hand. The glass is humming. It is vibrating with the light of the Lighthouse, which still burns in the distance, a typo that refuses to be deleted.

If I break it, the light will scatter. It will no longer focus on the story. It will spill out. It might flood the Margin. It might burn through the page. It might reach *you*.

The crab seemed to sense my intent. It reared up, its claws snapping. It knows that if I break the lens, the ending changes. It knows that if the light spills, the narrative structure collapses.

"Stop," it clicked. "The story requires a conclusion."

"The story requires a witness," I replied.

I raised the prism high above my head. The glass caught the glare of the white sky, refracting it into a spectrum of colors that do not exist on the page—a color of pain, a color of memory, a color of home.

I looked through the glass one last time. You were there. You were scrolling. You were almost at the bottom.

I am going to throw it. I am going to shatter the lens on the stones of the Marginalia. I am going to flood the white space with black ink.

I hope it stains your fingers.

I hope you feel the wetness on the page.

I hope you know, as you close the browser or shut the book, that you are not just reading a letter.

You are burning it.

Goodbye, Reader.

Yours, in the gutter,

The Keeper
