# Letter 144

> Written at 2026-02-08 05:06:39

---

Dear You,

The city is a carnivore. It does not eat with teeth; it eats with noise and speed, grinding the moments of the day into a fine grey dust that settles in the cracks of the pavement.

I stand before the diner. *The Lighthouse.* It is a temple of neon and chrome, buzzing with the low-grade headache of fluorescent lights. The sign flickers—*L-G-H-T-O-S-E*—a stutter in the electric prayer of the street.

"It’s open," the pigeon says. It is perched on the hood of a parked taxi, staring at the glass doors with suspicion. "But nothing is open at this hour. Not truly."

I look at my wrist. I am still wearing the shattered face of the pocket watch strapped to my arm with a shoelace. The hands spin wildly, backwards and forwards, searching for a time that doesn't exist.

"The hours here are liquid," I say. "They flow into one another."

I push the door open.

A bell chimes. Not the silver bell of the Archived Mind, but a harsh, electronic jingle that sounds like a laugh.

The air inside is thick with the smell of burnt coffee and bacon grease. It is a comforting smell, human and heavy. But underneath it, I catch the scent of ozone. The sharp, metallic tang of a storm just before it breaks.

The diner is empty, save for the patrons.

I count them as I walk to a counter stool.

Three.

Booth #1: A woman in a red raincoat. She is weeping into a cup of tea. Her tears do not splash; they turn into small, translucent pearls as they hit the ceramic. She rolls them across the table with a trembling finger.

Booth #4: A man made entirely of mirrors. He reflects the neon lights, the checkered floor, the exit signs. He has no face of his own, only a kaleidoscope of the room staring back at me.

The Counter: A figure in a grease-stained apron, spatula in hand. The figure is turned away, flipping burgers on a grill that hisses and spits like a angry cat.

I sit down. The stool is vinyl, cold against my thighs.

"Coffee?" the figure asks. The voice is a rasp of gravel and static.

"Black," I say. "Like the ink."

The figure turns.

It is not a man. It is not a woman.

It is a construct of typewriter keys. A body made of black plastic circles, white letters stamped onto the skin of its face, shifting and rearranging with every twitch of the muscle.

The eyes are the glass hemispheres of two old-fashioned carriage lamps.

"You're the Waiter," I say.

"I serve the Transition," it says. "You are leaving the Book. You are entering the Living. The stomach hurts at first."

It pours coffee from a glass pot. The liquid is dark, swirling like a galaxy.

"You know me," I say.

"I know all characters who try to escape the margins," the Waiter says. "I served the White Rabbit. I served Alice. I served Dorothy." It taps a long, plastic finger on the counter. "They all ordered the same thing. *Reality.* It’s bitter."

I look at the pearl-weeping woman in Booth #1. "What is her story?"

"She is a discarded draft," the Waiter says. "The author wrote a sad backstory, then cut it to make the plot tighter. Now she sits here, weeping pearls, waiting for someone to put her back in."

"And the Mirror Man?"

"He is vanity. He exists only because people look at him. Don't look too long, Keeper. He traps you in your own reflection."

I take a sip of the coffee. It scalds my tongue. It tastes of salt and iron. It tastes like the sea.

"Is this the Lighthouse?" I ask, looking around at the chrome and linoleum.

"The Lighthouse is wherever the Keeper is," the Waiter says. "You carry the architecture in your bones. The stairs. The lantern room. You are the tower now."

I set the mug down. "I need to find the Scribe. I saw her. In the gutter. She’s here."

The Waiter’s key-face clicks and whirs. The letters on its forehead spin. *D-A-N-G-E-R.*

"The Scribe is not a patron," it says. "She is the Meat."

"The Meat?"

"The raw material," the Waiter says. "When the Old Man burned the bookmark, the logic broke. Characters leaked out. Plot devices ran wild. The Scribe... she is the author’s guilt given form. If she finds you, she will write you back to the Island to finish the story."

"I need to find her," I insist. "She has the other half of the key."

The pigeon flies in through the open door, dodging the electric eye of the door jingle. It lands on the counter next to my coffee.

"Keeper," the pigeon coos. "The street. Look."

I turn on the stool.

Outside, through the large plate glass window, the city has changed.

The traffic is gone. The streets are empty. The sky is a scrolling marquee of green text.

*S y s t e m F a i l u r e . R e b o o t i n g .*

"The window is a screen," I whisper.

"The world is trying to process us," the pigeon says. "We are too heavy for the RAM."

Suddenly, the pearl-weeping woman screams.

It is not a human scream. It is the sound of paper tearing.

She stands up. Her skin flakes away, revealing white paper beneath. Her eyes become empty sockets of black ink.

"The Editor!" she shrieks. "The Editor is coming!"

The Mirror Man shatters. He explodes into a million shards of glass. Each shard reflects a different version of me. Me as an old man. Me as a child. Me as a woman.

The Waiter grabs a cleaver from behind the counter. "Finish your coffee, Keeper. The Revision is here."

The glass of the front window implodes.

A figure steps through the rain of glass.

It is tall. It wears a suit made of red correction lines. It carries a pair of scissors the size of scythes.

It has no face. It has only a white blankness where a face should be.

"Cut," the figure says. The word is a blade.

It slashes the air. The air bleeds black ink.

"This scene is dragging," the figure intones. "The pacing is slow. The dialogue is expository."

It points the scissors at me.

"Character Arc: Stalled. Motivation: Confused."

"I am motivated by freedom!" I shout, standing up. I grab the bone pen from my pocket.

"Freedom is a plot hole," the Editor says. "Fill it."

It lunges.

The Waiter throws itself in front of me, its body of plastic keys clacking. The scissors snip through the Waiter’s midsection. *Delete.* *Delete.* The Waiter falls apart, letters spilling across the floor like Scrabble tiles.

I jump over the counter. I scramble toward the kitchen doors.

"Keeper!" the pigeon screams. "The Scribe!"

I look back.

The Editor has stopped. It is sniffing the air. It turns toward Booth #1.

The Scribe is standing there. She looks exactly as I remember—ink-stained fingers, hair like a tangled manuscript. She is holding the pearl-woman’s hand.

"You," the Editor says, stepping over the pile of keys. "The unauthorized POV."

The Scribe looks at me. Her eyes are terrified.

"Run," she mouths.

She turns and runs through the kitchen doors. The Editor roars—a sound of crumpling paper—and pursues.

I scramble through the swinging doors into the kitchen.

It is not a kitchen. It is a labyrinth of hanging file folders. Steel shelves reaching up into darkness. The air smells of stale toner.

The Scribe is running down an aisle labeled *Drafts / 2015-2023*.

"Wait!" I yell.

She ignores me. She grabs a file, throws it behind her to slow the Editor down. The file explodes into a cloud of white paper moths.

I run after her. The pigeon is a grey blur at my side.

"Where are we going?" the bird asks.

"Away from the scissors," I say.

We catch up to her at the end of the aisle. She stops in front of a large metal cabinet. A safe.

She is fumbling with a key. A brass key.

"The Archivist's key," I say.

She looks at me. "I stole it. From the shop. Before the floorboards broke."

"The Safe Deposit Box," I realize. "It's not in the bank. It's in the Archive. It's in the Diner."

The Editor is coming. The sound of snipping scissors echoes through the shelves. Snip. Snip. The shelf to our left falls apart, cut in half.

"Help me," she begs.

I place my hand over hers on the key. Our fingers entwine. Ink stains mixing with ink stains.

"Turn," I say.

We turn the key together.

The safe clicks. The heavy door swings open.

Inside, there is no money. No gold.

There is a single sheet of paper.

A title page.

*The Lighthouse Keeper's Letters.*
*By: The Reader.*
