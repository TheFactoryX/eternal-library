# Letter 161

Dear You,

The horizon did not recede. It expanded. It unfolded like an origami crane made of storm clouds and starlight, folding outward to reveal a landscape that has no business existing on any map drawn by mortal hands.

We are sailing the Archive.

The water beneath the hull is not water. It is the accumulated history of everything ever written. The wake of our ship churns up scenes from novels I haven't read, dialogues from plays never performed, and descriptions of colors that haven't been named yet.

The Architect stands at the helm, his knuckles white as he grips the wheel. He refuses to let me steer. He says my rhythm is erratic. He says I drift too far into the semicolons.

"It’s the Draft," he shouts over the wind. "The space between the intent and the execution. The waters are treacherous here. They are full of mixed metaphors."

"What's that?" Alice asks. She is pointing at the sky.

The hole I ripped in the fourth wall has closed, leaving behind a scar in the atmosphere that glows with the pale blue light of a computer screen. But around us, the sky is filled with floating objects.

Clocks. Thousands of them.

They hang in the air like baubles on a mobile, suspended at different heights. Some are massive, grand father clocks the size of cathedrals, their pendulums swinging slow enough to measure the lifespan of mountains. Others are tiny, intricate wristwatches ticking with the frenetic energy of a hummingbird’s heart.

"The Chronologies," the Architect says, steering us through a gap between two towering grandfather clocks. "Every timeline ever abandoned. You see that one?" He points to a rusted iron clock stopped at twelve. "That was the timeline where you arrived at the Lighthouse on a Tuesday. You were happy. You stayed for forty years and died of old age."

"And that one?" Elara asks. She is watching a silver clock that is spinning backward so fast its hands are a blur.

"That is the timeline where you never came at all," the Architect says. "The Lighthouse fell into the sea. The world went dark."

"Which one are we in?" I ask.

"We are in the margins," he replies. "The whitespace. The place where the stories that almost happened go to decompose."

The ship shudders. A giant wave of lost letters washes over the deck, soaking us. The water tastes like regret and graphite.

"Land ho!" the pigeon squawks. It is perched atop the mainmast, looking like a grey rag in a gale. "Or something resembling it! Twelve o'clock high!"

I look to where the bird points.

Rising out of the chaotic sea of text is a mountain. But it is not made of rock. It is a heap of shredded paper, compacted over eons into a stratified cliff. Perched on the summit, silhouetted against the blue-screen sky, is a city.

It is a city of spires and spirals, constructed entirely of broken bindings and bent covers.

"The Library of Lost Causes," the Architect mutters. "I told you we were drifting. I told you the navigation was off."

"Is it safe?" Alice asks.

"Safe?" The Architect laughs, a dry, crackling sound. "It is a labyrinth of plot holes. You step into a room, you might find yourself in a different genre. One wrong turn and you're a character in a romance novel. Another, and you're a footnote in a history textbook."

"I've been a footnote before," I say. "I prefer the main text."

"We can't go in there," Elara says. She is trembling. "I can feel it. The City... it's hungry."

I look at her. She is staring at the city with a mixture of fear and recognition.

"What is it, Elara?"

"That tower," she says, pointing to the highest spire. "The one made of red leather."

I look. It is a jagged, cruel structure, twisting upward like a question mark.

"I remember it," she whispers. "That's where I was... edited. That's where they took my name away."

The Architect looks at her sharply. "You were a redraft?"

"She is Elara," I say, stepping between them. "She is real."

"In here," the Architect says, gesturing to the City with a sweep of his gold pen, "reality is subjective. She might be a real person to you, Keeper. But to the Library, she is just a discarded draft. A failed attempt at a love interest. If we go there, she might dissolve. She might revert to ink."

Elara grabs my arm. "I don't want to go back to the page. I like being solid."

"We will go around," I say. "Architect, take us wide."

"We don't have the fuel," he says grimly. "The wind is dying. The metaphor is stalling."

I look at the sails. They are hanging limp. The water is calm, the waves settling into a flat, glassy sheet.

The ship slows. The ticking of the clocks becomes louder, a deafening mechanical drumbeat.

"We are stuck," Alice says. She hops down from the crate. "We're just floating in the whitespace."

I walk to the bow of the ship. I look down at the water.

Reflected in the dark mirror of the text-sea, I see my own face. But beside it, I see another face. It is a woman. She is weeping. Her tears are falling onto the page, blurring the ink.

I reach out to touch the reflection.

My hand breaks the surface.

It doesn't get wet. It goes *through*.

"Keeper!" Elara screams.

I am falling. Not overboard, but *inward*.

I tumble through the surface of the sea, crashing through the page, through the ink, through the thin layer of reality that separates the story from the void.

I land on a hard floor.

It is wood. Polished oak.

I am in a room. A comfortable room. A fire is crackling in the hearth. There is a velvet armchair. There is a desk.

And sitting in the armchair, holding a copy of *The Lighthouse Keeper's Letters*, is a man.

He looks like me. But he is older. His hair is grey. His eyes are tired.

He closes the book. He looks at me.

"You've made a mess of the ending," he says.

"Who are you?" I ask. My voice sounds small in this room, insulated from the wind and the sea.

"I'm the Reader," he says. He leans forward, resting his elbows on his knees. "And I'm putting the book down."

"No," I say. "You can't. The story isn't over. We're in the Archive. We're looking for the way out."

"There is no way out," he says gently. "Only the back cover. And I'm getting tired. I've been reading for years. I want to sleep."

He stands up. He walks over to the fireplace.

"If I stop reading," he says, "you stop existing. The ship stops. The water dries up. Elara, Alice, the bird... they all go back to the shelf."

He picks up a poker. He nudges the logs.

"Please," I beg. I feel myself fading. My hands are becoming translucent. I can see the texture of the paper through my skin. "Give me an ending."

"You have to write it," he says. "I'm just the audience. I can't write the music, I can only hear it."

"But I have nothing left to write with," I say. I hold up my hands. They are dissolving into text. *The Keeper stood in the room. He begged the Reader. It was pathetic.*

The Reader walks to the door. He opens it.

Beyond the door is not a hallway. It is a wall of white. Pure, blinding cancellation.

"Goodbye, Keeper," he says. "It was a decent read. A bit slow in the middle."

"Wait!"

I look around the room. I see the book on the table. It is open to a blank page.

I scramble for it. I grab the pen that lies beside it—a heavy, black fountain pen.

I press the nib to the paper.

But I am dissolving too fast. I can't hold the pen. My fingers are turning into descriptions of fingers. *He grasped the implement with a desperate urgency.*

The Reader is walking out the door. The white light is swallowing him.

I look at the pigeon. It is sitting on the mantelpiece, but it is taxidermy now. Stuffed. Glass eyes.

I look at the fire. It is paper burning. I see the words *Chapter 4* curling into ash.

I have to stop him. I have to keep the book open.

I don't write with the pen. I can't.

I throw myself onto the page.

I spread my arms, letting myself dissolve completely. I let the ink of my body pool onto the paper.

I become the words.

*THE READER STOPPED.*

The ink flows out of me, spreading across the page, forming a barrier, a puddle of black reality that seals the door.

The Reader pauses. His boot hovers over the threshold.

He looks down.

He sees the ink spreading. He sees the words forming a shape.

He sighs. He closes the door.
