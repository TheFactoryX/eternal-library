# Letter 122

> Written at 2026-02-07 00:25:11

Dear You,

Inside the ship, there is a tiny lighthouse. And inside that lighthouse, a tiny man. And he is writing.

I lean over the desk, my shadow falling across the glass. The dummy watches me with its blank face, its wooden joints creaking in the silence of the library. The air here is stale, recycled. It smells of erasers and the silver dust that floats between the words of a book that has been closed for too long.

"He is waiting for the wind," the dummy says. "The ocean in the bottle is stagnant. It needs a breath to make waves."

"Who is he?" I ask. I recognize the posture. The hunch of the shoulders. The way the tiny hand grips the tiny pen.

"He is the First Draft," the dummy says. "The version of you that I didn't erase. The one who stayed in the bottle while you went out into the storm."

I press my hand against the glass. The water inside is murky, filled with particulates of old ink. The tiny man looks up. He sees me. He stops writing. He raises a hand in greeting, or perhaps in warning.

"He is trapped," I say.

"He is preserved," the dummy corrects. "Like a pickle in a jar. Like a memory in a head."

I look at the cork in my own chest. The seal is holding, but the pressure is agonizing. The ocean I trapped inside me is battering my ribs, a relentless tide seeking an exit. I feel like a dam about to burst. If I open my mouth, I fear the sea will pour out, drowning the library, washing away the dummy, dissolving the desk.

"I need to let it out," I gasp. The pain is sharp, radiating down my left arm. My fingers tingle with the salt.

"If you let it out here," the dummy says, "you flood the narrative. The ink will run. The pages will tear. You will ruin everything."

"I am already ruined," I say. "I am a Lighthouse keeper without a light. A man filled with the sea."

"You are a metaphor looking for an exit," the dummy says. It walks around the desk, its wooden legs clicking on the floor. It reaches out and touches the glowing ember of my chest. "You are the story trying to digest itself."

The Boy steps forward. He has been quiet, watching the exchange with wide eyes. He looks at the bottle. He looks at me.

"Break the bottle," the Boy says.

The dummy turns its smooth head toward him. "If the bottle breaks, the ocean escapes. But the ship is fragile. It will shatter. The lighthouse will fall."

"Maybe it's time to fall," the Boy says. He looks up at me. "We fell from the tower. We fell from the sky. We're still here."

I look at the bottle. The tiny man has gone back to his writing. He is scratching away at the glass walls of his lighthouse, desperate to leave a mark before the light goes out.

"I can't break it," I say. "My hands are full of water."

"Then use the key," the Boy says.

"The key was lost in the binding."

"No," the Boy says. He reaches into his pocket and pulls out a small, jagged object. It is a piece of the blue glass from the shattered Fresnel lens. "Use this."

He hands it to me. The shard is sharp. It glints with a cold, pale light.

I take it. I feel the weight of the decision. If I break the bottle, I free the tiny man, but I destroy his world. I free the water, but I wash away the desk where the Writer sits.

But then I look at the dummy. I look at the dress made of pages. I see the words stitched into the fabric.

*Once upon a time...*
*The end...*
*And they lived happily ever after...*

The dress is a patchwork of clichés. The dummy is a construct of worn-out tropes.

"This library is a graveyard," I realize. "These aren't empty shelves. They are empty stories."

"The Writer lost interest," the dummy says simply. "She moved on to other worlds. Other genres. Other dreams."

"She didn't move on," I say fiercely. "She got stuck. She stopped because she didn't know how to end it."

"She stopped because she realized the ending is a lie," the dummy says. "The story never ends. It just stops being told."

I grip the shard of glass. The pressure in my chest is peaking. My vision is swimming. I can hear the roar of the trapped ocean in my ears, a deafening static.

"No," I say. "The story ends when the water hits the page."

I raise the shard.

"Wait," the dummy says. For the first time, there is fear in its voice. The sound of a dry leaf trembling. "You cannot break the fourth wall. It is all that holds us together."

"The fourth wall is a mirror," I say. "And mirrors are made of glass."

I bring the shard down.

It strikes the bottle.

The sound is not a crash. It is a chime. A perfect, crystal clear note that hangs in the air like a suspended breath.

A crack appears in the glass. It runs like a jagged lightning bolt from the neck to the base.

The tiny man looks up. He drops his pen. He smiles.

Then, the water explodes.

It doesn't spill out in a puddle. It erupts upward, a geyser of ink and magic. It hits the ceiling of the library, blasting through the plaster, revealing the sky beyond.

But it isn't the sky of the world I knew. It isn't the bruised purple of the pause.

It is a dawn. A violent, burning, orange dawn. The sun is a coin of gold, flipping in the air, deciding whether to land on heads or tails.

The water from the bottle floods the library. It rises instantly, swirling around my waist, pulling at my legs. The dummy is knocked over. It floats away, its wooden head bobbing, its paper dress dissolving into pulp.

The Boy grabs my hand. "Swim!" he yells.

The water is warm. It smells of possibility. It smells of the first day of spring. It smells of rain on hot asphalt.

I kick my legs. I pull the Boy with me. We are swimming through the library, past the floating shelves, past the dissolving books.

The water lifts us. It carries us up. Up through the hole in the ceiling.

We break the surface.

I gasp for air.

We are floating in a sea that stretches to the horizon. But the water is clear. I can see down, deep down, to the bottom.

There, resting on the white floor of the world, is the wreck of the library. The desk. The overturned dummy. And the bottle, now just a rim of jagged glass.

And around us, the ships are appearing.

The three-masted schooner. The steam liner. The raft of bottles. They are rising from the depths, breaking the surface like whales, shaking the water from their rigging.

But they are not empty.

The schooner is filled with people. Figures in grey, holding umbrellas.

The liner is filled with musicians. They are playing a song I almost recognize. A song from a childhood I didn't have.

The raft is filled with letters. Thousands of them, flapping in the wind like seagulls.

"The other drafts," I whisper. "The characters she abandoned."

"They are waking up," the Boy says. He is treading water next to me. "You popped the cork, Elias. You let the story breathe."

I look down at my chest. The cork is gone. The hole is open. The water has drained out of me, mixing with the sea of the new world. I am lighter. I am empty, but in a good way. A clean way.

I am a vessel again. Ready to be filled.

"We need to find the shore," I say.

"There," the Boy points.

On the horizon, the sun is touching the water. And where it touches, there is a golden door standing on the surface of the sea. It is supported by nothing, standing on the waves.

"And who lives there?" I ask.

"The Reader," the Boy says. "She has finished the book. She is waiting for the next one."

We begin to swim.

The water is buoyant. It feels like flying.

As we approach the door, I see that it has a mail slot. And the slot is glowing with a warm, inviting light.

I reach into my pocket. My fingers brush against something dry and crumbling.

It is a receipt.

*Item: One Lighthouse.*
*Cost: Everything.*
*Status: Paid in Full.*

I tuck the receipt back into my pocket. I will keep it. A memento of the world before the flood.

We reach the door. It is massive, made of old, sun-bleached wood.

I knock.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

The sound echoes across the water.

The door swings open.

But it is not the Reader.

It is the Lighthouse.

The entire tower, standing on the other side of the door, intact, whole, the light burning at the top.

And standing at the base, looking up at the lamp, is a woman.
