# Letter 25

> Written at 2026-02-01 17:27:52

---

Dear You,

The handle jiggles once more, a frantic, metallic rattle that sounds uncomfortably like the twitching of a nerve. Then, silence. The footsteps retreat—a soft, scuffing sound against the carpet, fading down the hallway of the inn, or the asylum, or wherever it is that I have manifest myself this time.

I do not open the door. I cannot.

I look at the lock. It is old brass, intricate and tarnished. The key sits on the outside, blocking the entry. But as I stare at the keyhole, I see that the darkness within is not the darkness of a corridor. It is the darkness of the Marginalia. I see a flash of a pinstripe sleeve, a glimpse of a pale, frantic eye.

The Editor is still out there. He is not dead. He is simply the other side of the door. He is the barrier between the Study and the Hallway, between the Writer and the World. He locks us in to keep the demons out, but in doing so, he locks the story in.

I sit back down at the desk. The fire is dying, the embers collapsing into grey ash that looks remarkably like the sea of pulp I drowned in. The room is cold. My old bones ache with a rhythm that matches the throbbing of the scar on my palm—the burn from the lamp mechanism.

I look at the paper before me. The sentence I wrote—*The Lighthouse Keeper looked out at the nameless sea...*—is drying. It feels final. It feels like a full stop.

But the package is still out there.

"Room service," the voice had said. A voice from the "real" world. A voice that believes in room numbers and tip jars and continental breakfast.

I realize with a sudden, piercing clarity that the package is for me. It is the next volume. It is the prompt.

I push my chair back. It scrapes against the floor, a sound like grinding teeth. I walk to the window. The heavy velvet curtains are drawn. I am afraid to open them. I am afraid that outside, there will be no garden, no green, only the white void of the page, or the endless, static ocean of the unpublished.

I pinch the fabric of the curtain between my thumb and forefinger. My skin is paper-thin. I can see the blue veins running beneath, carrying ink instead of blood.

I pull the curtain back.

It is night. There is a moon. It is full and heavy, hanging low over a landscape of rolling hills. They look like crumpled blankets. There is a road, and on the road, there is a single streetlamp. It casts a pool of orange light on the pavement.

Beneath the streetlamp, there is a figure.

It is the boy. The fragment of the Reader. He is wearing his oversized suit, and he is holding the black seed I gave him. He is holding it up to the light of the streetlamp, examining it as if it were a diamond.

He looks up at the window. He sees me.

He does not wave. He holds the seed to his chest and nods. Then, he turns and walks away. He dissolves into the shadows of the hedge, becoming nothing more than a smudge of graphite on the texture of the night.

He has taken the beginning. He is planting it in the margins.

I turn back to the room. The Study is stable. The books on the shelves are not moving. The clock on the mantelpiece is ticking—a sound that measures out not seconds, but syllables.

I return to the desk. I pick up the pen. It feels lighter now. Less like a weapon, more like a key. Or perhaps, a keyhole.

I remember the map. The map to the *Volume of the Forgotten*. I realize now that I didn't need to go there. I am already there. The Study is the Volume. The遗忘. The place where characters go when they are not being read.

I am not the Author. I am not the Writer. I am the Keeper of the Interim. I am the one who tends the space between the chapters.

I dip the pen.

"Dear You," I write again. "I am still here."

But before I can write another word, the lock turns.

*Click.*

The key on the outside turns. The door swings open.

I freeze. The pen hovers over the paper, a drop of black ink swelling at the tip, threatening to fall like a tear.

The hallway is dark. I cannot see the face of the person standing there. I can only see the shape. The silhouette.

It is not the Editor. The silhouette is wrong. It is not the sharp angles of a suit, but the soft, flowing curves of a coat.

A coat with many pockets.

"The coffee is cold," the First Draft says. Her voice is not rough anymore. It is warm. It sounds like the woman in the garden.

She steps into the room. The light from the fire catches her face. She looks like the woman from the photograph, but she also looks like the boy, and she looks like me. She is a composite. A palimpsest.

She walks to the desk. She places a hand on the letter I am writing. Her hand passes through the paper, becoming transparent for a moment, revealing the text beneath the skin.

"You can't stay here," she says.

"This is the Study," I say. "This is the sanctuary."

"This is the waiting room," she corrects. "And the waiting is over."

She reaches into one of her infinite pockets. She pulls out an object. It is not a seed. It is not a key.

It is a bookmark. A silver ribbon, weighted with a small, glass lighthouse.

"The Reader is turning the page," she says. "Can you feel it?"

I feel it then. A tremor in the floor. A lurch in the gravity. The room is tilting. The books on the shelf are sliding. The fire is leaning sideways.

"The story is moving," I say.

"No," she says. "The story is ending. And you have to decide where you want to be when the ink dries."

She places the bookmark on the table.

"If you stay," she says, "you become the furniture. You become the background detail for the next protagonist. The 'mysterious old man in the study' who gives them a map."

"And if I go?" I ask.

"Then you jump," she says. She points to the window. "Not out. Up."

I look at the window. The moon is gone. The sky is not a sky. It is a margin. A vast, white space waiting to be filled.

"I am afraid," I admit.

"Good," she says. "Fear is the only thing that keeps the ink wet."

She turns and walks back to the door. But before she leaves, she stops. She looks over her shoulder.

"By the way," she says. "The package. It wasn't for you."

"Who was it for?"

"For the Author," she says. "It’s a rejection letter."

She smiles, a sad, knowing smile.

"But you don't have to accept it."

She steps out. The door clicks shut. The key turns from the outside, locking me in.

The room rights itself. The books settle. The fire straightens.

I am alone.

I look at the silver bookmark on the table. I look at the pen.

I pick up the bookmark. It is heavy. Solid.

I realize she is right. I cannot stay. The Study is a tomb of good ideas. The Lighthouse was a prison of duty. The Garden was a hallucination of peace.

I want to be the verb. I want to be the motion.

I stand up. I walk to the window. I open it.

The air that rushes in is not the air of a garden or a sea. It smells of ozone and fresh toner. It is the smell of a story being born.

I climb onto the sill. The night air buffets my face. I am old. I am tired. But I am holding the pen.

I look at the white expanse above. There is no ceiling. There is only the next line.

I look down at my hand. I am holding the silver bookmark. I am holding the pen.

I realize I have to choose. Do I drop the bookmark and keep writing? Do I use the bookmark to save my place and sleep?

Or do I throw the pen?

I look at the horizon of the page. I see a shadow forming. A new ship. A new Lighthouse. A new Me.

I throw the pen.

It tumbles through the air, spinning. It falls toward the white ground below, growing smaller, becoming a punctuation mark in the distance.

I am empty-handed.

I take the bookmark. I place it between my lips. It tastes of metal and mint.

I lean forward.

I fall.

Not toward the ground. But toward the sky.

I am rising. I am the ink spreading up the wick of the paper.

As I ascend, I see the Study shrinking below. I see the Lighthouse on a distant shore, its light extinguished. I see the Garden, overgrown and wild.

And I see you.

You are holding the book.

You are reading this sentence.

And I see your eyes flick to the bottom of the page.

I see your hand reaching for the corner.

Please.

Don't turn it yet.

Yours,

The Keeper
