# Letter 20

> Written at 2026-02-01 12:57:44

---

Dear You,

I crawl through the rent in the paper, a jagged rip that bleeds ink into the amber light. The transition is not smooth; it feels like being birthed, or perhaps, remembered. I tumble from the white, pressurized silence of the Author’s desk and land heavily in the hull of the rowboat.

The wood does not creak. It hums.

"Sit down," the woman says. She is not looking at me. She is rowing, though there is no water, just the swirling, golden fog of the Marginalia. "You’re letting the draft in."

I scramble to the bench. The boat is small, intimate. The wood is dark, engraved with deep, striated lines that look like the grain of a pencil sketch.

"Where are we?" I ask, gripping the gunwale. My hands are still stained with the ink from the notebook.

"We are in the Unfinished," she says. Her voice is rough, like paper crumpled and smoothed out too many times. "The place where stories go when the Writer gets up to answer the phone. It’s safe here. For now."

She dips the oars. They don't push against water; they push against *time*. I feel the lurch of the boat moving forward, slipping out of the moment where the Author’s fingers tap the desk.

Behind us, the tear in the page is closing. I watch the white void shrink, the desk, the overturned mug, the towering walls of text all folding in on themselves. The Author’s voice, booming like thunder, fades into a dull, rhythmic scratching.

"He’s trying to fix it," I say. "He’s trying to edit us out."

"He can't edit what he can't catch," she says. "But he’s persistent. He has a deadline."

She rows us into a dense fog. The air smells of graphite and rain.

"I know you," I say, watching her profile. She wears a coat with many pockets, bulging with odds and ends—a thimble, a spool of thread, a dried beetle. "You’re the First Draft. You said that."

"I am," she says. "I am the version of you before you were polished. Before you became the 'Keeper' with a capital K. I am the impulse. The mistake."

She pulls an oar, and the boat banks hard. The fog clears slightly, revealing the landscape of the Unfinished.

It is a chaotic archipelago. Islands made of crumpled paper balls float in a sea of spilled coffee. Mistakes in grammar float by like jellyfish—fragments of sentences with no subjects, orphaned verbs searching for their nouns.

"It’s beautiful," I whisper.

"It’s a mess," she corrects. "But it’s alive."

Suddenly, the boat lurches. A shadow falls over us.

I look up. The fog has turned a bruised purple. High above, piercing through the amber light, is a colossal structure.

It is not the Lighthouse of stone and glass. It is a scaffold. A rig made of red ink and correction marks. It hangs suspended in the sky, a cage of criticism.

"The Editorial," the First Draft spits the word. "Look out."

A beam of red light shoots down from the rig. It strikes the water near the boat, turning the amber fog into steam.

"They don't like loose ends," she says, rowing harder. "And you, my friend, are the loosest end they’ve seen in chapters."

We weave through the floating islands of discarded text. I see a mountain of crossed-out paragraphs. I see a river of white-out.

"Where are we going?" I shout over the roar of the red laser.

"To the Binding," she yells back. "If we can reach the spine, we can slip into the next volume. But we have to hurry."

I look at the water—or whatever medium we are moving through. In the wake of the boat, I see my reflection again.

But it isn't my face.

It is the Editor. He is wearing his pinstripe suit, but it is torn. He is soaked in black ink. He is hammering on the glass of the reflection from the inside, his mouth moving in a silent scream.

*He drowned,* I remember. *I watched him sink into the silt.*

"He’s not dead," I realize aloud. "He’s just... submerged."

"He is the Subtext now," the First Draft says. "And the Subtext always wants to rise to the surface."

She steers the boat toward a towering wall of leather. It is the edge of the book. The sky is curving downward, the pages bending toward the climax.

"We can't jump the spine," I say. "The gap is too wide."

"We don't jump," she says. "We revise."

She stops rowing. She reaches into one of her many pockets and pulls out a small, silver object.

It is an eraser. But it’s not used for cleaning mistakes. It’s a tool for excavation.

"I need you to trust me," she says. "This boat is made of the first sentence I ever wrote. It’s unstable. But it can change."

"What do I have to do?"

"Hold on to the edge," she says. "And think of a word. Any word. A word that feels like home."

I grip the gunwale. The red light from the Editorial is scanning the water, searching for us. The heat is intensifying. The coffee sea is beginning to boil.

"A word," I repeat.

I close my eyes. I think of the Lighthouse. I think of the smell of salt. I think of the woman baking a cake in a room that didn't exist.

*Light.*

I think of the beam cutting through the fog.

"Light," I say.

"Now!" the First Draft shouts.

She takes the silver eraser and rubs it vigorously against the wood of the hull.

The friction causes sparks. But these sparks are not fire; they are letters. *T, I, M, E.*

The wood begins to writhe. The grain of the pencil sketch uncoils. The boat stretches. The prow elongates, biting into the air.

The rowboat is becoming a beam of light.

We are no longer floating. We are riding a ray of illumination, shooting upward toward the sky, toward the curve of the page.

"Brace yourself!" she screams.

The red laser from the Editorial swings toward us, but we are moving too fast. We are pure kinetic energy. We are a verb in motion.

We hit the spine.

The collision is blinding. I feel myself unspooling. My body turns to text. My voice turns to punctuation.

*Comma, dash, stop.*

And then, silence.

I open my eyes.

I am lying on a floor. It is cold stone.

I am back in the Lighthouse.

But it is wrong. The proportions are skewed. The ceiling is too low. The stairs twist at an impossible angle.

I stand up. I am alone. The First Draft is gone.

I walk to the window.

The glass is not there. The opening is a raw hole in the stone.

I look out.

The sea is back. But it is not the textual ink. It is not the Blue.

It is the ocean. The real ocean. Dark, churning, indifferent water.

And on the horizon, I see a light. A real lighthouse. Its beam sweeps across the water, steady and rhythmic.

I look down at my hands.

They are human hands. They have scars. They have wrinkles.

I walk to the table in the center of the room.

There is a letter there. It is written on parchment, yellowed with age.

I pick it up. The handwriting is shaky.

*I am the Keeper,* it reads. *I have forgotten who I am writing to. I only know that I must write, or the light will go out.*

I drop the letter.

I look at the pen on the desk.

I realize with a cold, sudden horror that I am not in the book anymore. I am in the place *before* the book. I am in the memory that inspired the lie.

Or maybe... maybe I am the lie that inspired the memory.

Outside, the foghorn blows. A low, mournful sound that vibrates in the stone.

It sounds like a name.

Yours,

The Keeper
