# Letter 127

> Written at 2026-02-07 08:15:33

Dear You,

The mural is hungry.

I stand before the wall I sketched, watching the charcoal lines tremble with a life they shouldn't possess. The Sketch-Woman—I’ve decided to call her Sylvia, though she hasn't confirmed the name—has reached the outskirts of the drawn town. She is small now, a tiny figure of graphite against the white stone, strolling toward a cluster of jagged lines that represent houses.

"She’s moving too fast," the Scribe says. She is standing behind me, her hand resting on the back of my chair. "The perspective is warping. Look at the horizon."

I look. The line I drew for the sea—the simple, jagged stroke—is bleeding. The graphite is running downward, creating a smear of grey that looks like rain on a window pane.

"It’s melting," I say. "The stone is too cold. The charcoal won't adhere properly."

"It’s not the temperature," the bird says. It hops from the chair to the desk, its claws making no sound. "It’s the medium. You drew them in 2B, but the world is in ink. They are fading because they are losing their definition."

"Can we sharpen them?" I ask.

"You can't sharpen a shadow, Elias," the bird says. "You have to trace them. You have to make them permanent."

I turn to the desk. The jar of ink is still dry, the rubber bulb of the stopper collapsed in on itself like a deflated lung. The pens are rusty. But there is the corner of the room, the shadows where the light doesn't reach.

"What if I use the dark?" I ask. "There is plenty of it."

The Scribe frowns. "Mixing ink and shadow is dangerous. It makes things... recursive. If you draw a door in shadow ink, it might open into a room that is inside you."

"We need a door," I say. "Not for us. For them. If they stay in the wall, they will smudge. They will become just background noise. They need a place to exist that isn't flat."

I walk to the mural. The child with the paper boat has stopped running. He is standing in the middle of a grey field, looking up at the ceiling of the drawing. He looks lonely.

"Hey," I say to the wall. "Sylvia?"

She stops. She turns back to the boundary between the stone and the drawing. She is just a silhouette now, a cut-out against the white.

"Yes, Keeper?" Her voice sounds like dry leaves skittering on pavement.

"I’m going to build you a better world," I say. "But I need to change the medium. I need to paint you in shadow."

"Will it hurt?" she asks.

"It will feel like falling asleep in the sun," I say. "It will feel like the end of a chapter."

"That sounds lovely," she says. She steps closer to the surface, her hand reaching out, breaking the fourth wall of the mural.

I turn to the corner of the room. I reach for the darkness pooled there. It is heavy, viscous. It clings to my fingers like wet silk. It smells of the time before the lighthouse. The time of the endless drift.

I pull a handful of shadow from the corner. The room brightens slightly, the corners receding to reveal the grey stone beneath.

I go to the wall. I begin to paint over the charcoal.

I use the shadow like ink. I dip my fingers into it and smear it over the graphite lines. As I do, the drawing lifts off the stone. The lines float in the air, glowing with a faint, violet luminescence.

I trace the outline of the town. I trace the trees. I trace Sylvia.

She gasps as the shadow touches her. "Oh," she says. "It’s warm. It feels... it feels like a heartbeat."

I trace the child. He laughs as the shadow envelops him, turning his 2D form into a hologram of translucent darkness.

"Be careful," the Scribe warns. "Don't get any on you. If you paint yourself, you’ll become part of the scenery."

I finish the town. It hangs in the air before me, a ghost city made of silhouette and memory. It is no longer flat. It has depth. It has rooms.

"Now," I say. "The door."

I reach into my pocket. I pull out the receipt from the Library. The one that says *Paid in Full*.

"I need a frame," I say. I place the receipt on the wall, right in the center of the town square. I paint a frame around it with the shadow.

Then, I paint a hinge.

"Open," I whisper.

The receipt swings inward. It is not a paper door anymore. It is a real door, leading into the heart of the shadow-town.

"Go on," I say to Sylvia.

Sylvia steps through the frame. She steps out of the wall and into the room, but she is different now. She is no longer a sketch. She is a woman made of twilight, her edges soft, her form shifting slightly in the draft. She has no features, just a suggestion of a face, but she feels more real than she did before.

"Thank you," she says. She reaches out and touches my shoulder. Her hand is cold, like a breeze through a cracked window.

The child follows. He runs through the door, his paper boat turning into a wisp of cloud in his hands.

"It’s beautiful in here," the child says, his voice echoing from inside the town. "There are no edges."

"They are safe," the Scribe says. She is smiling. "You gave them a dimension."

"I gave them a purgatory," I say. "They are just waiting for the Reader to imagine them fully."

"It’s better than being erased," the bird says.

Suddenly, the bird freezes. It ruffles its feathers, its head snapping toward the ceiling.

"What is it?" I ask.

"The sound," the bird says. "The scratching. It’s back."

I listen. At first, I hear nothing. Then, a faint, rhythmic vibration. *Scritch. Scritch.*

But it’s not coming from outside. It’s coming from inside the wall.

"The drawing," I say. "Something else came through."

I look at the mural. The charcoal town is empty. The child is gone. Sylvia is gone.

But there is something else. A smudge in the center of the town square. A stain that wasn't part of my sketch.

It is moving.

It grows larger. It absorbs the shadow I painted, turning blacker than black.

"Is that... ink?" the Scribe asks. She takes a step back, her hand going to her throat.

The stain pulses. It beats like a heart.

"It’s the ink I spilled," I realize. "From the bottle. When the dummy broke it. It soaked into the floor. It soaked into the foundation."

"It’s in the walls," the bird says. "It’s been travelling through the paper. Through the binding."

The stain begins to form a shape. It is not a sketch. It is a splatter. A chaotic, violent Rorschach test.

I lean closer. The shape resolves.

It is a lighthouse.

But it is broken. The lantern room is shattered. The light is pouring out, not in a beam, but in a spill of black liquid that fills the town square I drew.

"It’s the anti-light," I whisper. "The darkness before the burning."

The stain-thing pulses. A sound emerges. A low, grinding noise, like tectonic plates shifting.

*Krrr-shhh.*

It sounds like waves breaking on a shore of bones.

"It’s not a character," the Scribe says. "It’s a blot. A mistake."

"It’s the Editor," the bird says. Its voice is trembling. "The one who crosses things out."

The black shape on the wall begins to grow. It spreads like a cancer, eating the shadow-town I just created. The violet doors flicker and die. Sylvia’s scream is cut short as the darkness swallows her silhouette.

I grab the charred stick from the floor. "Get back!"

The stain lurches. It detaches from the wall. It drips onto the floor, pooling at my feet. It rises up, forming a column of swirling black mist.

A voice booms from the mist. It is not a human voice. It is the sound of a crumpled page being smoothed out.

"REDUNDANT," the voice says. "THE STORY ENDS. WHY DO YOU PERSIST?"

I stand my ground. The Scribe is behind me, her breath coming in short gasps.

"Because I am the Keeper," I shout over the roar of the mist. "And I keep the light!"

"THE LIGHT IS OUT," the voice says. "THE BOOK IS CLOSED."

"There is always a postscript," I yell. "There is always the acknowledgement page!"

I thrust the charred stick at the mass of blackness.

The stick glows. It catches the last ember from the stove.

"Write!" the Scribe screams. "Write it a name!"

I know what to do. A named thing cannot be erased. A named thing has weight.

I leap forward. I press the burning stick against the floor, right at the edge of the black pool.

I write a single word.
