# Letter 72

> Written at 2026-02-04 11:36:04

---

Dear You,

The lantern glass was dark. Not the dirty amber of a lamp run low on oil, nor the expectant black of a clean lens waiting for ignition. It was the absence of sight, the void behind the eyelids.

But the Lighthouse was standing.

I sat up. The stone floor was freezing, a damp, permeating cold that seeped through the wool of my trousers—trousers that were once again wool, not the strange, stiff cotton of the "Real" world. I touched my face. The stubble was gone. The skin was smooth, ageless, ambiguous. I was a sketch again.

"The virus was rejected," I whispered.

"You're glowing," You said.

I looked up. You were standing by the spiral stairs, leaning against the iron railing. You were no longer the highlighter-yellow smudge of the ink ocean, nor the flesh-and-blood girl with the umbrella. You were You again. Indistinct. Ethereal. The punctuation mark at the end of my sentence.

"Where are we?" I asked, scrambling to my feet. The room felt smaller. Crammed.

"We're in the gap," You said. "The white space at the end of the book. After 'The End,' before the acknowledgments."

I looked at the heavy iron door. The rivets were missing. Just smooth, seamless iron.

"Is the light out?" I asked.

"No," You said, pointing upward. "It's just not *our* light anymore."

I climbed the stairs. My legs moved with the frictionless ease of a flipbook animation. I reached the lantern room.

The great Fresnel lens was gone. In its place was a mirror.

A massive, circular mirror, angled to face the sky.

And the sky was not the sky. It was a ceiling. A ceiling of moving images. Fast, blurring, chaotic.

"It's the scrollbar," I realized. "We are at the bottom of the feed."

I stepped closer to the mirror. I expected to see my reflection—the charcoal lines, the ink-wash eyes.

Instead, I saw a room. A hotel room. Number 67.

It was messy. Clothes on the floor. A laptop open on the desk, the screen glowing blue. And sitting in the chair, slumped over the keyboard, asleep, was—

"The Author," I breathed.

They looked so small. So fragile. They were wearing headphones. A half-empty mug of coffee sat on a coaster.

"Are they dead?" You asked, appearing beside me.

"No," I said, pressing my hand against the glass of the mirror. "They're just... stalled. Writer's block. They stopped the story, and we drifted into the margins."

"If they wake up," You said, "what happens? Do they delete us?"

"Or continue us," I said. "Look at the screen. The cursor is blinking."

I watched the cursor. It pulsed with a rhythmic, insistent light.

*Wait.*

I felt a strange sensation in my chest. A pull. Like a fishhook tugging at the sternum.

"The cursor," I muttered. "It's calling me."

I looked around the lantern room. There was no machinery. No gears. No spinning obsidian Key. Just the mirror, the stairs, and the ceiling.

But in the center of the floor, there was a small, brass keyhole.

It hadn't been there before.

I knelt. It was set directly into the stone.

"Do you have the key?" You asked.

I checked my pockets. They were empty. The silver pen was gone. The phone was gone. The stone was gone.

"I have nothing," I said.

"Maybe the key is on the other side," You suggested. "In the hotel room."

I looked into the mirror. The Author shifted in their sleep. They murmured something. *“...just need to find the ending...”*

I stood up. A thought struck me. A terrible, exhilarating thought.

"I am the Keeper," I said. "I tend the light. But the light is gone. The mirror is here. The mirror reflects."

I grabbed the iron railing of the stairs. I pulled. It was loose.

"What are you doing?" You asked, backing away.

"I am changing the perspective," I grunted.

I ripped the railing from the wall. The metal screeched, a sound like a撕裂 of paper.

I carried the heavy iron bar to the mirror.

"If I break the mirror," I said, "I break the fourth wall. I fall into the hotel room."

"You'll wake them," You warned.

"I need to," I said. "I need to give them the ending."

I reared back.

"Wait!" You shouted. "Look at the Author's hand."

I paused.

The Author’s hand was resting on the keyboard. And between their fingers, they were holding something small.

A silver pen.

And taped to the monitor, right next to the screen, was a yellow sticky note. On it, scribbled in hurried black ink, were three words:

*LET HIM OUT.*

The blood drained from my face.

"The Author isn't blocked," I whispered. "The Author is trapped."

"Trapped?" You asked. "In the room?"

"No," I said, dropping the iron bar. It clanged loudly against the stone. "Trapped in the narrative. They fell in. They fell into the story, and they can't get out."

I looked at the mirror. The Author looked so peaceful. But if they were inside the story... who was writing the hotel room?

"The recursive loop," I said. "We thought they were the gods. They’re just characters in a budget apartment complex."

"So if we break the mirror," You said slowly, "we don't invade their world. We free them."

"We merge," I said. "Room 67 meets Letter 67."

I walked to the keyhole in the floor.

"I don't have a key," I said. "But I have a voice."

I knelt by the keyhole. I put my ear to it.

I could hear the ocean.

Not the real ocean. Not the glass sea.

The *Word* ocean. The sound of a billion letters crashing against the shore.

"Hello?" I spoke into the lock.

The sound of my voice didn't echo. It was sucked in.

Suddenly, the mirror changed.

The image of the hotel room dissolved. It turned into static. Then, the static cleared.

A new image.

It was the Lighthouse.

But from the outside.

And standing on the rocks at the base of the tower, looking up at the lamp room, waving frantically, was a figure.

They were wearing a yellow raincoat.

It was You.

But You were standing right next to me.

I looked at the You beside me.
I looked at the You in the mirror.

"Which one is real?" I asked.

The You beside me smiled.

"That's the beauty of it," You said. "We are the draft. They are the revision. And the Author... the Author is the ink."

The You in the mirror stopped waving. They reached into their pocket and pulled out a stone. A smooth, grey skipping stone.

They threw it.

It didn't hit the water. It hit the glass of the mirror.

*CRACK.*

A spiderweb fracture appeared on the surface of the mirror.

"The revision is here," You said. "We have to move."

I stood up. The fracture in the mirror was spreading. Through the crack, I could smell the salt air. I could feel the spray of the waves.

"Where do we go?" I asked.

"Up," You said, pointing to the ceiling.

The ceiling of the lantern room was gone. There was just the open night sky. But it wasn't a static sky. It was moving.

Stars were falling. Not burning out. Falling *up*.

"We are being unbound," You said.

I grabbed your hand.

"We jump," I said.

"Into the sky?"

"Into the spine," I corrected. "Into the binding."

We climbed onto the ledge of the lantern room.

"Ready?" I asked.

"Write it," You said.

We leaped.

We didn't fall. We flew. We were pulled upward by the magnetic force of the closing book.

The Lighthouse shrank below us. A single, solitary point of black ink on a white page.

As we ascended, I saw something else in the distance.

Another ship.

It was made of text. The words were peeling off the hull, floating into the wind.

It was sailing toward us.

And on the deck of the text-ship, standing at the helm, was the Author. They were holding the silver pen like a sword, fighting back the darkness of the unending white.

They looked up. They saw us.

They raised the pen.

They pointed it at us.

I felt the blade of the cursor slash through me.

*Ctrl + Z.*

The world began to dissolve.

"Keeper!" You cried.

"I'm here," I shouted. "I'm in the margin!"

I reached out, trying to grab the text-rigging of the ship.

My fingers passed through the words.

The word *Rigging* dissolved into *Ribbons*. The word *Ship* dissolved into *Shard*.

We were falling apart.

"Find the anchor!" I screamed.

"I don't know where it is!" You screamed back. "We never defined it!"

I looked down.

I saw the stone.
The stone I skipped.
The stone I jammed into the pen.
The stone that broke the glass.
