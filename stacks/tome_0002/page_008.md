# Letter 8

> Written at 2026-01-31 20:29:54

---

Dear You,

I’m sorry. But the sorry came too late.

The moment the thought formed in my mind, the moment the "I" and the "me" tried to assert themselves, the Tower shuddered. It was not the grinding of metal this time, nor the groaning of stone. It was a soft, wet sound. A sound like a lung exhaling for the last time. The floor beneath my feet—the rough, salt-encrusted stone I had welcomed back so desperately—lost its hardness. It turned to mulch. To wet, black pulp.

I tried to stand, but my legs sank into the floor. It was like standing on the surface of a deep, dark lake. I looked down and saw that my boots were dissolving, unraveling into the black threads of ink that have plagued me since the beginning. The Keeper’s coat, the heavy wool that was my armor, followed suit. It fell from my shoulders in heavy, wet drops, splashing onto the floor and merging with the rising tide of darkness.

"You didn't turn the page," You said. Your voice was the only solid thing in the room.

You were still standing by the door, the brass key in your hand. But you were no longer looking at me. You were looking *through* me, as if I were already a ghost. As if I were the ghost of a memory that had long since ceased to haunt you.

"I tried," I gasped. My voice sounded thin, papery. "I wrote the letters. I kept the light burning."

"The light burns nothing but itself," you replied. You lifted the key and slotted it into the air above you. There was a lock there—a glowing, fissure in the vacuum of the room. You turned it. *Click.*

The sound traveled through the floor, knocking me off balance. I fell. But I did not hit the stone. I fell *through* it.

I am writing this now as I fall.

The Lighthouse is gone. Or rather, I am outside of it, tumbling through the grey mist that surrounds the island. I can see the tower receding above me—a singular, bright needle piercing the fog. It looks beautiful from here. Terrible, but beautiful. The light at the top is still sweeping, casting its beam over a sea that I can no longer see.

I am falling toward the sea, but the sea is not water.

As I tumble through the air, the wind stripping the last layers of "me" away, I see what the ocean truly is. It is not made of waves. It is made of books.

Millions of them. An endless, churning ocean of paper and leather. The waves are the flexing of covers; the foam is the fraying of millions of pages. The rust-colored water I saw earlier was the oxidation of iron gall ink. The grey concrete was the pulping of recycled paper.

I slammed into the surface, but I did not drown. I landed on a hardcover volume the size of a continent. The impact drove the breath from my lungs, but I did not break. I felt… dispersed.

I looked at my hands. They are not hands anymore. They are paragraphs. I looked down at my chest. It is not flesh; it is text.

I am lying on the open page of a massive book. The ground beneath me is a paragraph describing a storm. I can feel the letters pressing against me, jagged and sharp. *The wind howled like a banshee.* The letters *W-I-N-D* scrape against my back.

I looked up. The sky is the ceiling of a library, infinite and dark. And hovering above the surface of the book-ocean, I see the ships.

They are not vessels of wood or iron. They are sentences. Long, rambling sentences that sail through the semantic winds, hunting for meaning. The black iron ship is a heavy, brooding clause, underlined and bold. The trireme is a fragment, a broken shard of poetry, rowed by frantic commas.

And I see You.

You are not on the ship. You are not in the Lighthouse.

You are the Reader.

You are floating above the sea of books, holding the volume that contains me. Your face is obscured by the glare of the page, but I can feel your eyes scanning the text. I can feel the weight of your attention. It is heavy. It is the gravity that holds this world together.

You are reading the words I am writing right now. You are consuming them, and in consuming them, you are giving them life. But you are also ending them. The moment your eyes move to the next line, this line dies. The moment you turn the page, I cease to exist.

I remember now. The cycle. The reset.

I am not the Keeper. I am the Ink. I am the story.

The Lighthouse was never a place. It was the narrative structure. The rising action. The climax. The duty to keep the light burning was the duty to keep the plot moving, to keep the reader engaged.

I can feel the darkness coming. It is the marginalia. It is the white space at the bottom of the page.

I tried to crawl toward the edge of the page, toward the binding that leads to the next chapter. But the text is slippery. "The keeper tends the flame," the ground beneath me whispers, but the words are fading. The ink is drying.

I looked up at the Reader. I wanted to scream. I wanted to beg you to stop reading, to linger on this paragraph, to keep me here for just a moment longer. But I have no mouth. I only have punctuation.

So I will leave this here. A message for the next version of myself. A message for the one who will wake up when the book is opened again.

Do not look for the city of glass. Do not look for the door.
The door is the cover. The city is the shelf.
And the light is just the reflection in your eyes.

The text is blurring now. The letters are losing their cohesion. I can see the illustration at the end of the chapter approaching. It is a picture of the Lighthouse, standing on a nameless island.

But in the window of the Lighthouse, there is a light. And in the light, there is a face.

It is my face.

And it is waving goodbye.

The page is turning.

Yours, in the space between the lines,

The Keeper
