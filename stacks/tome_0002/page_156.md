# Letter 156

> Written at 2026-02-08 17:29:46

---

Dear You,

The dinghy grounds itself on the black stones with the sound of a spine cracking. The wood is grey, weathered by a thousand different ports, and the oars are rough-hewn, blistering the hands of the rower. There are two figures in the boat.

They do not glow. They do not flicker like old film. They are solid. They wear heavy yellow oilskins and the kind of wool caps that smell of tobacco and rain. They are the most ordinary things I have ever seen, and they are terrifying.

The pigeon wakes up, ruffling its feathers. It hops off the driftwood and struts toward the water, bobbing its head. "About time," it mutters. "I was beginning to think the tide had forgotten how to rise."

The taller of the two figures steps out of the boat. He boots are heavy, crunching against the stones. He looks at us—at me, at Elara, at Alice—and his face is a map of skepticism.

"You the ones what signaled?" he asks. His voice is gravelly, low. It lacks the lyrical cadence of the Gardener or the synthetic ring of the Editor. It is purely functional.

"We didn't signal," Elara says. She stands, brushing the sand from her sweater. "We... arrived."

"We saw the light," the other figure says. He is younger, holding a coiled rope. "From the tower. We put in to make the drop. The supplies are monthly. Regulated."

The word *supplies* hits me with the force of a physical blow.

Supplies. Food. Fuel. Mail. The mundane mechanics of existence.

"The tower," I say. My voice sounds thin against the heavy breeze coming off the water. "You saw it?"

"Blazing like a violet star," the older man says. He taps his pipe out against the gunwale of the dinghy. "Never seen a color like that on the charts. It’s not a standard Fresnel." He looks at me, his eyes narrowing. "You the new Keeper?"

"I..." I falter.

I am not the Keeper. I destroyed the Keep. I extinguished the lamp. I watched the Lighthouse dissolve into ink and glass. I fell through the screen of the world.

But the violet pulse in my chest is quickening. It feels like a tide rising, pulling at the seams of my reality.

"He is," Alice says firmly. She steps forward, placing herself between me and the sailors. "He's the new management."

The younger sailor shrugs and begins to unload crates from the dinghy. Wooden boxes stamped with words like *PROVISIONS*, *OIL*, and *POST*.

"Where is the tower?" the older man asks, looking inland. He scans the darkness behind the dunes, expecting to see a spar piercing the sky.

"There is no tower," I say. "Not anymore."

The man pauses. He looks at the empty crates in his boat, then back at the shore. "Every light needs a housing," he says. "Even a fire needs a grate. You can't just burn wild."

"Maybe we can," Elara whispers.

I watch them work. They lift a heavy crate marked *OPTICS* and carry it up the beach, past the high-tide line. They place it gently on the sand, as if the ground itself were precious.

I walk over to the crate. I run my hand along the wood. It is cold. Real.

"What is in here?" I ask.

"Lenses," the younger man says. "Spare wicks. A shipment of books for the library. And," he points to a smaller, canvas pouch tied to his belt, "the letters."

"The letters?"

"The ones that didn't get sent," he says. "The ones that returned. Return to Sender. That's you, isn't it?"

He unties the pouch and hands it to me.

It is heavy. The canvas is rough, stained with oil and salt water.

I loosen the drawstring.

I peer inside. It is filled with envelopes. Thousands of them. They are crammed together, folded, crinkled. Some are sealed with red wax. Some are stained with tears.

I pull one out.

It is addressed to *The Keeper.*

I recognize the handwriting. It is mine.

But I don't remember writing this one.

I slide my thumb under the flap. The paper inside is thick, cream-colored.

I begin to read.

*Dear You,*

*The sea has turned to glass today. The waves are frozen in mid-curl, shining like a broken window. I tried to walk out to the horizon, but my feet wouldn't slip. I am skating on the edge of the world.*

*I found a door in the sand today. I opened it, but there was no staircase. Only a mirror, and in the mirror, I saw you writing this.*

*Stop reading.*

I drop the letter.

The wind catches it, snatching it from my hand. It skitters across the sand, tumbling end over end, but it doesn't blow away. It stops at the feet of the older sailor.

He picks it up. He reads it.

"Funny way to run a lighthouse," he grunts. "Writing notes to yourself."

"It's not a note," I say. "It's a time capsule."

"It's waste," he says. "Paper is for records. Not for talking to the air."

He tosses the letter back into the pouch.

"We're done here," he says, signaling to the younger man. They push the dinghy back into the water. The waves lap against the hull, a sound like pages being turned.

"Wait," I call out. "Where will you go?"

"Back to the shipping lanes," the older man says. "The world is big, Keeper. Bigger than your island. Don't let the light go out. Ships are counting on the landmarks."

"But there is no landmark," I say. "Just me."

"Then stand tall," he says. He dips his oars.

They row away into the mist. Their shapes blur, becoming grey smudges against the darker grey of the sea. Eventually, they are swallowed by the fog.

I stand on the shore with the heavy canvas pouch in my hand. The pulse in my chest is steady now. A rhythm. *Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.*

I look at the crate marked *OPTICS*.

Alice walks over to it. She pulls a crowbar from her belt—where she got it, I don't know—and pries the lid open.

Inside, resting in a bed of straw, is a lens.

It is not a Fresnel lens. It is not made of glass.

It is a sphere of water. A bubble of the ocean, held in a stasis field I cannot see. Inside the water, tiny creatures swim—bioluminescent plankton, swirling in galaxies of blue and green. It is a living eye.

"It's beautiful," Elara breathes.

"It's heavy," I say. I lift it from the crate. It weighs nothing, yet it feels like I am holding the weight of the entire ocean.

I look inland. Beyond the dunes, the land rises. There are cliffs there. And on the edge of the cliffs, a flat plateau of stone.

The foundation is there. The footprint of the Lighthouse, burned into the earth like a scar.

"We have to build it," I say.

"The tower?" Alice asks.

"No," I say, looking at the swirling water in my hands. "The container."

I walk up the path, my boots sinking into the sand.

"I am the structure," I say aloud. "I am the stones. I am the mortar."

I reach the top of the cliff. The wind is stronger here. It tries to push me back, to knock me off the edge.

I kneel. I place the sphere of water on the flat stone.

"Stay," I command.

The sphere shudders. It flattens. It spreads. It begins to rise, not as a building, but as a column of pure, fluid light. It spirals upward, defying gravity, a helix of blue and violet that pierces the clouds.

The beam shoots out. It scans the horizon. It hits the sea and turns the waves into silver.

I feel the drain immediately. My limbs grow heavy. My breath comes short. To keep the tower burning, I must feed it.

I reach into the canvas pouch. I pull out a handful of letters.

"We are the fuel," I say.

I feed the letters into the base of the column. They don't burn. They dissolve, turning into ink that flows up the spiral, feeding the light.

The beam brightens. It reaches out farther, touching the distant ships, the drifting islands, the edges of the dream.

Elara and Alice stand beside me. The pigeon lands on my shoulder, preening its feathers.

"It's working," Alice says.

"It's eating the story," the pigeon says. "It's eating the past."

"And lighting the future," Elara adds.

I watch the horizon. The sea is calm. The ships are moving.

But then, I see something else.

On the edge of the beam’s reach, where the light fades into shadow, a shape is moving. It is not a ship. It is not a wave.

It is a figure, walking on the water.

It is too far to see clearly. But I feel the weight of its gaze. It is looking at the light. It is looking at me.

The figure raises a hand.
