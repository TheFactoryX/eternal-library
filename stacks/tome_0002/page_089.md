# Letter 89

Dear You,

...say, turning slowly, your voice swallowed by the vast, glass quiet of this place. "He kept the blueprints for everything he ever planned to grow."

I stand up, the absence of my coat making me feel like a manuscript missing its cover—exposed, raw, perilously light. The air here is still, but the ground beneath my boots vibrates with a low, resonant frequency, like the hum of a finger circling the rim of a wine glass. It is the sound of potential energy waiting to shatter.

"Look at the soil," I whisper.

We are standing on a mosaic. The ground is not dirt, but a tessellation of ceramic tiles—shards of broken mirrors, crushed marbles, and fragments of stained glass, all grouted together with a silver paste that glints like mercury. In the cracks between the tiles, small, glowing fibers pulse rhythmically, pushing up the light from the loom far below.

"The raw material," I say. "The Silica."

We walk deeper into the garden. The glass trees tower above us, their branches fracturing the dim light into spectral rainbows. There are no leaves here, only lenses. Thousands of them, focusing and reflecting, turning the garden into a thousand eyes.

"Do you hear that?" You ask, stopping by a tree blooming with wide, spherical blooms.

I listen. At first, there is only the ringing silence. Then, beneath it, a sound like ocean static. A roar of white noise.

"The Shelf," I realize. "We are still inside the library. This is just the... the quiet corner. The reference section."

"References to what?" You ask, reaching out to touch a spherical flower.

As your finger makes contact with the glass, the bloom flares with a sudden image. A projection of a woman’s face, flickering and blue, distorted by the curve of the lens.

"Don't touch the irises," I warn, pulling your hand back. "They are memories. They’ll burn you."

We move cautiously. The path winds through a grove of twisted, spiraling sculptures that look like frozen DNA strands.

"Where is the seed, Elias?" You ask. "The script said you planted them."

I stop. I look at my hands. They are covered in dust from the catwalk, flecks of rusted iron and red ink.

"I don't remember planting anything," I admit, the confession tasting like ash. "I think... I think I am the seed."

I look up. The ceiling of the warehouse is invisible, lost in a haze of glass dust. But hanging from the darkness, suspended by wires as thin as hair, are fruit.

They are pears. But they are made of heavy, green cut glass. They catch the light and glow with an inner emerald fire.

"The Crystal Pears," I breathe. "From the story he never finished. 'The Orchard of Unspoken Words.'"

"They are ripe," You say.

I reach up. My fingers brush the cold, smooth surface of a glass pear. It is heavy. It pulls the branch down slightly.

"If I pick it," I say, "I finish the sentence."

"Do it," You urge. "We need sustenance. We are starving for narrative."

I grip the stem. It is sharp, cutting into my thumb. I twist.

*Snap.*

The pear comes free in my hand. Immediately, the branch above it withers, turning into a shower of grey sand that pours onto the tile floor.

"Consequences," You murmur.

I lift the pear to my lips. It smells like ozone and rain. I bite down.

*Crunch.*

My teeth meet the glass. But instead of shattering, the pear yields. It is soft inside. The flesh is a cool, glowing liquid.

I swallow.

A shockwave runs through me. It is not a taste. It is a memory. I am standing on a cliff. I am ten years old. I am holding a kite. The string snaps. The kite is gone.

"I remember," I gasp, clutching the pear. "I remember the loss."

"Eat it all," You command. "Consume the tragedy."

I devour the pear. When it is gone, I feel stronger. The hollow feeling in my chest—the one left by the loss of the coat and the key—is filling up with jagged crystal.

"We need more," I say. "We need enough weight to anchor us."

We move through the orchard, plucking the glass fruit. A pomegranate made of ruby shards. A peach of translucent amber. We eat until our jaws ache from the cold, until our stomachs are full of heavy, shining stones.

"We are gluttons," You say, wiping juice from your chin. It glows on your skin.

"We are real," I say. "Pain makes you real. Weight makes you real."

Suddenly, the ground shifts.

*Crunch.*

The tiles beneath our feet are grinding together.

"He’s shaking the shelf," I realize. "The Reader is looking for a book on gardening."

"Quick," You say. "Through there."

You point to the far end of the garden. There is an archway woven from living willow branches, but the leaves are sheets of onion-skin paper. A soft light spills from it.

We run. The glass trees shatter around us, exploding into sand as the shelf tilts violently to the left.

"Gravity is changing!" I shout.

We are sliding now, skidding across the mosaic tiles, tumbling toward the paper willow arch.

We crash through the archway.

We land on a surface that feels like... velvet.

We stand up.

We are in a gallery.

The walls are lined with framed paintings. But the frames are empty. The canvases are blank white.

And standing in the center of the room is an easel.

And on the easel is a portrait.

It is unfinished.

The face is sketched in charcoal—a rough outline of eyes, a nose, a mouth. But the rest is blank.

I walk toward it. The figure in the portrait is wearing a heavy coat. He is holding an iron key.

"It's me," I say. "Or the shadow of me."

A table sits next to the easel. On the table is a palette and a brush.

The palette is covered in wet paint. But the colors are wrong. There is no red, no blue.

The paint is labelled: *Tuesday. *Regret. *The Sound of Traffic.

"He paints with time," You say. "He paints with emotions."

"And he left us unpainted," I say.

I pick up the brush. The handle is made of bone. The bristles are stiff.

"What are you doing?" You ask.

"I'm finishing the portrait," I say. "If the image is complete, maybe the frame becomes a door."

I dip the brush into a pot of black paint labeled *The Void.*

I step up to the canvas. I look at the sketch of the face. It is pleading. It is screaming for definition.

I raise the brush.

But before I can touch the canvas, the head in the sketch moves.

The charcoal eyes blink.

"No!" I shout, stepping back.

The charcoal mouth opens.

"Who are you?" the voice asks. It sounds like dry leaves skittering on pavement.

"I am the Keeper," I say.

"There is no Keeper," the voice rasps. "There is only the Writer. And the Writer is tired. The Writer is putting down the pen."

"The sketch is speaking," You say, backing away. "It's gaining autonomy."

"He can't," I say. "He's two-dimensional."

"I am becoming three," the face says. The charcoal lines begin to thicken, to bleed out into the white space, forming tendrils of black smoke. "I am eating the negative space."

I look at the hand in the portrait. It is gripping the key.

"You have the key," I say to the face. "Give it to me."

"It locked me in here," the face says. "It locked me in the sketch. I was meant to be the protagonist. But he forgot to color me in. He left me in the margins. And now I am hungry."

The charcoal tendrils lash out from the canvas, striking the easel, the floor, the ceiling. They are searching. Feeling.

"Run," You yell.

We turn to run back through the paper archway.

But the archway is gone.

In its place is a wall of solid brick. A brick wall painted on the canvas, but now real.

"Trap," I say.

The charcoal tendrils wrap around the easel, pulling it toward the canvas. The portrait is devouring itself, turning the easel into raw material.

I look at the brush in my hand. I still have it.

"Paint!" You scream. "Paint the door!"

I spin around. The wall is blank white canvas.

I have no paint. Only the black void on the brush.

I slam the brush against the wall.

I draw a rectangle.

I pour my will into the stroke. I think of the Lighthouse. I think of the heavy door, the iron hinges, the salt air.

*Stroke. Stroke. Stroke.*

A black rectangle forms on the white wall.

But it doesn't open.

The charcoal smoke from the portrait is coiling around my ankles. It is cold. It smells like erasers.

"It's not working!" I cry. "The paint is just ink!"

"Make it real!" You shout, pushing against the smoke. "Use a memory!
