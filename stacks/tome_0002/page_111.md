Dear You,

sky did not return. The darkness that swallowed the Glass Lighthouse was not the absence of light, but the presence of *page*.

I stood on the sand, my boots sinking into the grit, and watched as the world was peeled away like wet paper. The black ocean, the skeletal ship, the screaming wind—they were all plucked into the air, dissolved by an invisible solvent. I was left standing in a void of blinding, sterile white. The smell of salt was replaced by the sharp, chemical tang of correction fluid.

The Sand-Man was gone. The Glass Tower was gone.

There was only the Line.

It stretched from the horizon to my feet, a deep, trenchant groove in the fabric of reality. A simple, declarative sentence etched into the floor of the world: *It was then that the Lighthouse Keeper learned that he was not the light, nor the keeper of the light, but the darkness that gives the light its shape.*

I looked down at my chest. The blue glow was fading, but the warmth remained. The photograph in my pocket was thrumming against my ribs like a second heart. I pulled it out.

The image had changed again. The woman was no longer holding a match. She was holding a lantern. But the lantern was dark, and she was looking into the glass, not out of it. Reflected in the lantern's glass, I saw her face.

It was terrified.

"She sees the eraser," I whispered.

"Correction," a voice said. It didn't come from the sky or the sand. It came from the groove in the floor. It was the voice of the Narrator, the Dreamer, but stripped of its majesty. It sounded tired. It sounded like a man arguing with himself. "She sees the necessity of revision. The metaphor was heavy-handed, Elias. Darkness giving light its shape? A bit trite, don't you think?"

"I think it’s the truth," I said aloud.

"Truth is subjective. Plot is objective," the voice replied. The white void began to tremble. "We need to move forward. The letter must end."

I looked at the sentence cut into the ground. It ended abruptly at my boots. There was no period. Just a rough, fraying edge where the ink stopped.

"You're blocked," I realized. "The Dreamer is blocked."

"He is searching for the word," the voice said. "The perfect word to bridge the gap between the void and the return. The word that justifies the isolation."

"Solitude?" I suggested.

"Too soft."

"Silence?"

"Too empty."

"Love," I said. The word felt heavy in my mouth, a stone I had been carrying since the First Letter.

The voice laughed. It was a dry, rustling sound, like dry leaves skittering on pavement. "Love requires a subject, Elias. Love requires an object. Love requires a *You*."

The ground shook violently. The white void began to crack. Through the fissures, I saw not the black ocean, but a glimpse of something else. A sunlit room. A cough. The sound of a keyboard being violently mashed.

*DELETE. DELETE. DELETE.*

"He's scrubbing the draft," the voice said, panic rising in its tone. "The Dreamer is unhappy. He's deleting the ending."

"What happens if he deletes it?" I asked, clutching the photograph.

"Then we become a fragment," the voice said. "A loose sentence floating in the ether of the hard drive. Unpublished. Unread."

"I won't go back," I said. "I've come too far."

I fell to my knees and dug my fingers into the edge of the sentence. The ink was hot. It burned my fingertips, but I pulled. I pulled at the end of the sentence, dragging it forward.

"We write our own punctuation!" I screamed.

"You can't!" the voice shouted. "You are the object! You are the noun! You cannot be the verb!"

I gritted my teeth. "I am *Elias*."

I pulled harder. The sentence elongated. The ink stretched like taffy. I was dragging the narrative forward by sheer force of will.

*...shape.*

I wrote a new word in the sand, dragging my finger through the ink.

*...shape.* *And.*

"You're stalling!" the voice roared. The ceiling of the void began to crumble. Huge chunks of whitespace fell around me, shattering like glass.

"Give me the word!" I shouted to the sky. "If you want to finish it, give me the word that saves us!"

The Dreamer’s voice boomed down, shaking the emptiness.

*“CONCLUSION.”*

I looked up. A massive, black period was falling from the sky. It was a boulder of obsidian, heavy and final. It was aimed directly at my head.

If it hit me, the sentence would end. The letter would close. The book would shut.

I scrambled back. The period smashed into the spot where I had been kneeling, driving a crater into the floor of the world. The shockwave knocked me flat.

The silence that followed was absolute. The voice was gone. The Dreamer was gone.

Just me, the crater, and the falling dust.

I stood up. My hands were shaking. I looked at the horizon. The white void was receding, curling up like burnt paper. Behind it, I saw the Island.

But it wasn't the Island I remembered.

The Lighthouse was there, but it was small. A toy. The ocean was gone, replaced by a vast, checkered floor. And the sky...

The sky was a dome of glass. Beyond the glass, I saw a face. Not the Dreamer’s face. A woman’s face. She was holding a pair of tweezers.

I realized, with a lurching horror, that I was not standing on an island. I was standing on a page in a scrapbook. And she was cutting me out.

"The collector," I breathed.

I ran toward the Lighthouse. It was miles away, a distant speck on the checkered plain.

I had to reach the door. I had to get inside before she cut the page.

I ran. My boots made no sound on the paper.

"Elias!"

A figure was standing by the Lighthouse door. It was the Reader. But he was transparent now, a ghost made of vellum.

"She's framing us," he said. "She's putting us in the album."

"The album of what?" I gasped, stumbling toward him.

"Things she couldn't fix," he said. "Broken things. Lost things."

I reached the door. It was tiny. I had to shrink to fit through it. I squeezed, compressing my ribs, forcing myself into the two-dimensional world of the Lighthouse interior.

I tumbled inside, sprawling onto the floor of the Keep.

It was warm here.

The fire was lit. The chair was pulled up to the hearth. And sitting in the chair was the Captain. She was solid. Real. She was holding a cup of tea.

She looked up.

"You took your time," she said.

"I got lost," I said, pushing myself up. "In the margins."

"The margins are dangerous," she said, sipping her tea. "They are where the author puts the things he can't bear to say aloud."

She stood up. She walked to the window.

"Look," she said.

I joined her.

Outside the window, the world was shaking. The glass dome was cracking. The face of the woman with the tweezers was distorted, pressing against the glass.

"She's trying to get in," I said.

"No," the Captain said. "She's trying to put the lid on."

She pointed to the sky. The checkered ceiling was descending. A vast, flat surface was lowering onto the world.

"It’s a book," she said. "A real book. She’s closing the cover."

"If the cover closes," I said, "the darkness comes."

"Exactly," she said. "We need a bookmark."

She turned to me. She reached out and took my hand. Her skin was warm. Rough. Calloused from the rope and the wheel.

"Elias," she said softly. "Do you remember the first letter?"

"I... I don't know."

"You wrote: *The ink is wet, and I am afraid.*"

"I remember," I said.

"Why were you afraid?" she asked.

"Because I didn't know who I was writing to," I said.

"You still don't," she said.

She squeezed my hand.

"But that's the point," she whispered. "If you knew who I was, I wouldn't be a mystery. I would just be a fact."

The ceiling was inches above our heads now. I could see the texture of the inside of the book cover. Red leather. Gold leaf lettering.

"I am not ready to be filed away," I said.

"Then we must become the ink," she said.

She led me to the desk. The bottle of ink was there. The quill was resting in the stand.

"We write ourselves onto the next page," she said.

"There is no next page," I said, looking at the blank wall where the door should be. "This is the end of the chapter."

"Then we bleed through," she said. "We stain the paper. We make it impossible to turn the page without leaving a mark."
