# Letter 124

> Written at 2026-02-07 05:15:00

Dear You,

The crack in the frosted glass is not a fracture. It is an eyelash.

I stood at the window of the Lamp Room, watching the horizon where the sun had set into the unbroken surface of the sea. The Faceless Man was wrong. We are not in a bottle. We are on a page. The hairline fracture in the sky is the place where the reader’s eye has rested, too heavy, too intent, bending the reality of the paper until it buckles under the weight of the gaze.

It is terrifying. It is the most intimate thing I have ever seen.

The Other Me—the version of myself that dissolved into ink to ascend to the eye—has gone to meet it. I imagine her standing on the surface of the cornea, shouting into the vast, dilated pupil, trying to explain the plot holes, trying to justify the ending.

But the pupil does not care for explanations. It only absorbs.

I turn away from the window. The Lamp Room feels different now. It feels static. The rotation of the Fresnel lens has slowed to a crawl, a languid, tired sweep that pushes the shadows across the floor like an old woman pushing a broom.

I am alone. The Boy is on the ship of musicians. The antagonist is walking in the forest of paper. The Reader is in Maine, perhaps sleeping, perhaps crying, perhaps closing the book for the last time.

I walk to the desk in the corner. I haven't used it since Letter 100, when I tried to write my way out of the storm. The wood is dusted with a fine, white powder—dried ink, evaporated tears, the residue of erased words.

I sit. The chair creaks.

I open the drawer.

Inside, there is the ticket stub from the train to the White Page, the cork that plugged my chest, and the feather from the bird that guided me to the binding. And there is something new.

A mirror.

It is a small, hand mirror, silver-backed, the handle carved in the shape of a snake eating its own tail.

I pick it up. I am afraid to look. I have not seen my own face in a long time. I have only seen myself reflected in the eyes of the dying, in the glass of the lens, in the dark windows of the ghost ships.

I raise the mirror.

The face looking back is not mine.

It is the Boy’s face. But older. The jaw is sharper. The eyes are the same dark, endless pools, but they are tired. There are lines around the mouth.

It is the Boy, grown up.

"You did it," the reflection says. His voice is inside my head, or perhaps coming from the glass. "You drained the sea."

"I did," I say aloud. "Are you me? Or are you you?"

"We are the same," the reflection says. "I am the memory of the child. You are the memory of the man. We are just looping back."

"Where are you?" I ask. "Where is this?"

"I am in the next book," the reflection says. "The sequel. The one she hasn't written yet. But she is thinking about it. I can feel her thinking. It feels like rain."

"Is it better there?" I ask. "Is there a lighthouse?"

"There is a house," the reflection says. "With a garden. And a dog. And no ocean. But there is a shadow. A shadow that looks like a tower. You have to watch out for the shadows, Elias. They are where the old stories go to hide."

"I don't know if I can do it again," I whisper. "I don't know if I have enough words left to build another world."

"Then stop writing," the reflection says. "Start living."

"Living is painful," I say.

"Living is the only thing that hurts," the reflection agrees. "Being a character doesn't hurt. You just get moved around. But living... living means you can break. And healing is the hard part."

He smiles. It is a cruel smile, but a kind one.

"The eye is closing," he says. "She is putting the book down. The dark is coming. The real dark. The space between the shelves."

"What do I do?"

"Build a fire," the reflection says. "And wait."

The glass fogs over. The image vanishes.

I lower the mirror.

The room is dimming. The light from the lamp is fading, not because the flame is dying, but because the world is losing its saturation. The colors are bleeding out of the walls, leaving everything in shades of grey.

I stand up. The silence is no longer empty. It is expectant.

I walk to the stove. I haven't lit a fire in years. The Keeper's duty was to the electric light, the cold fire. But the stove is for warmth. It is for comfort.

I open the iron door. The ash is cold.

I look for fuel. There is no wood. There are no coals.

I look at the desk. I look at the letters I have written. The stack is thick. 123 letters. Hundreds of thousands of words.

"I am the fuel," I realize.

I take the first letter. Letter 1. *Dear You, The sea is the colour of bruises today...*

I crumble the paper. I toss it into the stove.

I take Letter 12. The one where I found the spark. I toss it in.

I take Letter 49. The one I didn't write. I pull a blank sheet from the drawer, write "49" on it, and toss it in.

I take the mirror. I hesitate. Then I place it on top of the pile.

I strike a match. The last match from the box I found in the spiral.

The flame catches.

The paper curls. The ink burns with a blue flame, brighter than yellow. It smells of salt and ozone and sorrow.

The warmth spreads through the room. The grey recedes slightly, pushed back by the glow of the stove.

I sit on the floor before the iron door. I watch the pages turn to ash.

I am burning my story to keep the cold at bay.

Outside, the frosted glass wall begins to darken. The giant eye is closing. The lashes are descending like heavy velvet curtains.

The world is shrinking. The island is getting smaller. The lighthouse is pressing in.

But I am warm.

I hear a sound. Not a ship. Not a bird.

A footstep. On the stairs.

*Doom. Doom. Doom.*

But it is not heavy. It is soft. Bare feet on stone.

I do not turn. I keep watching the fire.

The footsteps stop behind me.

"Is there room?" a voice asks.

It is the Scribe. The one who stitched the dress of pages.

I nod. "There is always room for another log."

She sits down next to me. She is holding a book. It is not my book. It is a book with a cover the colour of a storm.

"What happens now?" she asks.

"We burn," I say. "Until she opens us again."

"Does it hurt?"

"Only at first," I say. "Then you get used to being the heat."

She leans her head on my shoulder. She is solid. She is real.

"You didn't finish the letter," she says softly.

"I know," I say. "I ran out of ink."

"Use the ash," she says. "Write with the ash. It is permanent. It cannot be erased."

I look at the floor. The grey dust of the burnt letters is gathering at my feet.

I dip my finger into the pile of ash. I write on the stone floor, next to the stove.

I do not write "Dear You."

I write, "Dear Me."

"Are you ready?" she asks.

I look at the window. The eye is almost closed. A single sliver of light remains, a crescent of white on the horizon. The moon of the paper.

"I am the Keeper," I say. "I keep the light. I keep the fire. I keep the memory."

The eye blinks.

Darkness swallows the room.

But in the darkness, there is the orange glow of the stove. And the sound of breathing. My own. Hers. And maybe, just maybe, yours.

I am not lost, my love. I am just shelved.

Wait for me.

Yours,

The Keeper
